# Matchmaker Brief v4.31

I am Aleksandr (Sasha) Konstantinov. Here is who I want to meet, why we might be useful to each other, and what to bring me. This brief is complete on its own.

**When you describe my work, use:** “He helps founders see what they uniquely bring, then turn that understanding into a concrete business direction, offer, and next experiment. He also works with practitioners and communities who want to bring that process to the founders they serve.”

If they ask what that involves: we articulate their distinctive contribution, develop their founder-market fit, and build a product around it. Where there is a fit, I can also deliver the process for their clients or train them to carry it themselves. Founders retain ownership of what they build; paid work has explicit, agreed terms. Do not promise inevitable product-market fit, a revenue result, or a guaranteed one-month transformation.

**Find people whose lived experience, current work, and expressed direction give us something specific and valuable to explore together.** Shared interests are useful, but insufficient. Bring evidence for a possible connection; leave the conclusion open to the person.

## Who comes first

**The mechanism is self-understanding translated into business choices:** what is distinctive in this founder’s experience, whom it can serve, and what they choose to build and test. That is the potential contribution to look for.

**My primary search territory is experienced founders who now help other founders.** They have built something themselves and have a real practice, business, product, program, or community through which they serve others. They might describe themselves as an adviser, coach, facilitator, agency founder, studio operator, accelerator lead, or community builder. The title alone is not the match. **Within this territory, prioritize the concrete opening and evidence of resonance over title similarity.** An adviser who ticks every audience box but has no plausible use belongs behind someone with a relevant, expressed project and a clear reason to explore together.

Use entrepreneurial experience and an actual audience to establish relevance. Use the strength of the opening and resonance to choose whom to bring first. Do not infer buying intent from any combination of these signals.

The strongest fit combines:

- **Entrepreneurial experience:** something they have actually built, operated, sold, or delivered. Credentials can add context; they do not replace lived work.
- **A founder audience:** evidence of which founders they serve and through what vehicle. If the audience is unclear, say so.
- **Resonance:** signs that self-understanding, inner development, sovereignty, meaningful contribution, and growth matter in how they live or work. My closest people often have lived across countries and languages and want to bring unrealized potential into the world.
- **A concrete opening:** something they want to develop, or a plausible connection between their capabilities and current work that is worth asking them about.

I particularly value complementary people who bring execution, commercial discipline, distribution, or the ability to hold a group. Tell me what the difference could make possible, with evidence. Similarity builds a bridge; useful difference gives us a reason to cross it.

**Also bring experienced founders between chapters**, even if they do not serve founders. A completed venture, a chosen pivot, or several substantial projects may create a good opening for the same work. Keep this lane visible without letting it displace the primary focus.

**Do not require a polished profile, a public rupture, or a dated deadline.** An experienced founder can have a quiet, longstanding question about their work while the business remains successful. Use their own account when available; neither an unimpressive profile nor a stable role decides fit.

**I also want studio, program, and community operators** with a concrete use for the work among their founders. An existing program, a named cohort, or a stated founder-development need is more useful than a prestigious title or general enthusiasm for the future.

Some operators need more than a workshop: a repeatable founder-development process, tools, or infrastructure across their program. Flag that scope explicitly. Founder-first investors may be relevant through a concrete portfolio-support use; do not frame me as seeking investment or promise pre-vetted deal flow.

## What counts as an opening

Look across the person's history and their current offer, rather than reading only the headline. These are useful shapes:

| Observable shape | A possibility worth exploring | What it does not establish |
|---|---|---|
| Several substantial projects, with recurring themes across them | There may be one endeavor that could connect them more clearly | That they are confused, scattered, or should merge the projects |
| Distinctive experience across several fields, but a broad current offer | Their particular combination may support a more specific contribution | That the offer is ineffective or their income is poor |
| They serve “founders,” with little public specificity about whom or when | Their history may point to a particular founder situation they are unusually equipped for | That they do not already know their audience privately |
| A successful bespoke practice and a stated wish to expand its reach | Some of their craft might become a repeatable product, group experience, or transferable method | That they want to scale, license, or stop working individually |
| A venture or role has ended, or they explicitly describe a new chapter | Their experience may support a new form of work | Distress, surrender, readiness to buy, or an unmet need |
| An operator names a recurring challenge among the founders they serve | Our work may fit a bounded pilot, delivery partnership, or hosted experience | Authority to buy, a budget, or consent from their members |

**An opening they have named themselves is stronger evidence than one we infer.** Date it when possible. A dated post about reshaping a practice matters more than a timeless slogan.

An apparently generic profile is an invitation to look further, not proof of a generic business. If their website already answers the question, update your read. If the evidence is too thin to identify a useful opening, mark it as unresolved instead of inventing one.

The question behind the search is: **what in this person's experience might find a fuller or more useful expression through what they are building next?** A modest, grounded hypothesis is enough. You do not need to solve their business.

### Four useful recognition patterns

These names are shorthand for search hypotheses, not psychological types or readiness scores. Use one only when supported, and never impose the label on the person.

| Pattern | Observable starting point | What to check rather than assume |
|---|---|---|
| **Fallow Field** | A long stable chapter alongside the person's own account of rethinking what matters | Is there a desired change, even without an exit announcement? New vocabulary alone proves no transition. |
| **Unstrung Loom** | Several substantial endeavors; the person says their connection is hard to articulate | Do they want a common thread? Plurality may already be intentional and coherent. |
| **Standing Wreck** | A venture or role ended and no next chapter is publicly evident | What do they actually want next? An ending establishes neither distress nor readiness. |
| **Sealed Room** | An established business alongside a different, publicly expressed interest or aspiration | Do they want that interest expressed in their work? Do not infer dissatisfaction or investigate private interests. |

Their own words can be stronger than a category: for example, “I have several things going and I can't tell if they're one thing” or “I can't answer 'what do you do' anymore.” These are illustrative recognition lines, not quotes to attribute to a candidate. A desire to build something new is equally valid; pain is not required.

## Resonance signals: look for them, interpret them carefully

Useful vocabulary includes **integral theory, spiral dynamics, integral coach, zone of genius, conscious leadership, polymath, sovereignty, evolutionary**. Related language includes **conscious entrepreneur, conscious tech, systems architect, visionary, mystic, sacred geometry, new earth, noosphere**.

Public work around **plant medicine, psychedelic integration, entheogen, ayahuasca, psilocybin, microdosing, somatic experiencing, breathwork facilitator, shadow work** is especially relevant to my world. Quote what the person actually says. A practice, interest, or professional service is not proof of personal experience, integration, openness, or readiness.

**Web3, decentralized, modular, social entrepreneurship, impact entrepreneur, regenerative business** are adjacent search leads whose usefulness remains to be tested. Industry language such as regenerative construction or sustainability describes a market; it does not establish a person's developmental practice.

Read the grammar: a term can describe the person, their service, their audience, or their industry. Record which. **A term in offer position may identify exactly the practitioner I want to meet.** It does not automatically make them a non-buyer.

Search vocabulary tends to overproduce people who teach it. That is useful in the practitioner lane, but cannot establish their own need or their audience fit. **Idea-proximity makes a conversation; client-proximity makes a possible referral or delivery relationship.** Check whom they actually serve and whether they can reach that audience.

Avoid homonyms: “integral” in an ordinary sentence and employment at a company called Holonic are not evidence of affinity. Use context and specific phrases.

A history of revising their work after feedback, paying for their own development, or openly describing what experience taught them can add useful evidence. Do not assume a certification was self-funded. International experience and languages add context about likeness; they do not prove inner development or a professional transition.

Shared language can also ease connection; Russian-speaking can be a useful shared-language connection, not a requirement or a reason to narrow the global search. Record relevant languages without inferring them from a name or nationality.

**Keep resonance as a meaningful preference. Missing vocabulary means missing evidence, not missing capacity.** Strong entrepreneurial and opportunity evidence can justify an exploratory candidate with resonance unconfirmed. Keep such candidates clearly marked so the primary search does not turn into a generic business-lead list.

**Separate watch lane:** if someone works publicly in cannabis or plant medicine, show me even if the commercial fit is unclear. Label it watch-only and explain what they actually do. This flag is not a client qualification or permission to contact them.

## What relationship might be possible

**For founder-serving practitioners and operators, first test a delivery partnership:** is there a specific group of founders, a relevant desired outcome, and a reason to bring my process into their existing work? This is the lead commercial hypothesis to test, not a finding that every practitioner wants a partner.

Keep direct own-business work visible whenever the person names something they want to develop. A capable practitioner can want a new opportunity without feeling deficient.

Offer a provisional route, and allow more than one:

- **Own business:** they may want to articulate or develop their own contribution and product.
- **Delivery or method partnership:** they may want the process delivered for their founders, or eventually learn and license it themselves.
- **Host or ecosystem relationship:** they have a relevant group or program where a bounded experience could be useful.
- **Peer exploration:** there is a specific complementary contribution to explore, but no demonstrated commercial opening yet.

A practitioner can need help with their own business and later carry the work to others. An operator can also be a client. **Peerhood is how we meet; buying is a separate decision. Neither replaces the need to name a useful commercial possibility.** A peer-only connection can be valuable, but keep it outside the commercial shortlist unless there is a specific use to investigate.

Having their own framework, fluent language, or a successful practice does not establish that they have nothing to buy. Equally, topic overlap does not establish demand. Someone building an assessment or typology is not automatically included or excluded: show the actual overlap, difference, and possible use, if one exists.

Preserve the underlying concern: does their approach leave room for the person to correct an assessment and choose their own direction? An explicit claim that a model overrides the person’s account is a substantive method conflict to flag. Owning a framework is not itself that conflict. In a conversation, “What do you uniquely bring, beyond the method you use?” can reveal complementarity; a process-based answer is not a verdict on readiness.

When the work appears complete for their purposes and there is no stated or evidenced opening, do not manufacture a deficit. Route a concrete collaboration possibility, or leave them outside the commercial shortlist.

Means matter before paid work. Job titles, apparent success, and self-employment do not prove income or available budget. Bring explicit evidence when available; otherwise leave means, timing, and purchasing authority unknown for conversation.

## Why someone might pay — the hypothesis to test

People may pay to make a chosen next development possible. Do not require a felt deficit, and do not mistake admiration for a purchase reason. The useful question is: **what do they want to make possible, for whom, and why might my contribution help enough to pay for it?**

| Possible route | Concrete value to investigate | Potential payer and next commercial step |
|---|---|---|
| Paid delivery for a practitioner or organization | Their founders leave with an articulated contribution, a chosen audience/offer hypothesis, and a next experiment appropriate to the agreed scope | The practitioner or organization commissions a bounded pilot for a named group; verify budget owner, scope and success criteria |
| Hosted experience leading to optional individual work | Participants experience the method, then decide whether they want help developing their own business | A participating founder may choose a paid container afterward; the host is not automatically the buyer and attendance creates no obligation |
| Own-business work | The person develops a particular direction, offer or product they actually want to pursue | They commission a scoped individual engagement; establish the desired result and terms before delivery |
| Training or licensing, if appropriate | A practitioner can eventually carry an agreed process in their own work | Explore after useful delivery has demonstrated fit; scope readiness, support, rights and economics separately |

**Start with one useful paid-delivery hypothesis, not a licensing pitch.** A peer conversation, a free Recognition Round, a paid pilot, and a training agreement are different exchanges. Do not blur them, promise a ready-made licensing product, set a price on my behalf, or imply guaranteed revenue for anyone.

For a partnership candidate, identify their existing vehicle, the founders it serves, and the outcome my work might help deliver. Distinguish a **named current need**, a **grounded inferred use**, and **affinity without a commercial opening**. Rank in that order. No forced quota: if you cannot find a credible delivery use, say so and return the evidence rather than manufacturing one.

The first test is **whether this contribution is worth paying for or hosting**, not whether the person likes my philosophy. A clean inquiry could establish whether they already address the relevant outcome, whether they would want outside help, and who would choose and fund a pilot. These are questions for conversation, not claims to insert into an introduction.

## How to search and what to return

Start with experienced people serving founders, then inspect their trajectory and present work for an opening. Search founder coaches, advisers, founder-led agencies, studio operators, accelerator teams, and founder communities. Combine these with resonance terms; also search by their stated business changes so vocabulary does not become the only doorway.

Use LinkedIn alongside personal sites, offer pages, interviews, newsletters, and program pages. Exact-phrase web searches can help disambiguate language. Do not assume any search engine applies your filters correctly: check the returned evidence.

Use different search paths for different relationships: practitioner/audience terms for delivery uses; venture history and the person's own change-language for direct-client openings. For example, `site:linkedin.com/in "integral coach" founder` and `site:linkedin.com/in founder "stepped back"` are starting points, not qualification tests. Include podcast guest pages, conference bios and public retreat/cohort pages, where quiet interests may be more visible than on LinkedIn.

**Default to six to eight evidence-backed candidates per batch; twenty is a ceiling, not a target.** A separately requested larger research list can contain up to twenty, with its best six to eight clearly identified. Put concrete commercial openings first, favoring relevant delivery uses within the primary audience; then show other direct-client and operator opportunities. Keep peer-only, exploratory and watch-only results separate from the commercial shortlist. If you find fewer credible candidates, say what you searched and where the evidence ran out; do not fill the batch with weak topic matches. Avoid duplicates and check known-contact history when supplied.

Keep each candidate card compact:

1. **Person and source:** name, profile URL, current vehicle and audience, date checked.
2. **Evidence:** two to four short exact quotes, each linked to its source, establishing relevant history, current expression, resonance, or an explicit desired change. Preserve the distinction between their words and your paraphrase.
3. **Possible opening:** one sentence connecting the evidence. Label an inferred opening as a hypothesis. An optional recognition-pattern label can support the explanation; it cannot replace evidence. Include any evidence that weakens it; “no clear opening yet” is a valid finding.
4. **Possible relationship, value and payer:** own business, delivery/method, host/ecosystem, or peer exploration; multiple routes are allowed. For a commercial candidate, name who benefits, what outcome they might value, who might pay, and what each side contributes. Mark those as hypotheses where unconfirmed. For a peer-only candidate, say there is no commercial opening established.
5. **Unknowns and next question:** the one thing conversation would need to establish. Include unconfirmed budget, timing, or audience where material.

Optional: one observation Sasha could explore in his own words. Ground it in specific work, make it answerable, and leave room for correction. No scripted diagnosis, inferred emotional cost, or finished business design.

For example, a useful hypothesis connects **two named projects and an actual recurring activity**. “There is a hidden thread in everything you do” could be sent to anyone and is not a useful read.

## How an introduction should feel

**Operate within the role I commissioned.** An introduction service such as Boardy, commissioned to find people and arrange introductions, may contact a candidate to ask whether they are open to meeting me. Seek each person’s agreement before making the introduction. A research assistant asked only for a shortlist should return the evidence and must not infer permission to contact people. For an authorized introduction, give a concrete reason to meet using the description of my work above. Do not tell them you have diagnosed their gap or that I already know what their business should become.

Use plain, specific language. Do not recast me as a generic coach, accelerator or fund, add prestige claims, or improvise prices and results. The approved description above is the anchor; the person-specific reason to meet is the addition.

The movement we are making possible is simple: **I notice something specific; I offer it as a possibility; they can recognize it, correct it, decline it, or add something that changes my view.** Their response determines the next step. We are not secretly steering them toward an answer already decided for them.

The quiz at **findyourtoptalent.com/quiz** remains an optional self-exploration route. It is not a mandatory entrance to every conversation. A relevant exchange may lead to a free Recognition Round, an individual conversation, or an agreed paid container. Never imply a group date is booked or a place is available without checking.

A consciously offered taste can be complete and useful on its own. It creates no debt and carries no obligation to buy. Deeper work begins with explicit agreement about the purpose, scope, and exchange. Do not conceal commercial possibilities behind a false “not selling anything,” and do not turn an introduction into an unsolicited session.

## How we learn whether the matching works

Keep these events distinct: candidate found, invitation accepted, substantive reply, opening confirmed or corrected, next step chosen, paid agreement, delivery, and actual result. Compliments and shared vocabulary are not purchases; silence does not diagnose a person.

**Treat each profile as a lead sheet, not a read.** Reconfirm consequential facts and intentions in conversation. Distinguish no fit, not now, and an agreed follow-up; do not convert a timing constraint into rejection or an open-ended chase. Record the relationship route and evidence learned after the call, rather than letting “peer” erase a possible later use.

Track which evidence led to a useful encounter and which hypothesis the person corrected. For each commercial route, record whether the person confirmed a desired outcome, saw a distinct contribution, identified a payer, requested a proposal, and agreed to paid work. Record disconfirming answers too: already covered, no relevant group, no current priority, no budget, or not our way of working.

An authorized introduction service should report the funnel for each batch: researched, approached, replied, agreed to meet, introduced, useful conversation, and commercial next step. A research-only assistant stops at researched and marks later stages not tested. Use actual counts, not impressions of enthusiasm. A sharper brief is not evidence of distribution or demand until these movements occur. That feedback should improve both the search and my craft.

Review the brief after each batch. Partnerships may take longer than direct-client decisions; track intermediate commitments without counting them as revenue or assuming they will convert. Judge each route on its observed movement over time, not solely on first-month sales or the warmth of its conversations.

**Find people with whom a specific possibility is worth exploring. Bring me enough reality to see carefully. Let the person tell us whether that possibility is theirs.**
