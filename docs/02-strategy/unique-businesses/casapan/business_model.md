## 1. Situation (one paragraph)

Benjamin bakes excellent sourdough and pastry in a seasonal tourist town. The good season (2024–25) peaked at 400–500 loaves a week. He mirrored it into the next season: new oven, machine, staff on promised wages, rent paid ahead. Sales came in at roughly 40% of plan. Wages ate everything, he borrowed for rent, and a settlement with a fired employee was paid from his wife's savings. He nearly closed. **The business only lives in his head.** This season he works alone, bakes less, and wants to sell everything.

## 2. What he wants (his words)

- *"¿Cuánto hay que vender para que funcione?"* — asked 3 times. **This is the engagement's first answer.**
- Stability, not growth: pay rent, pay himself, save, vacations, be home with his son. *"Quiero un modelo más chiquitito, más auténtico."*
- *"Prefiero volver a producir poco pero vender todo."*
- *"Necesito que esto funcione solo."*

**Method:** serve the want completely and fast. Let the numbers show the need; ask *"¿qué ves aquí?"* Use his words, not ours.

## 3. The core diagnosis

| # | Finding | Status |
|---|---|---|
| D1 | **The acute killer was fixed costs sized to the boom case** (wages, investment, prepaid rent). Sold "4" against a cost base built for "10". | Agreed (Session 1 math) |
| D2 | **Waste is the silent ongoing killer.** *"Ese 30% que estoy regalando es mi ganancia."* Every season, every market day. | Agreed |
| D3 | **Stockouts are hidden lost margin.** Market sells out in ~2h, 3h of disappointed customers. A missed sale loses ~90 pesos; a wasted loaf loses ~30. | Inferred, needs data |
| D4 | **No memory system.** ~80% in his head; resets after vacations. | His words |

**Survival rule:** *keep fixed costs at the low case.* Margin protection is second.

## 4. The bottleneck (focus)

Working hypothesis: **the constraint is the forecast — matching bake quantity to demand per product, per day.** It drives D2 and D3 at once.

Candidate intervention (one, not thirty): **bake to the low case, capture the overflow.**
- Market waitlist: *"apúntate aquí y mañana te horneo un pan"* (his own idea, Session 1).
- WhatsApp pre-order with a cutoff (also solves the 10pm messages).

Not yet chosen. Confirm once the market-day log shows the actual pattern.

## 9. Seasonality

- High season: November → May/June. Market runs to July, weak.
- Heat arrives → ~70% of people leave; those who stay have little money.
- 2026–27 outlook: weak. Retreats cancelled, US/Canada uncertainty, beach closure news.
- Swing seen: plan "10", sold "4" (season to season).

## 10. Product mix (volatile)

Croissant ↔ pan de chocolate and white ↔ rye flip week to week. Rye and kamut are expensive and slow to restock. Prefers surplus of white over surplus of rye.

## 10b. Capacity and inputs (what limits the upside)

- **Fixed vs flexible.** The season rule ("fixed costs at the low case") applies to wages, machines, prepaid rent. Flour and batch size are flexible: bought weekly (100–200 kg), so a busy week just means a bigger order.
- **Flour shelf life.** In his heat and humidity, bugs arrive in ~7–10 days on the floor (Benjamin, S1 [26:39]). General guide, cool and dry: white flour keeps months; whole-grain and rye much less (their oils go rancid). Not the limiter while he buys weekly; matters for rye/kamut (10–15 day lead time from Chihuahua) and for any bulk-buy discount.
- **Likely real ceiling: his own hands and oven, alone.** No staff this season. If demand surges, the cap is how many loaves one person can shape and bake per day. The waitlist moves surplus demand to the next day, inside that cap. Measure: max loaves/day he can bake alone (to ask).
