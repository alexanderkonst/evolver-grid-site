## Teaching notes: Deliverable 01 (for Sasha)

How to carry the one-pager live:

1. **Every loaf has two numbers.** Costs ~30, sells 120, leaves ~90. That 90 pays everything, him included.
2. **Leftovers and stockouts aren't equal.** A leftover loses 30. A customer leaving empty-handed loses 90. The prize is the 3 hours of disappointed customers at the market.
3. **Break-even is one division.** Monthly need ÷ 4.3 ÷ 90. 30k MXN → ~78 loaves/week vs 400–500 at his best. He isn't failing at volume. Do the division in front of him; better, let him do it.
4. **Costs at the low case, sales catch the high case.** Fixed costs sized for a bad week. Good weeks caught with free tools: market waitlist (*"apúntate y mañana te horneo"*, his idea) and a WhatsApp cutoff (also ends the 10pm messages).
5. **The homework is the forecast in disguise.** Baked / sold / sell-out time per selling day. Sell-out time shows the hidden demand. A few weeks = pattern by product and day = the bottleneck answer.

Likely pushback:
- *"I can't predict anything!"* → "You don't have to. Bake the safe amount, the list catches the rest."
- *"What about pastry?"* → "Same logic, we need its cost first."
- *"52 loaves sounds too low."* → "That's the floor that keeps you safe. Everything above is savings."

Adjustments: confirm the 30 MXN includes rent; restaurant loaves leave 80 (break-even ~10% higher if wholesale-heavy); replace example rows with his real monthly number; let him pick the cutoff hour.
