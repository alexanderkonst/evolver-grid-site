# Partner offer · Bring your module into the Game of Being You

## The offer in one line

Let's connect our systems: my players will come to you. When the game brings you a buyer, 10% of that sale goes back to the game. That's our social contract: it grows the whole game, and everyone in it.

## How it works

1. **Paste a link** to your project (site, course, product, community).
2. **Our AI drafts your module cards:** one card per thing you offer, each with one result and one button.
3. **You check and confirm** the cards. Nothing goes live without your yes.
4. **Your module appears in the village** in the place that fits it: a course in the Academy, a product in the Market, a session in the Mastermind. A whole ecosystem may get its own building, by Sasha's decision.
5. **Players click through** to your site with a referral id. They buy what they want, on your site, on your terms.

## Terms (draft text for partners)

- **No contract.** We agree in words, and that's it. You can leave any time.
- **Your module stays your own business.** You sell it yourself, on your own site, under your own terms. You handle your own taxes and payment fees.
- **When the game brings you a buyer, 10% of what they paid goes to the game.** This is our social contract. No lawyer or court stands behind it, only our word to each other. It is a gift, and we count on it.
- **Gifts go in crypto** to the game's wallet: [wallet address and network, to choose].
- **Mark it as a gift everywhere:** in the transfer note and in your own books.
- **Who brought the buyer:** the game remembers a player's first visit. No time limit.
- **Once a month,** tell us how many buyers came from the game. We trust your word.

## What the 10% is for

- **The game itself:** new places, new modules, a better path for every player.
- **The team that keeps it running:** the people and the AI that build it, review cards and bring players to you.
- **Everyone in it:** more players for every module, yours included. The game exists to help each person find and live their unique gift, and through that, to help the whole planet flourish.

The game brings you players for free. The 10% is how you keep that engine going for everyone.

## Why it is built this way (Sasha, 2026-11-22)

- **Money goes one way only:** author → game. The game never collects money for others and never pays out to many people. Paying "bringers" from the platform was rejected as a legal, financial, accounting and tax nightmare (Sasha 11:48).
- **No pooled funds** (Spotify-style shared pot rejected, 11:36): against canon Three Terms.
- **Modules are unique businesses** that sell a clearly described, measured result, not a commodity. So there is no competition between modules inside the game.
- **Gift, not commission:** an obligatory 10% tied to sales is a commission in substance, whatever it's called. The 10% is a firm social norm (Sasha 14:14: «слишком мягко… надо быть более прямыми»), but nothing enforces it: no contract, no penalty, no removal for not giving. That keeps it a gift. Between partner and game, nothing for a legal system to hold: no contract, only trust (Sasha 14:15). What remains is one narrow question on the receiving side: gifts that arrive in the game's wallet are still money the game receives, so how the game records them (income or gift, in which country) is the one thing to check with an accountant or lawyer before real money moves.
- **Gift, not donation:** "donation" reads as giving to a charity or nonprofit; the game is neither.
- Replaces canon 66.6/33.3 and 90/10 author splits for partner modules at this stage (those assumed payment inside the platform).

## Tax: what holds in any legal system (Hark, 2026-11-22 14:2x, for Sasha's question)

Sasha's five traits: gift; optional, no enforcement, no consequence; a request on trust; transparent flow; crypto with minimal KYC.

- **No label is invariant.** Tax systems judge substance over form. A payment made *because* the game brought a buyer is, in substance, payment for a service, whatever it is called (US: Commissioner v. Duberstein, 1960, a gift needs "detached and disinterested generosity"; Mexico and Russia treat money a business receives as income too).
- **Crypto and minimal KYC change nothing** about whether it is income. Low KYC aimed at tax reads as evasion and is the one trait that adds risk.
- **What actually holds everywhere:** the recipient declares what it receives. Then there is nothing to dig into: the game reports gifts as income and pays its tax; for the partner, the 10% is a normal business expense.
- **Keep:** gift (culture), no enforcement (no contract to argue over), trust, transparent one-way flow. **Drop:** the goal of being non-taxable; minimal KYC as a tax tool.
- **Only real way to make it a true gift:** cut the link to sales ("give what you want, when you want"). That loses the 10% rule, so not recommended.

## Linux Foundation / Red Hat analogy

- Game (shared core, open to every module) ≈ Linux; Sasha's company, courses and AI on top ≈ Red Hat (commercial layer on an open core).
- Difference: the Linux Foundation is a separate nonprofit funded by fixed member dues, governed by its members. Here core and commercial layer have one owner, so the analogy holds fully only if the game later becomes its own entity with its own governance.

## Quality bar

- One clear result per module, a real working link, honest description.
- Stage 1: our AI checks each card, then one human review before it goes live.
- We may decline or remove a module that misleads players or breaks the game's tone (inform and inspire, no pressure selling).
