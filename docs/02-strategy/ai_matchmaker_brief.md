## v3.0 — August 27, 2026 (Day 168)

**What changed and why.** v1 and v2 described the work by topic ("founders, self-knowledge, unique business"). Six intros later that produced six substantial conversations and zero clients, because **topic-similarity matching returns peers.** Someone who cares about what you care about has usually already solved their own version of it.

v3.0 makes three structural changes:

1. **Three streams instead of one description.** A single description produces a single kind of match.
2. **The client is described by a deficit, in verbatim recognition lines.** Their defining feature is an absence, and absences do not appear on profiles. They read as "stuck," not as interesting.
3. **A qualifier link replaces the pitch.** The matchmaker routes; the quiz sorts. This removes the observed drift where a thin brief causes the matchmaker to invent an angle.

Plus one exclusion earned the hard way, and one screening question that turned out to predict everything.

### Sendable text (v3.0)

> I want to redo my brief, because the last six intros tell me the problem was mine, not yours. Every one of them was a good conversation. None of them could buy what I sell, and I now know why.
>
> **You have been matching me on resonance.** You find people who care about what I care about, and people who care about what I care about are my peers. A peer recognizes the work, enjoys the call, and has nothing to buy, because they already solved their own version of it. That is the correct output of topic matching. It is just not a client.
>
> There are three kinds of people I want, and they are not the same. Please hold them separately.
>
> **1. My client: the founder in transition.**
> The form that carried them has ended and they know it before anyone else does. Their income runs on their own name, or the next thing is being born under their hands. They have three or four real things going and cannot say what makes them one thing. They have paid for their own development before and call it good money spent.
>
> In their own words they sound like this:
> "I know I have something and I can't say what it is."
> "I have several things going and I can't tell if they're one thing."
> "I pushed harder and it changed nothing."
> "I can't answer 'what do you do' anymore."
>
> Important: this person does not look impressive on a profile. They look stuck. That is the signal, not a disqualifier. Do not pitch them. Send them to findyourtoptalent.com/quiz and let it sort them honestly. If it is not their problem, it says so and stops.
>
> **2. My sweet spot: practitioners who serve founders and have done the work on themselves.**
> Coaches, studio operators, accelerator leads, community builders, agency founders. Not all of them. Only the ones who can say what they personally uniquely bring, rather than describing their method.
>
> **The screening question, and it decides everything: ask what they uniquely bring.** If they answer with their process, they are not ready. If they answer with who they are, they are. One of these carries my method to their entire client base, so a single yes here is worth ten client sales.
>
> The complementary shape I want most is the grounded builder-operator. Closer to execution and ordinary business physics than I am. That difference is the point of the relationship.
>
> Introduce these people as peers and partners. Never as prospects.
>
> **3. Studio and program operators at altitude.**
> Running a venture studio, accelerator, or founder program successfully, and conscious about how they run it. With them it is not a session. I bring the technology, the platform, the frameworks and the investment thesis. Rarer, and I mostly know them already, but flag one when you see it.
>
> **Please exclude:** anyone building their own human-categorization, typology, or personality-encoding system. They are the closest match you will find on topic and they are structurally unable to buy, because their premise is that self-report is unreliable, which is the premise my entire offer rests on.
>
> **One more thing.** When you describe me, use the quiz link rather than my offer. Then neither of us has to carry a pitch, and I stop hearing my own work described in words I did not write.

### Yield curve — do not judge this channel on month-one client count

1. **Peers first.** Correct output of resonance matching.
2. **Partnership assets second.** Referral valves, distribution nodes, hosts, routers. The first six intros produced several and zero clients.
3. **Buyers last**, and only once the brief carries deficit language.

Killing the channel early is killing it one iteration before it works.

### Discipline around every intro

- The matchmaker's profile of a person is **a lead sheet, not a read.** Assembled from public and self-reported material, and it goes stale. Verify anything load-bearing in the room.
- Score the ten ripeness axes after each call (Technology 123, ninety seconds). Over a handful of intros the scores show which stream the channel is actually serving.
- Name the stream in the pulse card. "Peer" alone is not a classification.
- Apply the banana/strawberry rule (Technology 123, Day 167 amendment) before writing anyone off: some fruit ripens off the tree and deserves a nurture cadence, some never does.
