Share of the cult this analysis credits to the mechanics below: **part.** *Judgment:* her fame is the thesis, the TED talks and the book; the large numbers (I Love Bees, Lost Ring) were bought by marketing budgets and ran on fiction. The mechanics with evidence are the quieter ones: real acts with a witness (SuperBetter, Evoke), a season that ends in a real project (Evoke), one night in a real place (Find the Future).

Her frame, quoted so we use the same words: games share four traits, "a goal, rules, a feedback system, and voluntary participation"; people crave four intrinsic rewards, "satisfying work", "the experience, or at least the hope, of being successful", "social connection", "meaning, or the chance to be a part of something larger than ourselves" [15, pp. 21, 49–50]. Her 14 "fixes" [15, pp. 346–348] are principles, not mechanics; the ones that touch GoBY are #9 "Have more fun with strangers", #12 "Seek out more epic wins" (real volunteer tasks), #14 "Develop massively multiplayer foresight". *Judgment:* the four rewards overlap the §15.8 Koster check and §10a; they add no new rule, so nothing is proposed from them.

## 2. Mechanics that demonstrably worked

### JM-1. Every real act leaves one piece of evidence, seen by one real person
- **In the source:** Evoke: each weekly mission = activities done in the real world (interview someone in your community, photograph a place and imagine it changed); the player posts "evidence" (photo, video, text); peers and **mentors** read it against a one-line rubric ("Does this evidence view a familiar thing in a different light?") and answer [7]. SuperBetter concussion study: the research coordinator was always an ally who saw the logged acts and answered [2]. World Without Oil: daily awards for "authentic" stories [8].
- **Evidence it worked:** A (one RCT [7]): Evoke beat a control that **also did real fieldwork**; the authors name the difference as "submit evidence of their activities ... and, in return, they receive feedback from local and global mentors". B: Evoke's open run produced 23,500 posts, 4,700 photos, 1,500 videos in 10 weeks [5]; non-randomized concussion study [2].
- **What it did NOT prove:** the RCT tested the whole Evoke package (story, missions, evidence, mentors) and cannot isolate the evidence-plus-witness part; instrument built by the Evoke team. Evoke's open run also had peer **ratings and votes** on evidence: no data that they helped, and they rank people (rejected below).
- **Driver:** a real act becomes real for the player when a real person sees it and answers (feedback system + social connection [15]); the evidence is the act, not a score.

### JM-2. Allies with a named part (SuperBetter "Recruit your allies")
- **In the source:** Mission 2: "Pick the people you want to count on most ... Ask each one to play a specific part"; "Be sure to ask at least one ally to give you daily or weekly achievements". McGonigal's own: her sister calls every day for a report; her husband keeps the records; friends come once a week to cheer her up. Her husband gave her an achievement called "The Harvest" for eating vegetables instead of cookies [15, pp. 136–138]. The app: allies see your activity and send a line or an acknowledgement [2].
- **Evidence it worked:** A for the SuperBetter package [1], but **not for allies**: only 6 of 190 users invited an ally in the RCT, so the effect came from the solo daily acts. The only study where allies were present for everyone (the coordinator) is non-randomized, n=19 [2] (B). Author's account [15] (C).
- **What it did NOT prove:** that allies drive the effect. People did not invite allies when inviting meant posting to Facebook [1].
- **Driver:** asking for a specific, actionable kind of help removes the cost of asking ("It's HARD to ask for help when you're sick", player quote [2]); a real close person, not a crowd.

### JM-3. The season ends in a real project that gets real backing (Evoke "Evokation")
- **In the source:** 10 weeks, one mission per week; to "win", players finish the missions and submit an Evokation: "a detailed and innovative plan of how the player will tackle a self-chosen challenge in the physical world after the game has ended" [6]. Real backing for the best: seed money, a mentor, a summit seat, a crowdfunding challenge [5]. The Colombia version is 8 missions over a semester ending in a team project [7].
- **Evidence it worked:** B: 74 Evokations against a target of 7; 10 seeded, 22 mentored [5]. A: Colombia RCT on skills [7]. **Not proven:** how many Evokations became real, lasting projects (no follow-up found).
- **Driver:** a clear goal that lives outside the game (her "epic win", Fix #12); the reward is real (a mentor, a client, a first sum), not points.

### JM-4. One night, one real place, one book written by everyone (Find the Future)
- **In the source:** 500 players locked overnight in the NYPL main building; teams find 100 real artifacts (Jefferson's draft of the Declaration, Kerouac's harmonica); each artifact unlocks a writing prompt; the 500 pieces are bound on site into a book that the library keeps; at dawn every player signs it. A "game within the game": 500 handwritten postcards dated 2021 hidden in the stacks, each addressed to one player and referring to their application; each player had to find a stranger's card and deliver it by hand [14].
- **Evidence it worked:** B: 5,000 applications for 500 seats; players met in person and online for weeks before; the book exists (partly bound by morning) [14]. **Not proven:** any lasting effect; one night only.
- **Driver:** a rare, fixed-time gathering in a loved real place (same driver as PG-2), plus a shared artifact that stays in that place: your page is in the village's book.

### JM-5. Players found structures that others join, with an open kit anyone can host (Superstruct, Cruel 2 B Kind)
- **In the source:** Superstruct: players invented "superstructures" (new organizations for a 2019 crisis); others joined and linked them [12][13]. Cruel 2 B Kind: rules and a one-page cheat sheet free online; anyone could run a game in their own town without permission [15].
- **Evidence it worked:** B: ~600 player-founded structures from 7,000 players in 6 weeks [12][13]; C: 50+ cities from voluntary reports [15]. **Not proven:** that any Superstruct structure existed in reality (it was fiction set in 2019).
- **Driver:** authorship (the founder built the place) and low cost to start; the same driver as H3-3 and FB-2.

## 5. Our version

**JM-1. Show it to one person.** When a player names today's real act (A13: "Day 12: wrote to a client") or finishes a village quest, they can attach one thing: a photo or one line. It goes to **one person they chose** (an ally, Sasha, a mentor from the village), never to a feed. That person answers with one written line (FB-3), and only then does the badge of a village quest light (A12). Nothing is scored, voted or ranked; nobody else sees it unless the player shares it. This is the answer to §23.4's open question "how a real-life action is verified": a person who knows you saw it.

**JM-2. Your allies.** After the reveal (inside A4's micro-quest), one question: "Who should know you're doing this, and what's their part?" The player names 1–3 real people and one concrete part each ("Ana: ask me every Sunday what I sold"). They appear by name in Your House. Allies are invited with a plain link (no social login, the RCT shows people won't post to Facebook to ask [1]). No enemies, no fight; the "dark guardian" half of §15.1 stays parked.

**JM-3. The Season Harvest.** A Mastermind season (J9) does not end in a certificate; it ends at a full-moon Harvest where every player who finished shows **one real project**: who it serves, what they offer, who already bought or helped. Villagers answer with real backing they can give on the spot: a mentor hour, a first purchase, a room, an introduction. No winners and no jury; every finished project is shown. A project that gets backing lights its stall and opens Town Hall for its owner (J13).

**JM-4. The village book.** Once per full moon (or once a year, Sasha's choice) the village spends one evening in a real place in San Pancho. Each person writes or draws one page about a real person, place or object of the village, signs it, and the pages are bound into one book that stays in that place. Each player also hands a stranger a card written for them by someone who came before (the Find the Future postcard, turned into a gift between real villagers, close to the particles of §15.4).

**JM-5. Found and host.** Supports the accepted direction (constructor, "Create a new digital village", FB-2 circles): a one-page kit (what a circle is, how it meets, what counts as "met") so any player can start one in their town without asking; a circle counts once it has really met twice. No new row.

## 6. Smallest testable version

**JM-1 (do first).**
- **What:** no code. The next 10 players who finish a Journey step or the reveal's micro-quest are asked to send one photo or line of the real act to Sasha (or a person they name) in the chat they already use; that person answers with one line.
- **Who:** 10 players from Sasha's current funnel or San Pancho.
- **Signal:** share who do a second real act within the same week, against the previous 10 players who got no witness.
- **Kill line:** fewer than 3 of 10 send any evidence, or no difference in second acts.
- **Depends on:** nothing built; later A13 and #356.

**JM-2.**
- **What:** one added question at the end of A4's micro-quest (copy only, or asked in chat).
- **Who:** the next 20 players after the reveal.
- **Signal:** share who name someone with a part; one week later, share whose ally actually asked or helped (ask the player).
- **Kill line:** fewer than 30% name anyone → drop; the RCT pattern (6 of 190) repeats.
- **Depends on:** Sasha's yes to unpark §15.1 (allies half only); A4 copy is a core-flow change → his yes to the exact text.

**JM-3.**
- **What:** at the next Mastermind close or full-moon Harvest, each finished player shows one real project in 3 minutes; villagers write the backing they offer on a card.
- **Who:** the current Mastermind circle.
- **Signal:** projects whose backing turned into a real act (a session given, a sale, a meeting) within the moon cycle.
- **Kill line:** no backing turns into an act.
- **Depends on:** a Harvest happening (PG-2, A11); public-facing → Sasha's yes.

**JM-4.**
- **What:** one evening in one real San Pancho place, paper and a stapler.
- **Who:** anyone in the village, not only players.
- **Signal:** people outside Sasha's circle who come; a second evening asked for by villagers.
- **Kill line:** fewer than 10 come, or nobody asks for another.
- **Depends on:** a place that agrees to keep the book; Sasha's call.

**JM-5:** no separate test; it rides FB-2's test (circles that meet twice).

## 7. Risks and anti-patterns
- **Attrition is her games' rule, not the exception:** 26% of SuperBetter's RCT sample left at posttest [1]; Evoke went 19,324 → 4,693 active → 223 certified [5]. Plan for the few who stay; never measure success by sign-ups.
- **Evidence becoming a feed:** Evoke's 23,500 posts were a public stream with votes. Ours goes to one person; no likes, no stream (FB-x).
- **Witness as judge:** a mentor who grades turns the act into school. The answer is one human line, never a pass/fail.
- **Allies nobody invites:** the RCT's 6 of 190 [1]. Make it a person and a part, asked at the moment of the reveal, invited by plain link.
- **Harvest as a contest:** Evoke picked 10 winners for seed money. Ours shows everyone; backing comes from villagers, not a jury.
- **Fiction creep:** her biggest numbers came from invented crises and mysteries. GoBY must stay with the real present (§10a).
- **Conflicts of interest in the evidence:** both SuperBetter studies list McGonigal as author/founder; the Evoke RCT is by Evoke's team. Treat effect sizes as upper bounds.

## 8. Priority
- **JM-1:** Impact 3 (closes §23.4's open verification and makes every real act social) × Confidence 2 (one in-house RCT that cannot isolate it, plus B) × Ease 3 (no code to test) = **18 → High**.
- **JM-2:** Impact 3 × Confidence 1 (C for the ally part itself; the RCT does not credit it) × Ease 3 = **9 → Medium**.
- **JM-3:** Impact 3 (Town Hall and the season get a real end; real income for villagers, §22.10) × Confidence 2 (B + A on skills) × Ease 2 (needs a Harvest) = **12 → Medium**.
- **JM-4:** Impact 2 × Confidence 2 (B demand, nothing on lasting effect) × Ease 2 = **8 → Medium**.
- **JM-5:** Impact 2 × Confidence 2 × Ease 1 (the constructor is big) = **4 → Low** (evidence for an accepted direction; nothing new to decide).
Priority is a recommendation. Sasha sets the order.
