## 1. Arnold van Gennep, *Les rites de passage* (1909)

**Header.** Van Gennep, French ethnographer; Paris 1909. Why in: **core** — the three-phase grammar every later model inherits.

| phase | what happens | who acts | initiate feels/does |
|---|---|---|---|
| Separation (preliminal) | symbolic detachment from prior fixed point, e.g. hair cut on entering army | community, master of ceremonies | "cutting away" the former self [W-RoP] |
| Transition (liminal) | strictly prescribed sequence under a master of ceremonies | master of ceremonies | ambiguous, threshold state [W-Lim] |
| Incorporation (postliminal) | re-entry with new status; ring, belt, cord, crown | society | public new identity [W-RoP] |

- Q1 Call/consent: not addressed; status change driven by life cycle (pregnancy, initiation, marriage, death) [W-RoP].
- Q2 Separation: symbolic "cutting away", e.g. haircut [W-RoP].
- Q3 Ordeal: liminal rites "destructive" so identity can change [W-Lim].
- Q4 Revelation: not addressed in sources read.
- Q5 Death/rebirth: separation = metaphorical "death"; incorporation = "new" being [W-Lim].
- Q6 Witness: "master of ceremonies"; society receives [W-Lim].
- Q7 Mark: "sacred bond… cord, knot… belt, ring, bracelet, crown" [W-RoP].
- Q8 Return: incorporation rites, e.g. graduation [W-RoP].
- Q9 Integration: modern programmes lose effect when incorporation is missing — Cushing (1998) at Outward Bound; Bell (2003) [W-RoP].
- Q10 Degrees: four passage types (status, place, situation, time) [W-Lim]; not a ladder.
- Q11 Surface/depth: modern secular groups keep customs "traced to an earlier sacred phase" [W-RoP].
- Q12 Risks: not addressed.
- Q13: house metaphor — society as "rooms and corridors"; a passage = changing rooms [W-RoP].

**Mechanics.** (1) Three-phase sequence with explicit exit; C (comparative theory); driver: a bounded transition with known way in and out [W-Lim]. (2) Incorporation token (ring/cord); C; driver: visible new tie [W-RoP]. (3) Missing third phase weakens effect; B (Cushing 1998, Bell 2003 as reported) [W-RoP].

**Criticism.** Evolutionist scale of "simplest" societies [W-RoP]; universality claim unusual for anthropology [W-Lim].

**Quote.** "I propose to call the rites of separation from a previous world, *preliminal rites*, those executed during the transitional stage *liminal (or threshold) rites*, and the ceremonies of incorporation into the new world *postliminal rites*." (van Gennep 1977:21, via [W-RoP])

## 2. Victor Turner — liminality, communitas, liminoid (1964–1974)

**Header.** Turner, British anthropologist, Ndembu fieldwork in Zambia; "Betwixt and Between" (1967), *The Ritual Process* (1969), "Liminal to Liminoid" (1974). Why in: **core + closest to target** (liminoid = optional, modern, play-based passage).

| phase | what happens | who acts | initiate feels/does |
|---|---|---|---|
| Separation | detachment from structural position | elders | leaves status |
| Liminality | status stripped; anti-structure | instructors | "nameless, spatio-temporally dislocated" [W-Lim] |
| Communitas | bond among liminal peers | cohort | "direct, immediate, and total confrontation of human identities" [W-Lim] |
| Reaggregation | stable state with rights/obligations | society | new structural role [Turner-LC] |

- Q1: not central; liminoid is **optional** vs obligatory liminal [W-Lim].
- Q2: withdrawal from normal modes of social action [W-Lim].
- Q3: rituals "often involve acts of pain" [W-Lim].
- Q4: liminality as "period of scrutiny for central values and axioms of the culture" [W-Lim].
- Q5: liminality "likened to death, to being in the womb, to invisibility, to darkness" [Turner-Fest].
- Q6: peers (communitas) plus "ceremony masters" who have been through it [W-Lim].
- Q7: not addressed in sources read.
- Q8: "all liminality must eventually dissolve"; return to structure or form "normative communitas" [W-Lim].
- Q9: normative communitas institutionalizes the bond, "denaturing the grace" [W-Lim].
- Q10: three communitas forms (spontaneous, ideological, normative) [W-Lim].
- Q11: liminoid = secular, leisure, "play"; concerts, sport [W-Lim].
- Q12: society-wide liminality without a ceremony master breeds "self-proclaimed ceremony masters" who perpetuate it [W-Lim].
- Q13: liminoid experiences "do not result in a change of status" [W-Lim].

**Mechanics.** (1) Cohort communitas; B (ethnography + later fieldwork); driver: status levelling makes peers bond [W-Lim]. (2) Guided liminality with a known exit; C; driver: an elder who has crossed prevents perpetual limbo [W-Lim]. (3) Liminoid framing; C; driver: voluntary play is accepted in modern life — *Judgment*: but without status change it risks "experience without passage."

**Criticism.** Concept restricted to small-scale societies; Horvath (2013) argues for wider use; Turner over-romanticizes liminality, which can be anguish or "facing of the abyss" [W-Lim].

**Quote.** "Liminal entities are neither here nor there; they are betwixt and between the positions assigned and arrayed by law, custom, convention, and ceremonial." [Turner-LC]

## 3. Mircea Eliade, *Rites and Symbols of Initiation* (1958; orig. *Birth and Rebirth*)

**Header.** Eliade, historian of religions; Haskell Lectures, Chicago, 1958. Why in: **core** — initiatory death/rebirth as an ontological, not just social, change.

| phase | what happens | who acts | initiate feels/does |
|---|---|---|---|
| Separation | from mother, isolation in bush | instructor | treated as ghost, eats without hands [Eliade-ER] |
| Ordeal / death | circumcision, tooth extraction, swallowing by monster-hut | masked elders | ritual "death" (descensus ad inferos / regressus ad uterum) [Eliade-ER] |
| Revelation | sacred objects (bull-roarers), myths of origin | old masters | learns sacred history [Eliade-ER] |
| Rebirth | new name, new language, return | community | "has become another" [Eliade-RSI] |

- Q1: puberty rites obligatory for all; secret societies and shamanic vocations selective [Eliade-ER].
- Q2: separation from mother, bush seclusion [Eliade-ER].
- Q3: sleeplessness, fasting, ants, circumcision; sometimes fatal [Eliade-ER].
- Q4: sacred objects and myths: the novice learns he is "the creation of supernatural beings" [Eliade-ER].
- Q5: death of the "natural, acultural man"; birth of one "born to the spirit" [Eliade-ER].
- Q6: "old masters… the spiritual elite" [Eliade-ER].
- Q7: new name, secret language [Eliade-ER].
- Q8: "responsible member of the society" [Eliade-RSI].
- Q9: not addressed directly; *Judgment*: degrees (Q10) serve as the sustaining structure.
- Q10: ladder — puberty rites can have stages; sacred history "revealed only gradually"; continuity to secret societies with "constant increase in the severity of the ordeals" [Eliade-ER; Eliade-RSI].
- Q11: initiatory motifs survive in novels, cinema, the "imaginary"; modern "longing for… a renovatio" [Eliade-ER].
- Q12: cruelty; deaths concealed from mothers ("killed by the spirit") [Eliade-ER].
- Q13: three types — puberty, secret society, shamanic (dismemberment, skeleton, new organs, ascent, resurrection) [Eliade-ER].

**Mechanics.** (1) Symbolic death then rebirth; C/B (comparative ethnography); driver: death is the strongest image of an end [Eliade-RSI]. (2) Graded revelation; C; driver: depth requires vocation and will [Eliade-ER]. (3) Modelling on a superhuman pattern; C; driver: one "becomes a real man to the extent that one resembles a superhuman being" [Eliade-ER].

**Criticism.** J. Z. Smith: comparison stresses sameness, ignores difference; ahistorical, crypto-theological [https://s-space.snu.ac.kr/bitstream/10371/4921/3/ReligionandCulture_v11_223.pdf]; Eliade's 1930s Iron Guard support and its link to his concepts debated [https://www.tandfonline.com/doi/full/10.1080/01916599.2024.2437372].

**Quotes.** "The central moment of every initiation is represented by the ceremony symbolizing the death of the novice and his return to the fellowship of the living." [Eliade-RSI PDF]. "…the novice emerges from his ordeal endowed with a totally different being from that which he possessed before his initiation; he has become another." [Eliade-RSI archive.org]

## 4. Joseph Campbell — the monomyth (*The Hero with a Thousand Faces*, 1949)

**Header.** Campbell, comparative mythologist, Jungian-influenced; 1949; popularized by *The Power of Myth* (1988) [W-HJ]. Why in: **control/closest to target** — narrative template already used by games and film (Lucas, Vogler).

| phase | what happens | who acts | initiate feels/does |
|---|---|---|---|
| Departure | call, refusal, supernatural aid, first threshold, belly of the whale | mentor, threshold guardian | reluctance, then commitment [W-HJ] |
| Initiation | road of trials, goddess, atonement with father, apotheosis, boon | helpers, powers | ordeal, realization [W-HJ] |
| Return | refusal of return, magic flight, rescue, return threshold, master of two worlds, freedom to live | world "comes and gets him" | brings boon to community [W-HJ] |

- Q1: call may be chosen, imposed, or "a mere blunder"; refusal is a stage [W-HJ].
- Q2: crossing the first threshold; belly of the whale = "self-annihilation" [W-HJ].
- Q3: trials "again, again, and again" [W-HJ].
- Q4: apotheosis; ultimate boon [W-HJ].
- Q5: hero "swallowed into the unknown and would appear to have died" [W-HJ].
- Q6: mentor; "rescue from without" [W-HJ].
- Q7: talismans from the mentor [W-HJ]; no formal mark.
- Q8: boon for "the renewing of the community" [W-HJ].
- Q9: **return threshold explicitly addresses fading**: "dreams that were momentous by night may seem simply silly in the light of day" [W-HJ].
- Q10: one cycle; *Judgment*: implicitly repeatable.
- Q11: framed as story, not rite — which is why it travels into secular media [W-HJ].
- Q12: Brin: template supports "despotism"; privilege bias (Li) [W-HJ].
- Q13: Rogers et al. 2023 (JPSP) reportedly found that seeing one's life as a Hero's Journey increases meaning in life [W-HJ] — paper itself not opened.

**Mechanics.** (1) Refusal-then-commitment; C; driver: consent made explicit by overcoming hesitation [W-HJ]. (2) Return threshold as a named stage; C; driver: anticipates devaluation [W-HJ]. (3) Life re-narrated as journey; A (as reported, single multi-study paper) [W-HJ].

**Criticism.** Toelken and Dundes: source-selection bias, "non-expert"; Crespi: "ethnocentrism", devoid of ethnographic context; masculine bias (Murdock, Frankel) [W-HJ].

**Quote.** "A hero ventures forth from the world of common day into a region of supernatural wonder: fabulous forces are there encountered and a decisive victory is won: the hero comes back from this mysterious adventure with the power to bestow boons on his fellow man." (Campbell 1949:30, via [W-HJ])

## 5. Ndembu *Mukanda* boys' circumcision (Zambia; Turner fieldwork 1953)

**Header.** Ndembu, Mwinilunga District, NW Zambia; Turner observed one performance, seclusion 14 June–8 Aug 1953 [FoS]. Why in: **core ethnography** — best-documented case of the van Gennep/Turner model.

| phase | what happens | who acts | initiate feels/does |
|---|---|---|---|
| Kwing'ija (induction) | senior novice insults circumciser to "invite" him; night dance; boys snatched from mothers, through mukula gateway, to circumcision site called "place of dying" | headmen, circumcisers | carried, feet not touching ground; mothers wail as at a death [eGK; Bush] |
| Kung'ula (seclusion) | lodge in bush; healing; food/salt and parental sex taboos; discipline; makishi masked dancers; lore, riddles, dances | lodge instructor, elders | obedience, speak only when spoken to [eGK] |
| Kwidisha (return) | white clay decoration; night dance; ku-tomboka war dance evaluated publicly; back to villages | community | presented as changed persons [eGK] |

- Q1: elders decide, endorsed by women; collective, pre-puberty [eGK]; boys do not consent individually.
- Q2: abduction, stripped of clothes [Biebuyck]; lodge far from village [FoS].
- Q3: real: circumcision; plus months of discipline [eGK].
- Q4: secrets of the lodge, tribal values; makishi = shades of dead chiefs [eGK].
- Q5: passage from mudyi (mother) tree, over muyombu (ancestors, ritual death), onto mukula (male community) [FoS-sym].
- Q6: circumcisers, fathers feed the boys; mothers greet; public evaluation of the dance [eGK; FoS-sym].
- Q7: circumcision itself; uncircumcised = "one who lacks whiteness"; initiated = "one who jumped already" [FoS]; white clay at return [eGK].
- Q8: member of "male moral community", linked to wider vicinage and tribe [eGK].
- Q9: post-rite food and sex taboos commanded in final speech [eGK]; *Judgment*: identity is re-anchored in a new social network, not only in memory of the rite.
- Q10: also hunting cults and curative cults later in life, entered via affliction [eGK].
- Q11: origin myth frames it as "healing" and cleanliness [eGK].
- Q12: rite is also a prestige contest among headmen [eGK].
- Q13: seclusion shortened (two months vs usual longer) [FoS; eGK].

**Mechanics.** (1) Physical separation from mother + reattachment to fathers; B; driver: identity shifts by changing whose world one belongs to [eGK]. (2) Symbol triad traversed bodily (mudyi → muyombu → mukula); B; driver: meaning enacted through place sequence [FoS-sym]. (3) Public performance evaluated on return; B; driver: community witnesses competence [eGK].

**Criticism.** Turner's reading is functionalist (equilibrium, prestige) [eGK]; *Judgment*: single performance, informant exegesis filtered by one ethnographer.

**Quote.** "The rite represents (1) the removal of the boy from dependence on his mother… (2) his ritual death and subsequent association with the ancestors… and (3) his incorporation into the male moral community of tribesmen." [FoS-sym]

## 6. Negative: severe initiation and hazing research (1959–2024)

**Header.** Social psychology (Aronson & Mills 1959) and evolutionary anthropology (Cimino 2011–2024). Why in: **negative** — tests the claim that suffering creates belonging.

| phase | what happens | who acts | initiate feels/does |
|---|---|---|---|
| Lab "severe" initiation | read embarrassing/erotic words aloud before joining boring discussion group | experimenter | reports liking group more [A&M; BPS] |
| Field hazing induction | ~10-week fraternity pledging; harsh and fun activities | actives | surveyed 5 times [C&T] |
| Outcome | solidarity measured toward pledges, actives, chapter | — | fun predicts solidarity; harshness barely [C&T] |

- Q1: voluntary joining; hazers' motive rises with perceived near-term group benefits newcomers could exploit [Cimino-XC].
- Q2: not addressed.
- Q3: real cost; lab versions "relatively trivial" compared to months of real hazing [C&T].
- Q4: none.
- Q5: none.
- Q6: hazers; dependence on hazers increased in lab inductions [Keating].
- Q7: membership.
- Q8: membership; no role change studied.
- Q9: no evidence harshness yields durable solidarity [C&T].
- Q10: not addressed.
- Q11: not addressed.
- Q12: core: humiliation reduces affiliation [Mann]; hazing may select out less-committed members rather than bond [Kent].
- Q13: dissonance theory (effort justification) is the original rationale [A&M].

**Mechanics.** (1) Effort justification: A (controlled experiment, n=63 women) but replications mixed — Gerard & Mathewson 1966 supported it with shocks [G&M]; Kamau 2013 (n=70) did not replicate; reward after mild initiation produced more group identity [Kamau]. (2) Fun predicts solidarity: B (longitudinal field, N=126, six pledge classes); harshness significant for only one of seven measures (B=0.11), fun B=0.23–0.41 [C&T]. (3) Harshness + fun both predict group importance; harsh lab inductions raise dependence on group opinion: B/A [Keating].

**Failures/criticism.** Lab paradigm tests dissonance, not real initiation; external validity weak [C&T]; Lodewijkx & Syroit 1997: in real Dutch student groups, perceived severity predicted *less* liking [C&T]; one chapter, WEIRD sample, non-experimental [C&T].

**Quotes.** "Our results provide little support for common models of solidarity and suggest that hazing may not be the social glue it has long been assumed to be." [C&T]. "In our data, what appeared to be driving solidarity was having fun." — Aldo Cimino [Kent]

## Cluster patterns

- **Three-phase grammar is invariant across all five positive sources**: separation → threshold/ordeal → return (van Gennep, Turner, Eliade, Campbell, Mukanda). Sources above.
- **Return is the weak link, and the tradition knows it**: van Gennep's successors find programmes without incorporation lose effect [W-RoP]; Campbell names a "return threshold" where the vision looks "silly in the light of day" [W-HJ]. *Judgment*: this is the closest classical analogue to Sasha's devaluation dip (frame steps 3–4).
- **A guide who has crossed is structural, not decorative**: master of ceremonies [W-Lim], old masters [Eliade-ER], lodge instructor [eGK]; liminality without one breeds "self-proclaimed ceremony masters" [W-Lim].
- **Revelation is staged and graded**, not given at once: sacred history "revealed only gradually" [Eliade-ER]; Ndembu add later cults [eGK].
- **Change is anchored by re-wiring relationships, not by the peak moment**: Mukanda moves the boy from mother's world to the male moral community [FoS-sym]; communitas bonds the cohort [W-Lim].
- **Marks are public and bodily or worn**: ring/cord [W-RoP], circumcision and white clay [FoS; eGK], new name [Eliade-ER].
- **Modern secular form = liminoid**: optional, play-based, but without status change [W-Lim]; Eliade sees initiatory motifs surviving in novels and cinema [Eliade-ER]. *Judgment*: a digital rite sits here by default and must add witness + role to become liminal.
- **Severity is not the active ingredient**: best field data show fun/reward, not harshness, predicts belonging; humiliation reduces affiliation [C&T; Kamau; Mann]. *Judgment*: the ordeal should be a real test of the self, not imposed suffering.
- **Consent is mostly absent in classical rites** (obligatory puberty rites; Ndembu boys snatched) [Eliade-ER; eGK]; only vocational/secret-society and hero-myth forms feature an explicit call and refusal [Eliade-ER; W-HJ].
- **Universalist theorists are the most criticized** (Campbell, Eliade: selection bias, ahistorical) [W-HJ; SNU]; ethnography and experiments are the stronger evidence base.
