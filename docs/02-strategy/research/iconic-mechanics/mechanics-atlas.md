## I. Games

Sources read 2026-11-22. Wikipedia figures were read from the full article text (en.wikipedia.org API extract), not search snippets. Grades: A = peer-reviewed / official data (incl. SEC filings, federation data); B = serious journalism, books, well-sourced encyclopedia; C = company claim, fan site, or attribution without direct evidence.

### Chess (as an institution: ratings, titles, federations)
- Kind: competitive board game → global sporting institution · Longevity: rules stable since ~1475; FIDE since 1924 (102 yrs); Elo since 1960/1970 · Holon: H6 (H3 clubs inside H5 national federations) · Quadrant strongest: ITS
- Mechanics:
  1. **Public numerical rating (Elo)** — every rated game moves your number up or down by a formula anyone can check. Evidence: USCF adopted it in 1960 and FIDE in 1970, replacing the Harkness system as "fairer and more accurate"; the FIDE lists now hold 1,643,067 rated players (502,209 standard), May 2025 ([Wikipedia: Elo](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Elo_rating_system); [ChessBase](https://en.chessbase.com/post/chess-statistics-today)), grade A/B. Driver: you get a status that is legible, portable and earned, with no committee deciding it.
  2. **Titles earned by results (GM, IM, Arbiter)** — fixed, lifelong titles the federation awards for norms reached in real play. Evidence: grandmasters grew from 524 (1993) to ~1,730–1,800 (2025); India went from 2 GMs to 75+ as its rated base grew past 32,000 ([ChessBase](https://en.chessbase.com/post/chess-statistics-today)), grade B. Driver: a permanent rung above the fluctuating number, so progress you bank can't be lost.
  3. **Federation of federations, one nation one vote** — FIDE founded by players in Paris (1924) "as a kind of players' union"; Euwe (1970–78) and Campomanes (1982–95) grew membership with one vote per country. Evidence: ~200 member federations ([Wikipedia: FIDE](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/FIDE)), grade B. Driver: every country owns a piece of the ladder, so local clubs feed one shared world ranking.
- Invariant hint: one transparent ladder shared by everyone, with local entry points everywhere.

### Go (dan/kyu ranks, handicap stones)
- Kind: competitive board game with a teaching guild · Longevity: game >2,000 yrs; formal ranking since early 17th c. (Japan) · Holon: H3 teacher-student circle → H6 · Quadrant strongest: WE
- Mechanics:
  1. **Two-tier rank ladder (kyu → dan)** — beginners start ~30 kyu and count down; dan ranks count up to 9. Evidence: Japan formalised Go teaching and ranking from the early 17th century and the system is still in use worldwide; European federations later layered Elo on top ([Wikipedia: Go ranks](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Go_ranks_and_ratings)), grade B. Driver: "student" vs "master" grades give you a clear point of arrival (shodan) and a long road after it.
  2. **Handicap stones calibrated to rank** — one rank difference ≈ one free stone, so any two players can have an even game (a 3d gives a 1d two stones and wins half the time). Evidence: same source, grade B. Driver: strong and weak play together and both have to try; learning happens across levels instead of only among equals.
- Invariant hint: rank as a promise of a fair game, not just as status.

### Monopoly (+ origin: The Landlord's Game)
- Kind: commercial board game born as a teaching folk game · Longevity: 1904 patent (Magie) → Parker Brothers 1935; ~120 yrs · Holon: H3 (table) · Quadrant strongest: ITS
- Mechanics:
  1. **Rent that escalates with ownership** — owning a full colour group or more railroads multiplies rent until one player absorbs everything. Evidence: Magie built it on Henry George's ideas "to demonstrate how rents enrich property owners and impoverish tenants" ([Wikipedia: Landlord's Game](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/The_Landlord%27s_Game)); Monopoly then sold an estimated 275 million copies by 2015 ([Wikipedia: Monopoly](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Monopoly_(game))), grade B. Driver: you feel the economic lesson in your body instead of being told it (it's also why games end in a runaway).
  2. **Folk spread through home-made, localised boards** — before any company sold it, Quakers in Atlantic City and college students drew their own boards with local street names and taught the rules hand to hand; Darrow copied one of those boards. Evidence: same sources (the "Marvin Gardens" misspelling came from the Todds' home-made board), grade B. Driver: players who rename the streets after their own town feel they own the game, and teaching it becomes a social act.
  3. **House rules (Free Parking jackpot etc.)** — groups change the rules themselves; Hasbro eventually made it official with a Facebook vote and a House Rules edition (2014). Evidence: same source, grade B. Driver: a group that writes its own rules feels like it belongs to the game.
- Invariant hint: a game spreads as a folk object when people can rename it and change its rules.

### Dungeons & Dragons (the game master)
- Kind: tabletop role-playing game · Longevity: 1974 → 52 yrs · Holon: H3 (table of 3–7) inside an H5 network of tables · Quadrant strongest: WE
- Mechanics:
  1. **A human game master (DM)** — one player is referee, narrator and every non-player character, and rules on whatever the players try. Evidence: core role since 1974; Wizards of the Coast/Hasbro claim more than 50 million people have played ([Hasbro 2024](https://newsroom.hasbro.com/news-releases/news-release-details/dungeons-dragons-celebrates-50th-anniversary-2024-more-50); [Wikipedia: DM](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Dungeon_Master)), grade C (company claim). Driver: a person who reacts to your choices makes the world feel alive in a way no rulebook can.
  2. **Experience points → levels across sessions** — characters grow over a campaign. Evidence: progression is the core loop ([Wikipedia: D&D](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Dungeons_%26_Dragons)), grade B (attribution, no isolated test). Driver: you come back next week because your character is someone you invested in.
  3. **Open rules (OGL 2000 → Creative Commons)** — the d20 system was opened to other publishers. Evidence: credited with a "boom in the RPG industry"; when WotC tried to revoke it in Jan 2023, the reaction forced it to put the rules into CC BY 4.0 (same source), grade B. Driver: third parties invest in a commons they can't lose, which multiplies the content and the places to play.
- Invariant hint: a small circle held by one human facilitator; an open commons around it.

### SimCity (1989)
- Kind: simulation "software toy" · Longevity: 1989 → franchise ongoing (37 yrs) · Holon: H1 (solo player governing an H4–H5 simulated city) · Quadrant strongest: ITS
- Mechanics:
  1. **No win state: open-ended system to tinker with** — Wright built it after finding he enjoyed designing levels in the editor more than playing his earlier game; scenarios with win/lose were added later. Evidence: it sold poorly at first, then positive press boosted it to 500,000 home-computer + 1.98 million SNES units and numerous awards ([Wikipedia](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/SimCity_(1989_video_game))), grade B. Driver: you author your own goals; the reward is watching a system respond to you.
  2. **Visible feedback loops (zoning → traffic, crime, budget)** — every choice ripples through readable systems. Evidence: no isolated measurement; widely credited, grade C. Driver: you learn how systems work by poking them.
- Invariant hint: a sandbox where the player sets the goal and the system answers back.

### Ultima Online (1997)
- Kind: MMORPG (term popularised with it) · Longevity: 1997 → still running (29 yrs) · Holon: H4–H5 (server shards, player towns) · Quadrant strongest: ITS
- Mechanics:
  1. **Persistent player housing + player-driven crafting economy** — houses stay in the world; goods are made and traded by players. Evidence: first MMORPG to reach 100,000 paying subscribers, within six months ([Wikipedia](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Ultima_Online)), grade B. Driver: you leave a permanent mark in a shared place, and other people depend on what you make.
  2. **Consent split (Trammel vs Felucca, 2000)** — a mirror world where non-consensual player-vs-player combat was off and there was more land for housing, while the original world kept the harsh rules. Evidence: subscribers rose after the split and peaked ~250,000 in July 2003 (same source), grade B (it's a correlation). Driver: newcomers and builders get a safe place to be, and those who want risk choose it.
- Invariant hint: a durable home in a shared world, with opt-in risk.

### The Sims (2000)
- Kind: life-simulation "dollhouse" · Longevity: 2000 → 26 yrs · Holon: H1 (player) authoring an H2–H3 household · Quadrant strongest: I
- Mechanics:
  1. **Open-ended play on everyday life** — no fixed goal; you run people's days, homes and relationships. Evidence: best-selling PC game in history by March 2002 (6.3 million copies, passing Myst); 16 million shipped by 2005; franchise ~200 million ([Wikipedia](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/The_Sims)), grade B. Driver: you project yourself and others into ordinary life and tell your own stories.
  2. **Motive model drawn from Maslow and *A Pattern Language*** — Sims have visible drive bars (hunger, social, fun…) that the player balances. Evidence: Wright's stated design sources (same source), grade C for causation. Driver: simple readable inner states make little characters feel alive and give you something to care for.
- Invariant hint: a mirror of everyday life that the player authors.

### StarCraft (1998) + Korean e-sports / PC bangs
- Kind: competitive RTS → national spectator sport · Longevity: 1998 → Brood War still played; 28 yrs · Holon: H4 (the PC bang as neighbourhood third place) → H6 (televised national leagues) · Quadrant strongest: WE
- Mechanics:
  1. **A cheap third place to play together (PC bang)** — pay-by-the-hour rooms with broadband. Evidence: PC bangs grew from ~100 (1997) to 13,000+ (1999); Korea bought one-third of 1998 global sales, "primarily by PC bang" ([Rea, Education About Asia 2016](https://www.asianstudies.org/publications/eaa/archives/crafting-stars-south-korean-e-sports-and-the-emergence-of-a-digital-gaming-culture/)); 4.5 million of ~9.5–11 million copies sold were in Korea ([Korea Times 2017](https://www.koreatimes.co.kr/business/companies/20170806/understanding-korea-key-to-developing-starcrafts-facelift)), grade A/B. Driver: the game became a group outing ("let's play a round of StarCraft" replaced billiards), not a solitary habit.
  2. **Spectatorship turned into leagues** — PC-bang owners saw people coming to watch, ran local tournaments, then sponsored players; cable channels began broadcasting in 1999. Evidence: pro-gaming recognised as an official job category in 2000; ~100,000 fans at the 2004 league finals; corporate teams (Samsung, SK Telecom, KT) from ~2003 (Rea, same link), grade A/B. Driver: watching people a little better than you, from your own neighbourhood, creates heroes and a path upward.
- Invariant hint: a local gathering place feeds a visible ladder of local heroes.

### Diablo II (2000)
- Kind: action RPG with online co-op · Longevity: 2000 → remaster 2021; 26 yrs · Holon: H2–H3 (party of up to 8) · Quadrant strongest: IT
- Mechanics:
  1. **Free online co-op through Battle.net** — no subscription; matchmaking for parties; "closed realms" stored characters on servers to stop cheating. Evidence: Wikipedia lists free Battle.net access as one of the "major factors that contributed to the game's success"; fastest-selling PC game at the time (1 million in two weeks, Guinness), South Korea its largest market outside the US ([Wikipedia](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Diablo_II)), grade B. Driver: having no paywall to play with friends, plus a fair shared space, keeps the group together.
  2. **Randomised loot across difficulty tiers** — random dungeons and item drops, better rewards at higher difficulty. Evidence: design fact (same source); causation is widely asserted but not isolated, grade C. Driver: an uncertain reward keeps "one more run" attractive.
- Invariant hint: a free shared space plus a reason to come back each time.

### Minecraft (2009)
- Kind: sandbox building/survival game + UGC platform · Longevity: public alpha May 2009 → 17 yrs · Holon: H1 → H3 (friend servers) → H5 (public servers) · Quadrant strongest: IT
- Mechanics:
  1. **Open sandbox sold while still being built** — paid public alpha from May 2009, full release Nov 2011. Evidence: best-selling video game of all time, 400 million+ copies ([Wikipedia](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Minecraft)), grade B. Driver: you can build anything, and early buyers feel they are co-creating the game.
  2. **User-made servers, mods, maps + creator videos** — the community adds mechanics and spreads them. Evidence: 4 million+ Minecraft videos on YouTube by May 2012; second most-searched term on YouTube in 2014 (same source), grade B. Driver: what you build is worth showing, and every video brings in new players.
  3. **Education Edition (2016)** — schools adopted it as a learning space. Evidence: same source, grade B (adoption, not measured outcomes).
- Invariant hint: give people blocks and a stage; they bring the content and the audience.

### Roblox (2006)
- Kind: platform of user-made games with a creator economy · Longevity: 2006 → 20 yrs · Holon: H5 (millions of experiences, each a small world) · Quadrant strongest: ITS
- Mechanics:
  1. **Free creation tools + real-money payouts (Developer Exchange)** — creators earn Robux and cash it out. Evidence: creators earned $922.8M in 2024 and $1,503.1M in 2025; 24,500 → 35,500 creators registered in DevEx ([Roblox 10-K FY2025 via SEC](https://www.sec.gov/Archives/edgar/data/1315098/000131509826000024/rblx-20251231.htm); [Roblox newsroom Sep 2026](https://about.roblox.com/newsroom/2026/09/global-impact-of-creation-on-roblox)), grade A. Driver: players become producers, and the move from playing to making is a short, paid step.
  2. **Catalogue of player-made worlds instead of one game** — 85.3 million daily active users in Q4 2024, averaging 2.4 hours/day ([Roblox newsroom Mar 2025](https://about.roblox.com/newsroom/2025/03/unveiling-future-creation-native-3d-generation-collaborative-studio-tools-economy-expansion)), grade C (company). Driver: endless variety, and friends move between worlds together. Note: the median DevEx creator earns ~$1,500/yr, so most creators are hobbyists.
- Invariant hint: let the players own the means of production.

### Animal Crossing (2001; New Horizons 2020)
- Kind: real-time life sim · Longevity: 2001 → 25 yrs · Holon: H4 (an island village) + H2/H3 visits · Quadrant strongest: WE
- Mechanics:
  1. **Real-time clock and calendar** — the game runs on the console's clock: shops close at night, seasons and holidays are real, resources refill tomorrow. Evidence: the series' defining feature ([Wikipedia: series](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Animal_Crossing)); New Horizons sold 13.41 million in six weeks and 50 million+ total, though success is "attributed in part" to its release during pandemic lockdowns ([Wikipedia: NH](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Animal_Crossing:_New_Horizons)), grade B. Driver: a small daily ritual with no penalty for skipping; the world waits for you.
  2. **Visiting each other's islands** — travel to friends' islands, gifts, trading. Evidence: during lockdown it was used for weddings, graduation ceremonies, campaigning and protests (same source), grade B. Driver: a place that is yours becomes a place to host others.
- Invariant hint: a ritual on a real rhythm, in a home you can host others in.

### Stardew Valley (2016)
- Kind: farming/life sim by a solo developer · Longevity: 2016 → 10 yrs · Holon: H4 (Pelican Town, ~30 villagers) · Quadrant strongest: WE
- Mechanics:
  1. **Community Center bundles** — collect sets of goods to restore a shared building and unlock new areas. Evidence: a core progression goal ([Wikipedia](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Stardew_Valley)); the game sold 50 million by Feb 2026, grade C for causation. Driver: your personal grind visibly rebuilds a commons.
  2. **Long tail of free updates, incl. co-op (2018)** — the solo creator kept adding content and multiplayer for free. Evidence: 425k sold in two weeks, 3.5M by end-2017, 50M by 2026 (same source); the tie to updates is inferred, grade C. Driver: trust in a single craftsman who keeps showing up builds word of mouth.
- Invariant hint: personal effort that restores a shared place, on a seasonal rhythm.

### Among Us (2018, boom 2020)
- Kind: online social-deduction party game · Longevity: 2018 → 8 yrs · Holon: H3 (4–15 players per lobby) · Quadrant strongest: WE
- Mechanics:
  1. **Meeting-and-vote deduction (Mafia structure in a task game)** — crewmates do tasks; bodies trigger a discussion and a plurality vote ejects a suspect. Evidence: inspired by Mafia ([Wikipedia](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Among_Us)), grade B. Driver: the fun is talking, accusing and bluffing with people you know.
  2. **Discovery led by streamers** — little attention in 2018; Sodapoppin popularised it on Twitch in July 2020, then many large creators followed. Evidence: SuperData estimated ~500 million players by Nov 2020, 97% on the free mobile version (same source), grade B/C. Driver: the reactions are as entertaining to watch as to play, and a free mobile version removes the cost of joining in.
- Invariant hint: a small circle whose core activity is conversation, and fun to watch.

### Journey (thatgamecompany, 2012)
- Kind: wordless cooperative adventure · Longevity: 2012 → 14 yrs · Holon: H2 (two anonymous strangers) · Quadrant strongest: I/WE
- Mechanics:
  1. **Anonymous pairing with no words** — you meet one stranger; no names until the credits, no text or voice, only a musical chime. Evidence: fastest-selling game ever on PlayStation Store (US and EU) at launch; more fan letters in two weeks than for *Flower* in three years ([PlayStation Blog, Chen 2012](https://blog.playstation.com/2012/03/29/journey-is-psns-fastest-selling-game-soundtrack-coming-soon); [Wikipedia](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Journey_(2012_video_game))), grade B/C. Driver: without status, names or a way to be cruel, people assume the best about each other and bond.
  2. **Only helping actions exist** — chiming recharges the other player's scarf so they can fly; there's no way to hurt them. Evidence: same sources; in testing, 3 of 25 testers cried at the end, grade C. Driver: when the only possible move is helping, help becomes the language.
- Invariant hint: strip away status and give people only one way to act: helping.

### Foundation (Asimov, 1942–53: psychohistory, Encyclopedia, Seldon Plan)
- Kind: fiction that became a civilisation-scale myth · Longevity: 1942 → 84 yrs · Holon: H6 · Quadrant strongest: ITS
- Mechanics (narrative mechanics; the evidence is their effect on real readers):
  1. **Psychohistory: you can steer the masses, not individuals** — a statistical science of whole societies that shortens a dark age from 30,000 to 1,000 years. Evidence: Hugo "Best All-Time Series" 1966 ([Wikipedia](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Foundation_series)); Nobel laureate Krugman: "I wanted to be one of those guys… the closest you can get… is being an economist" ([Nobel interview 2008](https://www.nobelprize.org/prizes/economic-sciences/2008/krugman/26028-interview-transcript-2008-2/)), grade A for the quote, B for its influence. Driver: it gives a young reader a role, the social scientist who saves civilisation.
  2. **The Encyclopedia as a legible cover task + messages recorded for future crises (Time Vault)** — the Foundation thinks it is compiling an Encyclopedia while the real plan plays out; Seldon's pre-recorded messages appear at each crisis. Evidence: only within the story (Krugman also mentions the Time Vault: [Guardian 2012](https://www.theguardian.com/books/2012/dec/04/paul-krugman-asimov-economics)), grade C. Driver: people commit to a concrete, honourable task, and the bigger meaning is revealed in stages at the right moments.
- Invariant hint: a long plan delivered in stages to people doing a concrete daily task.

### Ender's Game (Card, 1985)
- Kind: fiction about a learning institution run as a game · Longevity: 1985 → 41 yrs · Holon: H3 (cadet teams of ~40, in toons of ~5–8) inside H5 · Quadrant strongest: IT
- Mechanics (described as organisational learning):
  1. **Learning through escalating team simulations** — trainees learn command by running teams in zero-gravity practice contests whose rules keep changing. Evidence: Hugo (1986) and Nebula (1985) best novel; suggested reading for organisations such as the US Marine Corps ([Wikipedia](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Ender%27s_Game)), grade B. Driver: skills form fastest under real stakes, live feedback and rising difficulty.
  2. **Peer leaders and reframing ("the enemy's gate is down")** — young leaders get small teams and are rewarded for redefining the problem rather than following doctrine. Evidence: no independent measurement; the book's place on leadership reading lists suggests readers value it, grade C. Driver: trust to lead early, plus permission to see the game differently.
- Invariant hint: learn by playing real contests in small teams whose leaders are peers.

### Martial-arts belts (judo kyu/dan, BJJ)
- Kind: embodied grading system · Longevity: judo ranks 1883 (Kanō); coloured kyu belts ~1926 (Budokwai London) / 1935 (Kawaishi, Paris) → 140+ yrs · Holon: H3 (dojo) → H6 (worldwide grading) · Quadrant strongest: WE
- Mechanics:
  1. **A visible rank worn on the body** — the belt colour shows your grade on the mat. Evidence: Kanō's 1883 kyu/dan system was extended with coloured belts in the early 1900s–1930s and copied by other arts, BJJ included ([Wikipedia: judo ranks](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Rank_in_Judo); [Wikipedia: Kawaishi](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Mikonosuke_Kawaishi)), grade B (spread across many arts is the evidence of fitness). Driver: you see progress at a glance, and the next step is always visible on someone near you.
  2. **Promotion by a higher-ranked teacher, with minimum time at each belt** — rank is granted by someone who walked the path; BJJ is known for conservative, informal promotion. Evidence: the IBJJF requires at least two years at blue belt before purple (waived for an adult world champion) ([Wikipedia: BJJ ranking](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Brazilian_jiu-jitsu_ranking_system)), grade B. Driver: a rank that can't be bought or rushed keeps its meaning.
- Invariant hint: status granted by peers who walked the path, visible on the body.

### Humans vs Zombies (campus game, 2005)
- Kind: live-action campus tag game · Longevity: 2005 (Goucher College) → 21 yrs · Holon: H4 (a campus) inside an H5 network of campuses · Quadrant strongest: WE
- Mechanics:
  1. **Infection instead of elimination** — a tagged human becomes a zombie and keeps playing on the other side; zombies wear headbands. Evidence: played at 1,000+ locations on six continents ([Wikipedia](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Humans_vs._Zombies)), grade B (adoption). Driver: nobody gets knocked out, so the game grows as it goes and losing is a new role, not an exit.
  2. **Played over ordinary campus life, with safe zones and missions** — the game runs for days across real routes; dorms, dining halls and academic buildings are safe zones; moderators run story missions. Evidence: same source, grade B. Driver: everyday paths become an adventure without taking over study or sleep.
  3. **Open rules, local moderators** — the founders published guideline rules that each campus adapts. Evidence: same source, grade B. Driver: a free kit that local organisers own spreads virally.
- Invariant hint: a game laid over real daily life, where losers switch sides instead of leaving.

### Cross-cutting patterns in this class
- **A legible ladder that anyone can climb** (Elo, kyu/dan, belts, XP levels, KeSPA leagues): status is earned in public play, often granted by people who walked the path, and banked as a title that doesn't fluctuate. The strongest versions pair the ladder with fairness across levels (Go handicaps, BJJ minimum time).
- **Small circle, big whole**: almost every success is a 2–15 person unit (table, party, lobby, dojo, pair) inside a large network (federation, Battle.net, servers, campuses). The human facilitator of the small circle (DM, teacher, moderator, PC-bang owner) is often the real engine.
- **Players become producers / owners**: folk Monopoly boards and house rules, OGL, Minecraft servers and videos, Roblox DevEx, HvZ open rules. Adoption jumps when the community can rename, change or monetise the game, and openness has proven load-bearing (the OGL reversal in 2023).
- **Physical or real-time anchoring**: PC bangs as third places, Animal Crossing's real clock, HvZ overlaid on campus routes, belts worn on the body. Games that live in real places and real rhythms create habits and gatherings, not just sessions.
- **Safety and consent shape who stays**: UO's Trammel split, Journey's help-only actions, HvZ safe zones. Lowering harm for newcomers while letting others choose risk widens the base.
- **Watchability as distribution**: StarCraft spectators → leagues; Among Us and Minecraft spread through streamers and videos. Mechanics that create visible human reactions (deduction, rivalry, building) get their own free marketing.

## II. Products

Scope: 19 products. Already done elsewhere and skipped: Duolingo, Apple Watch rings, Facebook, LinkedIn, Couchsurfing, Stack Overflow.
Grades: A = peer-reviewed or official data (company filings, official stats pages); B = serious journalism or books; C = company claim, blog or fan site. Company blog numbers are graded C unless they are audited filings.

### Khan Academy
- Kind: learning platform (non-profit) · Longevity: ~18 yrs, 2008 · Holon: H1 person (inside an H3 class) · Quadrant strongest: IT
- Mechanics:
  1. **Mastery levels per skill** — each skill moves Attempted → Familiar → Proficient → Mastered, and you advance only by showing the skill again on mixed reviews. Evidence: in a 3-year Newark panel, each skill raised to proficient added +.003 SD on the state test, and 60 more skills per year roughly doubled average year-to-year gains ([Newark white paper](https://blog.khanacademy.org/wp-content/uploads/2025/11/Newark_White_Paper-2.pdf)), grade C (company research); a PNAS study of ~200K students found gains roughly linear with practice ([PNAS 2026](https://www.pnas.org/doi/10.1073/pnas.2507708123)), grade A. Driver: progress is visible and earned, and gaps get closed before the next step.
  2. **A small fixed weekly dose (30 min/week)** — a single, modest target for practice time. Evidence: in an RCT of ~11K students run with the University of Toronto, students in grades 3–6 averaging ~35 min/week scored .12–.17 SD higher; grades 7–8, averaging ~10 min/week, did not ([KA blog on the RCT](https://blog.khanacademy.org/university-of-toronto-randomized-controlled-trial-of-11k-students-demonstrates-a-meaningful-positive-effect-of-khan-academy-on-student-learning)), grade A (RCT, reported by KA). Driver: a target this small is reachable, so the habit forms. Only ~9% of students reach it.
- Invariant hint: "competence proven step by step, at a steady small rhythm".

### Oura
- Kind: wearable + subscription · Longevity: ~13 yrs, 2013 · Holon: H1 person · Quadrant strongest: I (body read as IT)
- Mechanics:
  1. **One morning score (Sleep/Readiness 0–100)** — the app turns dozens of signals into one number with plain bands (85+ optimal, under 70 means pay attention). Evidence: 5.0M paid members, ~85% weighted 12-month membership retention, >94% of activated rings convert to paid ([Oura S-1, SEC](https://www.sec.gov/Archives/edgar/data/2133022/000119312526381855/d119865ds1.htm); [TechCrunch](https://techcrunch.com/2026/09/03/oura-files-to-go-public)), grade A. In a longitudinal field study, 65 of 82 users checked the app daily, typically on waking ([Oulu study](https://oulurepo.oulu.fi/bitstream/10024/45201/1/nbnfi-fe2023040334705.pdf)), grade A. Driver: a daily verdict on yourself that is cheap to read and invites small experiments, such as drinking less.
  2. **Crowns for high scores** — symbolic marks on days scoring 85+. Evidence: interviewees named them as motivating ([PACIS 2025](https://aisel.aisnet.org/pacis2025/hci/hci/7)), grade A (qualitative). Driver: competence feedback. Side effect: score-chasing and "orthosomnia" (see the Oura blog on [orthosomnia](https://ouraring.com/blog/what-is-orthosomnia/)).
- Invariant hint: "a mirror that speaks one honest number every morning".

### Day One (journaling)
- Kind: private journaling app · Longevity: ~15 yrs, 2011 · Holon: H1 person · Quadrant strongest: I
- Mechanics:
  1. **On This Day resurfacing** — shows your entries from the same date in past years. Evidence: only reviews and user stories call it the most rewarding feature ([Day One feature page](https://dayoneapp.com/features/on-this-day)), grade C. No causal data.
  2. **Streaks + reminders + daily prompt** — a count of consecutive days, a custom nudge, and a question to answer. Evidence: the company reports 15M downloads, ~500K monthly actives and an Apple Design Award ([press page](https://dayoneapp.com/press)), grade C. Streak stories exist (e.g. 1,900 days), but nothing ties retention to streaks. Driver: commitment to an unbroken chain, plus a lower barrier to starting.
- Evidence overall is thin (C). The durable pull appears to be that the past becomes a gift to your future self.
- Invariant hint: "the past self returns as a gift to the present self".

### Instagram
- Kind: social photo network · Longevity: ~16 yrs, 2010 · Holon: H5–H6 · Quadrant strongest: WE
- Mechanics:
  1. **Filters make an ordinary phone photo worth sharing** — one tap makes a bad 2010 phone photo look good. Evidence: 25,000 users on day 1 and 1M in about 2 months ([TechCrunch 2012](https://techcrunch.com/2012/04/09/instagram-story-facebook-acquisition/); [NYT Bits](https://bits.blogs.nytimes.com/2010/12/21/instagram-quickly-passes-1-million-users)), grade B. Systrom names "my photos don't look good" as the core problem they set out to solve ([NPR](https://www.npr.org/transcripts/493923472?storyId=493923472)). Driver: it removes the fear of looking bad, so people share.
  2. **One-tap cross-posting to existing networks** — each post also went out to Twitter, Facebook and others. Evidence: Hoffman and Systrom call this "the true secret to scale" ([Masters of Scale](https://mastersofscale.com/kevin-systrom-how-to-keep-it-simple-while-scaling-big/)), grade B/C. Driver: every post doubled as an invitation.
  3. **Stories (24-hour, low-stakes posts at the top of the feed)** — Evidence: 250M daily users within 10 months of launch, more than Snapchat's whole app at 166M ([TechCrunch](https://techcrunch.com/2017/06/20/instagram-live-video-replays)), grade B. Driver: posts that disappear take away the pressure to be perfect.
- Invariant hint: "lower the cost of showing yourself, then let every act carry an invitation".

### Telegram
- Kind: messenger + broadcast platform · Longevity: ~13 yrs, 2013 · Holon: H3 (groups) to H5 (channels) · Quadrant strongest: ITS
- Mechanics:
  1. **Channels: one-to-many broadcast with view counters and permanent URLs** — unlimited subscribers, and new members see the full history ([Telegram blog 2015](https://telegram.org/blog/channels)). Evidence: the company reports 1T+ channel views per month and >1B MAU ([press page](https://telegram.org/press); [TechCrunch 2025](https://techcrunch.com/2025/03/19/telegram-founder-pavel-durov-says-app-now-has-1b-users-calls-whatsapp-a-cheap-watered-down-imitation/)), grade C. Driver: anyone can get a broadcast audience at no cost.
  2. **Very large groups (up to 200K) with fine-grained admin rights** — Evidence: feature only; no attribution data, grade C.
  3. **Ready as a fallback when other networks fail** — Evidence: 70M sign-ups in one day during the October 2021 Facebook/WhatsApp outage ([The Verge](https://www.theverge.com/2021/10/6/22712191/telegram-70-million-new-users-facebook-outage-surge-whatsapp)), grade B. The company reports it never paid for advertising ([GWI](https://www.gwi.com/blog/what-is-telegram)), grade C. Driver: when a trusted habit breaks, a free, near-identical option already in reach catches people.
- Invariant hint: "a stage plus a commons, built on infrastructure that stays up when others fail".

### Airbnb
- Kind: peer-to-peer marketplace · Longevity: ~18 yrs, 2008 · Holon: H2 pair (host–guest) inside H6 · Quadrant strongest: WE
- Mechanics:
  1. **Two-sided reputation (reviews)** — after each stay, the guest and host review each other in public. Evidence: a host with no reviews is ~4× less likely to get booked; 75%+ of trips are reviewed voluntarily ([Airbnb Eng](https://medium.com/airbnb-engineering/building-for-trust-503e9872bbbb)), grade C. Enough positive reviews offset homophily, i.e. trusting people who are similar to you ([PNAS 2017](https://www.pnas.org/doi/10.1073/pnas.1604234114)), grade A.
  2. **Simultaneous reveal (double-blind reviews)** — neither side sees the other's review until both have written. Evidence: in a field experiment, review rates rose and retaliation fell ([Marketing Science 2021](https://pubsonline.informs.org/doi/10.1287/mksc.2021.1311)), grade A; internal figures were +7% reviews and +2% negative reviews (Airbnb Eng, above), grade C. Driver: honest feedback without fear of retaliation, plus curiosity about what the other side wrote.
  3. **Verified professional photos** — Evidence: verified listings were booked 9% more often (difference-in-differences) ([paper](https://cdn.prod.website-files.com/5ca95f7a3be192f65a7b4e4f/617afbf92fc4b3a49b9aa0a4_how-much-is-an-image-worth-research-paper.pdf)), grade A-/B; insiders said 2–3× more bookings ([Forbes](https://www.forbes.com/sites/emilyjoffrion/2018/10/18/how-this-operator-leveraged-remote-work-to-build-airbnbs-trust-at-scale/)), grade C. Driver: the place looks real, so the leap of faith feels smaller.
- Invariant hint: "trust in a stranger is built from the testimony of people who were there before".

### BlaBlaCar
- Kind: peer-to-peer carpooling · Longevity: ~20 yrs, 2006 · Holon: H2–H3 (a car of 2–4 people) inside H5 · Quadrant strongest: WE
- Mechanics:
  1. **D.R.E.A.M.S. trust stack** — Declared identity, Rated, Engaged (prepayment), Activity, Moderated, Social. Evidence: in a survey of 18,289 members with NYU Stern, 88% highly trust a member with a full profile, versus 58% for colleagues and 92% for friends ([Entering the Trust Age](https://blog.blablacar.com/newsroom/news-list/entering-trust-age)), grade C (company survey). In a 2012 survey, peer ratings were the single biggest trust factor (3.4/5), and the full profile reached 4.2 against 4.7 for a friend ([report](https://blog.blablacar.com/wp-content/uploads/2016/05/betrustman_report.pdf)), grade C.
  2. **"Bla / BlaBla / BlaBlaBla" chattiness label** — each person declares how talkative they are in the car. Evidence: the brand is named after it, but no outcome data, grade C. Driver: it sets social expectations before you meet, so there is less awkwardness.
- Invariant hint: "a face, a verified fact and peer testimony turn a stranger into a neighbour".

### Wikipedia
- Kind: peer-produced knowledge commons · Longevity: ~25 yrs, 2001 · Holon: H5 (WikiProjects are H3–H4) · Quadrant strongest: ITS
- Mechanics:
  1. **Anyone can edit, with full history and a talk page per article** — Evidence: edits attract further edits (8 years and 63K edits analysed; [Stanford GSB](https://www.gsb.stanford.edu/insights/wikipedias-army-volunteer-editors-content-begets-content)), grade B; scholars link talk pages to coordination without a hierarchy ([ACM TOCHI review](https://dl.acm.org/doi/10.1145/3617369)), grade A. Driver: a visible gap invites contribution, and every change is reversible.
  2. **Barnstars (informal awards between peers)** — Evidence: in a randomized experiment on top editors, receiving one raised productivity by 60% ([Restivo & van de Rijt, PLOS One 2012](https://journals.plos.org/plosone/article?id=10.1371%2Fjournal.pone.0034358)), grade A. Caveat: there was no effect on less-active editors, and their retention was lower ([ICS 2014](https://www.tandfonline.com/doi/abs/10.1080/1369118X.2014.888459)).
- Invariant hint: "recognition from peers who do the same work".

### Product Hunt
- Kind: daily launch leaderboard / community · Longevity: ~13 yrs, 2013 · Holon: H4–H5 (the maker scene) · Quadrant strongest: WE
- Mechanics:
  1. **Leaderboard that resets daily** — every day starts from zero at 12:01 PT and ranks by votes and time ([Verge 2014](https://www.theverge.com/2014/10/8/6942993/product-hunt-is-where-silicon-valley-finds-out-what-s-cool); [PH help](https://help.producthunt.com/en/articles/17734306-1-how-product-hunt-works)). Evidence: when a product's rank was pushed down by an outside shock, seed and early-stage fundraising fell 9.5% ([Cao 2019, Cambridge JBS](https://www.jbs.cam.ac.uk/wp-content/uploads/2020/08/paper-cao-informationfrictions.pdf)), grade A-. Driver: a fresh chance every day, with a clear "today" for makers and visitors.
  2. **Seeding by hand-picked contributors via an email list** — Evidence: from a 20-minute email MVP to 2,000 users in 20 days, by inviting influential contributors one by one ([Hoover](https://www.ryanhoover.me/post/how-we-got-our-first-2-000-users-doing-things-that-dont-scale)), grade C. Driver: early taste sets the norms.
- Invariant hint: "a daily stage with a clean slate, seeded by curators".

### Kickstarter
- Kind: reward crowdfunding · Longevity: ~17 yrs, 2009 · Holon: H4 (a project's backers) inside H6 · Quadrant strongest: WE
- Mechanics:
  1. **All-or-nothing goal + deadline** — no money moves unless the goal is met before the deadline. Evidence: all-or-nothing campaigns succeed 34% of the time versus 17% for keep-it-all, and raise more ([Cumming et al.](http://leeds-faculty.colorado.edu/bhagat/CrowdfundingModels-KeppItAll-AllorNothing.pdf)), grade A-. Successes clear the goal by small margins while failures miss by large ones, consistent with backers rallying near the threshold ([Mollick, JBV 2014](https://www.sciencedirect.com/science/article/pii/S088390261300058X)), grade A. Driver: a commitment device that cuts risk for backers and creates urgency.
  2. **Visible progress bar (social proof)** — Evidence: a CEO claim that projects reaching 30% funding succeed 98% of the time ([cited here](https://press.rebus.community/media-innovation-and-entrepreneurship/chapter/section-5-crowdfunding/)), grade C. Overall: $8.97B pledged and 282K funded projects ([Kickstarter Stats](http://kickstarter.com/help/stats)), grade A (official).
- Invariant hint: "a shared threshold, and we all win or nobody pays".

### GitHub
- Kind: collaborative code platform · Longevity: ~18 yrs, 2008 · Holon: H3 (a core team) inside H5 · Quadrant strongest: ITS
- Mechanics:
  1. **Fork + pull request** — anyone copies the project, changes it, and offers the change back, and the maintainers decide. Evidence: 87M+ pull requests merged in one year ([Octoverse 2019](https://octoverse.github.com/2019/)) and 94M developers ([Octoverse 2022](https://octoverse.github.com/2022/)), grade A (official). Driver: you can contribute without asking permission, while the core keeps control.
  2. **Radical transparency of activity** — profiles, commits and followers are public. Evidence: interviews found people make rich social inferences from this activity and use them to coordinate and build reputation ([Dabbish et al., CSCW 2012](https://dl.acm.org/doi/10.1145/2145204.2145396)), grade A (qualitative); a contributor's track record predicts whether a pull request is accepted ([Tsay et al.](https://herbsleb.org/web-pubs/pdfs/tsay-influence-2013.pdf)), grade A. Driver: reputation earned through visible work.
- Invariant hint: "anyone may offer, the stewards decide, all of it in public".

### Hacker News
- Kind: curated discussion forum · Longevity: ~19 yrs, 2007 · Holon: H4–H5 · Quadrant strongest: WE
- Mechanics:
  1. **Gravity ranking** — score is roughly (votes−1)^0.8 / (age+2)^1.8, so nothing stays on top for long ([HN FAQ](https://news.ycombinator.com/newsfaq.html); [righto](http://www.righto.com/2013/11/how-hacker-news-ranking-really-works.html)). Evidence: mechanism only, grade C.

Evidence: YC reports that making some downvotes stronger reduced toxic comments ([HN 2014](https://news.ycombinator.com/item?id=7605973)), grade C.
- Evidence is weak overall (C). Success rests mainly on 19 years of stable norms around "intellectual curiosity" ([guidelines](https://news.ycombinator.com/newsguidelines.html)).
- Invariant hint: "stewardship handed out in proportion to proven contribution, plus a living human keeper".

### Buy Nothing Project

### Cross-cutting patterns in this class
- **Trust infrastructure between strangers** (Airbnb, BlaBlaCar, Nextdoor, GitHub): identity + verification + peer testimony. The evidence (PNAS, field experiments) is the strongest in this class, and peer ratings outweigh photos or profile text.
- **Small units inside a large whole** (Discord 5–15, Buy Nothing sprouting at ~1,000, Reddit subreddits, Nextdoor 10-household quorum, Peloton "Here Now"). When a unit grows past intimacy (Peloton's 10K leaderboard), the sense of community fades, and products rebuild smallness.
- **A fixed rhythm with a fresh start** (Wordle's daily puzzle, Product Hunt's daily reset, Oura's morning score, Khan's 30 min/week, Day One reminders): a recurring, bounded unit beats endless content.
- **Showing without exposing** (Instagram filters and Stories, Wordle's spoiler-free grid, Airbnb's simultaneous reveal): lowering the social risk of self-disclosure measurably increases how much people share.
- **Grafting onto what already exists** (WeChat red envelopes on an old ritual, Instagram cross-posting to existing networks, Telegram catching people when rivals went down): distribution borrowed from an existing habit or network.
- **Recognition from peers who do the same work** (Wikipedia barnstars +60%, GitHub track record, HN karma-gated powers). The effect concentrates in people who are already committed, and can push away newcomers.

## III.1–III.2 Builders of founders and leaders, guilds of mastery

Scope: 24 sources (Toastmasters skipped, already done). Evidence grades: A = peer-reviewed or official data; B = serious journalism or books; C = the organization's own claim or a fan site. Where the only evidence is an organization's own outcome claim, the grade is C and the selection or survivorship bias is named.

### Y Combinator
- Kind: startup accelerator (cohort + seed capital) · Longevity: 21 years (2005) · Holon: H3 inside H4 (sections of 6–10 companies inside a batch of ~150–250) · Quadrant strongest: ITS
- Mechanics:
  1. **Weekly growth number + office hours on a 1-week loop**: each team picks one metric (revenue or active users), aims for 5–7%/week, and reports to partners at office hours. Evidence: Graham, "during YC we measure growth rate per week… A good growth rate during YC is 5-7% a week" ([PG](https://www.paulgraham.com/growth.html)), grade C. Field study of top accelerators, comparing accepted with "almost accepted" ventures, finds the benefit comes mainly through learning, with signaling ruled out ([Hallen, Cohen & Bingham, Org Sci 2020](https://pubsonline.informs.org/doi/10.1287/orsc.2019.1304)), grade A (programs are anonymized). Driver: a short feedback cycle plus an outside witness turns vague effort into a number you can steer by.
  2. **Fixed public deadline (Demo Day)**: the batch ends in a pitch to investors. Evidence: YC partner Seibel calls Demo Day "a forcing function to the founders" ([TechCrunch](https://techcrunch.com/podcast/ycs-michael-seibel-clarifies-some-misconceptions-about-the-accelerator/)), grade B. YC is the only accelerator in one sample whose startups raised significantly more early funding than matched controls ([Annals of OR 2023](https://link.springer.com/article/10.1007/s10479-023-05583-0)), grade A. Driver: a fixed date plus a public audience makes founders cut scope.
  3. **Small section inside a big batch, then a lifelong alumni network (Bookface)**. Evidence: "sections (6-10 companies)… intimate setting within the larger batch" and alumni "strong commitment to helping one another" ([YC About](https://www.ycombinator.com/about)), grade C. Outcome: 5,000+ companies funded, $1.3T combined valuation ([ycombinator.com](https://www.ycombinator.com/)), grade C.
- Invariant hint: a short metric-feedback loop with a witness, a fixed public deadline, and a small circle nested in a large network.

- Kind: rule-governed residential community · Longevity: ~1,500 years (Rule ~530) · Holon: H3–H4 abbey inside H5 congregation · Quadrant strongest: ITS / WE
- Mechanics:
  1. **Staged, mutual selection**: a guest period, then a 1-year novitiate, 3 years of temporary vows, and solemn profession with the community's consent. Evidence: ([Inauen, Rost, Frey et al., ARPA 2010](https://www.bsfrey.ch/wp-content/uploads/2021/08/monastic-governance-forgotten-prospects-for-public-institution.pdf)), grade A. Driver: self-selection plus long observation filters for fit before full commitment.
  2. **Elected abbot plus convent voice plus an advisory council**. Evidence: at Engelberg (900 years), abbots elected by the convent had fewer agency problems; 79% of poor abbots but only 6% of good ones had a weak prior record; across 151 abbeys the average lifetime is 463 years, only 26.5% closed for internal governance reasons, and after 500 years survival is 50% vs fewer than 10% of US listed firms reaching 83 years (same paper), grade A. Driver: people who live under a leader choose the leader and can correct him.
  3. **Periodic outside "visitation" every 4–5 years**, focused on spirit and relationships as well as the books (same paper), grade A. Driver: supportive outside review catches drift without crowding out intrinsic motivation.
- Invariant hint: a written rule plus a daily rhythm, voice from below, and supportive outside review.

### Alcoholics Anonymous
- Kind: peer mutual-help fellowship · Longevity: 91 years (1935) · Holon: H3 home group, H2 sponsor pair, inside H6 · Quadrant strongest: WE / I
- Mechanics:
  1. **Peer-led meetings with a 12-step path and sponsorship**: someone further along guides a newcomer. Evidence: Cochrane review of 27 studies with 10,565 participants. AA/TSF beats CBT and other treatments on continuous abstinence at 12 months (RR 1.21, high certainty), and the effect holds at 24–36 months; 42% vs 35% abstinent at 1 year ([Cochrane 2020](https://www.cochrane.org/evidence/CD012880_alcoholics-anonymous-aa-and-other-12-step-programs-alcohol-use-disorder); [news](https://www.cochrane.org/about-us/news/new-cochrane-review-finds-alcoholics-anonymous-and-12-step-facilitation-programs-help-people)), grade A. Driver: the effect "is achieved largely by fostering increased AA participation", meaning sustained exposure to peers who have already recovered.
  2. **Service and helping the next person (Step 12)**, plus health-care cost savings. Evidence: AA/TSF "reduced healthcare costs substantially more than other types of treatments" ([Alcohol & Alcoholism 2020](https://academic.oup.com/alcalc/article/55/6/641/5867689)), grade A. The specific helper effect of Step 12 was not separately verified here. Driver: helping others turns a patient into a giver, which builds identity and accountability.
- Invariant hint: people one step ahead guiding people one step behind, inside a circle that meets often.

### Cross-cutting patterns in this class
- **A small circle nested in a large whole**: YC sections of 6–10, EO/YPO forums of 8–10, Vistage groups of 12–16, T-groups of 12, and the AA home group all sit inside networks of thousands. The circle supplies trust and accountability, and the network supplies range and status.
- **Status granted by peers who already walked the path, through a live performance**: the Master Sommelier panel, the compagnon masterpiece, board exams, Masonic degrees and ICF mentor review. Where the gate is external and standardized, the evidence is strongest (medicine, grade A). Internal ratings carried no signal in the 2024 JAMA study.
- **A protocol that limits speech**: EO's "experience only, no advice", Vistage issue processing, HBS's protagonist's chair and cold calls, and T-group "here and now". These rules make honesty safe and preparation unavoidable.
- **A fixed rhythm plus a forcing deadline**: weekly YC metrics and office hours, Demo Day, weekly Rotary meetings, the monthly forum, the Benedictine horarium and AA meetings. Frequency correlates with the effect: in AA, benefit was mediated by participation.
- **Voice from below and built-in renewal**: the elected abbot and visitation (463-year average lifetime), JCI's one-year terms and age 40 cap, AIESEC's youth-only management, and Fulbright's binational boards. Organizations that let members choose and correct leaders, and rotate them, outlive those that do not.
- **Evidence gap**: most peer-forum and credential bodies (YPO, EO, Vistage, ICF, PMI, AIESEC, JCI) offer only self-reported or survivorship-biased numbers (grade C). The grade-A evidence in this class is concentrated in AA, medical boards, Benedictine governance, accelerators (learning channel), cold-calling and Masonic network history.

## III.3–III.4 Science, labs, universities and schools

Sources fetched or read directly are cited inline. Grades: A = peer-reviewed / official data; B = serious journalism, books, institution's own historical record; C = company/self claim, no independent check.

---

### Peer review (Royal Society, journals)
- Kind: knowledge-validation institution · Longevity: ~270 yrs (editing by committee at Phil. Trans. formalized 1752; expert referees 1832; universal by the 1960s–70s) · Holon: H6 civilization (run as H5 network of journals) · Quadrant strongest: WE
- Mechanics:
  1. **Judgment by equals before publication** — a paper reaches the record only after anonymous experts in the same field critique it. Evidence: 91% (2009, n=4,037) and 90% (2019, n=3,133) of researchers say peer review improves the quality of published research; 84% say without it "there would be no control in scientific communication" ([Sense about Science 2019](https://senseaboutscience.org/wp-content/uploads/2019/09/Quality-trust-peer-review.pdf); [2009 survey](https://senseaboutscience.org/activities/peer-review-survey-2009)), grade A/B (large surveys of practitioners, not outcome trials; whether it improves *validity* is contested). Driver: your blind spots are visible only to someone who walked the same path; their critique is credible because they are peers, not bosses.
  2. **Unpaid service as membership** — refereeing is done for free as a duty of belonging. Evidence: 90% of reviewers say they review "because they believe they are playing an active role in the community"; only 16% to improve their own acceptance chances ([Sense about Science 2009](https://senseaboutscience.org/activities/peer-review-survey-2009)), grade B. Driver: contribution is the price and the proof of membership in the guild.
- Invariant hint: status granted by peers who walked the path; reciprocity as the membership fee.

### arXiv
- Kind: open preprint commons · Longevity: 35 yrs (Aug 1991, Ginsparg, LANL → Cornell → independent nonprofit 2026) · Holon: H6 · Quadrant strongest: ITS
- Mechanics:
  1. **Publish first, judge later (moderated, not peer-reviewed)** — anyone in the field posts instantly and free; moderators only check topicality and scholarly form. Evidence: 3,258,222 submissions as of Nov 2026; monthly records keep breaking (30,045 in Mar 2026 → 32,040 in Jun 2026) ([arXiv stats](https://arxiv.org/stats/monthly_submissions); [arXiv blog](https://blog.arxiv.org/2026/07/09/arxiv-now-hosts-over-3-million-articles)), grade A. Driver: priority (the timestamp) is the scientist's core reward, and arXiv grants it within a day at zero cost.
  2. **Endorsement by an existing member** — since 2004 a newcomer must be vouched for by an established arXiv author to post in many categories ([Wikipedia summary of arXiv policy](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/ArXiv)), grade B (policy fact; no causal study of its effect). Driver: a light gate of personal vouching keeps the commons clean without a heavy editorial apparatus.
- Invariant hint: a fast, free, timestamped shared record + a light human gate at the door.

### CERN collaborations (ATLAS, CMS) 
- Kind: mega-collaboration / federated research organization · Longevity: CERN 1954; ATLAS collaboration since 1992 (~34 yrs) · Holon: H5 network of villages (200+ institutes, 40 countries, ~6,000 members) · Quadrant strongest: WE
- Mechanics:
  1. **One institute, one vote** — the Collaboration Board, where each institution has exactly one vote regardless of size, is the policy body; leadership (spokesperson) is elected; issues are debated openly in plenary before the vote ([ATLAS: Join](https://atlas.cern/Discover/Collaboration/Join); [ATLAS M&O MoU 2002](http://atlas.kek.jp/old/sub/ATLAS-RRB/M&OMoU.pdf)), grade A (official). Org-science analysis: the spokesperson "had no formal authority" and steered by consultation; 2/3 majority votes ([Organization Science 2014](https://doi.org/10.1287/orsc.2013.0894)), grade A. Driver: small members accept the whole because they cannot be outvoted by size; legitimacy without hierarchy.
  2. **Everyone signs, alphabetically** — every paper lists all ~3,000 qualified authors in alphabetical order; output "shared by all authors and subject to rigorous review" ([ATLAS: How ATLAS works](https://atlas.cern/Discover/Collaboration/How-ATLAS-Works)), grade A; intent "to render the individual subservient to the collaboration … and ensure everyone is motivated to contribute" ([Birnholtz 2008, J. Electronic Publishing](https://doi.org/10.3998/3336451.0011.105)), grade A (note: same paper argues individuals then seek credit through informal channels). Driver: removes first-author competition inside the group; credit flows to the shared work.
  3. **Non-binding MoU of contributions** — each institute commits deliverables "based on best effort", funding agencies oversee their own money ([CERN governance slides 2024](https://indico.cern.ch/event/1420449/contributions/5979730/attachments/2866650/5017718/CERNLHCExperimentsGovernanceMay2024.pdf)), grade A. Driver: commitment by public pledge to peers, not by contract enforcement.
- Invariant hint: a federation of equals bound by public pledges; collective credit over individual credit.

### Human Genome Project — Bermuda Principles
- Kind: international public science consortium / data-sharing charter · Longevity: rule adopted Feb 1996, still the template for genomic data policy (~30 yrs) · Holon: H5 · Quadrant strongest: ITS
- Mechanics:
  1. **Release everything within 24 hours** — sequence assemblies >1 kb deposited in public databases daily; finished data free "for both research and development"; in exchange, centres kept the right to be first to publish full analyses ([Nature milestone](https://www.nature.com/articles/d42859-020-00101-9); [J. Hist. Biol. 2018](https://link.springer.com/article/10.1007/s10739-018-9538-7)). Evidence of effect: genes held under Celera's private IP got 20–30% less follow-on research and product development than public genes (1.2 vs 2.1 publications per gene; 3% vs 5.4% used in a diagnostic test) ([Williams 2013, J. Political Economy](https://www.journals.uchicago.edu/doi/10.1086/669706); [NBER w21246](https://www.nber.org/system/files/working_papers/w21246/w21246.pdf)), grade A. Driver: a rule that makes hoarding impossible turns a race between labs into a shared base everyone builds on.
  2. **Priority-of-analysis traded for openness** — openness was made acceptable by guaranteeing the producers the first scientific story. Same sources, grade A. Driver: people give away raw output when their name stays attached to the meaning.
- Invariant hint: commons by rule, with the contributor's name protected.

### Gordon Research Conferences
- Kind: recurring closed scientific gathering · Longevity: 95 yrs (1931) · Holon: H4 village (~100–135 people, a remote place, 5 days) · Quadrant strongest: WE
- Mechanics:
  1. **Off-the-record rule** — no proceedings, no recording, no citing the conference; quoting requires the speaker's written consent; formally announced in the 1937 program ([GRC policies](https://www.grc.org/about/grc-policies-and-legal-disclaimers/); [GRC history](https://www.frontiersofscience.org/history.aspx?page=2)). Evidence: growth from 1 conference to 395+/yr and ~40,000 participants/yr, which GRC attributes to "adherence to the principles of free and open discussion" ([History of GRC](https://www.grc.org/about/history-of-grc/)), grade B (institution's own account; no controlled study). Science 1965: the ban on attributed publication "contributed largely to their effectiveness" ([Science 148:583](https://www.science.org/doi/pdf/10.1126/science.148.3670.583)), grade B. Driver: unpublished, half-formed ideas get shared only where they cannot be scooped or quoted.
  2. **Fixed small format in an isolated place** — capped attendance (60 → ~135), mornings and evenings for talks, afternoons free, shared meals; each meeting is surveyed and a conference is at risk of cancellation if survey return <80% or attendance declines; attendees elect the next vice-chair, who rotates to chair ([GRC history](https://www.frontiersofscience.org/history.aspx?page=2)), grade B. Driver: enforced idleness + shared meals = informal ties; rotating chairs prevent stagnation.
- Invariant hint: a safe container (what's said here stays here) on a fixed rhythm, in a place apart.

### Santa Fe Institute
- Kind: independent research institute (complexity science) · Longevity: 42 yrs (1984) · Holon: H4 (~30 resident researchers) inside an H5 network (110 external professors from 86 institutions, 1,500+ visitors/yr) · Quadrant strongest: WE
- Mechanics:
  1. **No departments, no tenure** — resident scholars get 3-year appointments renewable once; no permanent programs ([Science 2004](https://www.science.org/doi/10.1126/science.306.5693.33a); [SFI on Medium](https://medium.com/sfi-30-foundations-frontiers/computation-information-adaptation-and-evolution-in-silico-f8098d3ab13f)), grade A/B. Evidence of output: originated or shaped network science, scaling theory, agent-based modeling, genetic algorithms ([SFI overview](https://www.santafe.edu/about/overview)), grade C (self-description); a 1997 Business Week poll of researchers placed tiny SFI tied-5th among labs they'd choose, 1st for bio-inspired computation ([SFI Bulletin 2014](https://sfi-edu.s3.amazonaws.com/sfi-edu/production/uploads/publication/2016/10/31/Bulletin_Fall_2014_FINAL.pdf)), grade B/C. Driver: forced turnover keeps "new blood" and stops fiefdoms.
  2. **Voluntary problem-groups across fields** — "research is done by voluntary associations of people… it's not necessary that they all know the subject" (Gell-Mann, [Science News 2009](https://www.sciencenews.org/article/place-removed-pressure-received-ideas)), grade B. Driver: people self-select onto problems they find alive.
- Invariant hint: small permanent core, large rotating periphery; people over programs.

### Max Planck Society — Harnack principle
- Kind: national basic-research society · Longevity: 115 yrs (KWG 1911 → MPG 1948) · Holon: H5 (84 institutes, each an H4 built around directors) · Quadrant strongest: I (the chosen person) + ITS (resources)
- Mechanics:
  1. **Build the institute around the person** — institutes are "built up solely around the world's leading researchers"; they define their own subjects, choose their staff, get "the best working conditions"; renewal by new appointments and evaluation ([MPG portrait](https://www.mpg.de/short-portrait); [MPG annual report 2024](https://www.mpg.de/24875956/mpg-jahresbericht-2024-lagebericht-geschaeftsjahr-2024.pdf)). Evidence: 31 Nobel laureates (incl. KWG), six consecutive prizes 2020–2023; top-5 worldwide in Nature Index for years ([MPG Nobel](https://www.mpg.de/23542008/max-planck-a-powerhouse-for-nobel-prizes); [MPL](https://mpl.mpg.de/about-us/about-the-max-planck-society)), grade A for counts; attribution to the principle is the MPG's own (grade B/C).
  2. **Stable block funding, no grant chasing** — equally funded by federal and state governments, €2.2B budget (2026) ([MPG profile](https://www.mpg.de/11761628/profile-visions)), grade A. Driver: long time horizon + autonomy for those already proven = risk-taking.
- Invariant hint: trust concentrated in proven people, refreshed by appointment rather than by permanent structures.

### MIT Media Lab
- Kind: university research lab with corporate-member model · Longevity: 41 yrs (1985) · Holon: H4 (~25–30 research groups, ~800 in extended community) · Quadrant strongest: IT (the built demo) / ITS (funding model)
- Mechanics:
  1. **Demo or die** — "make the case for your idea with an unfaked performance of it working at least once"; "we don't write unless we've done it" (Negroponte in [Brand, The Media Lab, 1987](https://monoskop.org/images/4/47/Brand_Stewart_The_Media_Lab.pdf)); "the demo is the atomic unit of the Lab" (Ito). Evidence: constant demos are "how the Media Lab survives" with sponsors ([NYT 2000](https://www.nytimes.com/2000/11/09/technology/mit-media-lab-at-15-big-ideas-big-money.html)), grade B. Driver: a working artifact is undeniable proof and a public status signal; it shortens the idea→feedback loop.
  2. **Pooled consortium sponsorship** — 80+ (once 170) companies pay into one lab-wide fund; no sponsor directs research; every member gets royalty-free rights to all IP from its sponsorship period ([Media Lab sponsorship](https://media.mit.edu/sponsorship/funding-levels.html); [Ito, Practice of Change ch.4](https://www.practiceofchange.org/pub/ch4/release/1); [Chronicle 2001](https://www.chronicle.com/article/mits-media-lab-a-media-darling-seeks-global-role-and-new-missions-25727/)). Evidence: sustained ~$38–75M/yr budget, >90% from sponsors in 2001; 100+ spin-off startups (self-reported, [Getting Value](https://media.mit.edu/sponsorship/getting-value.html)), grade B/C. Driver: researchers get freedom; sponsors get "peripheral vision" and a club.
  3. **Antidisciplinary** — research groups with "almost full license" across fields ([Ito ch.4](https://www.practiceofchange.org/pub/ch4/release/1)), grade C (no outcome data). Pentland's badge data: at the Media Lab, oscillation between exploration and engagement "accounted for almost half of the differences in creative output of research groups" ([Pentland, HBR 2012](https://pdf.stafforini.com/pentland-2012-new-science-building.pdf)), grade B.
- Invariant hint: proof by showing, not telling; many patrons, no single owner.

### MIT Human Dynamics Lab (Sandy Pentland) — social physics / sociometric badges
- Kind: research program on how teams work · Longevity: ~2000s–present; badges deployed in 21 orgs, ~2,500 people over 7 yrs by 2012 · Holon: H3 circle · Quadrant strongest: WE (measured as ITS)
- Mechanics (findings, i.e. mechanics that make teams work):
  1. **Energy + engagement: everyone talks to everyone, face to face** — patterns of communication were "the most important predictor of a team's success… as significant as all the other factors—intelligence, personality, skill—combined"; 35% of variation in team performance explained by the number of face-to-face exchanges; good teams: equal turn-taking, short contributions, members talk directly to each other, not only via the leader ([Pentland, HBR Apr 2012](https://pdf.stafforini.com/pentland-2012-new-science-building.pdf)), grade B (HBR, author's own data; underlying badge papers are peer-reviewed, e.g. [Kim et al. J. Org. Behavior 2012](https://doi.org/10.1002/job.1776)).
  2. **Shared breaks** — bank call center: making each team take its coffee break together cut average handling time 8% overall, >20% for weakest teams; forecast $15M/yr at 10 centres ([same HBR](https://pdf.stafforini.com/pentland-2012-new-science-building.pdf)); Bank of America: 15–20% productivity, turnover 40%→12% ([MIT News 2014](https://news.mit.edu/2014/behavioral-analytics-moneyball-for-business-1114)), grade B. Driver: informal time spreads tacit knowledge and evens out who-talks-to-whom.
  3. **Exploration ↔ engagement oscillation** — creative teams alternate between bringing ideas in from outside and integrating them inside; distinguished high- vs low-creativity teams with ~90% accuracy in one industrial lab; on eToro, traders with balanced idea flow earned ~30% more than isolated ones ([HBR 2012](https://pdf.stafforini.com/pentland-2012-new-science-building.pdf); [NYT Bits 2014](https://archive.nytimes.com/bits.blogs.nytimes.com/2014/04/15/m-i-t-s-alex-pentland-measuring-idea-flows-to-accelerate-innovation/)), grade B. Plus: showing teams a daily visual map of their own interaction pattern shifted behavior within a week (same HBR), grade B.
- Invariant hint: how a group talks matters more than who is in it; a mirror of the group's own pattern changes the pattern.

### UN Global Pulse
- Kind: innovation lab of the UN Secretary-General · Longevity: 17 yrs (2009; Jakarta 2012, Kampala 2014/15) · Holon: H5 · Quadrant strongest: ITS
- Mechanics:
  1. **Embedded labs co-run with host government** — Pulse Lab Jakarta, a partnership with Indonesia's planning ministry (Bappenas), "ran more than 100 projects and forged more than 50 partnerships"; now an Asia-Pacific hub ([Global Pulse 2023 report](https://mptf.undp.org/sites/default/files/documents/2024-05/2023_narrative_report_global_pulse.pdf); [Pulse Lab Jakarta](http://pulselabjakarta.org/)), grade C (self-reported; no independent impact evaluation found).
  2. **Data philanthropy** — persuading private holders of big data (telcos, Twitter) to share it for public good ([Global Pulse/IMF 2014](https://www.imf.org/external/NP/seminars/eng/2014/statsforum/pdf/robert.pdf)), grade C. Own admission (2018): "too few examples of compelling operational projects" ([UN Chronicle](https://www.un.org/en/un-chronicle/decade-leveraging-big-data-sustainable-development)), grade B. Driver: local co-ownership makes outputs usable; evidence of durable effect is thin.
- Invariant hint: a small lab grafted onto a host institution survives by co-ownership; weakest evidence in this class.

### Bell Labs
- Kind: corporate research lab · Longevity: 1925–~1990s golden era (~65 yrs; survives as Nokia Bell Labs) · Holon: H4–H5 (peak ~15,000 staff, ~1,200 PhDs) · Quadrant strongest: ITS (building, funding) → WE
- Mechanics:
  1. **Architecture for collisions** — Murray Hill: labs and offices on different corridors so people had to walk between them; a ~700-ft corridor where "traveling its length without encountering… ideas would be almost impossible"; theorists and experimentalists mixed (Bardeen's office in Brattain's lab when the transistor emerged) ([Gertner, The Idea Factory](https://www.physics.rutgers.edu/~friedan/physics_readings/Gertner,%20Jon/Gertner,%20John,%202012,%20%22The%20Idea%20Factory%20-%20Bell%20Labs%20and%20the%20Great%20Age%20of%20American%20Innovation%22.pdf); [Physics Today review](https://physicstoday.aip.org/reviews/the-idea-factory-bell-labs-and-the-great-age-of-american-innovation)), grade B.
  2. **"Organized creative technology": unscheduled research tied to systems engineering** — Kelly (Royal Society 1950) split work into a non-scheduled research area and scheduled development, with systems engineers carrying ideas to manufacture ([Kelly 1950, Proc. R. Soc. A](https://royalsocietypublishing.org/rspa/article/203/1074/287/8325/The-Bell-Telephone-Laboratories-an-example-of-an); [NAS memoir](https://www.nasonline.org/wp-content/uploads/2024/06/kelly-mervin.pdf)). Evidence: 9 Nobel prizes, 4 Turing awards; transistor, laser, CCD, solar cell, information theory, UNIX, C ([Nature Rev. Physics 2022](https://pmc.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/articles/PMC8792522)), grade A for outputs, B for attribution. Driver: stable monopoly funding + freedom from deadlines for researchers + a pipe to real use.
- Invariant hint: design the space so that the right strangers meet daily; protect long time horizons.

### Xerox PARC
- Kind: corporate research lab · Longevity: 1970–2023 (golden decade 1970s) · Holon: H4 (~350 people in mid-1980s) · Quadrant strongest: WE
- Mechanics:
  1. **Hire the best, give a broad tangible goal, no directives** — mission "the office of the future"; researchers "unburdened by directives, instructions, or deadlines" ([Hiltzik, Dealers of Lightning](https://softouch.on.ca/kb/data/Dealers%20of%20Lightning.pdf); [LA Times 1995](https://www.latimes.com/archives/la-xpm-1995-09-13-fi-45446-story.html)). Evidence: Alto (first networked PC with GUI), Ethernet (1973), laser printer (EARS 1973) ([IEEE Spectrum](https://spectrum.ieee.org/xerox-alto)); laser printing "paid for PARC basically all of its life" (Chuck Thacker, [talk 2013](https://www.youtube.com/watch?v=pSC4E5gwGaA)), grade B.
  2. **"Dealer": one obligatory weekly meeting** — Tuesday noon, guest speaker or a researcher defending work before peers; Taylor's rule to move "Class One" disagreements (neither side can state the other's view) to "Class Two" (each can) ([Metcalfe interview](https://www.solarwinds.com/blog/conversation-robert-metcalfe-part1-techpod040); [LA Times 2017 on Taylor](https://www.latimes.com/business/hiltzik/la-fi-hiltzik-taylor-appreciation-20170417-story.html)), grade B. Driver: one fixed ritual of peer challenge + a manager who shields the team upward.
  3. **Build-and-use your own tools** — researchers used the Altos, Ethernet and printers daily; "once researchers tried the Alto, they wanted it" ([IEEE Spectrum](https://spectrum.ieee.org/xerox-alto)), grade B. Driver: living in the future you're building gives instant, honest feedback.
- Invariant hint: a fixed ritual of argument among equals + a protector at the boundary. (Counter-lesson: the parent failed to absorb it — no bridge to the core business.)

### Lockheed Skunk Works — Kelly Johnson's 14 rules
- Kind: autonomous advanced-development unit inside a corporation · Longevity: 83 yrs (1943) · Holon: H3–H4 (XP-80: 23 engineers + 103 shop mechanics) · Quadrant strongest: ITS
- Mechanics:
  1. **Small, autonomous team with one accountable lead** — rule 1: manager gets "practically complete control"; rule 3: restrict people "in an almost vicious manner… 10–25% compared to normal systems" ([Kelly's 14 Rules, Lockheed archive](https://web.archive.org/web/20090227060019/http:/www.lockheedmartin.com/aeronautics/skunkworks/14rules.html)). Evidence: XP-80 prototype in 143 days vs 150 promised ([Lockheed Martin](https://www.lockheedmartin.com/en-us/news/features/history/skunk-works.html)); U-2 flew ~9 months after contract ([Lockheed: Kelly Johnson](https://www.lockheedmartin.com/en-us/news/features/history/johnson.html)); 8 of 20 Collier trophies to Lockheed Martin ([Osprey 75 yrs](https://api.pageplace.de/preview/DT0400.9781472846457_A41373135/preview-9781472846457_A41373135.pdf)), grade B/C (company sources, widely corroborated).
  2. **Minimum reporting, maximum recording; monthly honest cost review** — rules 5–6: few reports, but important work recorded thoroughly; projected costs to completion every month, "don't surprise the customer" (same source), grade C (rule text; effect attributed by the company). Driver: trust replaces paperwork; frequent truthful numbers replace oversight.
  3. **Reward by contribution, not headcount** — rule 14: pay "not based on the number of personnel supervised" (same source), grade C. Driver: removes the incentive to grow empires.
- Invariant hint: a protected small cell with clear intent, freed from the parent's procedures but bound to radical transparency.

### Stanford Behavior Design Lab (BJ Fogg) — B=MAP, Tiny Habits
- Kind: behavior-change method / lab · Longevity: model published 2009, Tiny Habits since ~2011 (~15 yrs) · Holon: H1 person · Quadrant strongest: I
- Mechanics:
  1. **B = MAP** — behavior happens only when Motivation, Ability and a Prompt converge at one moment; if it doesn't, one is missing ([behaviormodel.org](https://www.behaviormodel.org/); [Stanford BDL](https://behaviordesign.stanford.edu/resources/fogg-behavior-model)). Evidence: a 2025 scoping review found 6 interventions built on the model, all with significant improvements (e.g., HPV vaccination intention 63%→97%) ([BMC Public Health 2025](https://link.springer.com/article/10.1186/s12889-025-24525-y)), grade A but small/heterogeneous.
  2. **Tiny + anchor + celebrate** — shrink the behavior until it's trivially easy, attach it to an existing routine ("after I…"), celebrate immediately. Evidence: RCT, n=154, 5-day Tiny Habits program raised gratitude scores (Δ +7.0 vs +2.9 control, p=.002) ([Frontiers Public Health 2022](https://www.frontiersin.org/journals/public-health/articles/10.3389/fpubh.2022.866992/full)), grade A (small, with co-authors from the Tiny Habits network); a meta-analysis of "small change" weight-loss approaches found no evidence of effect ([Obesity Reviews 2021](https://pubmed.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/34580993/)). Overall: grade B — no large independent trial of Tiny Habits itself. Driver: ability is easier to raise than motivation; a stable cue builds automaticity.
- Invariant hint: make the first step absurdly small and tie it to a ritual already in place.

### Google X (X, the moonshot factory)
- Kind: corporate moonshot incubator · Longevity: 16 yrs (2010) · Holon: H4 · Quadrant strongest: WE (norms) + I (courage)
- Mechanics:
  1. **Rewarded killing** — teams that end their own project get applause, a spot bonus, promotion; "we've bonused every single person in teams that ended their projects, from 2 to 30+"; 100+ investigations killed by the teams themselves in one year ([Teller, Wired 2016](https://www.wired.com/2016/02/the-secret-to-moonshots-killing-our-projects)); Foghorn team bonused for arguing its own shutdown ([The Atlantic 2017](https://www.theatlantic.com/magazine/archive/2017/11/x-google-moonshot-factory/540648/)), grade B.
  2. **Run at the hardest part first (Rapid Eval)** — weeks and a few thousand dollars to find each idea's fatal flaw; ~97% of Rapid Eval ideas killed ([X operating manual](https://x.company/blog/posts/a-peek-inside-the-moonshot-factory-operating-manual); [Felten, X blog](https://blog.x.company/how-to-kill-good-things-to-make-room-for-truly-great-ones-867fb6ef026)). Evidence: graduates Waymo, Verily, Wing, Google Brain ([X: Waymo graduation](https://blog.x.company/graduation-day-self-driving-cars-d86edd549aad)); Teller: ~2% of ideas reach exit, but 44% of spend goes to graduates ([TechCrunch 2025](https://techcrunch.com/2025/10/27/ceo-of-alphabets-x-astro-teller-on-what-makes-a-moonshot/)), grade B/C (company leader's figures). Driver: make honesty the path of least resistance — status flows to whoever kills a weak idea fastest.
- Invariant hint: celebrate the honest ending; status for truth, not for persistence.

### MIT (mens et manus, UROP, IAP, hacks)
- Kind: research university and its traditions · Longevity: 165 yrs (1861); UROP 57 yrs (1969); IAP 55 yrs (1971) · Holon: H4–H5 · Quadrant strongest: WE
- Mechanics:
  1. **UROP: undergraduates as real members of research teams** — any year, any major, for pay or credit. Evidence: 93% of the class of 2025 did at least one; ~6,000 projects/yr; 58% of professors mentor; widely copied (Caltech 1979, Michigan 1988) ([UROP](https://urop.mit.edu/about); [UROP strategic plan 2025](https://urop.mit.edu/wp-content/uploads/2025/10/MIT_UROP_strategic-plan-2025.pdf)), grade A (official). Driver: belonging comes from doing real work alongside masters early ("mind and hand").
  2. **IAP: one month a year with no regular classes** — January term where anyone (students, staff, alumni) organizes or joins any activity, "playful spirit and fusion of fun and learning" ([MIT IAP](https://web.mit.edu/iap)), grade C (persistence 55 yrs is the evidence; no outcome study). Driver: a recurring sanctioned window for self-directed exploration.
  3. **Hacks with an ethic** — anonymous, benign, technically sophisticated pranks (police car on the Great Dome, 1994); informal code: safe, no damage, funny; "a pact… keep it anonymous, harmless and fun and MIT will look the other way" ([IHTFP essay](https://hacks.mit.edu/Hacks/articles/where_the_sun_shines.html); [MIT Mind and Hand Book](https://handbook.mit.edu/hacking)), grade B (tradition since ~1870s documented by the Institute historian). Driver: anonymous craft for shared joy builds identity and releases pressure without personal fame.
- Invariant hint: apprenticeship inside the real work + a licensed space for play.

### Oxford / Cambridge — collegiate system and tutorials (supervisions)
- Kind: collegiate university · Longevity: ~800 yrs; tutorial in its modern form since the 1880s · Holon: H2 (tutor + 1–3 students) inside H4 (college, a few hundred, a place) inside H5 (university of colleges) · Quadrant strongest: I (thinking) + WE (college)
- Mechanics:
  1. **Weekly essay defended to a tutor** — student writes a 2,000–2,500-word essay or problem set (~13–25 hrs prep), reads/defends it to a tutor with 1–3 peers, gets immediate critique ([Oxford: tutorials](https://www.ox.ac.uk/admissions/undergraduate/courses/learning-at-oxford/personalised-learning); [GJES 2013](https://doi.org/10.5296/gjes.v6i2.17400); [The Conversation 2015](https://theconversation.com/are-oxbridge-tutorials-still-the-best-way-to-teach-students-how-to-think-44250)). Evidence: 59% of Oxbridge students "very satisfied" with course vs 31% at other Russell Group universities; 77% vs 46% see good value ([HEPI 2018](https://www.hepi.ac.uk/wp-content/uploads/2018/05/HEPI-How-different-is-Oxbridge_Report-107-FINAL.pdf)), grade A/B; but studies show students' experience varies widely and critical-thinking gains depend on the tutor ([Ashwin 2005](https://doi.org/10.1007/s10734-004-6369-6); [Cosgrove 2011](https://doi.org/10.1080/07294360.2010.487259)), grade A. Driver: weekly public accountability to a master, in a group small enough that no one can hide.
  2. **College as the home community** — students live, eat and get pastoral care in one of ~31–39 small interdisciplinary colleges; Cambridge credits the college tutorial and Director of Studies system for its 98.7% retention (TEF "++" flag) ([Cambridge TEF 2017](https://www.admin.cam.ac.uk/reporter/2016-17/weekly/6471/Cambridge-TEF-submission-2017.pdf)), grade A for the number, B for attribution (selectivity confounds).
- Invariant hint: small group inside a big whole; a weekly rhythm of making something and defending it face to face.

### Montessori
- Kind: school method · Longevity: 119 yrs (1907) · Holon: H3 (mixed-age class, 3-year span) · Quadrant strongest: I
- Mechanics:
  1. **Self-chosen work with designed materials, long uninterrupted work periods, mixed-age class** — Evidence: lottery-based longitudinal study (n=141, Hartford public magnet schools): Montessori children "fared better on measures of academic achievement, social understanding, and mastery orientation" and liked school tasks more; outcomes were also equalized — lower-income Montessori children did much better than lower-income controls ([Lillard et al. 2017, Frontiers in Psychology](https://www.frontiersin.org/journals/psychology/articles/10.3389/fpsyg.2017.01783/full)); earlier lottery study in Science found better reading/math, executive function, social reasoning at age 5 ([Lillard & Else-Quest 2006](https://www.science.org/doi/10.1126/science.1132362)), grade A. Caveat: lottery samples are families who chose Montessori; a French RCT and a national US lottery study show more mixed results ([Courtier et al. 2021](https://academic.oup.com/chidev/article/92/5/2069/8254759); [IES](https://ies.ed.gov/use-work/awards/longitudinal-efficacy-study-montessori-preschool-model-academic-and-social-emotional-outcomes)). Driver: autonomy + concrete mastery loops + older children teaching younger ones.
- Invariant hint: free choice inside a prepared environment; learning by mastering tangible tasks at one's own pace.

### Minerva University
- Kind: university (seminar-only, global rotation) · Longevity: 12 yrs (2014) · Holon: H3 (small online seminar) inside H4 (cohort living together in 7 cities) · Quadrant strongest: I / IT
- Mechanics:
  1. **Fully active learning** — lectures and readings moved outside class; every class is a small live seminar where every student must work (polls, debates, breakouts) on the Forum platform, with data-driven feedback ([Minerva curriculum](https://www.minerva.edu/public/media/enrollment-center/Minerva-21st-Century-Curriculum.pdf)).
  2. **~100 named "habits of mind and foundational concepts" practiced across all courses** — each assessed with rubrics over four years ([Minerva Student Achievement 2017](https://www.minerva.edu/public/media/enrollment-center/Minerva-Student-Achievement-2017.pdf)). Evidence for 1+2: on CLA+, the founding class rose from 80th to 98th senior percentile (performance task) in one first year; class of 2020 total score 78th → 99th senior percentile; first-year retention 89–97% (same PDF), grade C (self-reported, no control group; selection effect noted by critics — [Econlib 2019](https://www.econlib.org/library/Columns/y2019/Klingminerva.html)). Driver: no passive seat; explicit, named skills practiced everywhere transfer better than implicit ones (consistent with CAE's finding that explicit critical-thinking instruction raises CLA+ scores, [CAE 2026](https://cae.org/wp-content/uploads/2026/07/efficacy-of-critical-thinking-program-2026.pdf), grade A/B).
- Invariant hint: name the skill, practice it everywhere, never let anyone be a spectator.

### Waldorf (Steiner) education
- Kind: school method · Longevity: 107 yrs (1919) · Holon: H3 (class that stays together; class teacher for ~8 years) · Quadrant strongest: WE
- Mechanics:
  1. **One class teacher accompanies the same class for up to 8 years** — plus a daily ~2-hour "main lesson" block on one subject for 3–4 weeks ([Randoll & Peters 2015](https://www.scielo.br/j/er/a/8nyN7QDpx6JYdh4VvYsPBHN/?lang=en)). Evidence: 74% of former Waldorf students endorse the 8-year class-teacher period, but only 38.8% liked having so many subjects with one teacher (same source), grade A/B.
  2. **Arts and rhythm woven into all subjects** — Evidence (whole model, mechanic not isolated): 2026 meta-analysis, 30 studies, 7,823 Waldorf vs 187,015 mainstream students: Waldorf students better overall (d=.22; .37 after trim-and-fill), significantly better school/classroom climate and academic achievement; creativity and moral development differences not significant ([Suggate et al. 2026, Review of Educational Research](https://journals.sagepub.com/doi/full/10.3102/00346543261495960)), grade A; a 2026 systematic review finds academic results inconsistent once SES is controlled ([Acta Psychologica 2026](https://doi.org/10.1016/j.actpsy.2026.108128)). Driver: a long stable relationship with one adult builds belonging; climate is the most robust gain.
- Invariant hint: continuity of one guide with one small group over years; a daily rhythm.

### Cross-cutting patterns in this class
- **Judgment by peers who walked the path.** Peer review, arXiv endorsement, CERN's equal-vote board, GRC's real-time critique, PARC's Dealer, the Oxbridge tutorial: status and validity come from equals or masters in the same craft, not from managers.
- **Protected container + fixed rhythm.** GRC (off-record week, afternoons free), PARC (Tuesday Dealer), Oxbridge (weekly essay), MIT IAP (every January), Waldorf main lesson, Pentland's shared coffee break: a recurring, bounded slot where it is safe to show unfinished work.
- **Small cell inside a big whole.** Skunk Works team in Lockheed, colleges in Oxbridge, institutes in Max Planck, research groups in Media Lab, analysis groups in ATLAS, SFI core + external network: autonomy at H3–H4, resources and identity at H5.
- **Credit and commons are engineered, not assumed.** Bermuda's 24-hour release with protected first-analysis, CERN's alphabetical all-author lists, Media Lab's pooled IP: openness holds when the rule protects the contributor's name.
- **Make honesty and proof the path of least resistance.** Demo or die, X's bonus for killing projects, Skunk Works' monthly projected-cost truth, Pentland's mirror maps: the system rewards showing what is real (a working thing, a fatal flaw, the true number).
- **Collisions are designed.** Bell Labs corridors, Pentland's long lunch tables and shared breaks, SFI's visitors, Montessori mixed ages: who meets whom is set by space and schedule, and it predicts output better than individual talent does.

## III.5–III.6 Military and intelligence (open sources), global institutions and gatherings

Scope: open sources only. Military and intelligence entries are described as learning, team and decision mechanics, not as combat. Longevity counted to 2026.

### Mission command (Auftragstaktik, commander's intent)
- Kind: command/coordination doctrine · Longevity: ~157 yrs (Moltke's 1869 instructions; Prussian reforms after 1806) · Holon: H3–H5 (team up to army) · Quadrant strongest: WE
- Mechanics:
  1. **Intent over instructions** — orders state the purpose and end state, and leave the "how" to whoever is on the spot. Evidence: codified in Prussian/German doctrine from 1869 to *Truppenführung* 1933. Adopted as the US Army's formal approach to command (ADP 6-0), which names Auftragstaktik as its root ([ADP 6-0](https://armypubs.army.mil/epubs/DR_pubs/DR_a/ARN34403-ADP_6-0-000-WEB-3.pdf); [Army University Press](https://www.armyupress.army.mil/Journals/Military-Review/Online-Exclusive/2016-Online-Exclusive-Articles/German-Mission-Orders)). Grade A for adoption and longevity, B for causal success: historians credit it in 1870 and 1940, and also blame it for costly unilateral moves in 1914 ([Military Review 2022](https://www.armyupress.army.mil/Journals/Military-Review/English-Edition-Archives/July-August-2022/Herrera/)). Driver: autonomy plus a shared "why" lets people adapt faster than a central plan can.
  2. **Mutual trust as a precondition** — the doctrine treats trust between levels as a working requirement, not a nice-to-have. Evidence: Army analysis of the 1914 Marne breach links the failure to distrust between peer commanders ([Military Review 2022](https://www.armyupress.army.mil/Journals/Military-Review/English-Edition-Archives/March-April-2022/Jaksha)), grade B. Driver: people delegate only where they can predict how others will judge.
- Invariant hint: "share the purpose, delegate the method", with trust as the currency that lets delegation work.

### US Army After-Action Review (AAR)
- Kind: learning ritual · Longevity: ~51 yrs (formalised c.1975; "performance critique" early 1970s) · Holon: H3 · Quadrant strongest: WE
- Mechanics:
  1. **Structured, non-punitive debrief right after the event** — the participants themselves answer what was planned, what happened, why, and what to sustain or improve, with a facilitator. Evidence: Tannenbaum & Cerasoli meta-analysis (46 samples, N=2,136): debriefs improve effectiveness ~25% (d=.67) ([SAGE/Human Factors 2013](https://journals.sagepub.com/doi/10.1177/0018720812448394)). Keiser & Arthur (61 studies): d=0.79, with higher-structure AARs stronger in military settings ([JAP 2021](https://doi.org/10.1037/apl0000821)). Grade A. Driver: people find the lesson themselves (ownership), and the feedback arrives while memory is fresh.
  2. **Doctrine-backed standard format** — TC 7-0.1 makes it a guided analysis against standards, held during and after events ([TC 7-0.1, 2025](https://armypubs.army.mil/epubs/DR_pubs/DR_a/ARN42934-TC_7-0.1-000-WEB-1.pdf)), grade A. Driver: a fixed script lowers the social cost of naming mistakes.
- Invariant hint: "a ritual on a fixed rhythm that turns experience into shared learning".

### Sandhurst "Serve to Lead"
- Kind: leadership formation academy · Longevity: 224 yrs (since 1802; 44-week course since 1992) · Holon: H4 (resident cohort) · Quadrant strongest: I / WE
- Mechanics:
  1. **A compressed motto as a moral frame** — "Serve to Lead" is on the cap badge, and an anthology of the same name is issued to every cadet ([British Army](https://www.army.mod.uk/support-and-training/our-schools-and-colleges/rma-sandhurst); [Serve to Lead anthology PDF](https://www.mkbartlett.co.uk/data/further/0311MOIFR01.pdf)). Evidence of effect: no outcome study found, grade C. Longevity and foreign armies sending cadets are indirect signals, grade B ([ESD 2025](https://euro-sd.com/2025/09/articles/armament/46370/serve-to-lead)).
  2. **Single common pipeline with staged terms** — every Regular officer goes through the same 44-week course: Junior, Intermediate, Senior ([British Army, officer training](https://www.army.mod.uk/support-and-training/our-schools-and-colleges/rma-sandhurst/officer-training)). Grade A for the structure, C for causal effect. Driver: a shared rite of passage creates a common language across the whole officer corps.
- Invariant hint: "authority framed as service, carried by a short repeatable phrase and a shared passage".

### OODA loop (Boyd)
- Kind: decision model / doctrine meme · Longevity: ~50 yrs (briefings from 1976; in USMC FMFM 1 1989 / MCDP 1) · Holon: H1–H5 · Quadrant strongest: IT (cognition)
- Mechanics:
  1. **Tempo of the decision cycle** — observe → orient → decide → act. The claim is that cycling faster and with better orientation than a competitor disorients them. Evidence: shaped the USMC capstone doctrine *Warfighting* and joint doctrine ([MCDP 1](https://www.marines.mil/Portals/1/Publications/MCDP%201%20Warfighting.pdf); [UMD dissertation](https://drum.lib.umd.edu/items/98fd441e-6b8c-4d76-95c6-025901ec7da3/full)), grade A for diffusion. The theory itself is "advocated more than tested" ([Contemporary Security Policy 2013](https://www.tandfonline.com/doi/abs/10.1080/13523260.2013.839257)), grade C for effectiveness.
  2. **Orientation as the hub** — Boyd's full diagram puts mental models (background, experience) at the centre, not speed alone ([MCA Gazette](https://www.mca-marines.org/gazette/opening-the-loop)), grade B. Driver: a simple four-word loop is easy to teach and spreads. Its success is mostly diffusion, not proven outcomes.
- Invariant hint: "a four-step loop memorable enough to become shared vocabulary".

### Navy SEAL training: swim buddy and boat crews
- Kind: selection + team-formation pipeline · Longevity: ~70+ yrs (UDT/BUD/S lineage; SEALs 1962) · Holon: H2 (buddy) inside H3 (6–8-person boat crew) · Quadrant strongest: WE
- Mechanics:
  1. **Mandatory pair (swim buddy)** — trainees are never more than a few feet from their buddy. If a pair separates, both are penalised ([NavySEALs.com Hell Week excerpt](http://navyseals.com/files/pdf/HellWeek-Ebook.pdf); [Newsweek 1994](https://www.newsweek.com/hell-week-187302)). Grade B/C: practitioner accounts, no controlled study. Driver: shared accountability makes individual failure everyone's problem and builds mutual monitoring.
  2. **Small-boat crew as the unit of evaluation** — 6–8 trainees carry and paddle one boat, and the crew's performance counts. All this happens under 70–85% attrition, so passing is a team result ([BBC on Navy report 2023](https://www.bbc.co.uk/news/world-us-canada-65724065); [Sandboxx on report](https://www.sandboxx.us/news/special-ops/new-navy-report-reveals-rare-seal-training-attrition-data)). Grade A for attrition, C for team-mechanic attribution. Driver: a hard shared ordeal in a small group creates strong cohesion.
- Invariant hint: "pair inside a small crew inside a hard shared ordeal".

### IDF Talpiot
- Kind: elite talent program (selection + accelerated education + service) · Longevity: 47 yrs (1979) · Holon: H3 cohort (~50/yr) within H4 alumni (~1,000+) · Quadrant strongest: IT / WE
- Mechanics:
  1. **Extreme early selection, including group tasks** — about 50–60 cadets a year from roughly 10,000 top test-takers. Two-day assessments include team tasks whose rules change mid-test ([Israel MoD](https://english.mod.gov.il/About/Innovative_Strength/Pages/Nurturing_Future_Generations.aspx); [Talpiot official, Hebrew](https://talpiot.mod.gov.il/about/Pages/default.aspx)). Grade A.
  2. **Cohort through compressed degree + long applied service** — a physics/maths/CS degree in uniform, then about 6 years of R&D roles. Evidence: a Stanford study (Doron & Strebulaev) reports ~25% of graduates became entrepreneurs, ~3% founded unicorns (5× the Stanford MBA rate), and ~10% of Israeli unicorns had Talpiot founders ([Globes 2026](https://en.globes.co.il/en/article-study-idf-talpiot-program-excels-in-producing-entrepreneurs-1001540711)). Grade B: journalism reporting a study whose selection effects aren't separated from training effects. Driver: high-ability peers + real responsibility early + a dense lifelong peer network.
- Invariant hint: "select hard, then give a small cohort real responsibility early, together".

### Unit 8200 alumni network
- Kind: alumni network from an intelligence unit · Longevity: unit predates 1948; formal alumni association ~2008 (24,000 members by 2023) · Holon: H5 (network of cohorts) · Quadrant strongest: WE / ITS
- Mechanics:
  1. **Shared-service credential + alumni association** — the association has 24,000 members and runs startup programmes ([Ctech 2023](https://www.calcalistech.com/ctechnews/article/hjnmgbk00p)). Its accelerator reported 75% of alumni leading startups ([Times of Israel 2014](https://www.timesofisrael.com/8200-start-up-boot-camp-turns-entrepeneurs-into-tech-warriors/)). Grade B/C.
  2. **High responsibility at 19, mission-driven small teams** — about 1,000 recruits a year. Nearly 50% of founders of Israeli cyber firms acquired for >$100M served in the unit ([Ctech 2025](https://www.calcalistech.com/ctechnews/article/sjltwsk2kg)), and Forbes estimates 1,000+ companies were founded by alumni ([Forbes 2016](https://www.forbes.com/sites/richardbehar/2016/05/11/inside-israels-secret-startup-machine)). Grade B (journalistic estimates, selection not controlled). Driver: trust is pre-verified ("we served together"), and early ownership of hard technical problems builds founder skills.
- Invariant hint: "status granted by peers who walked the same path, kept alive as a lifelong network".

### Structured analytic techniques (CIA Tradecraft Primer, 2009)
- Kind: analytic method toolkit · Longevity: ~17 yrs for the primer (Heuer's ACH, 1970s–1999) · Holon: H1–H3 · Quadrant strongest: IT
- Mechanics:
  1. **Externalised reasoning (matrices, key-assumptions checks)** — the primer frames these techniques as ways to challenge mindsets ([CIA primer](https://www.cia.gov/resources/csi/static/Tradecraft-Primer-apr09.pdf)). Evidence AGAINST the flagship technique: a review of 6 experiments finds ACH has "little to no overall benefit … and may even harm" judgment ([INS 2024](https://www.tandfonline.com/doi/full/10.1080/02684527.2024.2304934)). Statistical aggregation instead cut error by 61% ([JDM 2018](https://www.cambridge.org/core/journals/judgment-and-decision-making/article/boosting-intelligence-analysts-judgment-accuracy-what-works-what-fails/1530E9DAE8F42B2E8FC2B5FE374DB50F)). Grade A, but the result is negative.
  2. **Devil's advocacy / brainstorming inside SATs** — these are the parts with empirical support ([IJIC 2023](https://www.tandfonline.com/doi/full/10.1080/08850607.2023.2243803)), grade A–B. Driver: an assigned dissenter makes disagreement safe.
- Invariant hint: "spread is driven by the legitimacy of a mandated procedure, not proven effect. Aggregating many independent judgments beats a solo procedure."

### Red teaming
- Kind: institutionalised dissent practice · Longevity: wargaming roots in the 19th century; US Army UFMCS ~2004; UK MoD guide 2010 (3rd ed. 2021) · Holon: H3 · Quadrant strongest: WE / IT
- Mechanics:
  1. **A designated adversary role** — a team or person is formally tasked to attack the plan's assumptions ([UK MoD Red Teaming Handbook](https://assets.publishing.service.gov.uk/media/61702155e90e07197867eb93/20210625-Red_Teaming_Handbook.pdf); [TRADOC Red Team Handbook](https://home.army.mil/wood/6115/8222/0759/RedTeamHB.pdf)). Evidence: Schwenk's meta-analysis finds devil's advocacy more effective than expert-only approaches ([OBHDP 1990](https://www.sciencedirect.com/science/article/pii/074959789090051A)). Lab studies show higher decision quality but lower group satisfaction ([AMJ](https://journals.aom.org/doi/10.5465/255859)). Grade A (lab).
  2. **From separate team to "red team mindset"** — the UK's 2021 edition shifts to quick techniques anyone can use routinely ([GOV.UK](https://www.gov.uk/government/news/new-red-teaming-handbook-published)). Grade A for the policy, C for effect. Driver: a sanctioned role removes the social penalty for dissent.
- Invariant hint: "conflict made safe by assigning it as a role".

### Bletchley Park
- Kind: wartime knowledge-intensive organisation · Longevity: 1939–1945 (successor GCHQ continues) · Holon: grew from H3/H4 (~150) to H5 (~10,000) · Quadrant strongest: ITS
- Mechanics:
  1. **Unconventional recruitment through trusted networks** — "professor type" people were recruited via Oxbridge contacts, later via crossword and chess channels. About 75% of the staff were women ([Bletchley Park Trust](https://www.bletchleypark.org.uk/our-story/who-were-the-codebreakers/); [Women in Bletchley Park](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Women_in_Bletchley_Park)). Grade A/B.
  2. **Industrialised pipeline of specialised huts with strict compartmentalisation** — intercept, decrypt, translate, index, distribute. Staff grew from <200 (1939) to ~10,000 (1944) ([Cambridge JBS working paper](https://www.jbs.cam.ac.uk/wp-content/uploads/2020/08/wp0608.pdf)). Grade A. Outcome: Hinsley estimated the war was shortened by at least 1–2 years ([British Academy memoir](https://www.thebritishacademy.ac.uk/publishing/memoirs/2/hinsley-francis-harry-1918-1998)). Grade B, a contested counterfactual. Driver: diverse cognitive styles + division of labour + a single concentrated centre (Hinsley names centralisation itself as a cause: [lecture](https://www.cdpa.co.uk/UoP/HoC/Lectures/HoC_08e.PDF)).
- Invariant hint: "a small creative core scaled into a pipeline, everyone serving one sealed purpose".

### United Nations (Charter)
- Kind: intergovernmental charter organisation · Longevity: 81 yrs (1945) · Holon: H6 · Quadrant strongest: ITS
- Mechanics:
  1. **Weighted inclusion of the most powerful members (P5 veto)** — a substantive Council decision requires the concurring votes of the five permanent members ([UN Charter Art. 27](https://www.un.org/en/about-us/un-charter/chapter-5)). The great powers made the veto a condition of joining. The League lost the US and saw Japan, Germany and Italy leave ([Egmont Paper 2005](http://aei.pitt.edu/8980/1/ep9.pdf); [CFR](https://www.cfr.org/education/learn/resource-collections/global-governance-introduction/what-un-security-council)). Grade A for the design, B for attribution. Driver: an organisation survives when its strongest members' exit is unattractive. The price is gridlock.
  2. **Universal one-member-one-vote assembly** — membership grew from 51 to 193 states ([UN Treaty Collection](https://treaties.un.org/pages/ViewDetails.aspx?chapter=1&clang=_en&mtdsg_no=I-1&src=IND)), grade A. Driver: formal equality gives every state a stake and a stage.
- Invariant hint: "two chambers: an equal-voice whole plus a small core that holds a guarantee".

### Red Cross / Red Crescent
- Kind: humanitarian federation · Longevity: 163 yrs (1863) · Holon: H5 (191 National Societies, local branches) · Quadrant strongest: WE / ITS
- Mechanics:
  1. **A protected emblem backed by treaty law** — the red cross, crescent and crystal are recognised under the 1949 Geneva Conventions, and states are obliged to prevent misuse ([IFRC emblems](https://www.ifrc.org/who-we-are/international-red-cross-and-red-crescent-movement/emblems-and-logo)). Grade A. Driver: a single universally readable sign grants safe access.
  2. **Neutrality and impartiality as access currency** — seven Fundamental Principles in the Statutes. Neutrality is stated as the means "to enjoy the confidence of all" ([IFRC Plan 2025–26](https://www.ifrc.org/sites/default/files/2024-12/IFRC%20Plan%20and%20Budget%202025-2026.pdf)). Grade A for the rule, B for causal effect.
  3. **One national society per country, auxiliary to its government** — 191 societies and more than 18 million volunteers, local first: 76.5% of DREF funds go straight to National Societies ([IFRC](https://www.ifrc.org/who-we-are/about-ifrc); [Annual Report 2024](https://www.ifrc.org/article/ifrc-annual-report-2024-executive-summary)). Grade A. Driver: local ownership under one global identity.
- Invariant hint: "small group inside a big whole, under one protected sign and a short creed".

### World Economic Forum (Davos, Young Global Leaders)
- Kind: membership convening platform · Longevity: 55 yrs (1971); YGL 22 yrs (2004) · Holon: H4 (an annual ~3,000-person gathering in one place) + H5 (communities) · Quadrant strongest: WE / ITS
- Mechanics:
  1. **Invitation-only annual gathering in one fixed place** — about 3,000 participants from 130+ countries, including about 65 heads of state and government and roughly 830 CEOs/chairs in 2026 ([WEF 2026](https://www.weforum.org/media/annual-meeting-2026-a-spirit-of-dialogue-ceb3ae9c08); [What is Davos](https://www.weforum.org/stories/forum-institutional/davos-annual-meeting-everything-you-need-to-know)). Grade A (own data). Driver: scarcity + density of decision-makers turns attendance into status and access.
  2. **Paid partner tiers funding the platform** — 973 partners in 2025–26 ([Annual Report 2025–26](https://www3.weforum.org/docs/WEF_Annual_Report_2025_2026.pdf)), grade A. Driver: tiered membership pays for neutral-seeming convening.
  3. **Referral-only cohort (YGL)** — about 100 a year from thousands of candidates, under-40s, a 3-year programme, 1,400+ members ([YGL nominations](https://www.younggloballeaders.org/nominations); [WEF YGL](https://www.weforum.org/communities/young-global-leaders)). Grade A for scale, C for impact. Driver: nominated-by-insiders status + a cohort bond.
- Invariant hint: "a fixed place and season where access itself is the reward".

### TED and the TEDx licence model
- Kind: ideas conference + franchise-style free licence · Longevity: TED 42 yrs (1984); TEDx 17 yrs (2009) · Holon: H4 (local event, ≤100 at the entry tier) inside H6 brand · Quadrant strongest: ITS / WE
- Mechanics:
  1. **Free open release of talks** — the first six talks went online on June 27, 2006 under a CC licence. Views reached 1 billion by Nov 2012 ([TED history](https://www.ted.com/about/our-organization/history-of-ted); [TED Blog 2012](https://blog.ted.com/ted-reaches-its-billionth-video-view)). TED's media lead said growth was "entirely driven by free and open distribution" ([Power of Open](https://thepowerofopen.org/assets/pdfs/tpoo_eng.pdf)). Grade A–C (own data and claims).
  2. **Free licence with a fixed format and tiered privileges** — independent organisers agree to rules (format, curation, no commercial agenda). New licensees start at the "Community" tier (≤100 attendees) and unlock bigger tiers by meeting criteria ([Licensing tiers](https://www.ted.com/tedx/before-you-apply/licensing-tiers)). Result: 44,260 events held and 4,000+ a year ([TEDx organize](https://www.ted.com/tedx/organize)). Grade A (own data). Driver: local volunteers get a prestigious brand for free, and the brand gets distribution for free. Graded tiers protect quality.
- Invariant hint: "a fixed format licensed for free to locals, with privileges earned step by step".

### Aspen Institute
- Kind: leadership seminar / fellowship institute · Longevity: 77 yrs (1949; first Executive Seminar 1951) · Holon: H3 (seminar circle, Henry Crown class 20–22) → H5 (AGLN, 4,121 Fellows) · Quadrant strongest: I / WE
- Mechanics:
  1. **Moderated text-based seminar on values** — close reading of classic texts in small groups with a moderator. 15,000+ leaders have taken the Executive Seminar since its start ([Aspen history](https://www.aspeninstitute.org/programs/aspen-leadership-seminars/about-seminars/history); [75th Impact Stories](https://www.aspeninstitute.org/wp-content/uploads/2025/09/Aspen-Institute-75th-Anniversary-Impact-Stories.pdf)). Grade A for reach, C for outcomes.
  2. **Fellowship cohort + a required self-designed leadership project** — the Henry Crown programme runs 4 seminars over 2 years plus a project of each Fellow's own choosing ([Henry Crown program](https://www.aspeninstitute.org/programs/henry-crown-fellowship/about-the-henry-crown-fellowship)). The network reports 2,500+ social ventures launched ([AGLN Fellows](https://www.aspeninstitute.org/programs/aspen-global-leadership-network/fellows/)). Grade C (self-reported). Driver: a peer circle that outlasts the course + public commitment to one's own project.
- Invariant hint: "a small circle reflecting on shared texts, then each member carrying out a personal project".

### Olympic Games
- Kind: global recurring competition + federated movement · Longevity: 130 yrs (1896) · Holon: H6 (NOCs, IFs, host city as H4 for a fortnight) · Quadrant strongest: ITS / WE
- Mechanics:
  1. **Fixed 4-year rhythm with a rotating host** — one moment the whole world shares. Grade A (historical record) as a structure. The causal link to longevity is an inference, grade B.
  2. **Exclusive top-tier sponsorship + redistribution** — the TOP programme (1985) limits global sponsors to 10–14 and grew from $96M to more than $3B a cycle. The IOC redistributes 90% of its revenue, about $6.8B in 2021–24, or $4.7M a day, to Organising Committees, NOCs, IFs and athletes ([IOC TOP history](https://www.olympics.com/ioc/1985-creation-of-the-olympic-partner-top-programme); [Marketing Fact File](https://stillmed.olympics.com/media/Documents/International-Olympic-Committee/IOC-Marketing-And-Broadcasting/IOC-Marketing-Fact-File.pdf)). Grade A. Driver: scarcity raises sponsorship value, and redistribution keeps the 200+ member bodies invested.
- Invariant hint: "a ritual on a fixed rhythm, financed centrally and shared out to every member".

### Nobel Prize
- Kind: prestige award · Longevity: 125 yrs (1901) · Holon: H6 recognition, decided by H3 committees · Quadrant strongest: ITS / I
- Mechanics:
  1. **Nomination by invitation only, no self-nomination** — thousands of qualified academics and former laureates are invited each year, chosen so that many countries are represented over time ([Statutes §7](https://www.nobelprize.org/about/statutes-of-the-nobel-foundation); [Nomination process](https://www.nobelprize.org/nomination-and-selection-of-nobel-laureates)). Grade A. Driver: recognition from qualified peers, not from campaigning, gives legitimacy.
  2. **50-year secrecy and no appeals** — deliberations and nominations stay sealed for 50 years, and decisions cannot be appealed ([Statutes §10](https://www.nobelprize.org/about/statutes-of-the-nobel-foundation)). Grade A for the rule, C for causal effect on prestige. Driver: closed deliberation shields judges from lobbying and keeps the outcome final and unquestioned.
- Invariant hint: "status granted by peers who walked the path, decided out of sight".

### XPRIZE
- Kind: incentive prize competition · Longevity: 30 yrs (1996 launch; first award 2004) · Holon: H3 teams competing in an H5 field · Quadrant strongest: ITS
- Mechanics:
  1. **Pay only for a measurable result, open to all** — the Ansari XPRIZE: $10M for a private crewed craft reaching 100 km twice in two weeks. 26 teams from 7 nations invested more than $100M ([XPRIZE](https://www.xprize.org/news/mojave-aerospace-ventures-wins-the-competition); [Stanford GSB case](https://www.gsb.stanford.edu/faculty-research/case-studies/x-prize-foundation-revolution-through-competition)). Grade A–B. Driver: competitors spend far more than the purse, chasing status and the market a win opens. The funder pays only for success.
  2. **Audacious but well-scoped goal with a public deadline** — "audacious but achievable, well-scoped, non-prescriptive" ([Bravo/Frangione/Wander chapter](https://investinresults.org/sites/default/files/book-chapter/WM_22_Bravo-Frangione-Wander_0.pdf)). Grade B. The later industry-size claims ($100B–$546B) are XPRIZE's own, grade C.
- Invariant hint: "a public quest with a clear finish line, open to anyone, rewarding only the result".

### Cross-cutting patterns in this class
- **Purpose shared, method delegated.** Mission command, XPRIZE (non-prescriptive goals) and TEDx (fixed format, local content) all fix the *what/why* centrally and free the *how* locally.
- **Nested small units inside a large whole.** Swim buddy → boat crew → class; Red Cross branch → national society → federation; TEDx event → global brand; NOCs → IOC. Cohesion lives in H2–H3, identity at H5–H6.
- **Status granted by qualified peers, with access as the reward.** Nobel invitation-only nomination, YGL referral-only, Talpiot and 8200 alumni credentials, Davos invitations. Scarcity plus peer gatekeeping keeps the signal valuable.
- **Fixed rhythms and rituals.** AAR after every event, the 4-year Olympiad, annual Davos, yearly YGL classes, the Nobel cycle from September to December. A predictable cadence produces both learning and a shared calendar.
- **Dissent and learning made safe through assigned roles and formats.** Red team, devil's advocate and the non-punitive AAR script. The evidence is strongest here (meta-analyses). By contrast, mandated analytic procedures such as ACH spread on legitimacy, not on proven effect.
- **Keep the strongest members inside, and share the value out.** P5 veto, IOC 90% redistribution, the auxiliary-to-government status of National Societies: durable global bodies make staying more attractive than leaving for their most powerful members.

## IV–V Places and ecosystems, long-lived social structures

Scope: 31 sources (high-HDI towns grouped into one block). Grades: A = peer-reviewed / official data; B = serious journalism, books, institutional histories; C = company claim, fan/self-report, or no outcome evidence. Where evidence for causation is thin, the block says so.

### Bhutan — Gross National Happiness
- Kind: national governance framework · Longevity: concept 1970s; GNH Index + policy screening since 2008 (~17 yrs) · Holon: H6 · Quadrant strongest: ITS
- Mechanics:
  1. **Policy screening gate** — every draft policy is scored against GNH criteria before Cabinet; failing ones are revised or dropped. Evidence: screening tool in use since 2008; 15 of 22 draft policies approved, "a majority… modified to some degree" by the process ([De Gruyter, GNH-Led Development](https://doi.org/10.1515/9783110730234-013)); WTO accession reportedly rejected on GNH grounds ([GNH Centre](https://www.gnhcentrebhutan.org/about/gnh_as_a_management_tool/), C). Grade B. Driver: a named veto point makes an abstract value binding at the moment of decision.
  2. **Measured multi-domain index tied to money** — 33 indicators / 9 domains, re-surveyed every ~5 yrs, used in 5-year plan targets and the local Resource Allocation Formula. Evidence: index 0.743 (2010) → 0.756 (2015) → 0.781 (2022); "happy" share 40.9% → 48.1% ([CBS/OPHI GNH 2022 report](https://ophi.org.uk/news/beyond-gdp-bhutans-pursuit-wellbeing-and-happiness-revealed-latest-gnh-results); [OECD KEP](https://www.oecd.org/en/publications/well-being-knowledge-exchange-platform-kep_93d45d63-en/bhutan-s-gross-national-happiness-gnh-index_ff75e0a9-en.html)). Grade A for the trend; causation from index to wellbeing not established.
- Invariant hint: what is measured on a fixed rhythm and wired to resources becomes what the system optimizes.

### NEOM (The Line)
- Kind: top-down greenfield megacity · Longevity: announced 2017; not yet functioning as a city · Holon: H5–H6 (planned) · Quadrant strongest: ITS (as plan)
- Mechanics: **none demonstrably worked.** Evidence: ≥$50bn spent, first phase "dramatically scaled back"; 2030 target cut from ~1.5m to <300k residents; work on The Line paused until after 2030 ([FT, Nov 2025](https://ig.ft.com/saudi-neom-line); [Semafor, May 2026](https://www.semafor.com/article/05/22/2026/saudis-neom-halts-work-on-the-line-until-after-2030)). Grade B. Evidence is thin by design: no residents, no community outcomes to attribute.
- Invariant hint (negative case): form-first megaplans with no existing community, iteration loop or demand signal stall; capital does not substitute for a seed group.

### Auroville
- Kind: intentional international township · Longevity: 58 yrs (founded 1968); ~3,527 residents from 60 nations (2026) ([Census](https://auroville.org/page/census%20-%20auroville-population-539)) · Holon: H5 · Quadrant strongest: WE
- Mechanics:
  1. **Graded entry (volunteer → Newcomer → member)** — ≥3 months volunteering, then a 12-month Newcomer trial with a place of work and stay, community may give reasoned feedback, then admission. Evidence: process documented ([Auroville, Joining](https://auroville.org/page/joining-auroville-the-process)); longevity plus steady growth from ~3,300 (2023) to ~3,527 (2026). Grade B (no causal study). Driver: a costly, time-based trial filters for fit and builds ties before full rights.
  2. **Self-selected working groups accountable to an all-resident assembly** — no fixed hierarchy; groups for planning, funds, education, etc. Evidence: described as a core structure since early years ([Auroville, internal structure](https://www.auroville.org/page/internal-structure-visible-and-invisible)); since 2022 the external Foundation Office has overridden it and conflict is ongoing ([Auroville Media](https://auroville.media/summary-feb-to-june-2026)). Grade C for effectiveness.
- Invariant hint: membership earned through a lived trial period, not a signup.

### Findhorn
- Kind: spiritual/eco community + education centre · Longevity: community 1962; Foundation Trust 1972–2023 (51 yrs); education relaunched 2024–26 · Holon: H4 · Quadrant strongest: I/WE
- Mechanics:
  1. **Experience Week as the single front door** — a fixed one-week immersion (work in mornings, group process afternoons) for every visitor. Evidence: "Since 1974, Experience Week has been the core programme"; created because individual onboarding of a stream of visitors was unmanageable ([Findhorn](https://www.findhorn.org/blog/the-magic-of-experience-week)); courses grossed ~£2.2m/yr before closure ([Wild Hunt](https://wildhunt.org/2023/09/findhorn-foundation-closes-educational-programmes.html)). Grade B. Driver: a standard rite of entry converts curious visitors into a shared baseline experience at scale.
  2. **Attunement before decisions** — silent inner listening before group choices. Evidence: described ([Wikipedia](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Findhorn_Foundation)); no outcome data. Grade C.
- Counter-evidence: Trust wound down 2023 (costs exceeded income ~£200k/yr; fires, Covid, Brexit) ([Findhorn Trust](https://www.findhorn.org/findhorn-foundation-trust)). The ecovillage community persisted under separate ownership.
- Invariant hint: one repeatable immersion week as the onboarding ritual.

### Zuzalu pop-up cities
- Kind: temporary co-living city · Longevity: 2023 (2 months) → network of 20+ successor pop-ups to 2026 · Holon: H4 (≈200 residents) → H5 · Quadrant strongest: WE
- Mechanics:
  1. **Medium duration × medium size** — ~200 people for 2 months: longer than a conference, larger than a hacker house. Evidence: attendees "almost universally reported enjoying"; spun off Edge City, Vitalia, Mu, ZuConnect etc.; Edge City reportedly cash-flow positive ([Buterin, Palladium](https://letter.palladiummag.com/p/why-i-built-zuzalu-cec); [Buterin 2025](https://vitalik.eth.limo/general/2025/12/17/societies.html); [ns.com](https://ns.com/dashboard/zuzalu): 20+ villages, 500 ETH deployed). Grade C (founder/organizer reports). Driver: "a four-day conference is a break from your life, a two-month stay is your life".
  2. **Permissionless programming** — organizers provide one AV room and a booking page; residents create events, tracks and rituals (cold plunges, karaoke) bottom-up. Evidence: same source. Grade C.
  3. **Fork-able format** — every successor was organized by a past attendee. Evidence: [Edge City](https://www.edgecity.live/blog/zone-of-scenius). Grade C.
- Invariant hint: thin scaffold + open calendar lets a temporary group self-generate culture; alumni replicate it.

### Bell Labs (Murray Hill 1941; Holmdel 1962–66)
- Kind: corporate research lab, building-as-mechanism · Longevity: golden era ~1940s–1980s (transistor, laser, solar cell, information theory) · Holon: H5 · Quadrant strongest: ITS
- Mechanics:
  1. **Forced-encounter architecture** — a ~700-ft corridor; labs and offices deliberately put in different corridors so walking between them produced chance meetings. Evidence: Gertner, *The Idea Factory* ([NYT 2012](https://www.nytimes.com/2012/02/26/opinion/sunday/innovation-and-the-bell-labs-miracle.html); [excerpt](https://blas.com/the-idea-factory/)); Holmdel's Saarinen design kept the principle with a 6-storey atrium, wide halls, lounges ([LoC HALS](https://www.loc.gov/pictures/item/nj1850)). Grade B. Driver: proximity cuts the cost of asking a stranger a question.
  2. **Mixed-discipline project teams under one roof** — physicists, chemists, metallurgists, engineers put on the same problem (transistor team). Evidence: Gertner via [Seattle Times review](https://www.seattletimes.com/entertainment/books/the-idea-factory-bell-labs-the-fountainhead-of-innovation). Grade B.
  - General evidence that proximity causes collaboration: MIT relocations natural experiment — moving a pair into the same building raised co-authorship, peaking +2.7 papers/100 pairs in year 3 ([PLOS One 2021](https://journals.plos.org/plosone/article?id=10.1371/journal.pone.0259965), A); same-workspace researchers 3× more likely to collaborate than at 400 m ([THE on Claudel 2017](https://www.timeshighereducation.com/news/study-confirms-physical-proximity-drives-research-collaboration), A).
- Invariant hint: design paths so different specialists must cross daily.

### Googleplex
- Kind: corporate campus · Longevity: 2003–present · Holon: H5 · Quadrant strongest: ITS
- Mechanics:
  1. **Food as a collision device** — micro-kitchens/cafés within ~150–200 ft of every desk, placed between teams. Evidence: Laszlo Bock (*Work Rules*) says food points are "strategically placed between two separate work teams" to nudge interaction ([Forbes](https://www.forbes.com/sites/davidburkus/2015/07/02/the-real-reason-google-serves-all-that-free-food/)); 2.5-min walk rule for new campus ([Vanity Fair](https://www.vanityfair.com/news/tech/2013/02/exclusive-preview-googleplex)). Grade C (company design intent; no published outcome data).
- Invariant hint: put the shared resource on the seam between groups.

### Apple Park (and its Pixar precursor)
- Kind: corporate HQ · Longevity: Apple Park 2017–; Pixar Emeryville 2000– · Holon: H5 · Quadrant strongest: ITS
- Mechanics:
  1. **Central atrium as the only magnet** — Jobs put mailboxes, café, meeting rooms and (in plan) the only restrooms in Pixar's atrium to force cross-team meetings; Apple Park's single Ring extends the idea. Evidence: Catmull via [Wired](https://www.wired.com/story/unit-103/); [Wired 2017](https://www.wired.com/2017/05/apple-park-new-silicon-valley-campus). Grade C: design intent and anecdote; no measured outcome for Apple Park.
- Invariant hint: one shared center everyone must pass through.

### Silicon Valley
- Kind: regional cluster · Longevity: ~70 yrs (1950s–) · Holon: H5 · Quadrant strongest: ITS
- Mechanics:
  1. **Unenforceable non-competes (CA §16600)** — engineers can leave and start competitors; knowledge flows with people. Evidence: Gilson 1999 thesis ([NYU L. Rev.](https://law.stanford.edu/wp-content/uploads/sites/default/files/publication/256234/doc/slspublic/NYULawReview-74-3-Gilson.pdf)); Michigan's 1985 accidental switch to enforcement reduced inventor mobility (Marx, Strumsky & Fleming, [Mgmt Sci 2009](https://pubsonline.informs.org/doi/10.1287/mnsc.1080.0985)) and caused brain drain of the most collaborative inventors ([Research Policy 2015](https://www.insead.edu/sites/insead/files/assets/faculty-personal-site/jasjit-singh/documents/Personal/Marx_Singh_Fleming_RP_PRINT.pdf)); higher job-hopping in CA computer clusters ([Fed 2005](https://www.federalreserve.gov/pubs/feds/2005/200511/200511pap.pdf)). Grade A. Driver: low exit cost raises spinout rate and recombination of tacit knowledge.
  2. **Low stigma of failure** — light bankruptcy penalties let founders retry. Evidence: [BRIE working paper](https://brie.berkeley.edu/sites/default/files/brie-working-paper-2017-1.pdf). Grade B.
- Invariant hint: let people and ideas move freely between teams; make starting over cheap.

### Kendall Square (Cambridge, MA)
- Kind: university-adjacent innovation district · Longevity: biotech since mid-1970s (~50 yrs) · Holon: H4–H5 · Quadrant strongest: ITS
- Mechanics:
  1. **Walkable density next to a research university** — everything within ~10-min walk of the T; MIT lab-to-company path. Evidence: ~⅓ of MA life-science orgs; 17 of top 20 life-science firms present; 42% of MA VC invested in Cambridge ([Kendall Sq. Assoc. report](https://kendallsquare.org/wp-content/uploads/Kendall-Impact-Report.pdf), C); MIT proximity studies above (A).
  2. **Shared cheap launch space** — Cambridge Innovation Center (1999), LabCentral: co-located startups share infrastructure. Evidence: CIC hosts companies holding ~half of Kendall VC ([CIC](https://cic.com/blog/why-kendall-square-remains-central-for-innovation/), C).
  3. **Early regulatory certainty** — 1976–77 Cambridge recombinant-DNA ordinance gave rules instead of a ban. Evidence: [RVO report](https://english.rvo.nl/files/file/2022-12/medtech%20report%20-%20final%20-%20complete.pdf). Grade B.
- Invariant hint: a dense walkable ring around a knowledge source, plus cheap shared first rooms.

### Shenzhen
- Kind: special economic zone city · Longevity: 46 yrs (SEZ Aug 1980) · Holon: H6-scale city · Quadrant strongest: ITS
- Mechanics:
  1. **Rule-exception zone** — SEZ status allowed foreign capital and market pricing ahead of the country. Evidence: 58%/yr GDP growth 1980–84 vs ~10% national; Shenzhen took 50.6% of China's FDI in 1981 ([World Bank](https://documents1.worldbank.org/curated/en/294021468213279589/pdf/564470PUB0buil10Box349496B01PUBLIC1.pdf)); GDP 196m yuan (1979) → 2.2tn (2017) ([UN-Habitat](https://unhabitat.org/sites/default/files/2019/11/the_story_of_shenzhen_2nd_edition_sep_2019.pdf)). Grade A.
  2. **Dense component market = fast prototyping** — Huaqiangbei: 1.45 km², ~110k businesses, >1m component types; prototypes in days–weeks; small-batch runs profitable. Evidence: [Shenzhen Gov](https://www.sz.gov.cn/en_szgov/news/latest/content/post_11351834.html) (official, B); [MIT Tech Review](https://www.technologyreview.com/2018/12/18/1661/inside-shenzhens-race-to-outdo-silicon-valley/) (B). Driver: when every part is a short walk away, iteration cycles collapse.
- Invariant hint: a protected zone with different rules + very short build-test loops.

### Tsukuba Science City
- Kind: government-planned science city · Longevity: decided 1963, built 1969–80 · Holon: H5 · Quadrant strongest: ITS
- Mechanics (mostly a negative case): concentration of ~13,000 researchers and ~40% of national government research staff produced **little spontaneous synergy or spin-off**. Evidence: "insufficient spin-off effects… lack of cooperation among companies and research institutes" ([ERSA paper, Shin 2002](https://www-sre.wu.ac.at/ersa/ersaconfs/ersa02/cd-rom/papers/493.pdf)); institutes keep vertical links to their ministries ([Cooke](https://idjs.ca/images/rcsr/archives/V24N1-COOKE.pdf)). Grade B. What did work: a stable, family-friendly town (population ~200k) ([Science 1981](https://www.science.org/doi/10.1126/science.212.4500.1239)).
- Invariant hint (negative): co-location without horizontal links, mixing spaces or exit paths yields density without encounters.

### Pangyo Techno Valley
- Kind: planned tech cluster · Longevity: ~20 yrs (opened mid-2000s) · Holon: H5 · Quadrant strongest: ITS
- Mechanics:
  1. **Curated tenancy + resale lock** — admission by business-plan review, use-compliance, 10-year resale restriction to keep land for high-tech firms; anchors (NCSoft, Nexon, Kakao, AhnLab) plus SMEs. Evidence: 1,780 firms, 83,465 employees, ~₩226tn sales (2024) ([Pangyo official](https://www.pangyotechnovalley.org/eng/main/view); [Gyeonggi survey](https://gnews.gg.go.kr/briefing/brief_gongbo_view.do?BS_CODE=s017&number=59995)); design rules in [2025 brochure](https://www.pangyotechnovalley.org/template/resources/file/2025_pangyotechnovalley_brochure.pdf). Grade A for size (official survey), C for attribution.
  2. **Adjacent to Seoul's talent pool** — unlike Tsukuba, inside the capital's commuting belt. Evidence: [InvestKOREA](https://www.investkorea.org/ik-en/bbs/i-5045/detail.do?ntt_sn=490802). Grade B.
- Invariant hint: plan the cluster where people already live; curate who gets in.

### Tel Aviv / Israel's startup ecosystem
- Kind: national cluster · Longevity: ~33 yrs (1993–) · Holon: H5–H6 · Quadrant strongest: ITS
- Mechanics:
  1. **Yozma matched VC with buy-out upside** — $100m government fund seeded 10 private VC funds (up to 40% each), each required a foreign VC partner; private partners could buy the state stake cheaply. Evidence: VC market grew from $27m (1992) to $653m (1998); Israel became 2nd-largest VC industry in absolute terms ([Avnimelech & Teubal, Research Policy 2006](https://www.sciencedirect.com/science/article/abs/pii/S004873330600151X); [OECD 2025](https://www.oecd.org/content/dam/oecd/en/publications/reports/2025/06/benchmarking-government-support-for-venture-capital-country-notes_2cacbf3f/israel_e79663e4/b5c8cc2e-en.pdf)). Grade A. Driver: public money as a catalyst that exits, transferring know-how from experienced foreign partners.
  2. **Alumni networks of elite technical service units** — e.g., an intelligence-technology unit's alumni association (15,000+ members) used for hiring, investment and mentoring. Evidence: [ETH CSS 2019](https://css.ethz.ch/content/dam/ethz/special-interest/gess/cis/center-for-securities-studies/pdfs/Cyber-Reports-2019-12-Unit-8200.pdf); Forbes estimate of 1,000+ founded companies (C). Grade B. Driver: shared intense early-career training forms trust networks that carry into business.
- Invariant hint: a seed that exits once the market self-sustains; cohort bonds formed in hard shared work.

### Bangalore (Bengaluru)
- Kind: IT services cluster · Longevity: ~40 yrs (1985–) · Holon: H5–H6 · Quadrant strongest: ITS
- Mechanics:
  1. **Shared infrastructure gate (satellite data link)** — 1984–85 policy permitted software export via satellite; TI set up the first link 1985; STPI earth station in Bangalore 1992 let small firms serve foreign clients offshore. Evidence: 13 software-exporting firms (1992) → 5,500+ ([Karnataka IT Policy](https://ebiz.karnataka.gov.in/ebiz/pdf/KarnatakaITPolicy2020-2025.pdf)); [STPI](https://stpi.in/about-stpi-bengaluru); [Springer chapter](https://link.springer.com/chapter/10.1007/978-981-13-8102-7_5). Grade A/B.
  2. **Pre-existing public research and talent base** — IISc (1909), HAL, ISRO, NAL supplied engineers; TI chose Bangalore for IISc proximity. Evidence: [ISOCARP case](https://www.isocarp.net/Data/case_studies/255.pdf); [Times of India / TI India](https://timesofindia.indiatimes.com/business/india-business/india75-landmark-moments/articleshow/93613725.cms). Grade B.
- Invariant hint: lower one critical shared cost so that many small players can reach a distant market.

### High-HDI towns that "grow people" (Palo Alto, Cambridge MA, Oxford, Zurich, Nordic cities, Singapore)
- Kind: places with high human-development outcomes · Longevity: decades–centuries · Holon: H4–H6 · Quadrant strongest: WE/ITS
- Mechanics:
  1. **Cross-class friendship exposure ("economic connectedness")** — share of high-SES friends among low-SES people. Evidence: among the strongest predictors of upward mobility found to date; growing up in high-EC counties ≈ +20% adult income for low-SES children ([Chetty et al., Nature 2022](https://www.nature.com/articles/s41586-022-04996-4)); UK: top-decile EC areas → +38% earnings ([BIT 2025](https://www.bi.team/wp-content/uploads/2025/03/Social-Capital-in-the-United-Kingdom-Research-summary.docx.pdf)). Grade A. Driver: aspirations, information and referrals travel through friendships.
  2. **Engineered mixing** — Singapore's Ethnic Integration Policy (1989) caps each group's share per housing block. Evidence: public housing reduced enclaves and increased integration ([Sin, Habitat Int'l 2003](https://www.sciencedirect.com/science/article/pii/S0197397502000504)). Grade A. Driver: exposure is the precondition for cross-group ties (half the friendship gap is exposure — [Nature 2022 II](https://www.nature.com/articles/s41586-022-04997-3)).
  3. **High-status, highly selected mentors** — Finland admits ~10% of class-teacher applicants, master's required, high autonomy; Singapore recruits teachers from top third. Evidence: [OECD/Schleicher](https://www.oecd.org/content/dam/oecd/en/publications/reports/2019/03/the-secret-to-finnish-education-trust_12377f45/e83bbb41-en.pdf); [OECD 2011](https://www.oecd.org/content/dam/oecd/en/publications/reports/2011/11/building-a-high-quality-teaching-profession_g1g138a0/9789264113046-en.pdf). Grade A (selection facts); causal link to outcomes B. Oxford/Cambridge: weekly tutorial of 1–3 students with one tutor on prepared work ([Oxford](https://www.ox.ac.uk/admissions/undergraduate/courses/learning-at-oxford/personalised-learning)); efficacy evidence mostly qualitative (C).
  - Context: Nordic countries top happiness rankings and rank highest for expected and actual lost-wallet return (trust) ([WHR 2025](https://www.worldhappiness.report/news/world-happiness-report-2025-people-are-much-kinder-than-we-expect/)), A.
- Invariant hint: a place grows people by exposing them to those a step ahead and by giving them close, high-quality mentors.

### Kibbutz
- Kind: communal settlement movement · Longevity: 116 yrs (1910–); "renewing" privatized model since 2005 · Holon: H4 (typ. ~150–500) · Quadrant strongest: WE
- Mechanics:
  1. **Shared costly commitments (ritual/ideology)** — religious kibbutzim outgrow secular ones; collectivized members cooperate more than city residents, privatized ones don't. Evidence: religious kibbutzim ~168 people larger than predicted for age vs secular ~4 fewer ([Dunbar & Sosis 2018](https://richardsosis.com/wp-content/uploads/2023/01/dunbarsosis2018.pdf)); field experiments ([Sosis & Ruffle 2004](https://richardsosis.com/wp-content/uploads/2023/01/2004-sosis-ideology-religion-and-evolution-of-cooperation.pdf)). Wider sample: 19th-c. US religious communes lasted mean 35.6 yrs vs 7.7 for secular ([Sosis & Bressler 2003](https://richardsosis.com/wp-content/uploads/2023/01/2003-sosis-cooperation-and-commune-longevity-a-test.pdf)). Grade A.
  2. **Size near natural layers** — community sizes ~50/150/500 are more common and longer-lived; kibbutzim start to leak/fission near ~470. Evidence: Dunbar & Sosis 2018, same. Grade A.
- Invariant hint: costly shared commitments + group size kept under a natural ceiling.

### Mondragon
- Kind: federation of worker cooperatives · Longevity: 70 yrs (1956–) · Holon: H5 (~70k people, ~90 co-ops) · Quadrant strongest: ITS
- Mechanics:
  1. **Inter-cooperation relocation** — surplus members are moved to other co-ops instead of being laid off, managed by LagunAro mutual. Evidence: 518 relocated in 2023 ([Mondragon AR 2023](https://www.mondragon-corporation.com/people/site/assets/files/103207/annual-report-2023-ext.pdf)); Spanish employment growth 1983–2019 outran Spain's by 3.4× ([Reuten, J. Labor & Society](https://brill.com/view/journals/jlso/26/3/article-p336_003.xml?language=en)). Grade A. Driver: job security inside the network makes members accept change and pay cuts.
  2. **Profit pooling** — each co-op puts ≥13% of profit into a divisional solidarity fund that offsets losses elsewhere ([AR 2024](https://www.mondragon-corporation.com/people/site/assets/files/110675/annual-report-2024.pdf)). Grade A (official).
  3. **Compressed pay ratio** — typically ≤6:1, max ~9:1 gross ([Reuten PDF](https://pure.uva.nl/ws/files/155270938/jlso-article-p336_003.pdf)); survival >97% of co-ops 1956–86 ([Whyte, Making Mondragon](https://library.uniteddiversity.coop/Cooperatives/Mondragon/Making_Mondragon-the_growth_and_dynamics_of_the_worker_cooperative_complex-1991.pdf)). Grade B. Counter-evidence: Fagor Electrodomésticos failed (2013).
- Invariant hint: small autonomous units inside a larger whole that pools risk.

### Quaker meetings (clerk, "sense of the meeting")
- Kind: religious decision practice · Longevity: ~370 yrs (1650s–) · Holon: H3–H4 · Quadrant strongest: WE
- Mechanics:
  1. **Discernment instead of voting** — no votes; speak once, silence between contributions, seek unity; dissenters may "stand aside". Evidence: codified in [Quaker Faith & Practice ch. 3](https://qfp.quaker.org.uk/chapter/3); ethnographic study of silence structuring decisions ([W. J. Communication 2014](https://www.tandfonline.com/doi/full/10.1080/10570314.2013.809474)). Grade B (no outcome trials); claimed higher implementation because decisions are owned ([MDPI Religions 2018](https://www.mdpi.com/2077-1444/10/1/35), B).
  2. **Clerk as servant, rotating** — clerk has no formal power, senses the emerging unity and drafts the minute in the meeting; term typically ≤3 yrs. Evidence: [J. Business Ethics 2021](https://link.springer.com/article/10.1007/s10551-020-04706-y). Grade B.
- Invariant hint: a facilitator who serves the group's discernment, not their own view; decisions written in the room.

### Open source: Linux kernel and Apache
- Kind: distributed peer-production · Longevity: Linux 35 yrs (1991–), ASF 27 yrs (1999–) · Holon: H5 · Quadrant strongest: ITS
- Mechanics:
  1. **Earned, project-scoped authority** — contributor → committer → PMC/member, granted by peers after sustained public contribution; merit is non-transferable between projects. Evidence: ASF passed 10,000 committers across ~295 projects (Dec 2025) ([ASF FY2026 report](https://www.apache.org/foundation/docs/FY2026AnnualReport.pdf); [Apache Way](https://www.apache.org/theapacheway/index.html)). Grade A (official counts) / C (attribution).
  2. **Trust-chained maintainer hierarchy + fixed release rhythm** — ~4,000 developers/yr send patches to ~1,500 subsystem maintainers, who forward up to Torvalds; 2-week merge window then ~7 weeks of fixes only (≈9-week cycle). Evidence: [Kroah-Hartman interview](https://newsletter.pragmaticengineer.com/p/how-linux-is-built-with-greg-kroah); 1,501 maintainers in v5.8 ([Linux Foundation 2020](https://www.linuxfoundation.org/hubfs/Reports/2020_kernel_history_report_082720.pdf)). Grade B.
- Invariant hint: status granted by peers who walked the path; a fixed cadence that alternates open and closed phases.

### Potlatch (Northwest Coast)
- Kind: competitive gift feast · Longevity: centuries; banned in Canada 1885–1951 yet persisted · Holon: H4–H5 · Quadrant strongest: WE
- Mechanics:
  1. **Status by giving away** — a host claims rank by distributing (sometimes destroying) wealth before witnesses who must reciprocate. Evidence: Boas/Mauss classical ethnography summarized in [Heins et al. 2018](https://duepublico2.uni-due.de/servlets/MCRFileNodeServlet/duepublico_derivate_00077842/Heins_et_al_2018_Gift-giving.pdf). Grade B. Persistence through a 66-year legal ban is itself evidence of social function. Driver: public witnessing turns generosity into durable reputation and redistributes surplus.
- Invariant hint: prestige earned by what you give in front of the group.

### Kula ring (Trobriand / Massim)
- Kind: inter-island ceremonial exchange · Longevity: documented 1915–present (prehistory debated) · Holon: H5 · Quadrant strongest: WE
- Mechanics:
  1. **Lifelong partnerships carried by circulating non-useful valuables** — necklaces clockwise, armshells counter-clockwise; nobody keeps them; "once in the Kula, always in the Kula". Evidence: Malinowski 1920/1922 ([Man 1920](https://acdigitalpedagogy.org/mpelzel/kula-ocr.pdf)); game-theoretic and simulation work frames it as a peace signal among potentially hostile communities with checks on cheating, enabling barter alongside ([Ziegler, Social Networks 2008](https://www.sciencedirect.com/science/article/abs/pii/S037887330700072X)). Grade A/B. Caveat: later archaeology says the ring was less stable than Malinowski thought ([LSE](https://researchonline.lse.ac.uk/id/eprint/120022/1/Steinmuller_archaeology_of_the_kula_and_malinowski_accepted.pdf)).
- Invariant hint: tokens that must keep moving turn strangers into standing partners.

### Barn raising (Amish mutual aid)
- Kind: collective work event · Longevity: ~300 yrs in North America · Holon: H4 (church district ~135 people) · Quadrant strongest: WE
- Mechanics:
  1. **Episodic all-hands mutual aid as insurance** — community rebuilds a barn in a day; informal aid replaces commercial insurance. Evidence: Amish population 177,910 (2000) → 410,955 (2025), doubling ~20 yrs, retention ≥85% ([Young Center 2025](https://groups.etown.edu/amishstudies/amish-population-profile-2025/)); retention 84.5% for 40+ ([PMC 2025](https://pmc.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/articles/PMC12909094/)). Grade A for growth/retention; barn raising's specific share of that is not isolated (B). Driver: visible, reciprocal help makes the cost of leaving the group concrete.
- Invariant hint: a recurring all-hands work day that turns help into a felt guarantee.

### Harvest festivals
- Kind: seasonal communal feast · Longevity: millennia across farming societies · Holon: H4–H6 · Quadrant strongest: WE
- Mechanics:
  1. **Calendar-fixed reunion around shared food** — Evidence: US Thanksgiving is the busiest travel holiday, record 81.8m travelers projected 2025 ([AAA](https://newsroom.aaa.com/2025/11/aaa-thanksgiving-travel-forecast-2025)), 74% dine with others ([Pew 2024](https://www.pewresearch.org/short-reads/2024/11/21/the-vast-majority-of-americans-celebrate-thanksgiving-but-their-traditions-and-activities-vary-widely/)). Grade A for participation. Causal welfare evidence is mixed: Mexican saint-day festivals that coincide with planting raise religiosity and social capital but lower incomes ([Montero & Yang, AER 2022](https://pubs.aeaweb.org/doi/pdfplus/10.1257/aer.20211094)), A.
- Invariant hint: a fixed date that pulls a dispersed group back to one table.

### Coming-of-age rites
- Kind: initiation ritual · Longevity: universal, prehistoric · Holon: H3–H4 (initiation cohort) · Quadrant strongest: I/WE
- Mechanics:
  1. **Shared ordeal → identity fusion** — sharing painful, self-shaping experiences produces fusion that predicts willingness to sacrifice for the group. Evidence: model + tests in veterans, fraternities, martial arts, twins ([Whitehouse et al., Sci Rep / PMC 2017](https://pmc.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/articles/PMC5349572/)); theory ([Whitehouse & Lanman, Curr Anthropol 2014](https://www.journals.uchicago.edu/doi/10.1086/678698)). Grade A.
  2. **Witnessed threshold** — related spectators synchronize heart rhythms with performers ([Konvalinka/Xygalatas, PNAS 2011](https://www.pnas.org/doi/10.1073/pnas.1016955108)). Grade A.
- Invariant hint: status granted after a hard threshold crossed together, in front of kin.

### Sabbath
- Kind: weekly rest ritual · Longevity: ~3,000 yrs · Holon: H1–H4 · Quadrant strongest: I/WE
- Mechanics:
  1. **Fixed 24-h withdrawal from work, filled with family/community** — Evidence: in 5,411 Adventists, Sabbath-keeping associated with better physical and mental health, partly mediated by social support and meaning ([Superville et al., IJPR 2014](https://experts.llu.edu/en/publications/sabbath-keeping-and-its-relationships-to-health-and-well-being-a--2/)). Grade A (cross-sectional, not causal). Driver: a non-negotiable rhythm protects rest and relationships from erosion.
- Invariant hint: a ritual on a fixed rhythm that cannot be traded away.

### Lunar festivals (Lunar New Year, Mid-Autumn)
- Kind: calendar reunion festival · Longevity: >2,000 yrs · Holon: H2–H6 · Quadrant strongest: WE
- Mechanics:
  1. **Obligatory reunion on a lunar date** — Evidence: 2025 chunyun recorded 9.02bn inter-regional trips in 40 days (+7.1% y/y), "driven by family reunions" ([Xinhua/gov.cn](https://english.www.gov.cn/archive/statistics/202502/23/content_WS67bac78cc6d0868f4e8efe63.html)); Mid-Autumn/National Day 2025: 2.43bn trips in 8 days ([gov.cn](https://english.www.gov.cn/archive/statistics/202510/09/content_WS68e759f4c6d00ca5f9a06a12.html)). Grade A for scale; effect on wellbeing not measured here.
- Invariant hint: a cosmic clock (moon) makes the date unarguable, so everyone returns at once.

### Enlightenment salons
- Kind: hosted recurring conversation · Longevity: ~1650–1790 Paris · Holon: H3 (a table) → H5 (network of salons) · Quadrant strongest: WE
- Mechanics:
  1. **Fixed weekly slot + segmented guest lists** — Mme Geoffrin moved the main meal to 1 pm to open the afternoon for talk and ran Mondays (artists) and Wednesdays (writers/philosophers). Evidence: [World History Encyclopedia](https://www.worldhistory.org/article/2374/parisian-salons--the-enlightenment/); network data on 550+ habitués of six salons ([IU, Mapping the Republic of Letters](https://scholarworks.iu.edu/journals/index.php/18cy/article/download/27504/32805)). Grade B.
  2. **A host who governs the conversation** — the salonnière chose guests and steered turn-taking. Evidence: [Goodman, *Republic of Letters*](https://cornellpress.cornell.edu/book/9780801481741/the-republic-of-letters/); her claim that salons were the Enlightenment's central institution is contested (Lilti) ([Stanford ARCADE](https://shc.stanford.edu/arcade/publications/rofl/issues/volume-1-issue-1/kingdom-politesse-salons-and-republic-letters)). Grade B.
- Invariant hint: a host + a fixed day + a curated mix.

### The Inklings
- Kind: writers' critique circle · Longevity: ~17 yrs (early 1930s–c.1949 for readings) · Holon: H3 (19 members over time, ~6 per night) · Quadrant strongest: WE
- Mechanics:
  1. **Reading drafts aloud weekly for critique** — Thursday evenings in Lewis's rooms; members read works in progress and got immediate feedback. Evidence: manuscript comparison shows substantial mutual influence; Tolkien drafted up to 18 versions of chapters in this loop (*The Lord of the Rings*, *Out of the Silent Planet*) ([Glyer, *The Company They Keep*](https://www.kentstateuniversitypress.com/2010/the-company-they-keep/); [C.S. Lewis Institute interview](https://www.cslewisinstitute.org/resources/diana-pavlac-glyer-and-the-importance-of-collaboration)). Grade B. Driver: a trusted audience on a fixed rhythm creates both deadlines and encouragement.
  2. **No rules, officers or agenda** — informal friendship base ([Mythopoeic Society](https://www.mythsoc.org/inklings.htm)). Grade B.
- Invariant hint: a small circle that sees unfinished work every week.

### Esalen
- Kind: residential retreat/learning center · Longevity: 64 yrs (1962–) · Holon: H4 · Quadrant strongest: I
- Mechanics:
  1. **No house ideology; a stage for many teachers** — weekend/week-long workshops, invitational conferences; "espouses no particular ideology". Evidence: [ERIC catalog 1962–92](https://eric.ed.gov/?id=ED347118). Grade B.
  2. **Incubating methods into fields** — gave Fritz Perls a platform from 1964; by 1970 Gestalt training centres were central to psychotherapy; 1993–94 conferences created ecopsychology; 12-year physics–consciousness series. Evidence: [Esalen CTR](https://www.esalen.org/ctr-main) (C, self-report); history in [Kripal, *Esalen*](https://press.uchicago.edu/ucp/books/book/chicago/E/bo5298906.html) (B).
- Invariant hint: a remote, beautiful container that hosts experiments without owning them.

### Cross-cutting patterns in this class
- **Proximity is causal but must be engineered into paths.** Same-building moves raise collaboration (MIT, A); Bell Labs, Pixar, Google design the daily walk across teams. Tsukuba shows density without shared paths, horizontal links or exits produces little.
- **Low-cost exit and free movement of people feed recombination.** California non-competes (A), Mondragon relocation inside the network (A), open-source contributors moving between projects: talent flows, the system keeps it.
- **Earned, peer-granted status beats appointed status.** Apache/Linux committership, Kula reputations, potlatch rank, initiation thresholds: standing is granted by those who already walked the path, in public.
- **Fixed rhythms carry culture.** Weekly (Sabbath, salons, Inklings, Linux 9-week cycle), seasonal (harvest, lunar reunions with 9bn trips), multi-year (Bhutan 5-year survey). The date is non-negotiable, so participation does not depend on motivation.
- **Graded entry and costly commitment sustain communities.** Auroville's 1-year Newcomer trial, Findhorn's Experience Week, religious-commune survival (35.6 vs 7.7 yrs, A), shared ordeals → fusion (A).
- **Seeds that exit or fork scale better than megaplans.** Yozma left once VC self-sustained; Zuzalu's alumni forked 20+ villages; NEOM's capital-first plan stalled. Small, size-capped units (≈150–500) inside a pooling whole (kibbutz, Mondragon, Amish districts) are the durable form.

## IV.1 Projects on the land: intentional communities and ecovillages (added 2026-11-22)


Scope note: Findhorn and Auroville already appear in `6-places-structures.md` (Experience Week, attunement, Auroville graded entry, working groups). Below they carry only mechanics not covered there. Zuzalu is also covered there; Edge City below adds only post-Zuzalu data. Base rates for the class: Kanter's classic sample judged only 11 of 91 19th-c. US communities "successful" ([Communal Societies, Natural Limits](https://doi.org/10.9707/0739-1250.1768)); founders' estimates put failure at ~80% in the first two years / ~1 in 10 succeeding (cited in [Baumber et al. 2022](https://doi.org/10.5751/ES-13166-270226)). Everything that follows is survivorship among the few.

### Damanhur (Italy)
- Kind: spiritual eco-federation · Longevity: 51 yrs (1975); peak ~1,000 citizens (2000s), ~400–600 now · Holon: H4 (federation of H3 households) · Quadrant strongest: WE
- Mechanics:
  1. **Nucleo households inside a federation** — citizens live in "nucleos" of 12–30 adults that eat, share costs and childcare; ~20 nucleos form the Federation. Evidence: structure and size documented at the 2005 ICSA conference held there ([Metcalf, ICSA](https://communa.org.il/call29/call29.pdf)); Damanhurians themselves report a "critical number" per social unit found by 30 yrs of trial ([Antipode 2018](https://doi.org/10.1111/anti.12414), excerpt only). Grade B. Driver: daily life runs in a group small enough for everyone to know everyone; identity comes from the larger whole.
  2. **Short-rotation top office** — two "King Guides" elected for 6-month terms; a College of Justice checks communities against the Constitution. Evidence: ([FIC](https://www.ic.org/communiversity-library/article/damanhur)). Grade C for effect. Driver: power rotates before it can harden.
  3. **Internal currency (Credito, since 1981) + civic spillover** — 1:1 with euro, used for all internal trade; a Damanhurian became local mayor and 22 sat on valley councils. Evidence: ([it.wikipedia](https://it.wikipedia.org/wiki/Federazione_di_Damanhur); Metcalf, same). Grade B/C.
- Counter-evidence: population fell from ~1,000 to ~400 after founder Airaudi's death (2013) ([MDPI Religions 2024](https://www.mdpi.com/2077-1444/15/2/219)). Charisma held the whole; the nucleo layer persisted.
- Invariant hint: small group inside a big whole, with office that rotates on a fixed rhythm.

### Tamera (Portugal)
- Kind: peace-research ecovillage · Longevity: 48 yrs as a group (1978 Germany), 31 on site (1995); ~150–190 people · Holon: H4 · Quadrant strongest: WE
- Mechanics:
  1. **Daily Forum (transparency ritual)** — members meet for Forum, revealing "what's happening inside and among them"; built because the group concluded no project lasts without a social frame of trust. Evidence: history ([Tamera](https://www.tamera.org/history/)); 2023 accounts name Forum as the core practice ([Tamera report](https://www.tamera.org/wp-content/uploads/AMH_Activities_and_Accounts_2023_EN_240704.pdf)). Grade C. Driver: conflict surfaced early and publicly costs less than conflict hidden.
  2. **Visible ecological demonstrator (Water Retention Landscape)** — since 2006, open water grew from 0.62 ha to ~8.3 ha across 29 lakes on 154 ha; water autonomy, stabilized groundwater, lakes used by firefighters. Evidence: EU Climate-ADAPT case study ([EEA](https://climate-adapt.eea.europa.eu/en/metadata/case-studies/tamera-water-retention-landscape-to-restore-the-water-cycle-and-reduce-vulnerability-to-droughts); [ePLANETe](http://eplanete.oieau.fr/case-study/water-retention-spaces-reforestation-and-grazing-management-southern-portugal?fa=89&fc=427&fp=49&gid=15)). Grade B. Driver: a tangible result neighbors and officials can walk through earns legitimacy that ideas alone cannot.
  3. **7-day intro → 3-year joining program** — education funnel from week course to a 3-year path into the General Assembly. Evidence: ([Tamera, Joining](https://www.tamera.org/learn/joining-tamera-3-year-program-of-education-and-engagement/)). Grade C.
- Invariant hint: a fixed-rhythm truth ritual as the community's immune system.

### ZEGG (Germany)
- Kind: ecovillage + seminar centre · Longevity: 35 yrs (1991; group from 1978) · ~115 people (2025: 77 members, 16 entrants, 12 volunteers) · Holon: H4 · Quadrant strongest: WE
- Mechanics:
  1. **ZEGG Forum as an exportable method** — one person in the centre of 10–50, guided by a facilitator, mirrored by the group. Evidence: "adopted worldwide by various social networks and communities" ([GEO](https://geo.coop/story/zegg-forum-%E2%80%93-ritualized-form-communication-larger-groups)); taught to other communities for 15+ yrs ([Litfin, Ecovillages](https://ecovillagebook.org/ecovillages/zegg/)). Grade B. Driver: a portable social technology spreads the community further than its members can travel.
  2. **Governance evolution from charisma → grassroots democracy (1994) → sociocracy (2008)** — the group survived the founders leaving (to found Tamera). Evidence: ([ZEGG, 33 Jahre](https://www.zegg.de/de/aktuelles/das-ziel-ist-im-weg-33-jahre-gemeinschaftsbildung)); staged entry (Einsteiger:innen) visible in 2025 report ([ZEGG Jahresbericht 2025](https://www.zegg-gemeinschaft.de/images/pdfs/Jahresberichte/ZEGG-Jahresbericht-2025.pdf)). Grade C.
- Invariant hint: the ritual outlives the founder when it is written down and taught.

### Sieben Linden (Germany)
- Kind: cooperative ecovillage · Longevity: 29 yrs (1997); ~160 residents, target ~300 · Holon: H4 · Quadrant strongest: ITS
- Mechanics:
  1. **Councils instead of all-consensus (2008)** — the founding rule "everything essential by consensus of all" was dropped "because it cost the community too much strength"; now 7 annually elected councils decide their domains by consensus, plus a plenary. Evidence: ([Sieben Linden facts file](https://siebenlinden.org/llms.txt)). Grade B (community's own audited self-report). Driver: delegating domains stops one meeting from carrying every decision.
  2. **Capital stake + trial + ⅔ vote** — ~€25k equity per adult (refundable coop shares) after a trial period, admission by ⅔ of all residents; solidarity shares for those who cannot pay. Evidence: same source. Grade B. Driver: costly, reversible commitment selects for staying power (they note it also skews intake toward academics).
  3. **Measured footprint as public proof** — 5.0 t CO₂e/person vs 10.5 t German average; ecological footprint ~56% of German (Univ. Turin study). Evidence: ([Bocco et al. 2019, Routledge](https://www.routledge.com/The-Environmental-Impact-of-Sieben-Linden-Ecovillage/Bocco-Gerace-Pollini/p/book/9780367670320); [Sieben Linden](https://siebenlinden.org/de/oekologischer-fussabdruck/)). Grade A/B. Plus >100 seminars and ~1,000 guests a year as an economic and outreach engine.
- Invariant hint: governance that is allowed to be redesigned when it exhausts people.

### Findhorn (Scotland) — only what is new
- Kind: ecovillage web of organisations · Longevity: 64 yrs (1962) · Holon: H4 · Quadrant strongest: ITS
- Mechanics:
  1. **Community-share investment co-op (Ekopia, 2001)** — one member one vote regardless of investment, capped returns; financed wind turbines, a 220-investor £225k buy-out of the community shop, affordable housing; >£2.5M asset base. Evidence: ([Ekopia](https://www.ekopia.org.uk/about-us/); [Moray Local Place Plan](https://www.moray.gov.uk/media/ef3nfyaf/file163230.pdf); [Aisling](https://www.aislingmagazine.com/aislingmagazine/articles/TAM33/monotheism/Regrowing%20local%20Economies.html)). Grade B. Driver: residents own the assets they depend on, so the place survives any one organisation.
  2. **Many legal bodies, not one** — 40+ charities/businesses under the New Findhorn Association; when the Foundation Trust closed education (2023), residents bought land and assets (Nov 2024). Evidence: ([Scottish Parliament visit report](https://www.parliament.scot/-/media/files/committees/local-gov/correspondence/2025/reportonfindhornvisit.pdf); [NFA](https://www.findhorn.cc/about/)). Grade B. Plus Eko local currency (£15–20k in circulation, >£100k turnover/yr) ([Ekopia](https://www.ekopia.org.uk/investments/eko-community-currency/)), grade C.
- Invariant hint: modularity — when the central body fails, the village does not.

### Auroville (India) — only ownership/funds side
- Kind: international township · Longevity: 58 yrs (1968) · Holon: H5 · Quadrant strongest: ITS
- Mechanics:
  1. **Non-ownership + stewardship + maintenance stipend** — all land/buildings held by the Auroville Foundation "in trust for humanity"; residents are stewards; those working for Auroville get a monthly "maintenance". Evidence: ([Entry Policy](https://files.auroville.org/auroville-org/Entry_Policy_-_Full_RA_approved_-_23.09.17.pdf); [FAQ economy](https://www.auroville.org/page/faq-economy)); interview study names "rejection of ownership, money and status" and shared Charter as resilience factors, and **nestedness** (Indian Parliament's 1988 Act) as what saved it after the founder's death ([Baumber et al. 2022, Ecology & Society](https://doi.org/10.5751/ES-13166-270226)). Grade A (qualitative, n=10 managers).
- Counter-evidence: the same nesting let a state-appointed Governing Board since 2021–22 void stewardships and replace them with "permissive occupancy" for Masterplan implementation ([Auroville Media](https://auroville.media/wp-content/uploads/2025/10/Voice-of-Auroville-Issue-9-October-2025-DIGITAL_compressed-1.pdf)). Grade B/C.
- Invariant hint: a protective outer shell is also a point of capture; who holds the title decides the future.

### Twin Oaks (Virginia, USA)
- Kind: income-sharing commune · Longevity: 59 yrs (1967); ~85 adults + 15 children · Holon: H4 · Quadrant strongest: ITS
- Mechanics:
  1. **Labor-credit system** — 1 hour of any work = 1 credit; quota ~38.5–43/week; ~100 work areas, chosen by members; hours budgeted by vote. Evidence: ethnography ([Social Analysis 2022](https://www.berghahnjournals.com/view/journals/social-analysis/66/3/sa660302.xml)); members and ex-members asked why it survived "to a person" answered "the labor system is the secret" ([C-VILLE 2007](https://c-ville.com/making_a_living/)). Grade B. Driver: a transparent, equal unit of contribution removes the freeloader suspicion that killed The Farm's communal phase.
  2. **3-week visitor program as gate** — required before applying; one month away afterwards before decision. Evidence: ([Twin Oaks](https://www.twinoaks.org/twinoaks-visits-60/visit-tour/visitor-program)). Grade C. Driver: both sides test fit through lived work, then decide cold.
  3. **Collective businesses (hammocks, tofu, seeds, indexing)** and a federation of sister communities (Acorn, FEC). Evidence: ([FEC](https://www.thefec.org/communities/twin-oaks/)). Grade C.
- Invariant hint: one shared unit of value that everyone can see and audit.

### The Farm (Tennessee, USA)
- Kind: spiritual commune → cooperative · Longevity: 55 yrs (1971); peak 1,200+ (1980), ~200–250 since 1985 · Holon: H4 · Quadrant strongest: ITS
- Mechanics:
  1. **"The Changeover" (1983): commune → dues cooperative** — at crisis the community took in ~$6k/week while spending $10k, with >$400k debt; it moved to a real board, voted a line-item budget, split it into ~$100/adult/month dues + $35 debt levy, privatized businesses to their managers. Debt-free within 4 years. Evidence: insider account ([Stevenson, Communities 2017](https://thefarmcommunity.com/why-the-farm-survived)); [Tennessee Encyclopedia](https://tennesseeencyclopedia.net/entries/the-farm/). Grade B. Driver: visible prices and personal accountability replaced invisible subsidy; the land stayed common.
  2. **Outward missions that carry the name** — Plenty International (1974) relief arm; the Farm midwives (Ina May Gaskin) delivered 1,250+ births and built a college. Evidence: ([Plenty](https://plenty.org/history); [Sun Magazine](https://www.thesunmagazine.org/articles/21290-the-end-of-a-sixties-dream)). Grade B. Driver: a craft valued outside gives members income and status beyond the village.
- Invariant hint: keep the commons shared, make contributions individual and visible.

### Crystal Waters (Queensland, Australia)
- Kind: permaculture village (legal subdivision) · Longevity: 38 yrs (1987/88); ~250 residents · Holon: H4 · Quadrant strongest: ITS
- Mechanics:
  1. **Private plots + common land via body corporate** — 83 freehold one-acre lots on 20% of 259 ha; the best 80% held in common and licensed to residents for farming/forestry; by-laws bind land use. First rural body corporate approved in Queensland. Evidence: ([Crystal Waters](https://crystalwaters.org.au/about/cw-history-and-background); [ABC 2018](https://www.abc.net.au/news/2018-07-03/crystal-waters-eco-village-celebrates-30-years/9929342)); 1996 World Habitat Award ([World Habitat](https://world-habitat.org/awards/winners/crystal-waters-permaculture-village)). Grade B. Driver: ordinary property law gives security and bankability; the commons gives the shared project.
  2. **Two bodies: property (Body Corporate) vs social (Co-op)** — and an annual PDC course, monthly markets as outward flow; children return to raise families there. Evidence: ([GEN Australia 2025](https://www.genaustralia.org.au/2025/09/05/crystal-waters-37-years-of-living-permaculture)). Grade C.
- Invariant hint: separate the legal container from the social life.

### Earthaven (North Carolina, USA)
- Kind: aspiring ecovillage · Longevity: 31 yrs (1994/95); ~75 adults + 25 children · Holon: H4 · Quadrant strongest: WE
- Mechanics:
  1. **Limiting the block** — after 18 yrs of unanimity, 2012 rule: a block is invalid if 85% of Council say so; valid blocks trigger up to 3 joint meetings to co-write a new proposal; 2014 removed blocking altogether (unanimity kept only for admitting members). Evidence: ([Christian, Earthaven 2013](https://www.earthaven.org/governance-and-legal/earthavens-new-decision-making-method/); [FIC 2014](https://www.ic.org/communiversity-library/article/radical-governance-changes-in-two-north-american-ecovillages/)); observers report "a more relaxed, cooperative atmosphere" ([FIC dispatch](https://www.ic.org/communiversity-library/article/in-community-on-the-road-dispatch-2-earthaven-ecovillage/)). Grade C.
  2. **Pods (neighborhood legal entities) under an HOA** — fixed after a 2010–2019 legal restructuring with a membership moratorium; seven new members joined within six months of reopening. Evidence: ([Legal at Last](https://www.earthaven.org/governance-and-legal/legal-at-last/)). Grade C.
- Invariant hint: protect the group from a single veto, but keep the veto at the gate.

### Svanholm (Denmark)
- Kind: large collective with organic estate · Longevity: 48 yrs (1978); ~130–140 people · Holon: H4 · Quadrant strongest: ITS
- Mechanics:
  1. **80/20 shared economy** — 80% of every member's income (in- or out-side jobs) goes to one fund covering tax, rent, food; 20% private (now a "40-40-20" scheme). Evidence: ([GEN](https://ecovillage.org/map/community/svanholm/); [Green Power Denmark 2025](https://greenpowerdenmark.dk/nyheder/46-aar-naturens-tjeneste); [Svanholm info](https://svanholm.dk/wp-content/uploads/2026/02/INFO-TIL-NYE-SVANHOLMERE.pdf)). Grade B. Driver: pooling without full communism; members keep a private zone.
  2. **Common meeting as top authority + autonomous working groups within an annual budget**, consensus without veto ("the right to be heard, the duty to find a solution all can live with"). Evidence: peer-reviewed case ([Int. J. Leadership in Education 1998](https://doi.org/10.1080/1360312980010403)); [bofaellesskab.dk](https://bofaellesskab.dk/bofaellesskaber/bofaellesskaber/729-svanholm). Grade A/B. Outcome: 30% of members have lived there 30+ yrs; 9 founders remain (2019) ([Expedition Ecovillage](https://expeditionecovillage.home.blog/2019/03/12/ideological-community-life-in-svanholm/), C); its farmers helped set Danish organic standards.
- Invariant hint: a shared purse with a guaranteed private margin.

### Dancing Rabbit (Missouri, USA)
- Kind: ecovillage · Longevity: 29 yrs (1997); ~50–60 people · Holon: H4 · Quadrant strongest: ITS
- Mechanics:
  1. **Binding ecological covenants** — no personal motor vehicles on site, no fossil fuels for heating/cooling/refrigeration/vehicles, organic-only land use; a vehicle co-op instead. Evidence: per-capita use <10% of average American in most categories ([Boyer 2016, Sustainability: Science, Practice & Policy](https://www.tandfonline.com/doi/10.1080/15487733.2016.11908153); [Lockyer report](https://www.atu.edu/research/facultyresearchgrants/14-15/Lockyer-FinalReport-Evaluating%20Progress%20Toward%20Sustainability%20Goals%20at%20Dancing%20Rabbit%20Ecovillage.pdf)); 2025 re-audit: 75–80% fewer miles driven ([DR 2025](https://www.dancingrabbit.org/newsletter/what-happened-in-2025/)). Grade A. Driver: a few hard, non-negotiable rules make the low-impact choice the default practice, not a daily willpower test.
  2. **Visitor session → letter of intent → resident → member (dues, can lease land)** — graded rights. Evidence: ([DR](https://www.dancingrabbit.org/ecovillage-life/residency-membership); [Communal Societies case](https://scholarworks.gvsu.edu/cgi/viewcontent.cgi?article=1213&context=communalsocieties)). Grade C.
- Invariant hint: few bright-line rules set at founding beat many soft norms.

### Global Ecovillage Network (GEN)
- Kind: network of villages · Longevity: 31 yrs (1995, founded at a 400-person Findhorn conference) · Holon: H5 · Quadrant strongest: ITS
- Mechanics:
  1. **Regional hubs hosted by mature villages** — first nodes at The Farm (Americas), Lebensgarten (Europe/Africa), Crystal Waters (Asia/Oceania), seed-funded 5 yrs by Gaia Trust; UN Habitat II 1996 presence; ECOSOC status. Evidence: ([Gaia Education history](https://www.gaiaeducation.org/blog/76984-the-evolution-of-gaia-education)); 2024: 252 new ecovillages joined, 1,295 mapped, 135 countries ([GEN COP30](https://ecovillage.org/resources-library/article/global-ecovillage-network-at-cop30-belem-2025/)). Grade C (self-reported).
  2. **A licensed, replicable course (EDE, 2005)** — month-long ~125 h, 4 dimensions (social, ecological, economic, worldview); graduates encouraged to run their own. Evidence: 380+ EDEs by 111 host sites, 26,000+ people, 55 countries ([CoFSA case](https://consciousfoodsystems.org/wp-content/uploads/2025/10/2024_CoFSA_Case-Study_Global_Gaia-Education.pdf)). Grade C. Driver: the curriculum travels without the village; each graduate is a potential new node.
- Invariant hint: the network grows through a teachable format plus host nodes, not a central HQ.

### Cross-class research notes (grade A/B on longevity)
- FIC data, N=454 US communes: survival 1995–2000 predicted by population size, owning the land, and age; religion had no direct effect once controlled ([J. Sci. Study of Religion 2006](https://onlinelibrary.wiley.com/doi/10.1111/j.1468-5906.2006.00006.x)). Grade A.
- Living ICs survey: decision-making structure mattered more than any other factor for reported success ([Sociological Spectrum 2019](https://doi.org/10.1080/02732173.2019.1645063)). Grade A.
- Centralized power and membership costs (fees/dues) both positively related to survival ([Communal Societies, life-spans](https://scholarworks.gvsu.edu/cgi/viewcontent.cgi?article=1615&context=communalsocieties)). Grade B.
- 70 established ecovillages: intangible resources (participatory decision-making, shared vision, shared ownership, non-monetary exchange) mattered more than physical assets ([Community Development Journal 2026](https://academic.oup.com/cdj/advance-article-abstract/doi/10.1093/cdj/bsag014/8663128)). Grade A (abstract only read).
- High-turnover communes stay stable through a core of permanent long-term members holding institutional memory ([J. Study of Radicalism 2012](https://doi.org/10.1353/jsr.2012.0005)). Grade B.

**New / emerging (picked for momentum in 2024–26)**
Why these: each tests a new answer to "how does a place-based community form now": (a) subscription society with daily ritual (Network School), (b) recurring month-long pop-up funded by tickets (Edge City), (c) legal jurisdiction + digital residency (Próspera/Infinita), (d) developer-built "amenities before houses" (Nuanu), (e) a well-funded network city that wound down (Cabin, as counter-case). ### Network School (Balaji Srinivasan)
- Kind: subscription co-living "startup society" · Longevity: 2 yrs (Sept 2024) · Holon: H4 · Quadrant strongest: WE
- Mechanics:
  1. **Daily ritual stack "Learn, Burn, Earn, Fun" bundled into one fee** — US$1,500–3,000/mo: room, 3 meals, gym, lectures, hackathons; morning group workout logged by a daily "burn" NFT scanned at the gym. Evidence: ([Straits Times](https://www.straitstimes.com/asia/se-asia/ghost-city-true-believers-and-an-israeli-row-inside-network-school-the-tech-commune-dividing-johor); [Brooklyn Rail](https://brooklynrail.org/2026/10/field-notes/how-to-ship-a-state/)). Grade B. Driver: the healthy, productive choice is the default of the environment.
  2. **Scarce first cohort → rolling growth** — 4,000+ applications for 128 places (v1, ~3%), v2 256 year-long, ~550 on site in May 2026, half long-termers. Evidence: ([InsideASEAN](https://insideasean.com/malaysia/network-school-forest-city-shutdown-johor-sfz-lesson/); [Parallel Citizen](https://www.parallelcitizen.xyz/p/ns-2026)). Grade C.
- Counter-evidence: Malaysian authorities revoked the licence (~22 July 2026); NS reopened in Astana, Kazakhstan within a month ([CNA](https://www.channelnewsasia.com/asia/network-school-johor-forest-school-closure-6270371); [Business Insider](https://www.businessinsider.com/balaji-srinivasan-network-school-reopen-kazakhstan-2026-8)). The community proved portable; the place was rented and politically exposed.
- Invariant hint: a community bound by ritual and network can relocate; one without its own land cannot stay.

### Edge City (post-Zuzalu pop-up villages)
- Kind: recurring month-long pop-up village network · Longevity: 3 yrs (2023/24) · Holon: H4 → H5 · Quadrant strongest: WE
- Mechanics:
  1. **Ticket-funded nonprofit, same place each year** — Edge Esmeralda (Healdsburg): 1,300 visitors in 2024 (~200 full month), 850 residents from 66 countries + 80 kids in 2025; ticket revenue intended to cover ~$2M costs. Evidence: ([Edge City](https://www.edgecity.live/blog/edge-esmeralda-2025-month-in-review); [Supernuclear](https://supernuclear.substack.com/p/case-study-edge-esmeralda); [tickets FAQ](https://edgeesmeralda2025.substack.com/p/tickets-for-edge-esmeralda-2025)). Grade C. Driver: annual return to one town builds local ties and a path toward a permanent town.
  2. **Thematic residencies + local inclusion** — Patagonia 2025: 11 residencies, 700+ sessions, 150+ local participants, 50 local kids weekly. Evidence: ([Edge City Patagonia](https://www.edgecity.live/blog/edge-city-patagonia-2025-month-in-review)). Grade C.
- Invariant hint: a fixed annual season in a fixed place turns an event into a village.

### Próspera / Infinita City (Roatán, Honduras)
- Kind: charter jurisdiction + longevity network city · Longevity: Próspera 2017/2020; Vitalia pop-up 2024 → Infinita 2025 · Holon: H4 (+ digital H5) · Quadrant strongest: ITS
- Mechanics:
  1. **Digital residency ahead of physical residency** — 2,262 e-residents from 40 countries and 478 companies vs ~100 physical residents (2026). Evidence: ([Limn 2026](https://limn.press/article/prosperas-stack)); zone registry 2,456 persons, 305 physical residents, verified direct jobs fell 950→409 (2024→2026) ([Próspera press note](https://www.rfsa.hn/documents/external/f90cfa35a47b-Prospera-ZEDE-Strengthens-its-Institutions_EN-.pdf)). Grade B/C. Driver: low-cost membership with an option to move later.
  2. **Pop-up seasons feeding a permanent district** — Vitalia 2024 pop-up → Infinita with 40–50 permanent residents, ~1,000 visitors targeted per season; 300+ at Feb–Mar 2026 games. Evidence: ([Infinita Manifesto](https://www.infinitatimes.com/p/infinita-manifesto-20); [Infinita April '26](https://www.infinitatimes.com/p/infinita-newsletter-april-26)). Grade C.
- Counter-evidence: Honduran Supreme Court ruled ZEDEs unconstitutional (Sept 2024); status contested, ~$10.8bn claim ([New Republic](https://newrepublic.com/article/201135/vitalia-roatan-honduras-island-cheat-death)). Founders split in 2025.
- Invariant hint: legal novelty attracts a network but leaves the place hostage to the host state.

### Nuanu Creative City (Bali)
- Kind: developer-built creative town · Longevity: started 2020, opened publicly Aug 2025 · Holon: H4 · Quadrant strongest: ITS
- Mechanics:
  1. **Amenities and culture before housing** — school, beach club, art park, festivals opened before most homes; 44 ha with 70% kept green; >Rp 2.4 tn (~US$150M) invested, ~$30M on infrastructure. Evidence: ([Windonesia](https://windonesia.com/article/nuanu-have-invested-rp24-trillion-to-build-nuanu-city-in-bali); [The Edge](https://theedgemalaysia.com/node/784849)); 4,000+ visitors/day (Aug 2026), 30+ venues ([Riserealbali](https://riserealbali.com/guides/nuanu-city-bali), developer-adjacent). Grade C. Driver: daily visitors create life and demand that later sell the residences (485 units in 12 projects).
- Counter-evidence: no independent data on resident community or governance; model is real estate first.
- Invariant hint: build the commons first, sell the private lots later.

### Cabin (network city) — counter-case
- Kind: DAO-governed network of co-living neighborhoods · Longevity: 2021–2025 (DAO wound down May 2025) · Holon: H5 · Quadrant strongest: WE
- Mechanics:
  1. **Graded node status by community vote** — Outposts promoted to "Neighborhoods" when meeting criteria (≥4 bedrooms, 20 Mbps) and 1,000 ₡ABIN votes; 4,200 members, 355 paying citizens, 23 locations (2023). Evidence: ([grin.io](https://grin.io/tns/); [BelongHub](https://communities.urbanodx.com/archive/cabin-neighborhoods-outposts-abin-votes)). Grade C.
  2. **What survived: supper clubs → shared "third place" → neighbors move nearby** — 10-week neighborhood-steward accelerator, now the Neighborhood Village Project. Evidence: ([Cabin](https://paragraph.com/@cabin/network-city); [LibaD 2025](https://startofsomethingnew.substack.com/p/cabin-wandering-around-network-villages)). Grade C.
- Counter-evidence: founders concluded "venture-backed businesses, DAOs, and community-driven networks serve different purposes"; treasury returned to token holders ([Cabin on X](https://x.com/cabindotcity/status/1920220527530225764)). "Citizenship" bundle met a "lukewarm market".
- Invariant hint: recurring shared meals near where people already live outlast a token.

### Cross-cutting patterns in this class
- **Governance is the main survival variable, and the winners redesigned it.** Sieben Linden (2008), ZEGG (2008), Earthaven (2012/14) all retreated from unanimity; The Farm and Damanhur moved from founder authority to boards/rotating offices. Survey data rank decision structure first for success (A).
- **Costly, graded entry.** Visits of 1–3 weeks → trial → vote (Twin Oaks, Dancing Rabbit, Sieben Linden ⅔ vote, Tamera's 3-year path). Membership costs correlate with survival (B); the cost can be money, time or a lived work trial.
- **Own the ground, separate the containers.** Land ownership predicts survival (A); durable places split property from social life (Crystal Waters body corporate + co-op; Findhorn's 40+ bodies and community shares). Rented or state-held ground is where newer projects broke (Network School, Próspera; Auroville's state trust).
- **A visible, auditable unit of contribution** (Twin Oaks hour, Farm dues, Svanholm 80/20) replaces suspicion of freeloading; hidden subsidy precipitated The Farm's 1983 crisis.
- **Small group inside a big whole + fixed-rhythm ritual.** Nucleos of 12–30, daily Forum, shared dinners, daily workout; the household/circle is where life happens, the federation is where identity lives.
- **Spread by exportable formats, not by growth of one site.** ZEGG Forum, EDE (26k graduates), Tamera/Sieben Linden seminars, Edge City seasons, Cabin supper clubs: the method travels; villages stay at ~100–300.

## IV.2 Transformational and pioneering festivals (temporary villages) (added 2026-11-22)


Grading: A = peer-reviewed or official/third-party data (government inspection, census with confidence intervals); B = serious journalism, books, filed accounts; C = organiser's own claim, fan site, or inference. Organiser-published counts are graded C unless a third party confirms them.
Caveat for the whole class: almost no festival has a controlled study that links its survival to one mechanic. Most "worked" claims below are inferred from longevity plus organiser data. Where the evidence is weak or points the other way, the block says so.

### Burning Man (Black Rock City + Regional Network + Decompression)
- Kind: participatory temporary city + global licensed network · Longevity: 39 years, 1986 (desert since 1990) · Holon: H5 network of villages · Quadrant strongest: WE
- Mechanics:
  1. **Theme camps placed for interactivity, with "good standing"** — groups get a spot in the city only if they offer daily interactivity open to all. Each camp is scored afterwards (post-playa report, MOOP record, neighbour feedback), and that score decides whether it is placed again. Evidence: criteria are official ([placement criteria](https://burningman.org/black-rock-city/camps/placement-process/criteria)). A record 1,545 theme camps were placed in 2019 ([2019 AfterBurn](https://journal.burningman.org/2019afterburn/)), C. In the 2025 Census, 85.5% returned "to feel a sense of belonging" and 56.5% "to volunteer with a theme camp or BMP department" ([BRC Census 2025](https://history.burningman.org/wp-content/uploads/report_2025_sociodemo.pdf)), A (random sample with CIs). Driver: a 5–50-person crew gives identity and obligation inside an anonymous 70k city. The reputation score makes your contribution count toward next year.
  2. **10 Principles as a portable charter → Regional Network** — principles written in 2004 *specifically* so others could replicate the culture without central control. Local volunteers run events under a letter of agreement. Decompression parties (SF since 1999) serve as the re-entry ritual and recruiting funnel. Evidence: [10 Principles page](https://burningman.org/about-us/10-principles) (written 2004 for the Regional Network). The network grew from 3 regions to 240 volunteer Regional Contacts in 34 countries, with 96 official Regional Events and ~110k participants in 2025, more than BRC itself ([2025 AfterBurn](https://journal.burningman.org/2025-afterburn/); [Our Impact](https://burningman.org/about-us/what-we-do/our-impact/)), C. Roughly 30–37% of BRC attendees attended a regional in every year 2013–2022 ([Census 2013–22](https://volunteers.census.burningman.org/reports/2013-2022/beyond-the-burn-regionals-media-and-volunteering.html)), A. Driver: a short, quotable, values-only code is cheap to copy, and local leaders gain standing by carrying it.
  3. **Leave No Trace as a public collective score** — a federal agency inspects the site (BLM post-event inspection, ≤1 sq ft of debris per acre at 120 test points). Volunteers then publish a colour-coded MOOP map that shows each camp's block. Evidence: passed every year. It was close in 2022 (8 test areas failed); 2023 had 11 of 120 points over and still passed ([MOOP 2022](https://journal.burningman.org/2023/03/black-rock-city/leaving-no-trace/2022-moop-maps), [MOOP 2025](https://journal.burningman.org/2026/03/black-rock-city/leaving-no-trace/moop-map-2025)), A (government test, reported by the organiser). Driver: an outside pass/fail exam that can end the permit, plus visible attribution by place, turns cleanup into shared pride and shared shame.
- Invariant hint: small group inside a big whole, governed by a short charter anyone can copy, with a public scoreboard for shared stewardship.

### Boom Festival (Portugal)
- Kind: biennial psychedelic-culture festival on owned land · Longevity: 29 years, 1997 (biennial since 1998; Boomland bought 2016) · Holon: H4 village (a place) · Quadrant strongest: ITS
- Mechanics:
  1. **Full-moon biennial rhythm + no sponsors + no line-up needed** — the festival is held every two years and funded only by tickets, bar and stall rents. It publishes a financial report. Evidence: 2025 was the "7th consecutive sold-out edition, selling out in 48 hours, without even announcing a line-up" ([Letter to the Boomers 2025](https://www.boomfestival.org/news/letter-to-the-boomers-2025)), C. Visitors came from 177 countries in 2022, and tickets also sell through 63 ambassadors in 60 countries ([Yourope case](https://yourope.org/wp-content/uploads/2023/05/1-boom-festival.pdf)), C. Driver: scarcity in time (a two-year wait) plus trust that the money isn't going to sponsors makes people buy the experience, not the headliners.
  2. **Regenerative infrastructure as identity** — compost toilets have been developed since 2006 (538 units in 2025, no water or chemicals), alongside on-site water treatment and reforestation of owned land. Evidence: Outstanding Greener Festival Awards 2008–2018, 2023 and 2025, plus the European Festival Award Green Operations 2023 ([awards](https://www.boomfestival.org/news/awards-nominations); [compost toilets](https://www.boomfestival.org/environment/compost-toilets)), B (third-party award). Driver: participants co-own a visible ethic ("leave the land healthier"), which turns rules into belonging.
- Invariant hint: a ritual on a fixed (rare) rhythm, anchored to a place the community regenerates.

### Lampu (Bali)
- Kind: unofficial burn-style community event (Russian-speaking diaspora core) · Longevity: 4 editions, 2023 · Holon: H4 village · Quadrant strongest: WE
- Mechanics:
  1. **No spectator tickets: entry only through a camp or department** — you join via a team whose lead vouches that members know the principles. There is an organiser fee plus a camp fee set by the camp, and a competitive low-income quota. Evidence: rules stated on [lampu.org](https://lampu.org/). Attendance was ~700 in 2023 ("far more than expected"), C. "Over 1,000" in 2024 ([Bitcoinmoney](https://bitcoinmoney.net/balis-lampu-festival-embraces-web3-innovation-with-nft-ticketing-and-crypto/)), C. Too young to attribute survival. Driver: belonging is granted by a small group before arrival, so culture is transmitted peer-to-peer and not by marketing.
  2. **Invite-chain onboarding through a chat** — "talk to people to get an invitation link" in the Telegram chat, then a questionnaire and a personal cabinet. Each year gets a theme (Hati ke Hati, Kontak, Kompas) ([lampu.org](https://lampu.org/)). Evidence: none beyond the organiser, C. Driver: friction plus a human sponsor filters for intent.
- Invariant hint: admission through a sponsor-led small group, not through a box office.

### Fusion Festival (Germany, Kulturkosmos Müritz e.V.)
- Kind: non-profit association festival on a former Soviet airfield · Longevity: 29 years, 1997 (association since 1999) · Holon: H5 network of crews around one place · Quadrant strongest: ITS
- Mechanics:
  1. **Ticket lottery for purchase options, personalised tickets, face-value-only exchange** — register once, two draws, groups can win together, and resale happens only on the official exchange at the original price. The price has been held at €220 incl. a €10 trash deposit. Evidence: 65–70k tickets, financed solely by tickets, no advertising, no sponsors ([Deutschlandfunk Nova](https://www.deutschlandfunknova.de/beitrag/links-unkommerziell-und-super-erfolgreich-die-besonderheit-und-der-zauber-des-fusion-festivals), B; [ticket FAQ](https://tickets.fusion-festival.de/faq), C). Driver: luck feels fairer than speed or wealth, and the cohort stays mixed.
  2. **Crew economy: volunteer time converted into funding for your own cause** — most crews (bars, build, services) are non-profit, cultural or political collectives. They earn money for their own projects outside the festival, and individuals work for ticket plus fee. Evidence: "over 200 crews", >1,000 helpers in the build-up, ~7,000 active during the festival ([fusion-festival.de](https://fusion-festival.de/de/festival); [Kulturkosmos infopage](https://kulturkosmos.de/infopage.html)), C. Organiser on record: "if your anti-repression group is looking for money, sign up with us" (Dlf Nova, B). Driver: contribution pays twice, as belonging now and as resources for a home community.
  3. **Planned rest years** — 2027 is announced as "Fusion-free" ("Rhythmus und Veränderung") ([tickets page](https://tickets.fusion-festival.de/)), C.
- Invariant hint: the big whole as a funding engine for many small homes; fair access by lot.

### O.Z.O.R.A. (Hungary)
- Kind: week-long psychedelic arts festival on a family farm · Longevity: 22 years annual, 2004 (seed: 1999 eclipse "Solipse") · Holon: H4 village · Quadrant strongest: ITS
- Mechanics:
  1. **Celestial origin → permanent home owned by a host family** — a one-off eclipse gathering led the landowner (Dániel Zimányi, d. 2018) to build an annual festival. His son now produces it, with ~15 people working year-round on organisation and site upkeep ([Telex 2022](https://telex.hu/after/2022/06/10/ozora-daad-gathering-fesztival-interju)), B. Official capacity is 30,000, but de facto 60–70k has been reported ([Wikipedia](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Ozora_Festival)), C. New permanent structures are added every year ([note.com guide](https://note.com/masaakiobari/n/n7f318bfae57b)), C. Evidence that these mechanics *caused* longevity: C (inference). Driver: a returning crowd finds the "same village, a bit more built" each year, which mixes continuity with novelty.
  2. **Global pilgrimage audience** — the large majority of attendees are foreigners, and the festival is barely known domestically (Telex, B). Driver: a journey raises commitment, and the crowd self-selects for the culture.
- Invariant hint: a sacred-time founding moment, then a stewarded place that accretes year by year.

### Envision Festival (Costa Rica)
- Kind: music + permaculture + education festival · Longevity: 16 editions, 2011–2026; **future announced "unknown", no 2027 edition** · Holon: H4 village · Quadrant strongest: ITS
- Mechanics:
  1. **Land regeneration and zero single-use operations** — the site went from cattle pasture to food forest. There is a reusable dish program with no single-use cups or cutlery, and the Somos el Cambio foundation was started in 2020. Evidence: 45,000+ native trees reported ([Costa Pacifica Living](https://costapacificaliving.com/costa-rica-news/costa-rica-entertainment-culture/envision-festivals-grass-roots)), C. Growth went from 700 to 5,000 attendees and from 4 to 7 days ([El Financiero](https://www.elfinancierocr.com/negocios/luigi-jimenez-del-festival-envision-la-comunidad/CYY4DCF2TZHDXGXTPALZRABJ4Y/story/)), B. Driver: attendees see their ticket become trees and water systems, which ties a party to a purpose.
  2. **Counter-evidence: capacity cut ("smaller by design") did not secure survival** — after the 2024 overload, the 2026 cap dropped to 5,500 (−2,000) and local hiring went up ([2026 improvements](https://www.envisionfestival.com/improvements)). Then: "the cost was unsustainable… we do not see a path to a 2027 festival" ([announcement](https://www.envisionfestival.com/)), C. Lesson: ethics and volunteer work exchange did not offset a production model that depended on an international crew and a single site.
- Invariant hint: a purpose visible in the land; a costly production model is the binding constraint, not values.

### Lightning in a Bottle (California, The Do LaB)
- Kind: company-run transformational festival · Longevity: ~20 editions, 2004 (hiatus 2020–21) · Holon: H4 village · Quadrant strongest: IT/ITS
- Mechanics:
  1. **"The Village" learning layer next to the music** — talks, workshops, a Learning Kitchen and yoga sit as a co-equal zone, and participants apply to present. Evidence: program described officially ([LIB FAQ](https://www.libfestival.org/info-faq)). No outcome data, C. Driver: the attendee can arrive as a learner and leave as a teacher, which widens the reasons to return.
  2. **Operational Leave-No-Trace with participant labour** — free water refills since 2006, mandatory pre-sorted waste stations, fees for unsorted waste, daily volunteer MOOP walks, and a Ranger corps for conflict mediation ([Dancing Astronaut 2022](https://dancingastronaut.com/2022/06/10-factors-that-make-lightning-in-a-bottle-a-one-of-a-kind-experience), C; [political.org 2026](https://political.org/2026/05/26/lightning-in-a-bottle-what-permanent-cities-can-learn-from-a-temporary-city/), C). The founders name finding a site as the biggest challenge (5 relocations) ([Press-Enterprise](https://www.pressenterprise.com/2023/05/17/lightning-in-a-bottle-celebrating-20-years-of-the-indie-music-and-arts-fest)), B. Evidence for attribution: C.
- Invariant hint: a burner-derived ethic transplanted into a ticketed format; the place, not the culture, is the fragile part.

### Shambhala Music Festival (Canada)
- Kind: family-owned farm festival, no corporate sponsors · Longevity: 28 editions, 1998 · Holon: H4 village · Quadrant strongest: IT (body/safety) + ITS
- Mechanics:
  1. **Sanctioned on-site drug checking with results shown publicly** — ANKORS (a non-profit) has tested substances since 2003. Results go up on a public screen, with an amnesty bin, fentanyl strips and naloxone training. Evidence: peer-reviewed study of 2,683 checks at Shambhala, results posted live ([Can J Public Health via PMC](https://pmc.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/articles/PMC6964406/)), A. In 2025, 4,592 samples were tested and ~7% were not what users expected ([NYT 2026](https://www.nytimes.com/2026/08/06/well/shambhala-music-festival-drugs-ketamine-ecstasy.html)), B. Government support is noted by [Billboard](https://www.billboard.com/music/music-news/shambhala-music-festival-drug-testing-2020-dance-issue-9332924/), B. Driver: non-judgmental truth-telling builds trust, and a visible screen turns private risk into shared knowledge.
  2. **Family land + independence + "Farmily" identity** — growth from ~500 (1998) to 23,500 guests/crew/artists, no sponsorship; the farm returns to working ranch after ([About](https://shambhalamusicfestival.com/about)), C; [The Tyee](https://thetyee.ca/News/2014/05/17/Shambhala-Festival/) on winning over the host town, B.
- Invariant hint: care infrastructure that treats people as adults; a family-held place as the stable core.

### Glastonbury (UK)
- Kind: farm-based mega-festival with charity mission · Longevity: 56 years, 1970 (most years since 1981) · Holon: H4 village (at city scale) · Quadrant strongest: ITS
- Mechanics:
  1. **Fallow year roughly every five years** — no festival, so the farm, the village and the organisers can rest (2026 was one). Profit is kept for the fallow year. Evidence: [BBC 2025](https://www.bbc.co.uk/news/articles/cz70l92j90vo), B. The 2024/25 accounts show £5.7m profit retained "for the 2026 fallow year" ([Spark Intel, from Companies House](https://www.spark-intel.co.uk/bright-sparks/deep-dives/glastonbury/)), B. Driver: absence renews desire, and the host community's tolerance is protected.
  2. **Charity-volunteer exchange** — partner charities (Oxfam, WaterAid, Greenpeace) supply stewards and run fields, and the festival pays them for the volunteering plus donations. Evidence: £4.2m to 300+ organisations in 2025, of which >£2m went to the three partners as "donations and payments made in exchange for volunteering" ([official](http://www.glastonburyfestivals.co.uk/news/glastonburys-support-for-charities-and-good-causes-reaches-4-2m-in-2025/)), C. Accounts show £2.7m donated + £1.5m paid to charities for services (Spark Intel, B). Oxfam stewards have been noted since at least 2003 ([Guardian 2003](https://www.theguardian.com/music/2003/jun/13/glastonbury2003.glastonbury)), B. Driver: volunteers work for a cause they believe in, not for the promoter.
  3. **Photo-registered, non-transferable tickets** — pre-registration with a photo printed on the ticket, checked at the gate ([2027 ticket info](https://glastonburyfestivals.co.uk/news/glastonbury-2027-ticket-information-confirmed)), C. 2027 general sale sold out in under 30 minutes ([BBC](https://www.bbc.co.uk/news/articles/cv62kp9enz08o)), B. Driver: identity-bound admission kills touting and keeps price fairness credible.
- Invariant hint: a rest rhythm, plus labour routed through causes larger than the event.

### Rainbow Gatherings (USA → worldwide)
- Kind: leaderless, non-commercial forest gathering · Longevity: 54 years, 1972 (July 1–7 annually) · Holon: H5 network ("non-organization of non-members") · Quadrant strongest: WE
- Mechanics:
  1. **Council by consensus with a talking object** — open circle, a feather passed around, no interruptions; Main Council is "the only time decisions affecting the Gathering can be made" (Rainbow Guide quoted in [US v. Kalb, 2000](https://law.justia.com/cases/federal/district-courts/FSupp2/86/509/2398546)), B; [Mini-Manual](https://www.welcomehome.org/info/mini-manual.html), C. Driver: being heard without interruption confers equal dignity, which legitimises decisions without officers.
  2. **Shanti Sena — everyone is the peace force** — "you do not call the Shanti Sena, you become the Shanti Sena". Conflict is handled by crowd presence, chanting and listening. Evidence: ethnographic and government-document study finds these methods effective and less violent than police approaches ([Niman 2011, Contemporary Justice Review](https://www.tandfonline.com/doi/full/10.1080/10282580.2011.541077)), A. Driver: distributed responsibility plus social capital replaces enforcement.
  3. **Magic Hat — pooled voluntary money, no commerce** — the only cash solicitation, pay-what-you-wish, which funds shared kitchens and supplies ([welcomehome.org](https://www.welcomehome.org/media/newyork.txt)), C; consensus and non-commerce documented in Niman's monograph ([People of the Rainbow, UT Press](https://utpress.org/9781572337466/people-of-the-rainbow/)), B.
- Invariant hint: authority held by the circle; safety held by everyone; money pooled, never priced.

### Nowhere (Spain) — regional burn, ended 2026
- Kind: official Burning Man regional, all-volunteer · Longevity: 2004–2025 (discontinued 2026) · Holon: H4 village · Quadrant strongest: WE
- Mechanics:
  1. **Do-ocracy + shift system for every participant (FIST)** — no hired workforce except transport/logistics, nobody paid, no free tickets (artists and organisers included), and the books are open ([Wikipedia](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Nowhere_(event)), C; [financial reports](https://www.goingnowhere.org/event/financial-reports/), C). Evidence of the mechanic working: cap raised from 1,500 to 2,000 (2016), then ~3,600 in 2018–19 ([BM Journal 2016](https://journal.burningman.org/2016/07/global-network/regionals/europes-13th-nowhere-takes-big-steps-into-the-future)), C.
  2. **Counter-evidence: decline and closure** — participation fell every year after 2019 (2,670 in 2023) and the event was discontinued in 2026 (Wikipedia, C). Its 2024 financial report shows €154k VAT on €758k gross revenue, ~20% lost to the "default world" tax regime ([2024 report](https://www.goingnowhere.org/wp-content/uploads/2026/05/2024-Nowhere-Financial-Report.pdf)), C. Successor: Elsewhere (see below). Lesson: an all-volunteer core without an ownership/governance renewal path can wear out even with sound principles (inference, C).
- Invariant hint: the volunteer engine requires a renewal mechanism for its leaders, not just for its participants.

### Midburn (Israel) — regional burn
- Kind: official regional burn in the Negev · Longevity: community 2011, recognised 2013, events from 2014 · Holon: H4 village · Quadrant strongest: WE
- Mechanics:
  1. **Community before event** — it started as beach gatherings of returning burners (2011). The group then built an art piece at BRC ("Hand of Inspiration") as its first collective project, before running its own event ([midburn.org](https://www.midburn.org/en/onmidburn)), C. Growth: 2,876 participants/40 camps (2014) → 8,264/130 camps (2016) → ~12,000 (2018), at the time the largest regional ([BM Journal 2015](https://journal.burningman.org/2015/01/global-network/regionals/midburn-2015-seeking-transcendence-at-the-israel-regional-burn), [2016](https://journal.burningman.org/2016/07/global-network/regionals/the-transformational-dance-between-midburn-and-israeli-society), C; [Pacific Standard](https://psmag.com/magazine/burning-mensch-inside-middle-east-version-of-burning-man/), B). Driver: shared making builds a crew before strangers arrive.
  2. **Translating the principles to authorities** — after a 2015 court order to halt the build, organisers negotiated compromises in court and recruited a former police officer to explain "no spectators, no consumers" to licensing officials ([BM Journal 2015](https://journal.burningman.org/2015/06/global-network/regionals/midburn-2015-the-burn-that-almost-didnt/), C; [Israel21c](https://www.israel21c.org/midburn-the-freedom-to-be-different), B; [Schusterman](https://www.schusterman.org/article/feeling-the-burn-an-insiders-perspective-of-midburn-israels-version-of-burning-man), C).
- Invariant hint: the crew forms through a shared project first; a boundary-spanner speaks the host society's language.

### Symbiosis Gathering / Oregon Eclipse 2017 (USA)
- Kind: one-off consortium production around a total solar eclipse · Longevity: Symbiosis 2005–2019; Oregon Eclipse single edition 2017 · Holon: H5 network of festivals · Quadrant strongest: ITS
- Mechanics:
  1. **Co-production by a consortium of festivals** — 12–14 festivals pooled crews and brands for one event: Symbiosis, LIB, Envision, Rainbow Serpent, Bass Coast, Origin, Universo Paralello, Hadra, Re:Birth and others. Evidence: ~30,000 attendees for 7 days, the largest organised event in Crook County history, with the county doubling its cap to 30k ([Bend Bulletin](https://bendbulletin.com/2017/04/23/30000-person-festival-approved-in-crook-county), B; [OPB](https://www.opb.org/radio/article/oregon-eclipse-festival-symbiosis-big-summit-prairie), B). Driver: each scene brings its own trusted crowd and crew, so the whole gets instant critical mass.
  2. **A natural event as fixed date and shared focus** — "one of the only times thousands of people will be focusing on the same thing" (organiser, [Bend Bulletin](https://bendbulletin.com/2017/03/12/30000-people-expected-for-solar-eclipse-party)), B. ### Self-selected 1: The Borderland (Sweden/Denmark) — why: most advanced participatory-budgeting tooling in the burn world
- Kind: regional burn run by members, with open-source co-funding tools · Longevity: ~2012 to present · Holon: H4 village with H5 tools · Quadrant strongest: WE/ITS
- Mechanics:
  1. **Dreams: members allocate the art budget themselves** — every member gets a share of the grant pot to fund other people's project "buckets" (all-or-nothing). Unspent funds are redistributed in three rounds, introduced because only 40–60% used to be spent. 33% is earmarked for large or permanent art (Fund 33). Evidence: ~half of a ~9M SEK 2026 budget goes to Dream grants, "more than any other burn" ([Borderland](https://talk.theborderland.se/main)), C. Rules are on the [Dreams 2023 page](https://cobudget.com/borderland/dreams-2023/about), C. Participation stayed high as the event tripled in three years to 3,210 co-creators, and the tools spread to ≥5 other events ([CCC Camp 2019 talk](https://media.ccc.de/v/Camp2019-10204-participatory_art_event_tools_co-creation_and_silk_road_networks)), C. Driver: giving money to others' dreams creates ownership and an early web of ties before the event.
  2. **Realities + advice process** — responsibilities are mapped publicly (who holds what), and decisions go through an advice process, not a board (Fund 33 itself was created by an advice process) ([fund33](https://fund33.theborderland.se/)), C.
- Invariant hint: distribute decision power by distributing the budget.

### Self-selected 2: Elsewhere (Spain, Nobodies Collective) — why: newest (2026); a rebuild that answers Nowhere's failure mode with earned membership
- Kind: official regional burn run by a member-governed non-profit · Longevity: 1 edition, 2026 · Holon: H4 village · Quadrant strongest: WE
- Mechanics:
  1. **Membership earned by leading** — anyone who has led an area or a barrio for at least one year can apply to become a voting member. Members elect the board at a General Assembly, and board notes are published ([nobodies.team](https://nobodies.team/)), C. Driver: voice is granted to those who carried responsibility, which creates a path from participant to owner.
  2. **Every ticket holder volunteers; "VIP" = Voluntarily Inflated Price** — no free entry for anyone. Higher-priced tickets carry no perks and subsidise low-income tickets and art. There are also local-resident discounts (same source), C. Evidence of results: none yet (first edition completed, site "Left No Trace" per organiser), C.
- Invariant hint: status granted by peers for responsibility carried; generosity as a ticket tier.

### Self-selected 3: AfrikaBurn (South Africa) — why: largest regional, Global South, and it pushes grants outward into the "default world"
- Kind: official regional burn in the Tankwa Karoo · Longevity: 19 years, 2007 · Holon: H5 (event + national grant network) · Quadrant strongest: WE/ITS
- Mechanics:
  1. **Members set the art-grant pot; Spark Grants fund community projects outside the event** — 11 default-world projects in townships and towns are selected each year by a volunteer panel ([2025 Annual Report](https://www.afrikaburn.org/wp-content/uploads/2026/03/2025-ANNUAL-REPORT-AFRIKABURN-AU.pdf); [grants policy](https://www.afrikaburn.org/creative-grants-policy/)), C. Scale in 2025: 10,782 participants from 107 countries, 1,073 volunteers, 109 theme camps, ~174 mutant vehicles. It was the largest regional event in 2023 and 2025 per Burning Man Project ([Dispatch 2023](https://dispatch2023.burningman.org/regional-network)), C.
  2. **Mutant vehicles licensed only if they invite participation** — a "DMV" refuses anything that still looks like a car ([mutant vehicles](https://www.afrikaburn.org/event/participation/mutant-vehicles/)), C. Survey finding: volunteer *numbers* are falling while volunteer *hours* rise, so "fewer participants did more" ([2025 survey](https://www.afrikaburn.org/wp-content/uploads/2025/09/2025-AfrikaBurn-Survey-Results-2.pdf)), C. This is a warning sign of core burnout.
- Invariant hint: the temporary village exports its gift economy to permanent communities.

### Cross-cutting patterns in this class
- **Belonging via a small crew, not a ticket.** Theme camps, barrios, Fusion crews, Lampu's camp-only entry, and Midburn's pre-event art build all place each person in a group of roughly 5–50 inside the big whole. The strongest quantitative signal in the class is Burning Man's Census: belonging is the #1 reason to return (85.5%, A).
- **A short, copyable charter enables spread without a center.** The 10 Principles were written in 2004 *for* replication. They now carry ~100 official regionals (and unofficial ones like Lampu) with more participants than the mother event. Spread runs through volunteer local leads plus a light license.
- **Rhythm and rest.** Boom's biennial full moon, Glastonbury's fallow year, Fusion's 2027 pause, and eclipse-anchored one-offs (Ozora 1999, Oregon 2017) all use scarcity in time to renew desire and protect land and hosts.
- **Fair, identity-bound access protects the culture.** Fusion's lottery with face-value exchange, Glastonbury's photo tickets, Burning Man's low-income and directed sales, and Elsewhere's "Voluntarily Inflated Price" decouple belonging from speed or wealth.
- **Public stewardship scoreboards.** The BLM pass/fail inspection with a public MOOP map, Shambhala's drug-check screen, and Boom's eco reports all make the shared commons visible and attributable, so care becomes social. The evidence is strongest here (A grades: BLM inspection, peer-reviewed drug-checking study).
- **Failure mode: volunteer-core and production-cost exhaustion, not loss of values.** Nowhere ended (2026) after six years of decline. Envision paused because costs were unsustainable. AfrikaBurn sees fewer volunteers doing more hours. The newest designs (Elsewhere's earned membership, Borderland's distributed budget) try to answer this by moving ownership to those who carry the work.
