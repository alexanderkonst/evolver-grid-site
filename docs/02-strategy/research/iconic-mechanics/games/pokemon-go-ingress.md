## 2. Mechanics that demonstrably worked

### M1. Players put real places on the map (Ingress portals → Wayfarer)
- **In the source:** Ingress started with a few hundred thousand seeded places (historical markers, public art), then asked players to submit "cool places in their neighborhood"; guidelines: safe, public, culturally interesting; a medal for submissions. Later, high-level players review each other's nominations (Operation Portal Recon, then Wayfarer, 2019) [6][7].
- **Evidence (grade B, scale; C, motivation):** ~15M places submitted and ~5M approved by July 2016 [8]; by Nov 2019, 27M submitted, 26M reviewed by players, 9.4M live [10]; 17M player-chosen Wayspots in 2022 [11]. These became the PokéStops and Gyms of Pokémon GO at launch [8][12]. Niantic's CEO: "a lot of gamers really enjoyed that search for cool places… To a lot of early players, that was the game." [6]
- **Driver:** discovery and authorship: you put your town on the map and see others play at a place you chose; peer review makes a place feel earned.

### M2. Play that only works together, in person, in a short window
- **In the source:** group battles at Gyms (2017) need several players standing at the same place; monthly Community Days give a few hours when special creatures appear everywhere [13][14].
- **Evidence (grade B, observational):** CHI 2019 study (510 survey respondents, 25 interviews, a year of observation): the design "explicitly incentivized players to collaborate IRL for a collective goal in ad-hoc groups" [13]. Badalona, 2018: a Telegram bot coordinated almost 29,000 unique players in nearly 6,500 group battles [15]. Survey of 449 players (2023): 88% met someone through the game, 78% visited a new location, 35% went to a new business; game level predicted sense of belonging to the community [16]. Players rearranged work schedules for Community Day; organisers called it "the biggest driver of the community at large" [14].
- **Driver:** a shared goal no one can reach alone; a fixed time makes the meeting happen; idle minutes at the place turn into conversation [13][17].

### M3. Walking as game currency (eggs hatch by km)
- **Evidence (grade A, and a warning):** meta-analysis of 17 studies, 33,108 people: +1,446 steps a day [18]. But the BMJ study saw +955 steps in week 1 fall back to baseline by week 6 [19]; Microsoft's sensor study saw +1,473 steps a day for engaged players over 30 days [20]. The physical effect is real and fades with novelty.
- **Driver:** the act is a side effect of play, not the point of it.

## 5. Our version

**M1. Villagers put real places on the map.** A villager nominates a real place in San Pancho (a stall, a café table, a beach spot for the circle) with a photo and one line on why it belongs in the game. It goes live when two other villagers confirm it (Ingress went from a small team reviewing to players reviewing players, which is what let it scale [7]). Places follow §16.3 rule 5: a business's place stays private until its owner claims it. Gift for the nominator: their name on the place, a badge on the shelf. This makes the twin a thing the village builds, not one we import.

**M2. One thing only presence unlocks.** The full-moon Harvest (§16.3 rule 3) gets a fixed short window and one in-game thing you can only get by being there: e.g. the Harvest badge, or a reference that only people who met at the circle can leave each other (links to CS-2). No "hard boss for 20 people"; the shared goal is real (stalls shown, offers tried on each other).

## 6. Smallest testable version
- **M2 first (no code):** one Harvest circle in San Pancho, Sasha's call on when and who. Signal: how many people come who are not already in Sasha's circle, and how many places/stalls get named that night. Kill line: only people who would have come anyway.
- **M1 second (design):** a paper version at that circle: people name places on a printed map; count how many and whether others confirm them. Code only if it works.

## 7. Risks and anti-patterns
- **Novelty fades in ~6 weeks** [19]; what lasted in Pokémon GO was the social layer [9]. Don't count on the twin's novelty.
- **Private places and safety:** Niantic required safe, public places [6]; a home or a practitioner's private space never goes on the map without consent.
- **Extraction:** a map of the town built by players can feel like taking the town's data. §16.3 rule 5 holds: invitation, never extraction.
- **Pressure to show up:** group play created "pressure to play with other people" [9]. Gatherings stay optional; missing one costs nothing but the badge.
- **Wrong vocabulary:** no group-battle words (Sasha 2026-10-27).
