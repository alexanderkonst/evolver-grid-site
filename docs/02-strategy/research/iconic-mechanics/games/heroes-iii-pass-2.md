## 2. Mechanics that demonstrably worked

### H3-3. The editor in the box → players author for each other → the best becomes canon
- **In the source:** the same editor the designers used ships with the game. Players build scenario maps with their own win conditions and share them. Fan portals add a review gate (waiting room → "main collection, tested by moderators") [7]. One fan crew went further: an expansion (HotA) built over 15 years, consulted with online players on balance [2], whose content the rights holder adopted into official products (board game 2024–2026) and official stores (2026) [1][2].
- **Evidence it worked:** C for the volume (17,739 maps in one archive [5]; 802 of 944 maps passed moderators on one portal [7]); B for the canon path (Ubisoft collaboration 2024, Steam/GOG 2026 [1][2]). What it did NOT prove: that the editor caused the longevity; no data separates it from music, factions or hot seat.
- **Driver:** (a) **community co-creation raises the value of the base product**, grade B: Poretski & Arazy (CSCW 2017), 45 games: when firms engage the modding community, the community's mods add to sales of the base game [10]. (b) **IKEA effect**, grade A, and its boundary: labour leads to love only when the work is finished; destroyed or unfinished work loses the effect [12] (already in ready-player-one.md). The review gate is what makes "finished" visible.

### H3-4. Hot seat: one screen, everyone in the same room
- **In the source:** up to 8 players take turns at one computer; the day ends when all have moved [4].
- **Evidence it worked:** B that it exists in every edition and that the 2027 Remake re-invests in it [4]; B that internet was scarce where the iconic grew (2% RU, 7% PL in 1999) [pass 1]; C that it explains popularity (Android.com.pl names playing together at one computer among the reasons the game lasted in Poland [4]). What it did NOT prove: that it matters once everyone has a phone.
- **Driver:** *Judgment*, no study read here: play as an occasion to be together physically; the screen is the excuse, the room is the point.

### H3-5. The week as the unit of growth
- **In the source:** creatures grow on day 1 of each week [8]; in towns, what you did not recruit stays and accumulates (HotA extended this to map dwellings: "unrecruited creatures remain available ... indefinitely") [8]. Each week opens with "Astrologers proclaim the week of …": 25% chance of a +5 growth bonus for one creature, otherwise a joke animal (Aardvark, Badger…) with no effect [8]. Strategy guides plan around the week ("attacks timed for Day 7", vi.ki [3] related pages).
- **Evidence it worked:** C (a widely remembered phrase; still echoed in 2025 by Olden Era's "astrology points", heroes3wog search excerpt, not relied on).
- **Driver:** **fresh start effect**, grade A (large-n field data): at the start of a week, gym visits rose 33.4% (11,912 students, 5.27M person-days), commitment contracts on stickK rose 62.9% (66,062 contracts), Google searches for "diet" rose 14.4% (3,104 days) [11]. Placebo terms (laundry, gardening) showed no such pattern [11].

## 5. Our version

**H3-3.** A player who has really passed a step can write that step for the next player, in the same form Sasha uses for his own §23 quests ("I did it this way; here is your step; it is done when…"). It goes to a waiting shelf. When Sasha, or two players who passed the same step, have walked it and confirm it, it moves to the Quest Board ("main collection"). The author sees one number on their House: how many people walked their quest, each a real person who did the step. The best player quests can be taken into the official line, with the author's name on them, as HotA's Cove became official. That is rung 3: the right to change the game itself, earned by having changed other people's lives. No rankings, no contest winners.

**H3-4.** At a live circle (the full-moon Harvest in San Pancho, §16.3), one phone goes round. Each person reads their reveal sentence or tagline aloud and asks the Share-card question ("Do you see me this way too?", §14.12a); the others answer in the room. Nothing is scored; the badge is still earned by the person's own step.

**H3-5.** Monday opens the week: the player names one real step for the week (from their Journey or the reveal's micro-quest). Sunday's Save Point closes it (already decided). A step not done is not lost or punished; it is still there next Monday, as unrecruited creatures stay in the town. The village version, if Sasha wants it later: the ambassador names one shared step for the week ("everyone names a price this week") and the Harvest reads it. Nothing random.

## 6. Smallest testable version (H3-3 first; it carries the ladder)
- **What:** no code. After the next cohort passes the Reveal (or Activation), ask 3 players to write the step for the next player in their own words, in Sasha's §23 quest form. Sasha (or two peers who passed it) picks one. Show it to the next 10 players alongside the current A4 micro-quest.
- **Who:** 3 authors, 10 next players.
- **Signal:** share of the 10 who complete the player-written step vs the current A4 micro-quest; and whether authors come back to see how many walked it.
- **Kill line:** player-written step completes at or below the current micro-quest, and no author asks how theirs did → keep quests Sasha-only.
- **Depends on:** A4 (micro-quest) for comparison; a Quest Board slot that can show one extra quest; no new storage for the test.

H3-5 test: one cohort gets a Monday "name your one step this week" prompt + the Sunday Save Point; signal = share who report the step done by Sunday vs a cohort without the Monday prompt. Kill: no difference. Depends on: Save Point existing (§10; not built as far as this file knows).
H3-4 test: one live Harvest circle; signal = how many share their sentence aloud and whether anyone books another person's session within a week (§22.13 cross-booking). Kill: nobody shares.

## 7. Risks and anti-patterns
- **Mush (H3-3):** an open editor fills the world with half-made quests. The waiting shelf + review is mandatory; HotA's quality bar ("careful ... over simply adding functionality", [1]) is the model, not the 17,739-map archive.
- **Unfinished authorship kills the effect** (IKEA boundary [12]): a quest that sits in the waiting room for weeks leaves its author worse off. Review must be fast, or the rung opens only when reviewers exist.
- **Status game:** "N people walked your quest" can turn into a leaderboard. Owner-only on the House, like A5; never sorted across players.
- **Free labour:** HotA is non-commercial and its crew earned nothing [2]. If player quests become canon, the author's name stays on them; any money question goes to Sasha, never promised.
- **Hot seat as performance (H3-4):** reading aloud can pressure people. Always opt-in; §10 rejected peer witness as XP, so the room's answer is never recorded as score.
- **Week as guilt (H3-5):** a Monday prompt can become a nag. No reminders beyond the one prompt; carry-over, never "you failed last week".

## 8. Priority
Impact (1–3) × Confidence (1–3) × Ease (1–3); High ≥ 18, Medium 8–17, Low ≤ 7. Sasha sets the order.
- **H3-3 Authoring + review + canon path:** Impact 3 (Sasha's direction; content scales beyond Sasha) × Confidence 2 (driver B + A, mechanic C, canon path B) × Ease 2 (no-code test; the built version needs a new row and storage) = **12 → Medium**. Same score as rung 2 in ready-player-one.md; this pass adds the review-gate and canon-path evidence.
- **H3-5 The week:** Impact 2 × Confidence 2 (driver A, mechanic C) × Ease 2 (Save Point not built) = **8 → Medium (low end)**.
- **H3-4 Hot seat:** Impact 2 × Confidence 1 (C only; the B facts are about 1999 internet scarcity, which does not transfer) × Ease 2 = **4 → Low**. A format for the first live circle, not a build.
