# Per-game navigation dossier schema (shared by all research agents)

For each game (each title in a series when navigation differs; group titles that share it):

## <Game> (<years>, studio, lead designer)
1. **Navigation layers, outermost to innermost** (table: layer | what the player sees | what task it solves | how you enter/leave it). Typical layers: front-end/title menu; meta/world map/overworld/galaxy; region/town/hub screen; local playfield; HUD (minimap, compass, markers, waypoint); journal/quest log/codex; character/inventory screen; fast travel; save/return-home.
2. **Answers to the navigation task questions** (one line each, or "not addressed"):
   Q1 Where am I? Q2 Where can I go (what's open/closed and what opens it)? Q3 What should I do next, and why? Q4 How do I get there (distance, route)? Q5 What changed / what's new since I was last here? Q6 What have I done / where have I been (history, progress)? Q7 How do I return to safety/home/save? Q8 How do I see the whole (zoom between scales)? Q9 How do I switch modes (world ↔ menus ↔ dialogue) without getting lost? Q10 Anything else this game's navigation solves that the list misses (add a new Q).
3. **The 1–3 most successful navigation solutions**, each with: exact mechanics; evidence it worked, graded A (study/large-n data), B (hard observational numbers, sales, telemetry, GDC talk with data), C (designer interview, critical consensus, fan memory); the driver (why it works, named effect); source URL + date.
4. **Known failures / criticism** of its navigation (with sources).
5. **Designer intent quotes** about navigation/wayfinding/UI (verbatim, source URL).

Rules: web_search + web_fetch only, no browser. Cite every fact with URL. Facts vs *Judgment* marked. English. Be dense; no filler. ~600–1200 words per major game.

At the end of your file: **Cross-game patterns in this cluster** (5–10 bullets: candidate invariants and recurring best solutions).
