# Cluster: CONTEMPLATIVE & RITUAL TRADITIONS (out-of-domain controls)
Compiled 2026-11-27. Sources: web_search/web_fetch only. Evidence grades: traditions are mostly **C** (practitioner tradition and anecdote, not controlled data). The one exception is affect labeling (lab research, **A–B**), which sits next to noting practice but is a separate literature. *Judgment* = my inference, not the source's claim.

## 1. Ignatian discernment of spirits (consolation / desolation)
**Header.** Ignatius of Loyola, *Spiritual Exercises*, Rules for Discernment (§§313–336), c. 1520s–1548. **Core**: the closest tradition to using a felt movement as an inner etalon for decisions.

| layer | what happens | who/what | when |
|---|---|---|---|
| Frame | rules "for understanding... the different movements produced in the soul" | text | before the retreat |
| Noticing | label each movement as consolation or desolation | the person | daily, during prayer |
| Tracing | check where a thought leads: its "beginning, middle and end" | the person | afterwards |
| Disclosure | tell the movements to a director | the director | regularly |

- **Q1** Prayer and imagination; also "consolation without preceding cause", "without any previous sense or knowledge of any object" (2nd-week rule 2) [ccel II](https://www.ccel.org/ccel/ignatius/exercises/exercises.xix.ii.html).
- **Q2** Test it by where it leads, not how good it feels. The bad spirit can console as "an angel of light", so watch whether the train of thought "ends in something bad… or less good than what the soul had previously proposed" (rules 4–5) [ccel II](https://www.ccel.org/ccel/ignatius/exercises/exercises.xix.ii.html). The true touch comes "sweetly, lightly and gently, like a drop of water which enters into a sponge"; the false one "sharply and with noise" (rule 7) [ccel II].
- **Q3** Two named poles. Consolation is "every increase of hope, faith and charity… quieting it and giving it peace"; desolation is "darkness of soul, disturbance… as if separated" [ccel I](https://www.ccel.org/ccel/ignatius/exercises/exercises.xix.i.html).
- **Q4** Write it down: once a deception is "known and noted", the person can guard against it next time (rule 6) [ccel II].
- **Q5** Not addressed as re-evocation. Desolation is answered by staying with the resolution "of the preceding consolation" (rule 5) [ccel I].
- **Q6** Central. "In time of desolation never to make a change" (rule 5) [ccel I]. Decide from consolation, and hold the decision through desolation.
- **Q7** Not addressed. No scales, so evidence is C.
- **Q8** False consolation; the "afterglow" (see practice 2); secrecy. The enemy wants his suggestions "kept in secret" (rule 13) [ccel I].
- **Q9** A director or confessor. Telling the movement out loud breaks the deception (rule 13) [ccel I].
- **Q10** *Judgment*: Ignatius already separates a pleasant feeling from a true one. This is the Barnum problem, centuries early.

**Best practices**
1. Hold decisions made in consolation through desolation. Grade C. Driver: a mood should not overwrite a calibration made in a clear state. [ccel I]
2. Separate "the time itself of such actual consolation" from "the following, in which the soul remains warm", because in that afterglow one forms resolutions "not given immediately by God" (2nd-week rule 8). Grade C. [ccel II]

**Failures.** Feeling-as-proof can be counterfeited. Ignatius's own rule 4 concedes this [ccel II].

**Quote.** "the good Angel touches such soul sweetly, lightly and gently, like a drop of water which enters into a sponge" [ccel II](https://www.ccel.org/ccel/ignatius/exercises/exercises.xix.ii.html)

## 2. Zen kenshō and its verification (dokusan / sanzen)
**Header.** Rinzai and Sanbō Kyōdan koan training (Hakuin lineage; Yasutani, Kapleau, 20th c.). **Core / witness model**.

| layer | what happens | who/what | when |
|---|---|---|---|
| Practice | koan or zazen until a breakthrough | student | sesshin |
| Presentation | student presents the koan alone to the teacher | student → teacher | 2–4×/day |
| Testing | checking questions (sassho) | teacher | same interview |
| Ripening | further koans and practice | both | years |

- **Q1** Long concentrated practice on a koan. Kenshō is "the first breakthrough in kōan study" [Kenshō](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Kensh%C5%8D).
- **Q2** A teacher tests it. If the master rings the bell, "the student's understanding is not right" [Sanzen](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Sanzen). For the Mu koan there are "between 20 and 100 checking questions" [Koan](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Koan). A memorised answer "is, by all accounts, immediately obvious" because what is tested is "the state from which it comes" [buddhistresources](https://buddhistresources.com/meditation/concentration-practice/koan-practice/).
- **Q3** The student shows it rather than describes it: a gesture, a sound [buddhistresources]. The test applies it to a context: "How did you kenshō this?" [Hori](https://terebess.hu/zen/mesterek/Koan-Hori.pdf).
- **Q4** Public acknowledgment. Yasutani held "a post-sesshin ceremony of bowing in gratitude" for those who had kenshō [Kenshō].
- **Q5** It deepens. "You should have enlightenment experience again and again" (Sheng Yen) [Kenshō]. Kenshō ranges from "a beginner's shallow glimpse" to deep clarity, and it is "the same 'thing'… in different degrees" [Kenshō].
- **Q6** Expressed in daily life. Not framed as a tool for decisions.
- **Q7** Passing koans in sequence. This is the teacher's judgment, so evidence is C.
- **Q8** "Dining out" on one experience (see quote); mistaking makyō for kenshō (§6); answers being standardised [Koan].
- **Q9** The witness is essential: "Without this individual guidance we cannot say that our practice of zazen is authentic" (Yasutani) [lectures](https://www.themathesontrust.org/papers/fareasternreligions/yasutani-lectures.pdf). A counter-current exists: self-verification, mushi-dokugo [Kenshō].
- **Q10** One student's account: "the teacher recognizes something very old and dear in me, and I'm reminded of my true nature" [Lion's Roar](https://www.lionsroar.com/dharma-dictionary-dokusan).

**Best practices**
1. Test the insight by applying it, not by asking how it felt. Grade C. Driver: words can be copied, but performance in a new context is hard to fake. [Koan]
2. Treat the first glimpse as a beginning. Grade C. [Kenshō]

**Failures.** Fixed answer sets have been preserved in "Records of secret instruction", so the test can be gamed [Koan]. The verification depends wholly on the teacher.

**Quote.** "To take it as an ending, and to 'dine out' on such an experience… is one of the greatest tragedies of which I know" (Jiyu-Kennett) [Kenshō](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Kensh%C5%8D)

## 3. Vipassanā noting, and research on affect labeling
**Header.** Mahasi Sayadaw (1904–1982) noting method, and the Lieberman lab's affect-labeling research (2007–2018). **Control**: what naming a feeling does to it.

| layer | what happens | who/what | when |
|---|---|---|---|
| Anchor | note the abdomen: "rising, falling" | meditator | continuously |
| Note | silently name what is prominent ("hearing", "thinking") | meditator | as it happens |
| Interview | report to the teacher | teacher | regularly on retreat |
| Fade | labels drop away when mindfulness is strong | — | later |

- **Q1** Whatever is most prominent in experience [vipassananepal](https://vipassananepal.com/techniques/mental-noting).
- **Q2** Not addressed. Noting observes; it does not judge whether something is true.
- **Q3** The core of the method. The label must match the moment, "not before, not after"; use "short, plain words"; it is "like a finger pointing" [vipassanathai](https://vipassanathai.org/english/practice/phong-yup/).
- **Q4** Not addressed. The labels are "a temporary crutch" that "fall away automatically" [vipassanadhura](https://www.vipassanadhura.com/howto.htm).
- **Q5** Not addressed.
- **Q6** Indirect: noting is meant to lessen identification with experience [Mental noting](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Mental_noting).
- **Q7** Affect-labeling lab work shows: lower self-reported affect, less amygdala activity, more vlPFC activity (Lieberman 2007, [doi](https://doi.org/10.1111/j.1467-9280.2007.01916.x)), and lower skin-conductance response in exposure therapy (Kircanski 2012, [PMC](https://www.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/pmc/articles/PMC4721564)) [Affect labeling](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Affect_labeling). Grade A–B for labeling. For noting itself, a secondary source says "No clinical trial has isolated Mahāsī-style noting" [psychedex](https://psychedex.org/practices/noting-meditation), so grade C.
- **Q8** **Naming dampens the feeling.** Labeling reduces the felt and physiological response. It has been studied mostly on negative affect [Affect labeling]. In grief, repeated labeling "can turn into pushing the feeling away" [vipassanathai].
- **Q9** A teacher guides through regular interviews [vipassananepal].
- **Q10** People expect labeling to *raise* distress, and it lowers it. Self-generated labels show more delayed effects; labels picked from a set show immediate effects [Affect labeling].

**Best practices**
1. Name the feeling as it happens, in a plain word. Grade A–B for down-regulation. Driver: symbolic conversion and psychological distance. [Affect labeling]
2. *Judgment*: for a subtle *positive* feeling we want to keep, naming may weaken it. Name it lightly and after the moment, and keep attention on the feeling rather than on the word [vipassanathai].

**Failures.** Lab findings may not generalise to life; paradigms vary [Affect labeling].

**Quote.** "A label is like a finger pointing at something; what you want to know is the thing, not the finger." [vipassanathai](https://vipassanathai.org/english/practice/phong-yup/)

## 4. Initiation rites of passage (van Gennep's three phases)
**Header.** Arnold van Gennep, *Les rites de passage* (1909); Victor Turner on liminality (1967/69). **Control / closest to "a before and an after"**.

| layer | what happens | who/what | when |
|---|---|---|---|
| Separation | "detachment… from an earlier fixed point" (e.g. head shaved) | elders/community | start |
| Liminal | "betwixt and between", ambiguous status | initiand | middle |
| Incorporation | return to society with the new status; bonds and symbols | community | end |

All rows: [Rite of passage](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Rite_of_passage).
- **Q1** Ordeal, seclusion, symbolic acts that cut away the old self [Rite].
- **Q2** Not addressed. The change is social and conferred, not an inner truth test.
- **Q3** A new name, title or status (*Judgment*: implied by "new identity"; the source does not mention names).
- **Q4** Objects: "the sacred cord, the knot… the belt, the ring, the bracelet and the crown" [Rite].
- **Q5** Not addressed. Anchoring comes from the community and from the object.
- **Q6** The new status shapes behaviour: the person "re-enters society with one's new status" [Rite].
- **Q7** Outdoor programs that skip reincorporation show a weaker effect (Cushing 1998; Bell 2003) [Rite]. Grade C/B-.
- **Q8** Severe initiation raises liking for the group through cognitive dissonance (Aronson & Mills 1959). Initiation also produces conformity [Rite]. Grade B (lab).
- **Q9** The community witnesses and confers the status. Recognition is the missing piece in modern versions [Rite].
- **Q10** *Judgment*: "before and after" is the explicit shape of the rite. Without phase 3, being recognised by others, the line fades.

**Best practices**
1. Mark the after: the community recognises it, and the person receives a token to wear. Grade C. Driver: the change becomes social and visible. [Rite]
2. Never skip reincorporation. Grade C. [Rite]

**Failures.** Hazing; manufactured dissonance [Rite].

**Quote.** "I propose to call the rites of separation from a previous world, preliminal rites, those executed during the transitional stage liminal (or threshold) rites, and the ceremonies of incorporation into the new world postliminal rites." [Rite](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Rite_of_passage)

## 5. Quaker clearness committee (Parker Palmer)
**Header.** Quakers (practice dated to the 1660s), described by Parker J. Palmer. **Core witness model without authority**.

| layer | what happens | who | when |
|---|---|---|---|
| Prep | concise problem, background, hunches | focus person | before |
| Questions | only "honest, open questions" | 4–6 members | ~1h45 |
| Mirroring | reflect back the person's own words and body language, only with consent | members | last 15 min |
| Affirm | celebrate the person's strengths | clerk/members | last 5 min |
| After | notes handed over; confidentiality | — | months |

All rows: [Palmer PDF](https://couragerenewal.org/wp-content/uploads/2022/06/Parker-Palmer_Clearness-Committee.pdf).
- **Q1** Open questions, including intuitive ones: "What color is your present job…?" [Palmer].
- **Q2** The inner teacher has the authority, and outside answers have "absolutely no value". Questioners must not "get ahead of the focus person's language" [Palmer].
- **Q3** In the person's own words. Mirroring gives "a chance to say, 'Yes, that's me' or 'No, that's not'" [Palmer].
- **Q4** Members hand over their notes "to remember the questions… in the hours, days and months to come" [Palmer].
- **Q5** "A good clearness process does not end—it keeps working"; an unanswered question may keep working [Palmer].
- **Q6** Used for decisions (membership, marriage, concerns) [Clearness committee](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Clearness_committee).
- **Q7** Not addressed. Grade C.
- **Q8** "If we do not believe in the reality of inner wisdom, the Clearness Committee can become an opportunity for manipulation"; "not for extremely fragile people" [Palmer].
- **Q9** Witnesses without authority. They hold back advice, comfort and interpretation [Palmer].
- **Q10** The focus person has "total power to set the limits", including the right not to answer [Palmer].

**Best practices**
1. Ask only open questions, defined as questions whose answer the asker "could not possibly anticipate". Grade C. Driver: it removes outside projection, so the answer stays the person's own. [Palmer]
2. Mirror the person's own language, with consent, so they can confirm or reject it. Grade C. [Palmer]

**Failures.** "Done poorly, it can cause hurt and even harm" [Palmer].

**Quote.** "not to give advice or 'fix' people from the outside in but rather to help people remove the interference so that they can discover their own wisdom from the inside out." [Palmer](https://couragerenewal.org/wp-content/uploads/2022/06/Parker-Palmer_Clearness-Committee.pdf)

## 6. NEGATIVE: makyō (false experiences in Zen) + spiritual bypassing
**Header.** Yasutani Hakuun, in Kapleau's *Three Pillars of Zen* (1965); John Welwood (coined "spiritual bypass" in 1984); Fox, Cashwell & Picciotto (2017). **Negative**.

| layer | what happens | who | when |
|---|---|---|---|
| Arousal | intense practice produces visions and sensations | practitioner | often ~day 3 of a sesshin |
| Misreading | the vivid experience is taken for awakening | practitioner | — |
| Correction | the teacher says to ignore it and keep sitting | teacher | dokusan |
| Bypass | peak states used to avoid wounds | practitioner | long-term |

- **Q1** Concentration. Makyō "are certainly a sign that you are at a crucial point" [Yasutani](https://www.themathesontrust.org/papers/fareasternreligions/yasutani-lectures.pdf).
- **Q2** Vividness is not truth. Makyō "can be mistaken… as 'seeing the true nature'" [Makyō](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Maky%C5%8D). A teacher reports meeting "a student who had a powerful makyō… and believed it to be an awakening" [Dosho Port](https://www.patheos.com/blogs/wildfoxzen/2019/02/is-awakening-kensho-a-hallucination-makyo.html).
- **Q3** Content often traces to personal history: masks seen at a childhood festival, a flute the student once played [Yasutani]. *Judgment*: this resembles projection.
- **Q4** Explicitly *not* anchored: "no reason to feel elated" [Yasutani].
- **Q5** Even satori, if clung to, becomes makyō [Yasutani].
- **Q6** Bypass: using spirituality "to sidestep… unresolved emotional issues" [Spiritual bypass](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Spiritual_bypass).
- **Q7** No psychometrically sound measure existed before Fox et al. 2017 [Spiritual bypass]. Grade C.
- **Q8** Spiritual narcissism, "blind allegiance to charismatic teachers", the "compensatory identity" of being a good practitioner (Welwood) [Spiritual bypass].
- **Q9** The teacher works as a deflator, not an amplifier.
- **Q10** *Judgment*: the danger runs both ways. A guide can inflate a peak experience (cult dynamic), or a seeker can collect them.

**Best practices**
1. Do not grant meaning to vivid states. Judge by the life that follows. Grade C. [Yasutani]
2. Pair awakening with psychological inquiry (Welwood). Grade C. [Spiritual bypass]

**Failures.** Clinical treatment of bypass rests on anecdote [Spiritual bypass].

**Quote.** "To have a beautiful vision of a Buddha does not mean that you are any nearer becoming one yourself, any more than a dream of being a millionaire means that you are any richer when you awake." [Yasutani](https://www.themathesontrust.org/papers/fareasternreligions/yasutani-lectures.pdf)

## Cluster patterns
- **Intensity is not the test of truth; where it leads is.** Ignatius traces the course of thoughts, Zen tests insight by applying it, Yasutani says to ignore vivid makyō. Quiet and gentle counts as true; sharp and loud is suspect (Ignatius, 2nd-week rule 7).
- **A witness who does not assert is the standard verifier**: the Zen teacher who tests, the Ignatian director, the clearness committee that may only ask. None of them supplies the content. Palmer: an outside answer has "absolutely no value".
- **Separate the moment from the afterglow** (Ignatius, 2nd-week rule 8). Resolutions formed in the afterglow are less reliable.
- **Naming has a cost**: affect labeling lowers intensity (A–B evidence, mostly on negative affect). *Judgment*: for a subtle positive felt sense, name it lightly, in the person's own word, and right after the moment, not during it.
- **Calibrate in a clear state, hold through a dark one** (Ignatius, rule 5). This is the only explicit protocol in the cluster for using a feeling to decide.
- **The first glimpse is a beginning; it deepens with repetition** (Sheng Yen, Jiyu-Kennett). Clinging to one peak is the named failure.
- **The before/after line needs a social phase 3**: recognition plus a token object (van Gennep). Programs without reincorporation fade (Cushing, Bell).
- **Rule of the person's own language** (Palmer: do not get ahead of their words). Mirroring with a yes/no is a cheap test of resonance.
- **Manipulation risks named inside the traditions**: severe initiation inflates group loyalty (Aronson & Mills); a committee without belief in inner wisdom becomes manipulation; bypass breeds allegiance to charismatic teachers.
- **Evidence is grade C across the cluster**; the only controlled data is affect labeling and the initiation-dissonance lab studies.
