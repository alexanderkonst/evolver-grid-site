# Cluster: ANCHORING, STATE RECALL & SAVORING (2026-11-27)
Sources: web_search / web_fetch only. Evidence grades: A controlled/meta-analytic, B credible data/practitioner numbers, C anecdote/tradition. *Judgment* = my inference, not a source claim.

## 1. NLP anchoring (NEGATIVE exemplar)
**Header.** Bandler & Grinder, 1970s; review Witkowski 2010; systematic review Sturt et al. 2012. In as *negative*: the closest folk technique to "catch the feeling and fire it later", with the weakest evidence.

| layer | what happens | who/what | when |
|---|---|---|---|
| Elicit | person fully accesses an intense state | practitioner, by recall | session |
| Set | unique stimulus (touch, word, gesture) applied as state peaks, held ~20-30 s | practitioner | at peak |
| Test | break state, re-fire same touch | practitioner | minutes later |
| Use / collapse | fire in target context; or fire positive + negative anchors together | client / practitioner | later |
Procedure: https://www.nlpacademy.co.uk/articles/view/nlp_anchoring ; collapsing: https://pmc.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/articles/PMC11293289

- Q1 Recalled intense state, guided by practitioner (NLP Academy above).
- Q2 Not addressed; a felt state is taken as valid if it appears.
- Q3 Caught by an arbitrary sensory stimulus, not named.
- Q4 Claimed associative (Pavlov-like) link (https://pmc.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/articles/PMC11293289).
- Q5 Re-fire the stimulus; claim it returns. Untested in controlled work: a 2026 NLP review found no completed studies on anchoring and rates it "insufficient data" (https://zenodo.org/records/23168852/files/NLP_review_EN_v1.0.pdf?download=1).
- Q6 Fire in performance contexts (exams, sport, sales) (same).
- Q7 Practitioner observes "client goes into state" (NLP Academy).
- Q8 Expectation/suggestion not controlled (zenodo review); operator controls the trigger.
- Q9 Practitioner sets and owns the trigger.
- Q10 Witkowski: of 33 qualifying studies, 18.2% supportive, 54.5% non-supportive, 27.3% uncertain (https://doi.org/10.2478/v10059-010-0008-0). Sturt: 4 of 5 RCTs null, risk of bias high/uncertain (https://pmc.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/articles/PMC3481516).

**Best practice (the salvage).** Pairing a distinct cue with a peak state is plain associative learning; keep the cue, drop the claims. Grade C. Driver: conditioning plausibility only. *Judgment:* do not let an operator install or fire the user's trigger.
**Failures.** Above; no evidence base (Witkowski, Sturt).
**Quotes.** "Results contradict the claim of an empirical basis of NLP." (https://doi.org/10.2478/v10059-010-0008-0) / "There is little evidence that NLP interventions improve health-related outcomes." (https://pmc.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/articles/PMC3481516)

## 2. State- and context-dependent memory (CORE, mechanism)
**Header.** Goodwin et al. 1969 (alcohol); Godden & Baddeley 1975 (divers); Eich & Metcalfe 1989 (mood); Smith & Vela 2001 meta-analysis; Murre 2021 replication. *Core*: the science under "re-evoke by reinstating the conditions".

| layer | what happens | who/what | when |
|---|---|---|---|
| Encoding context | external place or internal state at learning | environment / body | encoding |
| Retrieval match | same context at test helps recall | environment | test |
| Mental reinstatement | imagining the original context substitutes | person | test |
| Outshining | stronger non-context cues erase the effect | material | test |

- Q1 Not addressed (memory for word lists, not felt states).
- Q2 Not addressed.
- Q3 Not addressed.
- Q4 Memory binds to place and internal state: divers recalled land-learned lists better on land and water-learned underwater (https://psycnet.apa.org/record/1978-22375-001); alcohol state-dependence for recall but not recognition (https://www.science.org/doi/10.1126/science.163.3873.1358).
- Q5 Reinstate context physically or mentally; thinking about the study context matched physical return (Smith 1979, cited https://www.larryjacoby.ca/images/Smith2018.pdf). Self-generated ("internal") events are more mood-dependent than perceived ones (https://www.columbia.edu/cu/psychology/metcalfe/PDFs/Eich%20Metcalfe%201989.pdf). Mood-dependence is stronger for positive moods and real-life events (https://doi.org/10.1080/02699938908408077).
- Q6 Not addressed.
- Q7 Recall counts; meta-analytic d = .28 overall (https://pubmed.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/11495110).
- Q8 Fragile: effect shrinks with associative encoding (d .33 vs .13) (https://people.tamu.edu/~stevesmith/SmithMemory/Smith&Vela2001.pdf); 2021 divers replication failed (https://pmc.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/articles/PMC8568063).
- Q9 Not addressed.
- Q10 *Judgment:* a moment of self-recognition is an internal, positive, self-generated event: the very class most tied to its state.

**Best practices.** (1) Mental reinstatement: before recalling, picture the original scene. Grade A (meta-analysis). Driver: context cues re-enter retrieval. https://link.springer.com/article/10.3758/BF03196157 (2) Record a few concrete context cues at encoding (place, posture, light) for later reinstatement. Grade B/*Judgment* built on (1).
**Failures.** Murre 2021 non-replication (above); outshining (Smith & Vela).
**Quotes.** "Lists learnt underwater were best recalled underwater, and vice versa." (https://open.unisq.edu.au/pluginfile.php/92783/mod_folder/content/0/Memory%20Reading%20Files/Godden%20%20Baddeley%2C%201975.pdf?forcedownload=1) / "environmental context-dependent memory effects are less likely to occur under conditions in which the immediate environment is likely to be suppressed." (https://pubmed.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/11495110)

## 3. Savoring (Fred Bryant) (CORE / CLOSEST TO TARGET for "catch and prolong")
**Header.** Bryant 2003 (SBI); Bryant & Veroff 2007; Smith et al. 2014 meta; Chen et al. 2026 RCT meta-analysis. *Core*: the only cluster member with an RCT-tested method for deliberately catching and extending a positive moment.

| layer | what happens | who/what | when |
|---|---|---|---|
| Anticipating | enjoy ahead | person | before |
| Savoring the moment | notice, attend, amplify | person | during |
| Reminiscing | replay later | person, often with others | after |
| Strategies | sharing, memory building, heightened perception, appreciating transience, avoiding dampening | person | any |
SBI: 24 items, 3 time domains (https://www.frontiersin.org/journals/psychology/articles/10.3389/fpsyg.2021.771698/full); strategies list (https://iaap-journals.onlinelibrary.wiley.com/doi/full/10.1111/aphw.70134).

- Q1 Any positive experience; the person turns attention to it (Chen 2026).
- Q2 Not addressed (the experience is taken as given).
- Q3 "Memory building" and "behavioral expression" strategies: mentally record, express (Chen 2026).
- Q4 Memory building; sharing with others (Chen 2026).
- Q5 Reminiscing as a trained skill (SBI domain).
- Q6 Not addressed directly.
- Q7 SBI; RCT outcomes. 20 RCTs, n = 4805, g = 0.51; active controls g = 0.30 vs passive 0.77; median 14 days; duration not a moderator (Chen 2026). Earlier chapter meta: "small, but significant" (https://doi.org/10.1002/9781118315927.ch3).
- Q8 Heterogeneity I² = 86.6%, 30% of RCTs high risk of bias, self-report outcomes (Chen 2026); clinical gains often not kept at follow-up (https://link.springer.com/article/10.1007/s41042-024-00182-1).
- Q9 Sharing with others is itself a strategy (Chen 2026).
- Q10 Dampening (talking oneself out of the good feeling) is a named enemy.

**Best practices.** (1) Brief daily savoring (notice + memory-build + share). Grade A (g ≈ 0.3 vs active control). Driver: deliberate attention and rehearsal. Chen 2026 URL above. (2) Name and block dampening thoughts. Grade B (strategy taxonomy, Bryant & Veroff via Chen).
**Failures.** Effects shrink vs active controls; short follow-ups (Chen 2026, Cullen 2024).
**Quotes.** "Savoring refers to the process by which individuals consciously notice, deeply experience, prolong, and amplify positive experiences." (Chen 2026, https://iaap-journals.onlinelibrary.wiley.com/doi/full/10.1111/aphw.70134)

## 4. Peak experiences (Maslow) + quiet ego (CONTROL / caution)
**Header.** Maslow 1962/1964; Peak Scale (Mathes 1982); Privette EQ (1987); Wayment, Bauer & Sylaska Quiet Ego Scale 2014. In as *control*: a big-feeling tradition whose life-changing claims mostly don't hold, plus a measured counterweight (quiet ego).

| layer | what happens | who/what | when |
|---|---|---|---|
| Peak | rapture, wholeness, self-forgetting | person, often unbidden | rare moment |
| Interpretation | felt as self-validating, grace | person | after |
| After-effects | claimed value change | person | later |
| Quiet-ego stance | detached awareness, inclusive identity, perspective taking, growth | person | ongoing |

- Q1 Unbidden: love, nature, art, insight, creation (https://people.bu.edu/wwildman/relexp/reviews/review_maslow01.htm).
- Q2 Not addressed; peaks are "self validating, self justifying" (Maslow 1962 p.68, cited https://ceskoslovenskapsychologie.cz/index.php/csps/article/download/14/81/1619). *Judgment:* self-validation is exactly what a Barnum check must not rely on.
- Q3 Retrospective narrative; Peak Scale items (https://ppc.sas.upenn.edu/sites/default/files/transcendentexper.pdf).
- Q4 Memory only.
- Q5 Not addressed.
- Q6 Peakers report more meaning and Being-values (https://doi.org/10.1177/002216787801800307; https://doi.org/10.1177/0022167882223011).
- Q7 Peak Scale, EQ (https://journals.sagepub.com/doi/abs/10.2466/pms.1987.65.1.315); QES 14 items, 4 facets (https://link.springer.com/article/10.1007/s10902-014-9546-z).
- Q8 Maslow's method criticized as unsystematic (https://www.ebsco.com/research-starters/health-and-medicine/peak-experiences); self-actualization link "never definitively established" (https://hirr.hartfordinternational.edu/articles/4494/); most people's best moment fell short of a peak, change rarely initiated by peaks (https://onlinelibrary.wiley.com/doi/10.1002/1097-4679(199009)46:5%3C565::AID-JCLP2270460504%3E3.0.CO;2-3).
- Q9 Not addressed.
- Q10 Quiet ego = a self that is non-defensive, balanced (QES URL).

**Best practice.** Pair any intense self-moment with detached awareness (observe it, don't inflate it). Grade B (validated scale, correlational). Driver: non-defensive attention. QES URL.
**Failures.** As Q8.
**Quotes.** Peak experience as "moments of highest happiness and fulfillment" (Maslow, via https://people.bu.edu/wwildman/relexp/reviews/review_maslow01.htm).

## 5. Self-defining memories (Jefferson Singer) (CLOSEST TO TARGET for "etalon")
**Header.** Singer & Salovey 1993; Moffitt & Singer 1994; Blagov & Singer 2004; Singer 2013. *Closest to target*: identity-bearing memories that serve as inner reference for goals.

| layer | what happens | who/what | when |
|---|---|---|---|
| Event | vivid, affect-charged episode | life | once |
| Rehearsal | recalled repeatedly | person | years |
| Linkage | joins similar memories, enduring concern | memory system | over time |
| Meaning-making | stepping back to a life lesson | person / therapist | on recall |
| Script | repeated sequences become templates | personality | long term |

- Q1 Life events touching enduring concerns (https://onlinelibrary.wiley.com/doi/full/10.1111/jopy.12005).
- Q2 Five criteria: vivid, affectively intense, repetitively recalled, linked, tied to enduring concern (http://self-definingmemories.com/Singer___Blagov__2004_-_The_Integrative_Function_of_Narrative_Processing.pdf). *Judgment:* "linked to many similar memories" is a usable truth-test against one-off flattery.
- Q3 Narrative, the memory "you would tell someone else... to understand you" (http://self-definingmemories.com/Classification_System___Scoring_Manual_for_SDMs.pdf).
- Q4 Rehearsal; mean vividness ≥4.5 of 6 (Singer & Blagov 2004 URL).
- Q5 Recall still moves the person "at the very moment of recollection" (same).
- Q6 Serve as reminders for goals and decisions (https://selfdefiningmemories.com/Singer___Blagov__2004_-_SDMs__Narrative_Identity____Psychotherapy.pdf); support long-term goal pursuit and self-coherence (https://pmc.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/articles/PMC6971892).
- Q7 Scoring manual: specificity, meaning, affect (http://www.self-definingmemories.com/Four_Dimensions_of_SDM_s_-_Blagov___Singer__2004.pdf).
- Q8 Not addressed beyond defensiveness findings (Blagov & Singer 2004).
- Q9 Told to another person; therapist hears it (selfdefiningmemories.com).
- Q10 The memory must be ≥1 year old (scoring manual): a fresh moment is a candidate, not yet an SDM.

**Best practices.** (1) Elicit with Singer's prompt and score specificity + meaning (a lesson stated). Grade B (validated coding). Driver: integration into narrative identity. (2) Link the new moment to earlier similar ones. Grade B/*Judgment*.
**Failures.** Correlational; few interventions.
**Quotes.** "Self-defining memories are those memories that help you to define most clearly how you see yourself and that help explain who you are to another person." (http://www.selfdefiningmemories.com/)

## 6. Peak-end rule (Kahneman) (CORE, how the end shapes memory)
**Header.** Fredrickson & Kahneman 1993; Kahneman et al. 1993; Redelmeier & Kahneman 1996; Redelmeier et al. 2003; Kemp et al. 2008. *Core*: designs the end of the moment.

| layer | what happens | who/what | when |
|---|---|---|---|
| Experience | moment-by-moment affect | person | during |
| Snapshot | peak and end averaged; duration neglected | memory | after |
| Choice | future decisions use the snapshot | person | later |

- Q1 Not addressed.
- Q2 Not addressed.
- Q3 Retrospective global rating.
- Q4 Memory compresses to peak + end; colonoscopy recall correlated with peak and last 3 min (https://pubmed.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/8857625/).
- Q5 Not addressed.
- Q6 Majority chose to repeat the longer cold-water trial with gentler end (https://journals.sagepub.com/doi/10.1111/j.1467-9280.1993.tb00589.x); 69% of 32 (secondary: https://psychology.com/biases/peak-end-rule).
- Q7 Real-time vs remembered ratings; r = .67 (https://www.anderson.ucla.edu/faculty/keith.chen/negot.%20papers/Kahneman_ExperiencedUtility00.pdf). RCT n = 682 with gentler colonoscopy end: less painful memory (https://pubmed.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/12855328/). Holds for pleasant goods too (https://link.springer.com/article/10.3758/PBR.15.1.96).
- Q8 Weak for long, mixed experiences: vacation test, "not an outstandingly good predictor" (https://psycnet.apa.org/record/2008-03226-013; https://pmc.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/articles/PMC6668632). Ethics of engineering ends (https://pmc.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/articles/PMC12392854/).
- Q9 Not addressed.
- Q10 *Judgment:* a reveal is short and single-peaked, the conditions where the rule is strongest.

**Best practice.** End the moment on a calm, positive step, not a cliff or a sales screen. Grade A (RCT). Driver: end dominates the snapshot. Redelmeier 2003 URL.
**Failures.** Kemp 2008; heterogeneity.
**Quotes.** "such evaluations are often dominated by the discomfort at the worst and at the final moments of episodes." (https://journals.sagepub.com/doi/10.1111/j.1467-9280.1993.tb00589.x)

## 7. Proust effect / odor-evoked autobiographical memory (Rachel Herz)
**Header.** Herz & Schooler 2002; Herz 2004 (fMRI); Larsson et al. 2007; Proust meta-analysis 2026. *Core* for re-evocation by a sensory key.

| layer | what happens | who/what | when |
|---|---|---|---|
| Pairing | odor present during meaningful event | life | once |
| Cue | odor returns | environment | later |
| Retrieval | emotional, "brought back" memory | amygdala-hippocampus | on smell |

- Q1 Odor tied to a personal event (https://pubmed.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/14670575/).
- Q2 Not addressed.
- Q3 Naming hurts: adding the odor's name lowered pleasantness and the feeling of being brought back (https://link.springer.com/article/10.3758/BF03193499).
- Q4 Odor-memory pairing; olfaction feeds amygdala/hippocampus directly (https://pmc.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/articles/PMC5039451).
- Q5 Odor memories more emotional and evocative, not more vivid or specific (https://academic.oup.com/chemse/article/29/3/217/321318).
- Q6 Proposed therapeutic use to lift mood and cut stress (Herz 2016, PMC5039451). Grade C/B.
- Q7 Meta, 18 studies: advantage for emotional intensity (g = 0.23), pleasantness (0.20), evocativeness, age; none for vividness, specificity, rarity (https://link.springer.com/article/10.3758/s13423-026-03025-z).
- Q8 Small samples inflate effects (same meta); fMRI n = 5 (Herz 2004).
- Q9 Not addressed.
- Q10 Personally significant cue beats a comparable control odor (Herz 2004).

**Best practice.** Use a personal sensory key (a scent or sound present at the moment) for re-evoking feeling, not detail. Grade A for feeling (meta, small g), C for deliberate use. Driver: limbic access.
**Failures.** "selective rather than universal" (2026 meta).
**Quotes.** "fMRI analyses indicated significantly greater activation in the amygdala and hippocampal regions during recall to the personally significant odor than any other cue" (https://pubmed.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/14670575/) / "the olfactory advantage is selective rather than universal" (https://link.springer.com/article/10.3758/s13423-026-03025-z)

## Cluster patterns
- Re-evocation works through **reinstating the encoding context**, and mental reinstatement can stand in for physical (Smith & Vela; Smith 1979). An operator-installed trigger (NLP) adds nothing proven.
- The felt recognition moment is internal, positive and self-generated: the class of event most bound to its state (Eich & Metcalfe; Ucros). *Judgment:* expect it to fade unless context cues are captured.
- Sensory keys re-evoke **feeling, not detail** (Proust meta). Words can **dampen**: naming the odor lowered the "brought back" feeling (Larsson 2007). *Judgment:* capture the body place / image first, the word second.
- The only RCT-proven catch-and-prolong method is **savoring** (g ≈ 0.3 vs active control): notice, memory-build, share, block dampening.
- **Ends shape memory** for short, single-peak episodes (RCT n = 682); weak for long mixed ones. A reveal fits the strong case.
- **Truth-test lives in linkage and recurrence**, not intensity: Singer's SDMs are linked to many similar memories; Maslow's peaks are "self-validating" and rarely change lives. Intensity alone is no proof.
- A **witness** enters as sharing (savoring) and telling another (SDM), never as owner of the trigger.
- Evidence gradient: savoring, context reinstatement, peak-end = A; SDM, quiet ego, odor-memory use = B; peak experience life-change and NLP anchoring = C / negative.
- Classic findings are fragile (divers non-replication; small-sample odor effects). Build with modest expectations.
