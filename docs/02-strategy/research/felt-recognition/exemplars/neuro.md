## 1. Somatic marker hypothesis (Damasio, Bechara; Iowa Gambling Task) + critiques
**Header.** Antonio & Hanna Damasio, Antoine Bechara, Daniel Tranel, 1991–2005; critiques by Maia & McClelland 2004 and Dunn, Dalgleish & Lawrence 2006. Why in: **core**. It is the classic claim that a body signal ("gut feeling") guides choice before conscious knowledge, i.e. a felt etalon used for decisions.

| layer | what happens | who/what | when |
|---|---|---|---|
| Theory | VMPFC links situations to body states that bias choice | Damasio | 1991–1994 (https://www.mrc-cbu.cam.ac.uk/personal/tim.dalgleish/dunnsmhreview.pdf) |
| Test | IGT card game; anticipatory skin-conductance (aSCR) before risky decks | Bechara et al. | 1996–97 (https://www.science.org/doi/10.1126/science.275.5304.1293) |
| Critique | structured questions show explicit knowledge arrives early | Maia & McClelland | 2004 (https://www.pnas.org/doi/10.1073/pnas.0406666101) |
| Review | evidence correlational, replication thin | Dunn et al. | 2006 (https://pubmed.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/16197997) |

**Seed questions.**
Q1 Evoked by repeated reward/punishment; the body "marks" bad options (https://www.sciencedirect.com/science/article/abs/pii/S0149763405001053). Q2 Not addressed; aSCR cannot tell positive from negative arousal (https://sciencedirect.com/science/article/pii/S0028393206000248). Q3 Not named: the marker is claimed to work non-consciously. Q4 Stored as learned VMPFC association. Q5 Re-evoked automatically on facing the option again. Q6 Core claim: body signal steers choice "before knowing" (https://www.science.org/doi/10.1126/science.275.5304.1293). Q7 IGT deck choice + aSCR; a 2019 meta-analysis found an aSCR effect but flags likely publication bias (https://pmc.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/articles/PMC6785590/). Q8 Over-claiming "the body knows" when explicit knowledge may drive choice (https://www.pnas.org/doi/10.1073/pnas.0406666101). Q9 None. Q10 *Judgment:* the felt signal and the conscious answer travel together; you cannot assume the feeling came first.

**Best practices.** (1) Pair any felt signal with a structured verbal probe, since open questions under-detect what people know (Maia & McClelland) — B, https://www.pnas.org/doi/10.1073/pnas.0406666101. Driver: sensitive measurement.

**Failures.** "Correlational only… no causal" link of peripheral feedback to performance; key aSCR replications in healthy people lacking (https://users.phhp.ufl.edu/rbauer/cognitive/Articles/somatic_marker_hypothesis.pdf; https://link.springer.com/article/10.3758/s13415-019-00744-w).

**Quotes.** "participants report knowledge of the advantageous strategy more reliably than they behave advantageously." — Maia & McClelland 2004, https://www.pnas.org/doi/10.1073/pnas.0406666101

## 2. Interoception (Craig; Garfinkel's accuracy / sensibility / awareness; heartbeat detection)
**Header.** A.D. (Bud) Craig 2002–2009; Sarah Garfinkel, Anil Seth, Hugo Critchley 2015. Why in: **core**. Defines the body-sensing channel a "felt sense" rides on, and splits *feeling confident about it* from *being right about it*.

| layer | what happens | who/what | when |
|---|---|---|---|
| Pathway | lamina I → posterior insula → anterior insula re-represents body state as feeling | Craig | 2009 (https://www.nature.com/articles/nrn2555) |
| Measure | heartbeat tracking / discrimination tasks | lab | 1981→ (https://royalsocietypublishing.org/rstb/article/371/1708/20160015/42145/Towards-a-psychophysics-of-interoceptive-processes) |
| Model | accuracy (objective), sensibility (self-report), awareness (confidence–accuracy fit) | Garfinkel et al. | 2015 (https://www.sciencedirect.com/science/article/pii/S0301051114002294) |

**Seed questions.**
Q1 A body cue attended to (heartbeat). Q2 Key finding: in N=80 the three dimensions were "distinct and dissociable"; accuracy "only partly predicted" by sensibility/awareness (https://psycnet.apa.org/record/2015-04389-010). Q3 Body location: some count heartbeats at a specific spot, some diffusely, some estimate (https://www.uclep.be/labs/ARL/publications/Desmedt%20et%20al._2018.pdf). Q4 Not addressed. Q5 Facets fluctuate moment to moment (EMA pilot) (https://www.mdpi.com/1660-4601/18/9/4893). Q6 Craig ties anterior insula to decision making and sudden insight (https://www.nature.com/articles/nrn2555). Q7 Heartbeat scores + confidence ratings + questionnaires. Q8 Confidence ≠ accuracy: people can feel sure of body signals they don't detect. Q9 Not addressed. Q10 Craig frames the AIC as a candidate correlate of awareness itself (https://www.nature.com/articles/nrn2555).

**Best practices.** (1) Always measure *both* the felt confidence and an objective check; the fit (awareness) is the useful number — A (validated in N=80; partial support in https://www.sciencedirect.com/science/article/abs/pii/S0167876016307140). Driver: metacognition is separable from sensing.

**Failures.** Heartbeat counting is contaminated by beliefs and time estimation (see §6).

**Quotes.** "I regard awareness as knowing that one exists (the feeling that 'I am')" — Craig 2009, https://repositorio.unab.cl/bitstreams/9b68d4ee-84b7-43be-ac03-126e79567185/download

## 3. Neuroscience of insight "Aha!" (Kounios & Beeman; insight memory advantage, Danek)
**Header.** Mark Jung-Beeman, John Kounios 2004–2014; Amory Danek, Jennifer Wiley 2013–2020. Why in: **closest to target**. A sudden "click" of recognition, with a felt quality, that is measurably remembered better.

| layer | what happens | who/what | when |
|---|---|---|---|
| Prep | alpha burst over right parietal-occipital ~1.4 s before (inward attention) | Jung-Beeman et al. | 2004 (https://journals.plos.org/plosbiology/article?id=10.1371%2Fjournal.pbio.0020097) |
| Moment | 40 Hz gamma burst, right anterior superior temporal gyrus, ~0.3 s before response | same | 2004 |
| Felt | surprise, pleasure, certainty, suddenness | Danek & Wiley | 2017 (https://pmc.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/articles/PMC5247466) |
| Memory | Aha solutions recalled better after 14 days | Danek et al. | 2013 (https://pubmed.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/23007629) |

**Seed questions.**
Q1 A solution "pops" after restructuring; also when a solution is *shown* after failed attempts (https://link.springer.com/article/10.1007/s00426-015-0697-2). Q2 False Ahas exist but are rated weaker on surprise, pleasure, confidence (https://pmc.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/articles/PMC9166882). Q3 Self-report: "Aha or not" plus dimension ratings 0–100 (https://www.frontiersin.org/journals/psychology/articles/10.3389/fpsyg.2016.02077/pdf). Q4 Memory itself: 64.4% vs 52.4% recall at 14 days (https://pubmed.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/23007629). Q5 Global Aha rating rose between two assessments in one study (http://www.amorydanek.de/wp-content/uploads/2022/01/Danek-Kizilirmak-2021.pdf). Q6 Not addressed directly. Q7 EEG/fMRI, recall rates, rating scales. Q8 Aha can be primed onto wrong answers (https://link.springer.com/article/10.3758/s13423-021-02049-x). Q9 Magic-trick video as the "mirror"; a shown solution can still produce Aha. Q10 The memory advantage comes from correctness + certainty + pleasure, not restructuring itself (https://www.sciencedirect.com/science/article/abs/pii/S0010027720302304).

**Best practices.** (1) Ask for the felt dimensions separately (surprise, pleasure, certainty) right at the moment; weak profiles flag false insight — B/A (https://pmc.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/articles/PMC5247466). (2) Let the person generate or nearly generate before revealing; generation and Aha add up for memory — A (https://link.springer.com/article/10.1007/s00426-015-0697-2).

**Failures.** Aha does not guarantee truth; it can be manipulated and shows "strong-but-wrong" errors (https://psychweb.psych.unimelb.edu.au/resources/publications/Webb2019%20%5BMoment%20of%20Perceived%20Truth%5D.pdf).

**Quotes.** "insight is the culmination of a series of brain states and processes operating at different time scales." — Kounios & Beeman 2009, https://journals.sagepub.com/doi/10.1111/j.1467-8721.2009.01638.x

## 4. Memory reconsolidation (Nader 2000; human work: Kindt, Schiller, Sevenster)
**Header.** Karim Nader, Glenn Schafe, Joseph LeDoux 2000 (rats); Merel Kindt 2009, Daniela Schiller 2010, Sevenster 2013 (humans). Why in: **core**. Explains why each re-evocation of a felt memory is also a chance to change it.

| layer | what happens | who/what | when |
|---|---|---|---|
| Reactivate | recall returns a consolidated memory to a labile state | Nader et al. | 2000 (https://www.nature.com/articles/35021052) |
| Window | blocker works within hours; at 6 h no effect | same | 2000 |
| Gate | needs prediction error (something new) to destabilize | Sevenster et al. | 2013 (https://www.science.org/doi/10.1126/science.1231357) |
| Update | propranolol or extinction in the window weakens fear response | Kindt 2009; Schiller 2010 | (https://www.nature.com/articles/nn.2271; https://www.nature.com/articles/nature08637) |

**Seed questions.**
Q1 A reminder cue. Q2 Not addressed. Q3 Not addressed. Q4 Re-storage needs de novo protein synthesis (https://www.nature.com/articles/35021052). Q5 Recall reopens the trace; only *directly* reactivated memories become labile (https://www.pnas.org/doi/10.1073/pnas.0507168103); retrieval alone is not enough without something to learn (https://www.sciencedirect.com/science/article/pii/S1074742712000263). Q6 Not addressed. Q7 Startle, skin conductance, expectancy. Propranolol erased startle fear but left declarative memory intact (https://sciencedirect.com/science/article/pii/S1074742710000626). Q8 Each re-evocation can distort or overwrite; strong memories resist (https://www.sciencedirect.com/science/article/pii/S0361923022002593). Q9 Not addressed. Q10 Emotional and factual layers of one memory separate.

**Best practices.** (1) Re-evoke with a small new element (prediction error) if updating is wanted; replay without novelty if stability is wanted — A (https://www.science.org/doi/10.1126/science.1231357). *Judgment:* for an etalon this cuts both ways.

**Failures.** Schiller 2010 had misreported exclusions; a registered replication found no persistent attenuation (https://pmc.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/articles/PMC7115860/; https://pmc.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/articles/PMC7115861). Another lab failed with multiple indices (https://sciencedirect.com/science/article/pii/S030105111100250X).

**Quotes.** "consolidated fear memories, when reactivated, return to a labile state that requires de novo protein synthesis for reconsolidation." — Nader et al. 2000, https://www.nature.com/articles/35021052

## 5. Emotional granularity / constructed emotion (Barrett) + affect labeling (Lieberman 2007)
**Header.** Lisa Feldman Barrett 2006–2017; Kashdan, Barrett & McKnight 2015; Matthew Lieberman et al. 2007, 2011. Why in: **core**. Naming a feeling precisely is not neutral reporting: it shapes the feeling.

| layer | what happens | who/what | when |
|---|---|---|---|
| Theory | brain predicts body states and categorizes them with concepts | Barrett | 2017 (https://academic.oup.com/scan/article/12/1/1/2823712) |
| Skill | granularity: fine-grained words for feelings | Kashdan, Barrett, McKnight | 2015 (https://journals.sagepub.com/doi/10.1177/0963721414550708) |
| Label act | choosing an affect word lowers amygdala, raises RVLPFC | Lieberman et al. | 2007 (https://sanlab.psych.ucla.edu/wp-content/uploads/sites/31/2016/03/Lieberman_2007_PsychScience.pdf) |
| Experience | labeling lowers self-reported distress *and* pleasure | Lieberman et al. | 2011 (https://psycnet.apa.org/doi/10.1037/a0023503) |

**Seed questions.**
Q1 Concepts make the felt state; it is not triggered then read (https://pmc.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/articles/PMC5390700). Q2 Not addressed. Q3 Precise words; high differentiators used ~30% more regulation strategies (https://c3po.media.mit.edu/wp-content/uploads/sites/45/2016/01/Kashdan-Barrett-Mcknight_2015.pdf). Q4 Vocabulary as the store. Q5 Not addressed. Q6 Granular people are less likely to binge drink, aggress or self-injure under distress (https://journals.sagepub.com/doi/10.1177/0963721414550708). Q7 Momentary diary data preferred over trait questionnaires, which reflect self-beliefs (https://www.affective-science.org/pubs/2015/kashdan-et-all-unpacking-emotion-differentiation-2015.pdf). Q8 **Labeling a positive picture reduced self-reported pleasure** (https://collaborate.princeton.edu/en/publications/subjective-responses-to-emotional-stimuli-during-labeling-reappra/). Q9 Not addressed. Q10 People don't believe labeling works, even after it did (https://psycnet.apa.org/doi/10.1037/a0023503).

**Best practices.** (1) Expand vocabulary; trainings target emotion words — B (https://affective-science.org/wp-content/uploads/2024/04/kashdan-et-all-unpacking-emotion-differentiation-2015.pdf). (2) Affect labeling as implicit regulation — A for negative affect (meta-analysis cited at https://journals.sagepub.com/doi/full/10.1177/1754073917742706).

**Failures.** Small fMRI samples; mechanism inferred, not proven (https://www.thewhyweproject.com/studies/lieberman-putting-feelings-into-words-affect-labeling-disrupts-amygdala-activity). *Judgment:* the dampening applies to the positive "click" too, so naming it too early may flatten it.

**Quotes.** "Emotions are constructions of the world, not reactions to it." — Barrett 2017, https://www.affective-science.org/pubs/2017/barrett-tce-scan-2017.pdf

## 6. Negative / critique: power pose replication and the heartbeat-counting problem
**Header.** Carney, Cuddy & Yap 2010; Ranehill et al. 2015; Carney 2016; Zamariola et al. 2018; Desmedt et al. 2018. Why in: **negative**. Shows how felt-sense claims inflate.

| layer | what happens | who/what | when |
|---|---|---|---|
| Claim | posture changes hormones and risk | Carney et al. | 2010 |
| Replication | N=200: felt power replicated; hormones and behaviour not | Ranehill et al. | 2015 (https://faculty.haas.berkeley.edu/dana_carney/ranehill.pdf) |
| Retraction of belief | lead author disavows | Carney | 2016 (https://faculty.haas.berkeley.edu/dana_carney/pdf_my%20position%20on%20power%20poses.pdf) |
| Interoception | heartbeat counting scores mostly reflect under-counting and beliefs | Zamariola; Desmedt | 2018 (https://www.sciencedirect.com/science/article/abs/pii/S0301051118303739) |

**Seed questions.**
Q1 Instructions/expectation can produce the feeling. Q2 **Felt change survives where objective change does not** (Ranehill). Q3 Not addressed. Q4 Not addressed. Q5 Not addressed. Q6 Behaviour unchanged despite feeling. Q7 N=572: reported vs actual heartbeats r=.16; >95% under-reports (https://sites.uclouvain.be/FOOD4GUT/wp-content/uploads/2018/10/Zamariola-et-al.-Biological-Psychology.-2018..pdf). Telling people to report only felt beats cut scores ~50% (https://www.uclep.be/labs/ARL/publications/Desmedt%20et%20al._2018.pdf). Q8 Expectation, beliefs, p-hacking. Q9 Experimenter instruction shapes report. Q10 Dispute continues: commentaries defend the task (https://www.sciencedirect.com/science/article/abs/pii/S0301051120300302); a 2024 review still calls validity concerns 30 years old (https://journals.sagepub.com/doi/10.1177/17470218231172725).

**Best practices.** (1) Pre-register and separate self-report from behaviour — A (https://www.tandfonline.com/doi/full/10.1080/23743603.2017.1342447).

**Failures.** This exemplar is itself the failure.

**Quotes.** "I do not believe that 'power pose' effects are real." — Carney 2016, https://faculty.haas.berkeley.edu/dana_carney/pdf_my%20position%20on%20power%20poses.pdf

## Cluster patterns
- **Feeling ≠ accuracy.** Across interoception (Garfinkel), Aha (Danek & Wiley) and power posing (Ranehill), the felt signal is real but dissociates from objective correctness. *Judgment:* a felt etalon needs an outside check, not only intensity.
- **Profile beats intensity.** True insights carry a fuller profile (surprise + pleasure + certainty) than false ones; measuring dimensions separately is the best available Barnum filter in this cluster.
- **Recognition helps memory.** Aha moments are recalled better at 14 days (64% vs 52%), driven by correctness, certainty and pleasure. *Judgment:* a real click is a natural anchor.
- **Naming changes the feeling.** Affect labeling dampens both negative and positive affect; granularity helps regulation. *Judgment:* catch first (body place, image), name precisely after, or the subtle click may flatten.
- **Re-evocation is rewriting.** Reconsolidation: each recall reopens the trace, and novelty at recall gates updating. Re-evoking the etalon can deepen it or drift it.
- **Body-first claims are fragile.** SMH and heartbeat counting both over-claimed; explicit knowledge and beliefs often explain what looked like pure body wisdom.
- **Measure with two channels.** Strongest designs pair self-report with behaviour or physiology, and use momentary rather than trait reports.
- **Witness shapes the signal.** Instructions (Desmedt), shown solutions (Kizilirmak/Danek) and priming (false insights) all shape what is felt. *Judgment:* the reflection's wording is part of the evocation, so it can manipulate as easily as reveal.
