## 1. Reflected Best Self Exercise (RBSE)
**Header.** Roberts, Spreitzer, Dutton, Quinn, Heaphy, Barker; HBR Jan 2005 [R1]. In as **closest to target**: other people mirror back stories of you at your best, and you write a portrait from them.

| layer | what happens | who/what | when |
|---|---|---|---|
| Ask | 10–20 people each send stories of you at your best [R1][R2] | the person | step 1 |
| Pattern | cluster the stories into themes in a table [R1] | the person | step 2 |
| Portrait | 2–4 paragraphs of prose that open "When I am at my best, I…" [R1] | the person | step 3 |
| Redesign | change the job at its margins [R1] | person, maybe a coach | step 4 |

- Q1 Evoked by an "appreciation jolt": surprising stories that bring positive emotion and may change physiology [R3].
- Q2 Specific episodes from many unrelated sources that agree. Robert was "struck by their uniformity" [R1]. He "excised competencies that felt off the mark" [R1].
- Q3 Caught as a prose portrait in the person's own words, mixed with the respondents' words [R1].
- Q4 Kept as the portrait and the original emails [R1].
- Q5 Re-read: "Whenever he felt down… he reread the original e-mail feedback" [R1].
- Q6 Used to redesign the job, and to quit or apply for roles that fit [R1].
- Q7 PANAS, vagal tone, sIgA, burnout, quit intent [R3]; interview self-efficacy [R4].
- Q8 "Not designed to stroke your ego" [R1]; regret at thin stories unmeasured [R3].
- Q9 Essential: network stories beat self-written ones (interview d=0.35; only network condition cut 1-year burnout and quit intent, field N=1,398) [R3].
- Q10 Adding improvement feedback did **not** clearly change outcomes. Mixing personal and professional sources did [R5].

**Best practices.** (1) Collect concrete episodes, not adjectives, from people across life domains. Grade B (quasi-experiments [R5], working-paper RCTs [R3]). Driver: specificity plus convergence across sources [R1]. (2) Have the person write the portrait themselves and cut what does not ring true. Grade C [R1].
**Failures.** Evidence is mostly the authors' own group. [R3] is a working paper. One-off: "would not make sense to return regularly to the same social network" [R3].
**Quotes.** "When I am at my best, I…" [R1]. "He had more strengths than he knew." [R1]
Sources: R1 https://www.bgsf.com/_files/ugd/d4630a_95c38273ee3146fd953e4762d4c2e505.pdf · R2 https://www.scribd.com/document/351010703/7-Reflected-Best-Self-Exercise-Quinn-Dutton · R3 https://sites.insead.edu/facultyresearch/research/file.cfm?fid=56825 · R4 https://journals.aom.org/doi/10.5465/amle.2021.0347 · R5 https://webuser.bus.umich.edu/spreitze/Pdfs/BestSelfRevision.pdf

## 2. CliftonStrengths / StrengthsFinder reveal
**Header.** Clifton, Buckingham, Gallup; launched 1999 [R1]; 25M+ assessments by 2021 [R2]. In as a **core** product with a reveal moment.

| layer | what happens | who/what | when |
|---|---|---|---|
| Test | 177 forced-choice item pairs [R3] | online | ~1 sitting |
| Reveal | Top 5 or all 34 themes ranked [R4] | report | at once |
| Integrate | coaching and team talk [R4] | coach, manager | weeks |

- Q1 Evoked by a named theme plus a description.
- Q2 Not addressed by the product. Gallup's own interview quotes show recognition as reinterpretation: "Where 'over-analytical' was a bad thing, now it is great" [R5].
- Q3 Caught as one-word theme handles ("Woo") that become a shared vocabulary [R2].
- Q4 Kept in the report and in team language [R2].
- Q5 Re-evoked by noticing it live: "When my [talent] is kicking in, I take notice" [R5].
- Q6 Used for goal-setting: "Every week, I set goals… based on my strengths" (index item; only 3% strongly agree with all four items) [R6].
- Q7 Measured by Q12 item 3 ("opportunity to do what I do best every day"); about 1 in 3 strongly agree globally [R7]. Gallup reports that people who use their strengths are 6x more engaged [R8]. 34-theme feedback beats top-5 feedback: sales +7.8% [R4].
- Q8 Risks: labelling and selection misuse. Gallup itself says it is not for selection [R9].
- Q9 The witness is a test plus a coach. Teams with ≥90% knowing their strengths have higher engagement [R2].
- Q10 Gallup describes three stages: identification → integration → changed behavior [R5].

**Best practices.** (1) Give the fuller profile (34 themes), not only the top 5. Grade B (Gallup-run meta-analysis) [R4]. (2) Follow the reveal with use: weekly goals and a talk with a manager [R6]. Grade B.
**Failures.** Independent review finds the evidence "insufficient" [R10]. Distortion from ipsative item counts across 1.2M completions [R3]. Validation is mostly Gallup-authored [R11].
**Quote.** "Knowing [my talent] gives me more confidence and hope for myself." [R5]
Sources: R1 https://www.villanova.edu/dam/villanova/provost/teaching-learning/ignite-your-strengths/faculty-student-resources/clifton-strengths-student-development.pdf · R2 http://www.gallup.com/cliftonstrengths/en/344669/learned-million-cliftonstrengths-assessments.aspx · R3 https://pubmed.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/23045864/ · R4 https://www.gallup.com/cliftonstrengths/en/270350/2018-cliftonstrengths-meta-analysis-report.aspx · R5 https://media.gallup.com/documents/whitepaper--investinginstrengths.pdf · R6 https://news.gallup.com/businessjournal/167462/employees-strengths-company-stronger.aspx · R7 https://www.gallup.com/workplace/356045/q12-question-summary.aspx · R8 https://www.gallup.com/workplace/236561/employees-strengths-outperform-don.aspx · R9 https://www.gallup.com/file/services/176321/clifton-strengthsfinder-technical-report-development-validation.aspx · R10 https://psycnet.apa.org/record/2025-34934-001 · R11 https://www.tandfonline.com/doi/full/10.1080/13678868.2021.1959776

## 3. Enneagram "finding your type"
**Header.** Riso & Hudson (RHETI), Rohr, and practitioners; evidence from Hook et al. 2021 [R1]. In as **closest to target, with an evidence problem**: the moment of recognition is defined partly by its *discomfort*.

| layer | what happens | who/what | when |
|---|---|---|---|
| Test | RHETI ~144 forced choices [R2] | instrument | start |
| Read | read the descriptions of 2–3 candidate types [R3] | the person | days–weeks |
| Confirm | "talk about it with those who know you well" [R3] | friends, a teacher | ongoing |

- Q1 Evoked by reading a thick description of motives, not of behaviors [R4].
- Q2 The true type feels *exposing*: "waves of relief and embarrassment, of elation and chagrin" [R3]. Rohr: "if you don't sense the whole thing as somehow humiliating, you haven't yet found your number" [R5]. A practitioner image: "like someone is reading their journal out loud" [R4].
- Q3 Caught as a number plus a body cue: "chills… or an uneasy feeling in the pit of your stomach" [R6].
- Q4 Kept as an identity label. *Judgment*: the label can harden into a self-image.
- Q5 Re-evoked through ongoing self-observation; "the process of finding your number awakens a capacity" [R7].
- Q6 Used for growth work on the type's habitual pattern (not addressed empirically).
- Q7 Measurement is weak: 104 samples, "mixed evidence of reliability and validity"; factor analyses typically find fewer than 9 factors [R1].
- Q8 Risks: wrong self-typing or typing by an "expert" [R3]. Hudson warns against teachers who announce a person's type [R8]. Typing others "is an intrusion" [R9].
- Q9 The witness is discouraged from typing you. Self-discernment is the point [R8][R9].
- Q10 Shadow-inclusive recognition reads as truer than flattery. *Judgment*: this is directly relevant to the "phrase + shadow" reveal.

**Best practices.** (1) Treat the test as a hypothesis, then read and live with the descriptions. Grade C [R3][R7]. (2) Include the unflattering motive in the description, so recognition costs something. Grade C (tradition) [R5].
**Failures.** No clustering work recovers 9 types. Little support for wings and type movement [R1].
**Quote.** "When you do discover your type, you will know it." [R3]
Sources: R1 https://pubmed.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/33332604/ · R2 https://www.goodrx.com/health-topic/mental-health/enneagram-test · R3 https://www.penguinrandomhouse.ca/books/154581/the-wisdom-of-the-enneagram-by-don-richard-riso-and-ross-hudson/9780553378207/excerpt · R4 https://www.hannawoodycounseling.com/blog/how-to-know-your-enneagram-type-is-right · R5 https://abbeyhowe.com/blogs/enneagram/how-a-jean-jacket-revealed-the-complexity-of-the-enneagram/ · R6 https://www.enneagraminstitute.com/interpreting-your-enneagram-test-results/ · R7 https://www.soundstrue.com/a/resources/transcript/russ-hudson-the-enneagram-nine-gateways-to-presence · R8 https://closerthanbreath.com/part-two-the-enneagram-and-the-contemplative-path-an-interview-with-russ-hudson · R9 https://chrisheuertz.com/what-is-my-enneagram-type-2

## 4. Self-affirmation / values affirmation
**Header.** Steele 1988; Cohen & Sherman 2014 *Annual Review* [R1]. In as **core**: naming what you value restores integrity and has measured long-run effects.

| layer | what happens | who/what | when |
|---|---|---|---|
| Choose | pick your most important values from a list | the person | ~10–15 min |
| Write | write why they matter [R1] | the person | same |
| Repeat | a series of exercises at transitions [R2][R3] | teacher | months |

- Q1 Evoked by writing about core personal values, ideally before a threat [R1].
- Q2 Not addressed (the content is self-generated).
- Q3 Caught as a chosen value plus a short written reason [R1].
- Q4 Anchoring runs through the environment, not the memory: a "cycle of adaptive potential" between the self and the social system [R1].
- Q5 Repeated sessions keep it going. Effects "sometimes persist for months and years" [R1].
- Q6 It lowers defensiveness toward threatening information [R1].
- Q7 Measured by grades and enrollment. African American students in the affirmation group were more likely to enroll in college 7–9 years later [R3]; one cited figure is 92% vs 78% on-time 4-year enrollment [R4].
- Q8 Risks: awareness of the purpose weakened the effect [R5]. Choosing to affirm restored it [R6]. Null replications exist [R7].
- Q9 No witness. The self is the mirror, and the activity is framed as an ordinary exercise rather than as help [R8].
- Q10 Timing matters more than intensity: affirm before the threat, at a transition [R1].

**Best practices.** (1) Write about self-chosen values at a moment of threat or transition. Grade A/B (RCTs with mixed replication) [R1][R3]. (2) Give choice and do not frame it as a remedy [R6][R8]. Grade A (lab experiments).
**Failures.** A well-powered close replication found no effect and ruled out effects >0.10 [R7]. There are null results in the Netherlands, among others [R9].
**Quote.** "Small but timely experiences can have long-term benefits when their psychological effects interact with institutional processes." [R3]
Sources: R1 https://www.annualreviews.org/content/journals/10.1146/annurev-psych-010213-115137 · R2 https://psmag.com/magazine/self-affirmation-gets-minority-students-on-a-college-track · R3 https://www.pnas.org/doi/10.1073/pnas.1617923114 · R4 https://www.gregorywalton.com/uploads/4/9/4/4/49448111/waltonwilson2018.pdf · R5 https://pubmed.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/19856999 · R6 https://www.sciencedirect.com/science/article/abs/pii/S0022103112001576 · R7 https://psycnet.apa.org/record/2016-38399-001 · R8 https://labs.psych.ucsb.edu/sherman/david/sites/labs.psych.ucsb.edu.sherman.david/files/pubs/sherman.lokande.muller.cohen_.2021.sa_.pdf · R9 https://www.frontiersin.org/journals/psychology/articles/10.3389/fpsyg.2016.00691/full

## 5. Barnum/Forer effect: NEGATIVE
**Header.** Forer 1949; reviews by Dickson & Kelly 1985 and Furnham & Schofield 1987 [R1][R2][R3]. In as the **negative**: it shows how "this is so me" can be manufactured.

| layer | what happens | who/what | when |
|---|---|---|---|
| Test | students fill in a Diagnostic Interest Blank [R4] | experimenter | week 0 |
| Fake reveal | the same 13-sentence sketch for everyone, mostly taken from an astrology book [R5] | experimenter | week 1 |
| Rating | rate the accuracy of the sketch [R4] | students | same |

- Q1 Evoked by a "personal" result after a test ritual.
- Q2 This is the core failure: 39 students rated the sketch 4.26/5 [R6], and 38 of 39 rated the test 4 or 5 [R7]. "Acceptance by subject or analyst is no proof of correctness" [R4]. Acceptance rises with favorability, with apparent personal relevance [R2], and when the text is framed as feedback rather than as test items [R8].
- Q3 Generic statements ("You have a great need for other people to like and admire you") [R5].
- Q4–Q6 Not addressed.
- Q7 Measured by accuracy ratings. Bogus Barnum feedback was rated **more accurate** than real 16PF feedback, though not better liked [R9]. Yet people can tell true from false descriptions; astrology statements are judged true "of most people including themselves" [R10].
- Q8 The risk is the whole phenomenon. In recommender UIs, personalisation framing had at most a small, possibly negative effect [R11].
- Q9 An authoritative witness (a test or an expert) inflates acceptance [R2].
- Q10 Specific statements "are evaluated more cautiously" than the whole sketch [R4].

**Best practices (for detection).** (1) Base-rate check: ask "how true of you?" and "how true of most people?" Only the gap counts [R10]. Grade A (experiments). (2) Rate statements one by one, not the profile as a whole [R12]. Grade B.
**Failures of the paradigm.** It has rarely failed to replicate [R7].
**Quote.** "Minimum correspondence with self-evaluation seems to encourage acceptance of a total diagnosis." [R4]
Sources: R1 https://pubmed.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/18110193/ · R2 https://journals.sagepub.com/doi/10.2466/pr0.1985.57.2.367 · R3 https://link.springer.com/article/10.1007/BF02686623 · R4 https://www.researchgate.net/publication/5739334_The_fallacy_of_personal_validation_a_classroom_demonstration_of_gullibility · R5 https://www.oxfordreference.com/display/10.1093/oi/authority.20110810104425651 · R6 https://psychology.com/biases/forer-effect · R7 https://journals.sagepub.com/doi/abs/10.1177/00986283241240454 · R8 https://www.sciencedirect.com/science/article/pii/S0191886997000913 · R9 https://doi.org/10.1080/00223980.1985.10542920 · R10 https://bpspsychub.onlinelibrary.wiley.com/doi/10.1111/j.2044-8260.1978.tb00271.x · R11 https://dl.acm.org/doi/full/10.1145/3544548.3580656 · R12 https://www.journals.vu.lt/psichologija/en/article/download/4893/3148/3480

## 6. Expressive writing (Pennebaker)
**Header.** Pennebaker & Beall 1986 onward; meta-analyses by Smyth 1998 and Frattaroli 2006 [R1][R2]. In as a **control/core**: the person catches and names inner truth with no mirror at all.

| layer | what happens | who/what | when |
|---|---|---|---|
| Write | 15 min a day for 3–4 days, "very deepest thoughts and feelings", nonstop [R3] | the person | days 1–4 |
| No feedback | writing kept confidential, no reply [R3] | — | — |
| Language shift | causal and insight words rise [R4] | measured by LIWC | across sessions |

- Q1 Evoked by permission plus continuous writing: "really let go and explore" [R3].
- Q2 Not by a judge. Truth shows as a *trajectory* toward coherence: people who benefit increase their causal and insight words [R4][R5].
- Q3 Caught in words like "realize, understand, because" [R6].
- Q4 Kept as the text itself. Pennebaker advises it can even be thrown away [R7].
- Q5 Not designed for re-evocation.
- Q6 Not addressed.
- Q7 Health outcomes. Overall d≈0.16 [R1]; Frattaroli 146 RCTs, r=.075 (d≈0.15) [R2]; Smyth d=.47 in 13 studies [R8]. The first study's 15 min × 4 days was arbitrary [R9].
- Q8 Risks: writing too soon after a severe trauma [R10]. Some meta-analyses found no effect for most outcomes [R11].
- Q9 The absence of a witness is the design. *Judgment*: this is the control case for whether a mirror is needed.
- Q10 Making meaning, not venting, is the active ingredient [R5].

**Best practices.** (1) Use several short, private, nonstop sessions. Grade A (meta-analyses, small effects) [R2]. (2) Track the shift toward causal and insight language as a sign of integration. Grade B (correlational) [R4].
**Failures.** Small and heterogeneous effects; a role for publication bias has been debated [R2][R11].
**Quote.** "The only rule is that once you begin writing, continue to do so until your time is up." [R3]

## Cluster patterns
- **Telling real recognition from Barnum, five tests that come from the sources:**
  1. *Base rate*: true for me but not for most people (Hampson) [5·R10].
  2. *Specificity and episodes*: concrete stories, not traits (RBSE stories; Forer's specific statements are judged more cautiously) [1·R1][5·R4].
  3. *Convergence from independent sources*: family, army and work all said the same [1·R1].
  4. *Cost*: the true read includes something unflattering, so the feeling is relief **plus** embarrassment (Enneagram), where Barnum acceptance rises with favorability [3·R3][5·R2].
  5. *Agency*: the person edits the reflection and removes what does not ring true [1·R1].
- *Judgment*: a "phrase + shadow" reveal already holds test 4. A single flattering phrase alone sits in the Barnum zone.
- Felt *liking* and felt *accuracy* come apart: Barnum text was rated more accurate but not better liked [5·R9]. Accuracy ratings alone cannot validate a reveal. *Judgment*: compare against a decoy (the person's own phrase vs a generic one, chosen blind).
- Reflection from a witness outperforms self-reflection for best-self activation (RBSE field N=1,398) [1·R3]. Self-generated naming (affirmation, expressive writing) works through a different route: lowering threat and building coherence.
- Anchoring that lasts is ecological, not mnemonic: effects persist when the felt shift changes behavior that the environment then reinforces ("cycle of adaptive potential") [4·R1][1·R3].
- Keep a re-readable artifact in the person's own words: the RBSE portrait and its emails are re-read in hard moments [1·R1]. None of the six measures re-evocation directly (a gap).
- Subtlety matters: telling people the exercise is meant to boost them weakened the effect, and choice restored it [4·R5][4·R6]. *Judgment*: frame the reveal as a reading to check, not a gift to accept.
- Commercial reveals (CliftonStrengths, Enneagram) produce a strong "aha" but weak independent validity [2·R10][3·R1]. The felt moment is not evidence of a correct measurement.
- Use is the bottleneck: only 3% hit all four strengths-use items [2·R6]. Recognition without a scheduled use decays. *Judgment*: the 30-day "use it once to decide" step in the acceptance walk is where these products fail.
