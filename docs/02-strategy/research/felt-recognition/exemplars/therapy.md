## E1. Gendlin's Focusing (six steps)
**1. Header.** Eugene Gendlin (with Rogers' group, Univ. of Chicago), steps published 1978/1981. Why in: **core** — the most explicit protocol for catching a subtle bodily "rightness" signal and naming it.

**2. Layers**
| layer | what happens | who/what | when |
|---|---|---|---|
| Clearing a space | list concerns, set each aside, "don't go inside" | focuser, often with silent listener | start |
| Felt sense | unclear whole-body sense of one issue | focuser | 1–2 min |
| Handle | word/phrase/image arises "from the felt sense itself" | focuser | — |
| Resonating | check handle against felt sense until bodily "fit" | focuser (+ listener says it back) | — |
| Asking / Receiving | ask "what is in this sense?"; receive the shift kindly | focuser | end |
Source: https://focusing.org/sixsteps

**3. Seed questions**
- Q1 Evoked by an open bodily question ("How is my life going?") and attention to chest/stomach (https://focusing.org/sixsteps).
- Q2 Truth test is somatic: a "little bodily signal" of fit; quick answers "without a shift" are let go (https://focusing.org/sixsteps). A felt sense is "not an emotion… vague and murky… meaningful, but not known" (https://fluidself.org/books/psychology/focusing).
- Q3 Handle = quality-word ("tight, sticky, heavy") or image; body location noted (https://focusing.org/sixsteps).
- Q4 Not formalized; "mark the new place that has emerged" at the end (https://focusing.org/articles/uniting-image-and-its-contribution-therapeutic-process-according-focusing-approach).
- Q5 Re-evoked by returning to the handle; the felt sense must be "sensed again, freshly… not just remembered" and may change (https://focusing.org/sixsteps).
- Q6 Felt sense as next-step guide ("What does it need now?") (https://focusing.org/articles/focusing-orientedexperiential-psychotherapy-0).
- Q7 EXP Scale, Post-Focusing Checklist, Focusing Manner Scale (see E2; https://focusing.org/sites/default/files/upload/2022-05/Rob-Parker-Invitation%20to%20FOT%20Research%20v6.pdf).
- Q8 Hypnosis/suggestibility *impedes* focusing (Platt 1969); one kind of "depth" "brings suggestibility and eliminates the bodily feedback" (https://focusing.org/gendlin/docs/gol_2058.html).
- Q9 Focusing is "easier and deeper in the presence of an attentive, totally silent person" (https://focusing.org/gendlin/docs/gol_2058.html).
- Q10 ~1/3 of Western people reportedly cannot feel chest/stomach from inside (Gendlin's claim, unquantified source) (same URL).

**4. Best practices**
1. *Resonate, don't accept*: handle must produce a bodily "give"; else discard. Grade C/B. Driver: body as error-signal against projection. https://focusing.org/sixsteps
2. *Silent attentive witness + saying back verbatim*. Grade B (Tamura 1990 factor analysis: listener resonance, "safe field") https://focusing.org/articles/focusing-orientedexperiential-psychotherapy-0

**5. Failures.** No RCT outcome studies of Focusing (Parker 2022, URL above).

**6. Quote.** "Yeah, that's what it is allright, (long exhale breath)… I didn't know how true that was." — https://focusing.org/gendlin/docs/gol_2048.html

## E2. Experiencing Scale research (Rogers–Gendlin line) — VERIFIED
**1. Header.** Kirtner & Cartwright 1958; Rogers, Gendlin, Kiesler & Truax 1967 (Wisconsin schizophrenia project); Gendlin et al. 1968; Klein, Mathieu, Gendlin & Kiesler 1969/70 (EXP manual); Kiesler 1971; Hendricks 2002 review; Pascual-Leone & Yeryomenko 2017 meta-analysis. Why in: **core evidence base** for the claim under test.

**2. Layers**
| layer | what happens | who | when |
|---|---|---|---|
| Recording | therapy sessions taped | researchers | 1950s–60s |
| Rating | judges rate client speech on 7-stage EXP (1 = external story … 4 = feelings as datum … 6 = felt shift … 7 = felt referent guides action) | trained raters | samples of sessions |
| Correlation | EXP vs outcome | researchers | post-therapy |
Source: https://focusing.org/articles/focusing-orientedexperiential-psychotherapy-0

**3. Seed questions (what was found)**
- Q7 Hypothesis was that success cases would *increase* EXP; instead EXP was "significantly higher also in the first period of successful therapy" (Gendlin et al. 1968; Mathieu-Coughlan & Klein 1984; Klein et al. 1985) — https://focusing.org/gendlin/docs/gol_2058.html. Kirtner & Cartwright (42 clients): first-hour process manner separated success/failure; Rogers et al. 1967 (14 schizophrenic patients): higher EXP ↔ therapist/client ratings, MMPI, time out of hospital; Kiesler 1971 (38 clients): successful had higher EXP but "mean scores of all clients were in the low stages (1.77–2.44)" — Table 1 at https://focusing.org/articles/focusing-orientedexperiential-psychotherapy-0. Note: the r-values in that table (.76, .79) are **interjudge reliabilities, not effect sizes** (table footnote, same URL).
- Hendricks review: 89 studies; **27** EXP–outcome positive, "many studies found the correlation only in one of these phases"; one negative (Richert 1976); **23** focusing–outcome; **39** show EXP/focusing trainable (same URL).
- Strength today: meta-analysis, 10 studies, 406 clients, **r = .19** (95% CI .10–.29) with self-reported symptoms, ~.25 with observer measures; "small to medium predictor… probable common factor" — https://pubmed.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/26961204/
- Q6 Stage 7 definition = felt referent as "primary referent for thought and action" (Klein et al., via Parker PDF above).
- Q9 Gendlin: EXP "measures interaction, not just the client" — https://focusing.org/gendlin/docs/gol_2058.html. Therapist "deepening" proposals deepened client process 70% of time; flattening ones flattened 73% (Sachse, via Hendricks URL).
- Q1–Q5, Q8 not addressed.

**4. Best practice.** Teach the process instead of selecting for it: Focusing was invented because "failure could be predicted from the outset" (Hendricks URL). Durak et al.: 6 of 10 low-EXP clients trained, 4 of those 6 succeeded (same URL). Grade B (small n).

**5. Failures.** Correlational, small samples, low reliability in some, no controls (Hendricks' own admission, same URL); training gains "not always maintained" (same URL).

**6. Quote.** "Client experiencing is a small to medium predictor of treatment outcomes and a probable common factor." — https://www.tandfonline.com/doi/full/10.1080/10503307.2016.1152409

## E3. Emotion-Focused Therapy (Greenberg) — changing emotion with emotion
**1. Header.** Leslie Greenberg, Laura Rice, Robert Elliott, Antonio Pascual-Leone; York Univ., 1980s–now. Why in: **core** — marker-guided detection of the moment readiness appears, and a measured transformation sequence.

**2. Layers**
| layer | what happens | who | when |
|---|---|---|---|
| Marker detection | in-session behavior signals a specific processing problem and readiness | therapist | moment-to-moment |
| Arriving | access bodily felt experience, core pain, unmet need | client + therapist | working phase |
| Leaving / transformation | co-activate adaptive emotion (assertive anger, self-compassion) against maladaptive one | client | — |
Sources: https://psychiatryonline.org/doi/10.1176/foc.8.1.foc32 ; https://pure.strath.ac.uk/ws/portalfiles/portal/55738592/Elliott_Greenberg_OUP2016_Humanistic_experiential_psychotherapy_in_practice.pdf

**3. Seed questions**
- Q1 Markers: "client markers indicate… the type of intervention… [and] the client's current readiness" (psychiatryonline URL).
- Q2 Distinguishes secondary vs primary adaptive vs primary maladaptive emotion; fewer secondary and more primary adaptive emotions predicted outcome (30 clients) — https://doi.org/10.1080/10503307.2014.958597
- Q3 Includes "Focusing on bodily feelings" and articulating meaning (book TOC) — https://www.apa.org/pubs/books/changing-emotion-with-emotion
- Q4–Q6 Not addressed directly.
- Q7 Theme-related EXP in second half of therapy predicted outcome beyond early EXP and alliance (Goldman, Greenberg & Pos 2005) — https://eftiran.ir/files/403090802.pdf. 24-study programme (310 clinical, 130 subclinical cases): canonical sequences "show large effects" — https://www.tandfonline.com/doi/full/10.1080/10503307.2017.1349350
- Q8 Moderate (not maximal) arousal; arousal must be reflected on (phenomena quote below).
- Q9 Attuned, present therapist is a measured variable.

**4. Best practice.** Track a *sequence* (global distress → core pain/need → adaptive emotion), not a single peak. Grade A/B. Driver: incompatible adaptive emotion "undoes the old response" (Strathclyde URL).

**5. Failures.** Naturalistic network study: pre–post d 0.26–0.54, no change in interpersonal problems, 26% dropout — https://www.tandfonline.com/doi/full/10.1080/10503307.2025.2454455

**6. Quote.** "Moderate to highly aroused emotions that are deeply experienced and reflected on… predicts therapeutic outcome." — https://phenomenajournal.com/index.php/phenomena/article/download/239/241/1678

## E4. Coherence Therapy / memory reconsolidation (Ecker)
**1. Header.** Bruce Ecker, Laurel Hulley (1996→), Robin Ticic; *Unlocking the Emotional Brain* (2012). Why in: **closest mechanism for "before/after"** — a juxtaposition experience claimed to end a schema permanently.

**2. Layers**
| layer | what happens | who | when |
|---|---|---|---|
| A–B Discovery | find the emotional learning that makes the symptom necessary | therapist + client | early |
| C | find vivid disconfirming knowledge from client's own life | therapist | — |
| 1 Reactivate | client inhabits the schema, affect + soma | client | session |
| 2 Mismatch | contradictory knowing felt *at the same time* | client | same session |
| 3 Repeat | a few repetitions of the pairing | client | — |
| V Verify | markers: reaction gone, symptom gone, effortless permanence | therapist | after |
Sources: https://www.coherencetherapy.org/files/Ecker-etal-NPT2013April-Primer.pdf ; https://www.sciencedirect.com/science/article/pii/S0732118X22000150

**3. Seed questions**
- Q1 Evoked by deliberate reactivation of the schema "including the affective and somatic aspects" — https://www.coherencetherapy.org/files/Ecker-2020-Erasing_Problematic_Emotional_Learnings-Psychotherapeutic_Use_of_Memory_Reconsolidation_Research.pdf
- Q2 Disconfirmer must be "living knowledge from the client's own experience" (Primer URL). *Judgment:* this is an anti-Barnum rule — the contrary truth is the client's, not the therapist's.
- Q3 Client states the schema in own words (e.g., "His withdrawal means he doesn't really love me") — https://coherencetherapy.org/files/Handout-Ecker-PNS-Mar2007.pdf
- Q4 Not addressed in sources read (between-session practice exists in Coherence Therapy literature but was not verified here).
- Q5 Verification re-triggers old cues; success = no reaction (FAQ: https://coherencetherapy.org/discover/reconsolidation-FAQ.htm).
- Q7 Markers above; outcome evidence is case-based. *Judgment:* I found no controlled outcome trial of Coherence Therapy in this search.
- Q8 "Erasure" overstated; authors later prefer "annulment" (ScienceDirect URL).
- Q9 Client "empathetically accompanied" while inhabiting the schema (2020 PDF).

**4. Best practice.** Juxtapose, don't counteract: keep old truth and new truth both vivid at once. Grade C/B (clinical + lab analogy). Driver: prediction error destabilizes the memory. Primer URL.

**5. Failures.** Registered replication of Schiller 2010 reactivation-extinction found no effect — https://pmc.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/articles/PMC7115861 ; theory "close to impossible to refute" — https://pmc.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/articles/PMC7614440/

**6. Quote.** "It is this mismatch that destabilizes the neural ensemble encoding the target learning." — 2020 PDF above.

## E5. Internal Family Systems (chosen over Hakomi)
**1. Header.** Richard Schwartz, 1980s→. Why chosen: has RCT-level data (Shadick 2013) and a codified recognition probe; *Judgment:* Hakomi's outcome literature appeared thinner in searches, so IFS is the better documented for "felt recognition". Why in: **core** — a felt test of being in "Self".

**2. Layers**
| layer | what happens | who | when |
|---|---|---|---|
| Find / Focus / Flesh out | locate a part in body, describe it | client | — |
| Feel toward | "How do you feel toward the part?" | therapist | each part |
| Unblend | ask reactive parts to relax until answer is in the 8 Cs | client | — |
| Witness / unburden | part shows its story; burden released | client in Self | later |
Source: https://www.pesi.com/blogs/6-step-ifs-process-to-jumpstart-healing/

**3. Seed questions**
- Q2 Diagnostic question is "our Geiger Counter for Self-energy": any answer outside curiosity, calm, clarity, connectedness, confidence, courage, creativity, compassion "means that a second part is influencing" (PESI URL). *Judgment:* a built-in check against a "flattering" reading.
- Q3 Body location + description of part (same URL); felt markers "lighter, more space inside" listed in IFS rating constructs — https://www.foundationifs.org/images/banners/pdf/IFS_Constructs_August_8_2013.pdf
- Q7 RA RCT (IFS n = 39 vs education n = 40, 9 months): pain, physical function, self-compassion, depression improved, some sustained at 1 yr; no lasting effect on anxiety, self-efficacy, disease activity — https://www.jrheum.org/content/40/11/1831 ; https://www.tandfonline.com/doi/full/10.1080/13284207.2025.2533127. PTSD pilot (17 adults, uncontrolled): 92% no longer met PTSD criteria at 1 month — https://www.foundationifs.org/research/evidence
- Q1, Q4–Q6, Q8–Q9 not addressed in sources read.

**4. Best practice.** Ask relational, not descriptive: "How do you feel toward it?" Grade C/B. Driver: the answer's quality reveals who is perceiving. PESI URL.

**5. Failures.** IFS Institute itself: "current body of research remains limited in scope"; condition-specific RCTs needed — https://ifs-institute.com/resources/research

**6. Quote.** "How do you FEEL toward the part? … This question is our Geiger Counter for Self-energy." — PESI URL.

## E6. NEGATIVE: limits of the Focusing / Experiencing evidence
**1. Header.** Parker 2022; Pascual-Leone & Yeryomenko 2017; Hendricks 2002 (self-critique). Why in: **negative/control**.

**2. Layers**
| layer | problem | source |
|---|---|---|
| Design | no RCTs of Focusing | Parker PDF |
| Measurement | random 20-min samples + averaging hide rare level-6 moments | Parker PDF |
| Effect | r = .19 | PubMed 26961204 |
| Replication | phase-specific, mixed results | Hendricks URL |

**3. Seed questions**
- Q7 Watson & Bedard 2006: level-6 difference between good and poor outcome **not significant** within CBT or within PET, only p < .05 pooled (Parker PDF). Luborsky 1982: whole sample n.s.; Richert 1976: higher EXP ↔ *less* end-of-therapy satisfaction; several "early ns" (Table 1, Hendricks URL).
- Q8 Survivorship/advocacy risk: most reviews come from the Focusing Institute. *Judgment.*
- Q10 Gendlin warned against treating EXP as a trait that sorts "focusers" from "non-focusers" (https://focusing.org/gendlin/docs/gol_2058.html).
- Q1–Q6, Q9 not addressed.

**4. Best practice.** Sample for the rare moment (event-targeted, ordinal scoring), not averages — EXP II in development (Parker PDF). Grade C (proposal).

**5. Failures.** As above; plus reconsolidation-based claims face replication failures (E4).

**6. Quote.** "There are no RCT outcome studies showing that it actually is true." — Parker PDF.

## Verdict on the claim
«In Rogers–Gendlin studies the client's contact with felt sense predicted therapy success» — **partly accurate, overstated as written.** What was measured was *Experiencing level* (judge-rated from tapes: how far a client speaks from and works with directly felt inner experience), not "felt sense" as such; the term felt sense and Focusing came after, as the answer to these findings. The finding is correlational, from small samples, and its pooled strength is small-to-medium (r ≈ .19–.25). Correct phrasing: *"In client-centered research by Rogers, Gendlin and colleagues (1958–1970s), clients whose speech was rated higher on the Experiencing Scale — referring to and working from their direct felt experience — tended to have better outcomes, and this was often visible from the first sessions; a 2017 meta-analysis puts the link at small-to-medium (r ≈ .19)."*

## Cluster patterns
- The felt "yes" is checked **somatically**, not intellectually: bodily fit/give (Focusing), adaptive-emotion arrival (EFT), symptom gone on re-trigger (Coherence), quality of "feeling toward" (IFS).
- Every method has an explicit **anti-projection filter**: discard answers with no shift; disconfirmer must be the client's own knowledge; non-8C answers mean another part is speaking.
- The witness matters measurably: therapist responses deepen (70%) or flatten (73%) client process; Gendlin calls EXP an interaction measure.
- Naming is **co-produced and iterative** (handle ↔ felt sense resonate and both may change), never a fixed label handed over.
- Re-evocation is "fresh, not remembered"; the anchor is the handle, but the felt sense is re-contacted, not replayed.
- Rare moments drive outcomes; averaged measurement hides them — capture the event, not the mean.
- Evidence ladder: EFT/EXP (meta-analytic, small-medium) > IFS (one small RCT) > Focusing proper, Coherence (case/correlational, no RCTs found).
- Suggestion/hypnosis *reduces* focusing; *Judgment:* a guide that pushes the content risks replacing the felt signal with compliance.
