# Founder-Market Fit

> *Canonical definition, v0.1 draft — Day 170, August 29, 2026. The category-capture claim per [Technology 141](../01-vision/phase_shift_technology_library.md). Free to reuse with attribution. Not yet published.*

---

## The definition

**Founder-market fit is the degree to which a business is derived from who the founder actually is.**

High fit: the business could only have been built by this person.
Low fit: anyone could have built it, so it competes on execution alone.

---

## The three parts

1. **Articulation.** The founder can say what they uniquely do in one sentence, without flinching.
2. **Derivation.** The offer, the market and the message follow from that sentence rather than from a trend.
3. **Endurance.** They still want to build it on the worst day, because it is not a strategy. It is them.

---

## What it is not

Not passion. Not credentials. Not years in the industry. Those are ingredients. Founder-market fit is whether the business was actually **derived** from them.

---

## How you know

- The founder's sentence and their offer are the same thing said at two altitudes.
- A stranger recognizes the founder from the offer alone.
- A competitor cannot copy the offer without becoming a different person.

---

## The claim

Founder-market fit is treated everywhere as a verdict. Investors judge whether a founder has it. Founders hope they do.

**It is not a verdict. It is a property of the business, and it can be built in.**

That is the whole difference. A verdict is about you and someone else decides it. A property is about what you are building and you decide it.

---

## Founder-facing form

*Your business should only be buildable by you. If anyone could build it, you are competing on execution against people with more money.*

---

## The instrument

Founder-market fit is read, not guessed. Three instruments produce the reading:

| Instrument | What it reads |
|---|---|
| The uniqueness read | what the founder uniquely does, stated in one sentence |
| The ripeness read | whether they are ready to build from it now |
| The transition read | where they stand between their last fit and their next |

## Proof

A claim of this kind is proved at the unit level, one named founder at a time. Each case shows the same four things:

1. **Before.** How they described their business, in their own words, at the start.
2. **The sentence.** What was named.
3. **After.** The offer, the page, the market that was derived from it.
4. **The result.** What happened commercially, with dates and figures.

Written testimonials are the weakest form. The strongest is the artifact itself — the founder's own canvas, before and after — followed by the founder saying it in their own voice on camera. Consent in writing before any public use, every time.
