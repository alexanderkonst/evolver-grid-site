# The $1M Pathway

*Self-contained, no prior context. Aug 29, 2026. The model is decided; this is its arithmetic and its category.*

## The category (proposed, not yet locked)

Two traditions each hold half. **San Francisco** talks founder uniqueness and founder-market fit but hands over no playbook. **MIT** hands over a rigorous playbook (disciplined entrepreneurship, 24 steps) but derives the venture from the market and skips the founder. Alexander Konstantinov ("Sasha") stands between: **East Coast rigor applied to West Coast self-knowledge.** The venture is derived from the person with the discipline MIT applies to the market, and the depth work is what makes that derivation accurate. The space is unclaimed. Working name: **Derived Entrepreneurship.** Not approved.

## The model

The **Sovereign Founder Collective**. He selects ripe founder-material, reads their uniqueness, and builds their next venture as a crystallization of who they are. Bootstrapped. **The founder keeps 100%.** No equity, no fund.

1. **Productize Yourself, $555.** 1:1. The only money from their own pocket.
2. **Product Market Fit, 10% of the first $55,555, capped at $5,555.** A ~6-week *group* cohort, paid from revenue they did not have before.
3. **Scale, the ongoing 10%.** Delivered by a team he assembles, paid from that share.
4. **Node.** A founder runs the instrument for their own founders, takes 10% of theirs; he takes 10% of the node's.

## The arithmetic

The only 1:1 bottleneck is **sessions per week**, and it falls as the teaching block is recorded, prerequisites move upstream and trainers are trained. Cohorts are group containers and do not bind. **The model does not saturate its originator.**

His working assumption: **33% of founders cross $55,555 and keep growing.** Better selection could push this toward 70%.

At ~100 sessions a year, 33 crossers accumulate annually. At $100k average founder revenue, the ongoing 10% alone:

| Year | Cumulative crossers | Ongoing 10% |
|---|---|---|
| 1 | 33 | ~$330k |
| 2 | 66 | ~$660k |
| 3 | 100 | **~$1M** |

**$1M a year = ~100 successful founders = ~$10M of founder revenue under the model.** Roughly three years of intake at two to three sessions a week. Nodes are upside, not a requirement.

## The branch (why it is a tree)

Some founders are built like him and will want to run the model as their own business. Not a referral. **A new revenue branch, which branches again.**

The structure is self-similar, so the arithmetic repeats at every layer:

- ~100 successful founders under a layer throws off ~$1M at that layer.
- ~10 nodes at that scale throws off ~$1M one layer up.

His session capacity caps only the trunk. Branches do not run through his calendar, so the ceiling is structural, not personal.

## The real risk: attribution, not capability

Most capable people in developed markets eventually reach $55,555, some through a salary they will not count. The binding question is not whether they cross, but **whether they attribute the crossing to the work and honor the 10%.** The incentive to attribute it elsewhere is obvious and no contract argues it away.

The answer is selection, not enforcement. He works with people mature enough not to lose the thread, and reading that maturity is what the ripeness instrument was built to do. **The crossing rate is really an attribution-and-integrity rate, and it is set at intake.**

## Whose million

Two ledgers. While bootstrapping the share routes to him personally. Past a node threshold (~25, elastic, an educated guess) it transfers to a purpose entity: 40% founder steward, 40% operating treasury, 20% commons fund. Later a council co-governs and **the steward share declines by design.** The $1M is the collective's revenue; his slice shrinks by design.
