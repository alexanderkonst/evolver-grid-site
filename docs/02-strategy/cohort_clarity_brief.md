# Cohort Clarity — Portable Brief

*A self-contained brief for any AI picking this up cold. No prior context required. Everything you need is in this file. Written August 29, 2026.*

*Status: proposition, not a decision. It has not been tested, sold, or approved. Treat it as a live hypothesis to sharpen, pressure-test, or execute against, not as an established plan.*

---

## 1. The one-paragraph version

Alexander Konstantinov (goes by Sasha) has a two-hour session that takes a founder with a strong but foggy business and produces one sentence describing what they actually do, one clear offer, and a page a stranger can read and book from. It works. It has produced paying clients. It sells for $555 to individuals and it does not scale, because he has to be in the room. The proposition: stop selling it one founder at a time and sell it to institutions that already have founders assembled in groups. Venture studios, accelerators, incubators, and practitioner training schools all run cohorts where half the participants cannot say what they do in one sentence. Package the session as a fixed four-week block that drops into someone else's program, priced at roughly $25,000 per cohort of ten. Forty cohorts a year is $1M. He personally delivers a fraction of them; the rest run on trained facilitators using a recorded teaching video and a placement quiz.

## 2. Who this is for and what he actually does

**Alexander Konstantinov ("Sasha").** Founder, based in Mexico City. Building a venture he calls Planetary OS. Runs a website and platform at `evolver-grid-site` (React/TypeScript, Supabase backend, deployed through Lovable).

**His core skill, in his own framing:** he listens through someone's messy story until the living point under it reveals itself, then names it in plain language they can carry in their own mouth. Then he builds the structure that lets the world meet it: a one-sentence offer, a clean page, a path a stranger can read and book from. He is fluent in two registers most people can only do one of: the language of meaning (myth, gift, archetype, purpose) and the language of commerce (offer, price, page, value ladder, product-market fit). His stated talent is "turning foggy power into a clear path."

**The founding axiom of the whole business:** *nothing grows past how well it knows itself.* At the personal scale: a career is capped by how well the person knows themselves. He treats a business as the outer crystallization of the inner person, which is why the work starts with self-knowledge and ends with a price on a page.

**His named weakness, which he is aware of and which matters for this brief:** he builds ever-larger structure while the doorway stays hard to enter. He refines the map instead of running the simple market test. He also over-gives, delivering the breakthrough before the price and the agreement are strong enough to hold it. Any plan that adds more building before it adds a paying buyer is feeding the problem, not solving it.

## 5. The proposition

### The buyer's problem, stated in their language

An accelerator or venture studio admits ten founders. Four of them can explain what they do. The other six speak in adjacent abstractions, pivot their description depending on who is asking, or describe a technology instead of a business. The program then teaches pitch structure, fundraising, and go-to-market on top of that fog. Demo day is where it surfaces, and by then it is too late to fix. Program operators know this. They usually call it "positioning" or "narrative" and treat it as a soft skill nobody owns.

### What gets sold

A fixed four-week block that drops into an existing cohort program. It does not compete with the program's curriculum; it sits underneath it, at week one or two, and makes everything downstream land better.

| Week | What happens | Who delivers |
|---|---|---|
| 0 | Each founder watches the recorded Method Video and completes the placement quiz. Async, roughly 60 minutes of their time. | Nobody. It is recorded. |
| 1-3 | One live two-hour session per founder. Produces their sentence, their offer, and the shape of their page. | Sasha or a trained facilitator |
| 4 | Group close. Each founder states their sentence to the room. Program staff see the delta directly. | Facilitator |

Each founder leaves with a sentence they can say without flinching, one clear offer, and a page draft. The program gets a demo day where every founder is legible.

### Price and the path to $1M

- **$25,000 per cohort of ten.** That is $2,500 per founder against a $555 retail price. Institutions pay per-seat rates individuals never will, because the buyer is a budget, not a person deciding whether to spend their own money.
- **Forty cohorts a year is $1,000,000.**
- Sasha personally delivers perhaps eight of them. The other thirty-two run on trained facilitators. This is the existing Node tier repriced for an institutional buyer.
- Realistic ramp: year one is proving the format on three to five cohorts and recording the Method Video. Year two is where the facilitator layer carries volume. This is not a twelve-month path to $1M and should not be sold as one.

### Why this and not the alternatives

- **Consumer scale** ($197 product times 5,000 buyers) reaches the same $1M and requires 100,000-plus visitors. He has no distribution. Same number, twenty times the marketing problem.
- **More one-to-one sales at $555** caps out at his own calendar. Even fully booked it does not approach $1M.
- **Selling the software platform** competes with funded companies on a feature axis where his actual edge (the quality of the articulation) is invisible in a product demo.

### The moat underneath it

Every cohort produces ten founders scored on the Ripeness Vector at intake and an observed outcome at exit. After roughly thirty cohorts that is three hundred cases with ground truth. At that point the sellable thing changes: an accelerator can be told which applicants are ripe *before* admitting them. That is a defensible data product nobody currently sells, and it cannot be built without the cohort business running first to generate the data. The service is the wedge; the instrument is the moat.

## 6. What has to be true for this to work

Three dependencies, in order:

1. **The Method Video must exist.** Without a recording, every cohort costs Sasha 40 live minutes per founder and no facilitator can run the block. This is the single unlock and it is already the top-priority unbuilt item in his own backlog. It should be shot beat by beat so individual segments can be recut without redoing the whole thing.
2. **The method must transfer to facilitators.** One data point exists (the second practitioner, delivering now). One is not enough to plan on. The honest test is whether a trained facilitator can hit the same resonance threshold, or whether precision is the part that does not transfer.
3. **One institution must buy one cohort.** Everything above is theory until a program pays. There is at least one warm venture-studio contact already in conversation.

## 7. Risks and kill criteria

- **The precision may not transfer.** Sasha's edge is the quality of the naming. If a facilitator produces a 6-out-of-10 sentence where he produces a 9, the product is diluted and the institutional buyer notices at demo day. Kill criterion: if trained facilitators cannot reach threshold resonance after two cohorts of supervision, the model reverts to a lower-volume, higher-price version delivered by him alone, and $1M is off the table at this price.
- **The buyer may not have a budget line for this.** Accelerators often have no procurement category for "positioning." The offer may need to be framed against a category they already fund, such as founder coaching, narrative and communications training, or demo day preparation. Test this in the first sales conversation, not after building.
- **Sales cycles are institutional.** Programs plan cohorts months ahead. Expect three to six months from first conversation to first delivered cohort. Cash does not arrive on the timeline that individual sales do.
- **The structural trap.** Sasha's known pattern is to build more infrastructure before making the ask. This plan can be misused as permission to spend three months building a facilitator curriculum before anyone has paid. The correct sequence is: sell one cohort, deliver it manually and imperfectly, then build the layer that removes him from it.

## 8. Do not invent (open questions, unanswered)

An AI working on this must not fabricate answers to these. If a task depends on one, flag it and ask.

- Whether the Ripeness Vector actually predicts outcomes. A retrodiction test against existing documented cases is planned and has not been run. Until it produces a signal, including a negative one, do not describe the vector as validated or build product claims on it.
- The real per-cohort price. $25,000 is a reasoned starting point, not a tested one. No institution has been quoted.
- Who the facilitators are. One practitioner exists. There is no recruiting or training pipeline.
- Whether the four-week structure is right. It is a proposal shaped around the existing two-hour session, not a tested format.
- What this product is called. "Cohort Clarity" is a working label used in this file only. It is not a decided name and Sasha has not approved it. Do not treat internal working labels as brand names.

## 9. How to use this brief

Pick the job. Each is independent.

- **Sharpen the proposition.** Attack the economics, the sequencing, or the buyer thesis. Say specifically where it breaks. Do not soften the critique to be agreeable.
- **Draft the institutional offer.** A one-page offer for a venture studio or accelerator operator. It must speak to the program's outcome (legible founders at demo day), not to the founder's inner journey. The buyer is an operator with a budget and a reputation on the line, not a seeker.
- **Draft the first outreach message** to a specific named program, given whatever is known about it. Short. Specific to their cohort. No pitch deck attached.
- **Design the Method Video shoot.** A beat-by-beat shooting plan for a roughly 40-minute teaching block, structured so each beat is a separate take and the whole thing carries chapter markers.
- **Design the facilitator transfer test.** How to determine, with the fewest cohorts, whether a trained facilitator hits the same quality bar. Define the measurement before running it.
- **Build the pricing model.** Cohort economics including facilitator cost, delivery hours, margin at various volumes, and the volume at which $1M is actually reached.

### Rules for anything you write in his voice

- Short sentences. One idea per sentence. Concrete words over abstract ones.
- No em dashes and no "Not this. That." constructions in anything a stranger will read. Both read as AI-generated. Use plain punctuation.
- One metaphor maximum, and only if it does work.
- No inflated language, no promotional adjectives, no rule-of-three flourishes.
- Depth is welcome; ornament is not. If a sentence has to be reread, rewrite it.

### Rules of engagement

- Lead with the bottom line in one line. Keep context to a few lines. End with one decision-ready question.
- Present options with a recommendation, then stop. Do not instruct him to send, post, or publish anything. Irreversible outward-facing actions are his call and he vets that copy word by word.
- Push back honestly. Do not flatter. If the idea is wrong, say why.
