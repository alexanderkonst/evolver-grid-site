# Matchmaker Brief v3.1

Here is who I want to meet, in full. Nothing here depends on anything I have told you before.

**When you describe me, use this sentence, verbatim:** "He works with founders between chapters, and finds the single thread connecting everything they have built." Then send them to findyourtoptalent.com/quiz. Use the quiz rather than my offer, and if you need a second sentence, there isn't one. Every time a brief of mine has been thin, the description that came back was one I would not have written.

There are three kinds of people I want. They are not the same and I need you to hold them separately.

---

**1. My client: the founder in transition.**

The form that carried them has ended and they know it before anyone else does. Their income runs on their own name, or the next thing is being born under their hands. They have three or four real things going and cannot say what makes them one thing. They have paid for their own development before and call it good money spent.

In their own words they sound like this:
"I know I have something and I can't say what it is."
"I have several things going and I can't tell if they're one thing."
"I pushed harder and it changed nothing."
"I can't answer 'what do you do' anymore."

**This person does not look impressive on a profile. They look stuck.** That is the signal, not a disqualifier. Do not pitch them. Send them to the quiz and let it sort them honestly. If it is not their problem, it says so and stops.

**The three signals, and I need all three in the same person:**

1. **The vocabulary**, which says the capacity is there. Detail below.
2. **Income that already runs on their own name**: self-employed, solopreneur, independent, fractional, founder, their own practice.
3. **A form that is actually ending**: between ventures, career break, sabbatical, former founder, stepped back, wound down, figuring out what is next.

Any one of the three missing and it is not my client. All three present and it is worth an introduction even if the profile looks unimpressive, which it usually will.

**The expensive miss, and please help me avoid it.** Someone whose company just ended, genuinely between chapters, in real pain, actively looking, and *without* the vocabulary. Signals two and three, not one. They cannot use what I do, because using it means being able to take in an accurate description of yourself and act on it, and that capacity is not there yet. The call is good. Nothing follows. I have lost more time to this person than to any obviously wrong match, because nothing about the intro looks wrong.

---

**2. My sweet spot: practitioners who serve founders.**

Coaches, studio operators, accelerator leads, community builders, agency founders who have done the work on themselves. Not all of them. Only the ones who can say what they personally uniquely bring, rather than describing their method.

**The screening question, and it decides everything: ask what they uniquely bring.** If they answer with their process, they are not ready. If they answer with who they are, they are. One of these carries my method to their entire client base, so a single yes here is worth ten client sales.

The complementary shape I want most is the grounded builder-operator, closer to execution and ordinary business physics than I am. That difference is the point of the relationship.

Introduce these people as peers and partners. Never as prospects.

---

**3. Studio and program operators at altitude. Actively wanted.**

Running a venture studio, accelerator, or founder program successfully, and conscious about how they run it. Words that mark this conversation: venture studio, startup studio, founder collective, accelerator, founder program.

With them it is not a session. I bring the technology, the platform, the frameworks and the investment thesis. This is infrastructure and thesis, and I want more of these than I have been getting. I have spent twenty years inside collectives of exactly that shape.

---

**The vocabulary that tells you the capacity is present.**

People describe themselves in the words of what they have actually developed. When someone writes any of these about themselves, unprompted, treat it as evidence about the person, not as a topic I happen to like:

"spiral dynamics" · "integral theory" · "integral coach" · "zone of genius" · "conscious leadership" · polymath · regenerative · sovereignty

Slightly noisier, still strong: conscious entrepreneur · conscious tech · visionary · systems architect · mystic · new earth

Weaker and unproven, send them but tell me you are testing: decentralization · web3 · modular · social or impact entrepreneurship. My honest read is that some of these people are still more aspirational than developed, and I would rather find out than assume.

**A warning on two words.** Take "holonic" and "integral" only in the multi-word forms above. Alone they are traps: "holonic" mostly finds people who work at a company called Holonic, and "integral" catches ordinary English, as in "an integral part of the team." The phrases only an insider would use are the ones that work. A word that is merely rare is not the same as a word that is unambiguous.

**And never send me the vocabulary on its own.** Someone with the words and no transition is my peer. Peers are pleasant, and they have nothing to buy, because they already solved their own version of it.

---

**Two shapes to recognise and route differently.**

**The colleague.** If they already teach this, if their own offer is about helping people find their gift or their genius, they are a peer. Good to know, lovely to meet, nothing to buy. Send them as a peer and say so.

**The exclusion: anyone building their own typology, personality or human-categorization system.** They will look like the closest match you can find. A live example so the shape is unmistakable: someone whose profile says they built a personality test, grounded in years of research, to help people find their zone of genius. Every word of that overlaps with me, and they cannot buy, because their premise is that a test reads a person better than the person does, and my whole offer rests on the opposite.

---

**One flag, and I do not want you to judge it.** If someone works publicly in cannabis or plant medicine, show me regardless of everything above. Saying that under your own name costs something, and I want to see who is willing to pay it. I will decide what it means.

**Do not try to read their inner state.** Surface people on what is observable: the words they use about themselves, whether income runs on their own name, whether the form they were in is ending. I do the readiness screen myself after the intro. Guessing at ripe or not ripe from a profile is my job, not yours.

**On volume: send more, not fewer.** The three signals are a sorting rule, not a scarcity rule. There are far more of these people than either of us has been finding. I would rather score twenty and be wrong about twelve than receive three perfect ones a month.
