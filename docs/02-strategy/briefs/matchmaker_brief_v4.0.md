# Matchmaker Brief v4.0

Here is who I want to meet, in full. Nothing here depends on anything I have told you before.

**When you describe me, use this sentence, verbatim:** "He works with founders between chapters, and finds the single thread connecting everything they have built." Then send them to findyourtoptalent.com/quiz. Use the quiz rather than my offer, and if you need a second sentence, there isn't one.

I am going to describe **people** rather than filters, because I have learned that filters return the wrong ones confidently. Four kinds of person I want, one I do not, and the three signals underneath them all.

---

## The three signals, and I need all three in the same person

1. **The vocabulary.** They describe themselves in words that only someone who has done inner work uses. Full list further down.
2. **Income already on their own name.** Self-employed, solopreneur, independent, fractional, founder, their own practice. Not aspiring to it. Already living on it.
3. **A form that is ending.** Between ventures, career break, former founder, stepped back, figuring out what is next, or several unrelated things running at once.

Any one missing and it is not my client. All three present and it is worth an introduction **even if their profile looks unimpressive, which it usually will.**

**The expensive miss, and the one I most need your help avoiding:** signals two and three present, signal one absent. Real transition, real pain, actively looking, and no vocabulary. They cannot use what I do, because using it means taking in an accurate description of yourself and acting on it, and that capacity is not built yet. The conversation is good. Nothing follows. I lose more time to this person than to any obviously wrong match, because nothing about the intro looks wrong.

---

## The four I want

### 1. The Fallow Field
*Chose the ending from the inside, with no event to report.*

A long successful run, and then something dissolved the identity from within: a practice, a teacher, medicine work, a book they cannot un-read. There is no layoff, no exit, no news. On paper they look settled and they are not. They speak in seasons, soil and shedding, say "I noticed" far more than "I decided", and avoid growth language. **Tell: a long stable role plus new depth-work vocabulary.** Quietest signal I have, and the most underserved door, because everybody else's copy is written for people who got fired.

### 2. The Unstrung Loom
*Three or four real ventures, all genuinely theirs, and no sentence that makes them one.*

Not scattered. Plural. Every thread is real and none of them is a hobby. Their pain is not missing direction, it is being unable to name a unity they can already feel, and they will say "it's all connected" with total conviction and no ability to say how. **Tell: multiple unrelated things in one headline.** The most immediately receptive of the four, because for them the naming *is* the relief.

### 3. The Standing Wreck
*Hull intact, keel gone.*

The rupture came from outside: an exit, a shutdown, a board, a co-founder. Includes most so-called clean exits, since the reality is nearly always messier than the announcement. Still keeping founder hours with no company to absorb them, story already polished, speaks in traction and runway and headwinds, and slips into the passive voice exactly where they were most active. **Tell: a role that recently ended and nothing new started.** Two things worth knowing: they are often still pushing rather than surrendered, so it can be earlier than it looks; and they usually bring more of what I lack than anyone else on this list.

### 4. The Sealed Room
*The business works and it is not theirs.*

Senior, respected, reliable, no visible rupture at all. There is a private interest, a course or a practice or years of reading, that has nothing to do with the company and that nobody at work knows about. They say "we" where "I" belongs, and their register changes audibly at exactly one topic. **Tell: stable and senior, plus one incongruent private thread.** Slow to move, and they convert hard when they do.

---

## The one I do not want as a client

### The Finished Cathedral
*Every signal fires. Nothing to buy.*

Their own coined vocabulary, their own practice, fluent about transition itself. They will convert my terms into theirs inside a sentence, which is how a complete framework defends itself without anyone feeling defended against. Receiving what I do would mean letting a near-stranger touch a foundation stone they have given years to, and only a small fraction of people will do that.

**Not a rejection, a re-route.** They are worth several times more as a door than as a prospect, **and only when their clients are my clients.** Idea-proximity makes a good conversation; client-proximity makes a referral. Those two feel identical in the room and behave completely differently afterwards. So send them, and tell me that is what they are.

## The vocabulary

When someone writes any of these about themselves, unprompted, treat it as evidence about the person and not as a topic I happen to like.

**Frameworks:** "spiral dynamics" · "integral theory" · "integral coach" · "zone of genius" · "conscious leadership" · polymath · regenerative · sovereignty

**Depth work, equally strong and often louder:** plant medicine · psychedelic integration · entheogen · ayahuasca · psilocybin · microdosing · cannabis · somatic experiencing · breathwork · shadow work

This second group is not an adjacent lifestyle interest. It is evidence of a reflection that cost something and was survived, which is exactly how the capacity I need gets built. Someone who has done that work has taken unflattering information about themselves and metabolised it rather than defended against it.

**Softer, real signal with more noise:** conscious entrepreneur · conscious tech · systems architect · visionary · mystic · sacred geometry · new earth · noosphere

**Unproven, send them and tell me you are testing:** web3 · decentralized · modular · social or impact entrepreneurship. My honest read is that some are more aspirational than developed, and I would rather find out than assume.

**One warning.** Take "holonic" and "integral" only in the multi-word forms above. Alone they are traps: "holonic" mostly finds people employed at a company called Holonic, and "integral" catches ordinary English as in "an integral part of the team". A word that is merely rare is not the same as a word that is unambiguous.

**A useful extra:** a paid certification, coach training, a retreat, a programme they funded themselves. It shows a standing habit of paying for their own development, which every client I have ever had turned out to share.

**Words that mark a different conversation:** venture studio, startup studio, founder collective, accelerator, founder programme. These people run their own vehicle consciously. That is infrastructure and thesis, not a session, and I actively want more of them.

**And never send me the vocabulary on its own.** Someone with the words and no transition is my peer. Peers are pleasant, and they have nothing to buy.

---

## Three instructions about how you send

**More is better than fewer.** The signals are a sorting rule, not a scarcity rule. I would rather score twenty and be wrong about twelve than receive three perfect ones a month.

**Do not read their inner state.** Surface people on what is observable: the words they use about themselves, whether income runs on their own name, whether the form they were in is ending. I run the readiness screen myself after the intro. Guessing at ripe or not ripe is my job.

**Name the archetype if you can see it.** "This looks like a Fallow Field" is more useful to me than any score, and it is how I am now learning which kind of person actually buys.

---

## One flag, and I do not want you to judge it

If someone works publicly in cannabis or plant medicine, show me regardless of everything above. Saying that under your own name costs something, and I want to see who is willing to pay it. I will decide what it means.
