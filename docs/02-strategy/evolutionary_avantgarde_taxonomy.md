## What Is the Evolutionary Avantgarde?

Communities at the **frontier of technology AND consciousness**. They share three beliefs:

1. The current system is broken
2. A new paradigm is possible  
3. Change starts with **self-development → system change**

**Not just one movement.** A constellation of subcultures — each with different focus, unified by shared coordination infrastructure.

**Why v1 did not work.** Its six currents were cut on different grounds: Sovereignty and Awakening are *values*, Rooting is a *place*, Innovation and Creation are *activities*. Mixed grounds made everything overlap. And the people who hold communities had been treated as "channels", though stewarding a community is a profession.

### The test of a current

A current is real if:
1. **Self-name:** its people call themselves that.
2. **Its own infrastructure:** its own events, media and leaders.
3. **Its own money:** money circulates inside it.

One ground of division only: **what a person does and what they call their tribe**. A person's primary current is where they earn and spend their time; one person can stand in two or three. *Eight came out of the test, not to match the seven organs.*

### Nested WHO axis

**Current → shade → ecosystem → people.** Currents and shades are the map; ecosystems (level 3) and their stewards and members (level 4) are the territory.

### What changed from v1

- **Sovereignty dissolved.** Web3 and network states went to the Technology frontier, change-makers to Systems change. Sovereignty is a value, not a tribe.
- **Rooting + Regeneration became one current.** They are the same people on the land.
- **Hosts & stewards became a current, not a channel.** Festival producers, hub founders and city community leaders run, grow and steward their communities professionally. They are the buyers for the ecosystems vector (C): they hold people who need the result.
- **Conscious business split from technology.** Different tribes with different events.
- **New economy became the eighth current (v2.1).** ReFi had been filed under Land, Enspiral under Hosts, DAO treasuries under the Frontier. Being smeared across three currents was the symptom of a missing one. It passes the test: self-name; its own networks (New Economy Coalition, Platform Cooperativism, the Doughnut Economics movement); its own money (cooperative finance, treasuries, grants). For us: the "path to first money from one's gift" layer and the Venture Co-op belong here; these are natural allies.
- **Current 7 renamed "Culture, myth & story" (v2.1).** People who work with myths and stories land here.

### Shades worth naming

**Inner work: spiritual teachers by the way the path runs.** The largest current, so it divides by *what the path goes through*:
1. **Non-dual:** advaita, dzogchen, satsang, awakening outside tradition.
2. **Traditional lineages:** Buddhism, yoga, Sufism, Kabbalah, Taoism, Christian mystics.
3. **Shamanic and plant:** indigenous traditions, ceremony, ayahuasca, temazcal.
4. **Somatic:** breath, tantra, somatics, ecstatic dance.
5. **Esoteric and cartographic:** astrology, Human Design, Gene Keys, channeling, energy systems.
6. **Psycho-spiritual:** IFS, transpersonal psychology, shadow work, transformational coaches.
7. **New Age and ascension:** New Earth, starseeds, manifestation.

The shades differ sharply in language and in their relation to money: one offer does not sound the same to an advaitin and to an astrologer.

**Culture, myth & story.** Mythologists and the Campbell lineage, storytellers, narrative designers, ritual designers, authors of future myths. Sasha's myth broadcasts come from here. **Ritualists sit on the seam with Inner work.**

**Game designers / transformational games** (a shade of current 7): Games for Change, serious games, Nordic LARP for growth, community and collaboration games, life-as-a-game designers (in the spirit of McGonigal). A shade, not a current: they pass the test, but the transformational part is still small and the large game industry is not avant-garde. **Strategic for us:** they are co-authors of the game, not buyers; keep them as a separate partner list.

### Cross-cuts that are not currents

- **Language is an identity attribute, not a current.** It hangs on levels 3 and 4 (ecosystems and people). A person has one language or a seam of several (RU–ES–EN and other multilingual spaces); people on the seams are bridges between spaces, as Sasha himself is. This replaces the v3 reading of language spaces as a second axis next to the flavors.
- **Regeneration, defined.** Restoring the capacity of a living system to renew itself. The core is land: soil, water, forest, farms that leave the land richer than they took it. The same principle then carries over to cities, communities and finance (ReFi). That is why in the grid it lives with land.

Order: Hosts, Inner work, Conscious business first, where Sasha's people and money are closest. First applied result: **stewards list v1** (3 communities per current, 24 names, each with its vocabulary and what our game gives its people) as channel C for the second cohort; Hark gathers, Sasha writes to them. Second: one offer in eight phrasings, one per current's language.

## The silo problem

**Each flavor cooks in its own juice.** Each one sees something real about where the planet is going, and that is exactly why it is sure of its own view. The result: flavors rarely talk to each other, and often do not know the others exist. The person building digital twins of cities is unlikely to be talking with New Earth leaders. New Earth leaders are unlikely to be talking with web3 people, pop-up cities and network states, and the other way round. The same holds for most pairs in the table above.

**The integral view is not the felt map.** Integral people do have a view of the whole, but usually not the texture of each flavor: its language, its nuances, what its people actually need on a Tuesday. A map of the whole without the felt map of the parts does not connect anyone.

**What the silos cost.**
- Every flavor rebuilds the same infrastructure on its own: its own events, its own programs, its own marketplace, its own community platform, usually half-finished.
- A practitioner has to pick one tribe to make a living in, so their clients are capped at the size of that petal.
- Complementary people never meet: the breathwork facilitator, the city-twin builder and the venture studio partner each hold a piece the others are missing.
- The avant-garde as a whole stays a set of subcultures, not a force.

**The intersection.** Someone has to read all the flavors at once, in their own languages, and see the links between them. That is the role FindYourTopTalent.com and its founder take: not a fourth framework on top, but the bedrock underneath.

## Testing the order of the seven notes (v0 draft, 2026-10-30)

*Provenance: Hark draft for Sasha, 2026-10-30. Answers tension 9 of the Whole Map v-next: is the order real? In life a first sale (Market) often comes before co-creation (Circle). Nothing sent; Sasha picks the three people. Note names: Mirror, Initiation, Practice, Circle, Market, Village, Federation.*

### What we are testing
- **H1 (current order):** Circle (something exists with two names on it) sounds before Market (a stranger pays for the gift).
- **H0 (alternative):** Market sounds first, and Circle comes after, or the first sale and the first co-creation are the same event.
- Side questions: where people stalled (the two intervals), and whether notes 1–3 happen in order at all.

Rule of the interview: the person never sees the list of notes until the last question. We collect dated events in their own words first, then map them.

### Interview script (20 minutes, 10 questions)
*Opening (30 s): "I'm mapping how a gift becomes a living. No right answers. I'll ask for moments and rough dates; 'about 2019' is fine."*

1. **(2 min)** What is the thing you do that only you can do? Say it in one sentence. When could you first say it like that, and who confirmed it? → *Mirror*
2. **(2 min)** When did you first introduce yourself to a stranger by that work, without apologising or softening it? What happened? → *Initiation*
3. **(2 min)** When did this work start deciding how your weeks look, not just a hobby on the side? What changed in your calendar? → *Practice*
4. **(2 min)** Who was the first person outside friends and family who paid you for it? How did they find you, how much, and when? → *Market*
5. **(2 min)** What was the first thing you made together with someone else that neither of you could have made alone, something with both names on it? When? → *Circle*
6. **(2 min)** Looking at those two moments: which came first? Did one lead to the other? Was the first payer connected to that collaboration? → *order, causality*
7. **(2 min)** Where did you get stuck the longest, and what or who got you moving again? → *intervals*
8. **(1.5 min)** Do you now trade regularly with other people's gifts around you, using theirs while they use yours? Since when? → *Village*
9. **(1.5 min)** Does your method run anywhere without you: someone teaching it, licensing it, using it in another place? Since when? → *Federation*
10. **(2.5 min)** *(Show the seven cards with one-line meanings, shuffled.)* Put them in the order they happened for you. Leave out any that haven't happened. → *self-sort vs our reconstruction*

*Close (30 s): thank them; ask permission to quote anonymously.*

### 3. Synthesis: the primitive

**Three candidates.**
- **A. Access** — paying for time inside a held container (ticket, membership, subscription, dues, residency). Covers rows 1–5, 7, 10–14. Misses grants, retro funding, equity.
- **B. Stake** — owning a share of what grows (equity, tokens, co-op shares, property, revenue share). Covers rows 6, 9–11, 15. Misses ladders and subscriptions.
- **C. The witnessed step** — someone pays for a real move of a person or project to its next state, inside a held container, and the move leaves a trace others can see. Price scales with depth (time × intimacy × stakes). The payer and the timing vary: the person pays *before* (access), a host or employer pays *for* someone, a funder pays *after* the trace proves it (grants, retro funding), an investor pays *for a share of future steps* (stake).

**Hark picks C.** Reasons: (1) it contains A and B as its two timings (pay before = access, pay for the future = stake); (2) it is Sasha's own L1 primitive made economic: gift → step → trace → your AI reads the trace → the room sees the line; (3) it obeys holostrategy principle 2 (result defines the phase): money follows the trace, not attendance; (4) the strongest new models already pay for traces: RetroPGF pays after impact, Fortnite and Roblox pay by measured engagement, Shift pays faculty by registrations.

**How one synthesized model composes them (Hark proposal, mapped to the ladder Game → AI Quest → Hero's Journey → Economy → Village → Region → Planet).**

| Level | Rung | Step sold | Who pays | Borrowed from |
|---|---|---|---|---|
| L0 | Game | first gift → step → trace | nobody (free forever) | Duolingo, free summits |
| L1 | AI Quest | your AI reads your trace, every day | the player, subscription | AI subscriptions, Mindvalley |
| L1 | Hero's Journey | Academy unlocks in sequence; seasons (21-day / 90-day) whose completion credits the next | the player, per unlock or season pass | Landmark ladder, Fortnite season pass |
| L2 | Economy | 2-day intensive, 1–3 month container, high-ticket private; players sell their own steps from a Market stall | player or employer; buyers of stalls (platform take) | transformation ladder, coaching, Substack/Skool take rates |
| L3 | Village | host licence for a circle: host highlights or requires courses; dues for a village | host (33% co-host, 10% referral); members | SuperBetter hosts, Earthaven dues |
| L3 | Region | outside authors' courses on the unlock path; region-level gatherings with tiered tickets | buyers; author revenue share | Shift/Roblox creator economy, Burning Man tiers |

One ledger of traces serves every payer: the player sees progress, the host sees the circle, the funder sees impact, the investor sees traction. That ledger is the moat; the courses are the supply.

- **Outcomes.** Two real stars, both from 2020: Jay Vine (two Vuelta stage wins, UAE) and Neve Bradbury (Giro Women's stage, contract to 2027) ([Cyclingnews](https://www.cyclingnews.com/features/how-neve-bradbury-went-from-teenage-zwift-academy-winner-to-world-class-climber-in-just-four-seasons/)). Several did not last: Thorvilson left after two seasons, Pratt after one ([ProCyclingUK](https://procyclinguk.com/zwift-academy-champions-where-are-they-now/)).
- **Fragility.** Paused in 2025 when Zwift stepped back from elite racing ([Cycling Weekly](https://www.cyclingweekly.com/news/zwift-academy-which-brought-jay-vine-and-neve-bradbury-to-the-worldtour-put-on-pause-as-platform-steps-back-from-elite-racing)); back in 2026 but reportedly with no contract on offer ([BikeRadar](https://www.bikeradar.com/advice/fitness-and-training/zwift-racing), single source). Zwift and Canyon are now building a US U19 team for 2027 instead ([BikeRadar](https://www.bikeradar.com/news/zwift-canyon-north-america-development-team)).
- **Verdict.** It worked as talent ID and as engagement: the free structured programme gave value to all 100,000, the prize was paid by a partner that needed talent (the team), and the finale was tested in real life because "it's more complex to develop a world-class rider than with just data" ([Cyclingnews](https://www.cyclingnews.com/features/more-complex-to-develop-a-world-class-rider-than-with-just-data-narrowing-down-100000-entrants-to-two-pro-contract-winners-at-zwift-academy-finals)). **Sound mechanic, with one correction for us:** Zwift's finale is a *tournament* (one winner in 50,000). For the Game of Being You the finale must be a *threshold* everyone can reach (first paid offer, first sale), with a tournament layer on top (best traces get a real contract from a partner: host, employer, investor). A pure tournament finale would fail 99.99% of players.

#### 4.4 Open Collective / Open Source Collective: confirmed

- **OSC host fee = fixed 10%** on incoming funds; no other fees; no discounts ([OSC docs](https://docs.oscollective.org/welcome-and-introduction-to-osc/fees)). OSC 2024: $2.1M revenue, of which $1.2M host fees and $548k interest; $1.8M spent ([OSC board report](https://opencollective.com/opensource/updates/2024-25-board-and-strategic)).
- **Open Collective platform:** until Oct 2024 the for-profit Open Collective Inc. took a **15% share of each host's fee**; since Oct 2024 the platform belongs to a member non-profit (OFiCo) with an operating subsidiary (OFiTech), funded by member dues, plan subscriptions (Discover $0, Basic $60/mo, Pro) and voluntary contributor tips ([about](https://documentation.opencollective.com/our-organization/about-open-collective), [pricing](https://opencollective.com/pricing)). Open Collective Foundation (the charity host) dissolved in 2024 ([announcement](https://opencollective.com/foundation/updates/announcement-we-are-dissolving-open-collective-foundation-at-the-end-of-this-year)). Lesson: the host that takes a small cut of many flows survives; the VC company above it did not.

#### 4.5 The common denominator: what works for everyone (7 patterns)

1. **The median practitioner is part-time and earns from a portfolio.** Coaches average 11.6 h/week and 93% sell other services (ICF); 29% of yoga teachers live on teaching; 43% of Earthaven members piece income together; full-time authors earn half from non-book work. *Design for stacking a first income beside a job, not for "quit and replace your salary".*
2. **The floor product everywhere is live time with a person: a session, a day, a circle.** $150–300 readings, ~$234/h coaching, £500–1,000 facilitation days, $70–140 healing sessions. Audience products (courses, memberships, newsletters) come later and stay small for the median. *The first paid offer in our game should be a live session or small circle.*
3. **Income rises when someone other than the end person pays.** ICF: the higher the share of sponsored clients, the higher fees and revenue; 57% of clients are now sponsored. Facilitators bill institutions; Edge City runs on ⅓ grants and ⅓ sponsors; Zwift's prize was paid by the team. *Build the payer in: employer, host, institution, funder, partner.*
4. **Niche and years in the game compound; nothing else does as reliably.** Coaches with 10+ years average $69.7k vs $33.6k for Millennials, and 80% of them do business coaching; Substack's middle class is niche writers with 10k–50k followers. *Reward tenure and specialisation inside the game (trace over time).*
5. **Gatherings break even at best; margin lives in what continues after.** 78% of UK independent festivals lost money in 2023; Odysseus' tickets barely covered costs; Edge City events aren't profitable and the upside is land, venture and fiscal sponsorship; summits pay through the back-end program. *Use events as the door and the ritual; charge for the continuation (season, container, membership).*
6. **Every audience channel is a power law; the median there is near zero.** Patreon 2% at minimum wage; median Steam game ~$249; median indie SaaS $145 MRR; Gitcoin median project $1,511; average paid Substack ~100 subscribers. *Never build a model that needs the player to be top-decile; a living needs dozens of high-trust buyers, not mass reach.*
7. **The steady earner is the host that takes a small cut of many flows and keeps the ledger.** OSC lives on a 10% host fee ($1.2M in 2024); Substack keeps 10%; Shift keeps 75% of course revenue; ecovillages and co-ops survive by pooling. *For Sasha: the business is the host and the ledger of traces (players free, a small cut of the first sales and circles), not another practitioner selling sessions.*

*Gaps named plainly:* no median-income data exists for New Earth teachers, integral practitioners, human design or astrology readers, or ecovillage members as a group; those rows are estimates from adjacent data. Several prices in 4.2 come from individual providers or vendor price surveys, not neutral studies.
