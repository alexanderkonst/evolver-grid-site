### AI prompt skeletons, step by step

For running a single step, or redoing one after the trace comes in. `{S}` is the system, `{M}` the material, `{prev}` the earlier steps' output.

- **0 Boundary & centre.** "From {M}, draw the boundary of {S}: what is inside, what is outside, and why there. Then name the living pain or gift at its heart in one sentence a participant would say. If two centres compete, show both and say which one the material supports."
- **1 Primitives.** "List the 3–7 primitives {S} is assembled from. Test: rewrite the boundary and centre using only these words. Drop any primitive the rewrite does not need."
- **2 Who.** "List the participants of {S} by type. For each type give one verbatim line from {M}. Where {M} has no quote, write 'no voice yet'; do not invent one."
- **3 What for.** "Lay out the octave of {S}: up to seven phases in order, each with the change it brings and the result that shows it. Say which note closes into a larger octave."
- **4 Where now.** "Make a grid: modules of {S} × the stages of {prev.3}. Fill each cell with the current stage and one line of evidence from {M}, or 'unknown'."
- **5 How it moves.** "Write the path a participant walks through {S} in time, as a game: steps, the transition between each, and the 'done when' of each step."
- **6 What is exchanged.** "Map what {S} offers: each offer × the path step it serves, the shift it sells, its price or terms, the surfaces where people find it, the rights attached."
- **7 How it's built.** "Describe the structure of {S}: roles, decision rights, money in and out, unit economics. Mark every number as from {M} or estimated."
- **8 Where it leads & with what.** "State the telos of {S} as the centre at full scale. List the tools it runs on and place each on the evolution axis (genesis → custom → product → commodity) with the direction it is moving."
- **9 Aim.** "Using {prev}, find the weakest load-bearing module: the one whose stage caps the others. State the system's current phase as that module's phase. Name one shift that would move it one stage, and the observable signal that proves the shift happened."
- **10 Act.** "Pick one technology from the Phase Shift Library that carries this shift and say why. Name the first small step doable this week, the trace to watch, and the date of the next See."

### The Act loop

Sasha's operating instruction for the AI in Act, and for the cycle between Act and the next See (2026-10-30, 20:47):

> «стратегизируй, оптимизируй, планируй, оптимизируй, оркестрируй, оптимизируй, делегируй, оптимизируй, и все это на повторе»

**Strategize → optimize → plan → optimize → orchestrate → optimize → delegate → optimize — on repeat.**

- *Optimize* between every step is the quality check: before moving on, ask whether this step could be simpler, cheaper, faster or truer to the centre.
- *On repeat* is the loop closing back into See: the trace of what was delegated and done becomes the material of the next reading.

### Lineage and what is new

Each face has a strong ancestor. **See** is close to Otto Scharmer's Theory U (open mind, open heart, open will: seeing the whole from the field before acting). **Aim** is close to Richard Rumelt's kernel of good strategy and its crux (diagnosis → guiding policy → coherent action, one critical issue at a time). **Act** is close to Simon Wardley's mapping and its evolution axis (components move from genesis to commodity; play follows the map). What is new here: the full cycle in one instrument (see, aim, act, and re-loop on the trace); an evolution axis for any system and any scale, inner development included; the Phase Shift Library as a ready repertoire of moves from phase n to n+1; fractality (the same eleven steps for a person, a venture, a village, a planet, and each module is itself a system); and AI speed (one button produces the whole set of maps in minutes, so the loop can turn weekly instead of yearly). What each school adds in detail: Technology 182 and the *Boost from three schools* section (2026-10-30), linked rather than repeated here.

### Boost from three schools

> *Added 2026-10-30 (Sasha, 21:08: «Делай!»). Framing: each school focused on one note of the trinity; we hold the whole trinity and now enrich it with their contributions. A boost to Holonavigation, no rework: the eleven steps stay as they are, each school adds checks and prompt lines to the steps it knows best. Sources and critiques: the knowledge-graph notes "Theory U (Scharmer)", "Kernel of Good Strategy (Rumelt)", "Wardley Mapping (Wardley)" (written 2026-10-30, primary sources cited there).*

**See ← C. Otto Scharmer, Theory U** (MIT / Presencing Institute; *Theory U*, 2007, 2nd ed. 2016; *Leading from the Emerging Future*, with Katrin Kaufer, 2013).
- *What it adds:* how to look, so that the maps are not a download of what we already believed. Suspending judgment; four levels of listening (downloading, factual, empathic, generative); presencing as the bottom of the U, where one lets go of the old picture and lets the new come; the voices of judgment, cynicism and fear as the blockers of an open mind, heart and will.
- *Step 0 (boundary & centre), check:* is the centre written at listening level 3–4 (in the participant's own felt words, something we did not know before), or at level 1 (our old idea of them)? Prompt line: "Before naming the centre, list what you expected to find; then show where the material contradicts it."
- *Step 2 (who), check:* each verbatim line passes "would they say: yes, that's me" (empathic listening), not "that's our persona". Prompt line: "For each participant type, give the line that surprised you most; if nothing surprised you, say the listening was downloading."
- *Steps 0–8, stall check:* when a map will not come, name which voice is speaking: judgment ("this won't fit our frame"), cynicism ("nothing changes here"), fear ("if we see this, we must act"). Suspend it, then redo the step. (The ease check above, "Before reading a holomap: is the observer in ease?", is the same calibration.)
- *Between See and Aim:* a short presencing pause: "Having seen all nine maps, what wants to emerge here that no single map shows?" Its answer goes into step 9 as a candidate, not a decision.

**Aim ← Richard Rumelt, the kernel of good strategy and the crux** (UCLA Anderson; *Good Strategy/Bad Strategy*, 2011; *The Crux*, 2022).
- *What it adds:* the discipline of one choice. A diagnosis that simplifies reality to its critical part; the crux, the challenge that is both critical and addressable; a guiding policy; coherent actions that support each other; and four hallmarks of bad strategy (fluff, failure to face the challenge, goals mistaken for strategy, bad objectives).
- *Step 9, written as a kernel:* **diagnosis** = the weakest load-bearing module and why it caps the others; **guiding policy** = the one shift, as a principle that rules many actions out; **coherent actions** = no more than three, each supporting the others. Crux test: "Is this module both the most critical and something we can actually move now? If it is critical but not addressable, name the addressable part of it."
- *Step 9, bad-strategy check* (reject and redo if any is true): (1) *goal mistaken for strategy*: the shift is a number to reach ("double revenue") with no obstacle named; (2) *fluff*: the shift survives having every buzzword removed only as "be better"; (3) *not facing the challenge*: the diagnosis skips the module the material shows is weakest, because it is uncomfortable; (4) *bad objective*: the first step is as hard as the whole shift. Prompt line: "Rewrite the shift without any abstract noun; if nothing is left, it was fluff."
- *Beliefs bridge* (Dave Kellogg's addition to Rumelt): name in one line the assumption that links diagnosis to shift, so the trace can test it.

**Act ← Simon Wardley, Wardley mapping** (*Wardley Maps*, Medium from 2016, CC BY-SA 4.0), **plus Scharmer's prototyping**.
- *What it adds:* an evolution axis for every element, genesis → custom-built → product (+ rental) → commodity (+ utility); doctrine (principles that hold everywhere: focus on user need, use appropriate methods per stage, challenge assumptions, think small); gameplay (context-specific moves chosen on the map: why here over there); climatic patterns (what moves anyway: everything evolves, past success breeds inertia, efficiency enables innovation).
- *Step 8, check:* every tool and every module (not only tools) carries a stage and a direction on the evolution axis. Prompt line: "For each module, say: genesis, custom, product or commodity, and which way it moves."
- *Step 10, method by stage* (Wardley's "no one size fits all"): genesis is explored by experiment, custom by craft, product by refinement, commodity bought or automated. Check: "Are we hand-building something that is already a commodity, or industrialising something still in genesis?"
- *Step 10, climate check:* "Which climatic pattern is already moving this module (evolution, inertia of past success, a competitor's move)? Does the chosen technology ride it or fight it?"
- *Step 10, first step as a prototype* (Scharmer: "explore the future by doing"; the prototype is part of sensing, not the stage after analysis): the first small step is shaped to be shown to a real person within days; its trace is what they did, not what we thought.

### Three game mechanics it implies (proposals, not built)

Proposals for the Game of Being You and the platform. None of these exists yet; each needs Sasha's yes before any build.

1. **See — "What I see in you" (friend's reflection on the share card).** When a player shares a result card, a friend can add one reflection: "What I see in you", written at listening level 3–4 (Scharmer): what they feel is the player's gift, in their own words, with a gentle prompt that holds judgment back. The card then carries two voices, the AI's reading and a person's deep listening. Trace: number of reflections received; the player marks the one that felt most true.
2. **Aim — "Your crux" card.** After See, the player gets one card: one diagnosis (the weakest load-bearing module, in one line), one guiding principle (the one shift), three coherent actions (Rumelt's kernel). Before it is shown, it passes the bad-strategy check above. One card at a time; a new one only after the trace of the last.
3. **Act — the gift's path along the evolution axis, as the visible progression line.** The player's progress bar is Wardley's axis applied to the gift: **raw gift** (genesis: seen, not yet offered) → **first stranger pays** (custom: one real exchange with someone outside the circle) → **product** (a repeatable offer others can buy without the player explaining it) → **scale** (it runs, and is found, without the player present). Each move along the line is marked by a real-world trace, never by points alone.
