## Generative work and assistance

- Want another person to act: `-te hoshii desu` introduced in contrast with one's own `-tai desu`. Tutor initially prompted “try this tea” but supplied a form meaning “drink this tea”; it acknowledged and repaired the missing `mite`. Learner ultimately built `watashi no tomodachi ni kono ocha o nonde mite hoshii desu`, then `... katte hoshii desu` and `... katte hoshikunai desu`. Initial errors and correction cues were part of local learning, not evidence of independent first-attempt mastery.
- Benefactive direction: `-te agemasu` for one's action benefiting another, `-te kuremasu` for another's action benefiting the speaker. Learner correctly built `ashita watashi no tomodachi ni kono ocha o katte agemasu` and, after `ni` versus `wa` repair, `ashita watashi no tomodachi wa kono ocha o katte kuremasu`. Past transfers `kinō ... katte kuremashita` and `kinō ... katte agemashita` succeeded after time/role cues. Learner asked for the underlying meaning; tutor clarified both base verbs mean “give” with opposite direction, whereas `morau` means “receive.” ASR repeatedly dropped `o`; learner explicitly confirmed having spoken it. Do not count those omissions as learner mistakes.
- Verb-form insight: learner requested a slow, incremental account of Japanese conjugation groups, comparable to Spanish but without a full table. Tutor introduced dictionary forms and selected `-te` changes: `okiru → okite`, `yasumu → yasunde`, `morau → moratte`, `kiku → kiite`, plus familiar exception `iku → itte` and irregular `kuru → kite`. Learner built `okite kara mizu o nomimasu`, `yasunde kara tabemasu`, `kono ocha o moratte kara nomimasu`, `tomodachi ni kiite kara kono ocha o kaimasu`, `Kyōto ni itte kara kono ocha o kaimasu`, and `tomodachi ga kite kara ocha o nomimasu` with the stated key available. Learner felt overloaded when the tutor began naming all three groups; tutor withdrew the table-first approach.
- Recombination: `tomodachi ga kite kara ocha o katte agemasu` succeeded after a cue that plain `kaimasu` omitted the “for him” meaning.
- New verb: `hanasu` (“talk”), `hanashimasu`, `hanashite`; learner built `ashita Kyōto de tomodachi to hanashitai desu` after `de` cue and `ashita Kyōto de dare to hanashitai desu ka` after `ka` cue. `watashi no tomodachi wa dare to hanashite imasu ka` succeeded after `watashi to` versus possessive `no` repair.
- Ability: `hanasu → hanaseru → hanasemasu` introduced as “can talk.” Final `ashita Kyōto de watashi no tomodachi to hanasemasu` succeeded after `wa` versus `to` cue. No delayed retrieval or independent ability-form transfer yet.
