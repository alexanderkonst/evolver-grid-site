## Interpretation

Expanded from familiar polarity/question patterns into past, negative past, desire, negative desire, and links between complete statements. Construction and self-correction are observed; automaticity is not claimed. Lexical retrieval (eat/drink), negative-past selection, and occasional omitted connectors remain forming. Several errors arose after missing prerequisite instruction. Do not attribute all difficulty to learner memory.

Prior familiar vocabulary and patterns reappeared after the September learning period, supporting retention across dates; intervening exposure is unknown. Pronunciation, listening discrimination, literacy, and spontaneous real-world conversation remain unassessed.

## Teaching changes

Introduce genuinely new components before elicitation. Once components are known, protect retrieval. Stop mechanical permutation drills once functional use is demonstrated; reinforce within new useful contexts. Do not repeatedly ask to stop. Model target pronunciation after learner construction when useful, without unsupported claims that pronunciation sounds native. Distinguish response accuracy from praise. Correct question-scope misinformation before building upon it.

## Immediate path

1. Add objects: teach a concrete noun and then the object marker o (written を) in separate small steps. Start with ordinary eating/drinking constructions using familiar tense/negation; do not introduce the object-marking nuances of desire in the same step. This makes existing verbs specify what is eaten or drunk.
2. Add destinations: introduce a place and ni in separate small steps, then use known going, past, and desire forms in a meaningful plan. Teach ni only in its destination function initially; do not imply a universal English translation.

Keep both steps as practical situations, not a new retention test. Briefly clarify single-sentence questions versus two questions when needed; use two clearly separate questions rather than an untaught clause-linking system.
