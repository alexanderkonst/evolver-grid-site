## What was introduced and demonstrated

- Purpose of going: `kai ni ikimasu` (“go to buy”) introduced from the familiar `kaimasu`. Learner built `ashita Kyōto ni ocha o kai ni ikimasu` after rejecting an abstract explanation. ASR rendered the place unreliably in the later `nomi ni ikimasu` answer, but the intended purpose construction was apparent. Learner formed a what-to-buy question `nani o ashita Kyōto ni kai ni ikimasu ka`; tutor appropriately treated its time placement as acceptable, not an error.
- Purpose plus desire: first attempt put wanting on buying (`kaitai ni ikimasu`), then on going with an invalid `ikitaimasu`; after separate cues the learner produced `ashita Kyōto ni ocha o kai ni ikitai desu`. This is successful self-repair with assistance, not uncued first-attempt generation.
- Trial action: `-te mimasu` introduced using known `nonde`. Learner asked about the relation to `mimasu` (“see”), correctly understood “do it and see how it is,” and produced `kono ocha o nonde mimasu` after an omitted `nonde` was cued. `Nonde mitai desu` was supplied before elicitation, so the learner's correct repetition is not independent discovery. Learner then produced `kinō kono ocha o nonde mimashita` after the time word was reminded, and `kinō ano ocha o nonde mimashita` in the next meaning challenge.
- Natural final applications: learner rejected an ungrounded “try going to Kyoto” prompt; tutor withdrew it. Learner built `kono ocha wa takai desu. Demo, nonde mimasu` after a `wa` correction, then `watashi no tomodachi wa kono ocha o nonde mitai desu` after cues for possessive/topic and `mitai`. The final sentence was correct with those cues.

## Teaching corrections and learner preferences

- The tutor's “`ni` marks purpose” and “I go to Kyoto; why? buy tea” explanations failed. Learner preferred a natural Russian-to-Japanese bridge: “купить → пойду купить” corresponding to `kai → kai ni ikimasu`. Master v1.10 records plain-language explanation and the explicit base-language design principle for a future product.
- Tutor put source citations into the lesson. Learner requested that reference checking stay backstage unless asked or needed for a disputed point; master v1.11 records this. Verification itself remains required when the rule is uncertain.
- Tutor initially made `mimasu` sound as if it alone meant “try”; clarified that its standalone sense is “see,” while `-te miru` as a construction means doing something to see how it goes. Do not over-literalize it in future teaching.
- Learner corrected an unnatural “try going to Kyoto” exercise. Choose meanings with a real motive, not merely forms that can be combined. Do not infer pronunciation from ASR spellings.
