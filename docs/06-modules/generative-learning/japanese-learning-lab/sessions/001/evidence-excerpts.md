# Session 001 — selected transcript evidence

Source: voice transcript deltas delivered in this Codex conversation, including the final transcript-tail handoff. These are selected excerpts, not a complete raw transcript or audio record. Repeated streamed fragments are not counted as separate attempts. Timing and pronunciation cannot be assessed here. Extracted September 24, 2026.

E1 — After instruction to replace masu with masen, learner: “Tabemasen”. Application with the base and rule supplied immediately beforehand.

E2 — After “Nomimasu” and the same change were supplied, learner: “Nomimasen”. After “ikimasu” was supplied, learner: “Ikimasen”. Evidence of guided transformation, not independent lexical recall.

E3 — Asked “How would you say, ‘I don’t drink’?”, learner: “Donimasen”. Tutor supplied “nomimasen”; learner repeated it. Tutor called this a vowel difference, but the written forms differ in their initial consonant. Audio is unavailable, so the actual spoken error remains uncertain.

E4 — Learner explicitly requested vivid memory images using Russian, Spanish, or English; then produced “Nomimasen” after the nose/mineral-water cue. Cued success, not evidence that mnemonics improved retention.

E5 — Learner: “No, the point is not to get words, one after another. The point is to... get me to learn the hidden... keys... to the Japanese language”.

E6 — Asked “Do you drink?” with “Nomimasu” and “ka” supplied, learner: “nomimasu ka”; then objected to the tutor supplying the word before retrieval.

E7 — Final transcript tail: asked “How would you ask, ‘Do you go’?”, learner: “Ikimasu ka”. No Japanese answer ingredient in that immediate prompt. Prior exposure exists; novelty is not established.

E8 — Final transcript tail: asked “How would you ask, ‘Do you eat’?”, learner: “Nomimasen ka”. Tutor translated that response and repeated the English question. Learner: “Tabemase ka? Tabemasu ka”. Successful self-correction after corrective feedback, not entirely unassisted first-attempt success.

E9 — Final transcript tail: asked “How would you ask, ‘Do you drink’?”, learner: “Nomimasu ka”. Independent retrieval in that prompt; the sentence had already occurred, so this is not novel generation.

E10 — Learner requested natural Japanese pronunciation, then objected when explanations switched into unfamiliar Japanese. Preference: English explanations, natural Japanese pronunciation for taught material.
