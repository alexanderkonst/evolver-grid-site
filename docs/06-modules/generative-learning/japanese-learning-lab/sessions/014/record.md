Evidence: written chat and speech-recognition text. Japanese script in user turns is dictation, not literacy evidence; acoustic pronunciation is not assessed. Russian elicitation and romaji feedback. The learner explicitly designated the final reason sentence as the last of the day.

## Construction evidence

- Ability form: from `iku → ikeru → ikemasu`, learner initially said `kyō Kyōto ni ikemashita` (affirmative past) for “cannot go today.” After the tutor gave an overly revealing `-masen` cue, learner produced `kyō Kyōto ni ikemasen. Demo ashita ikemasu`. The learner explicitly asked for meaning-level cues before exact endings when the operation is familiar; master v1.16 records that preference.
- Transfer to drinking: `nomu → nomeru → nomemasu/nomemasen` introduced via analogy with `iku` and `hanasu`. First attempt was distorted by ASR; a clearer attempt was `nomoru imasu`. After a comparison to the known ability form, learner produced `kono ocha o nomemasen` (ASR uncertain but the intended form was confirmed in subsequent turns). `Ima sugu` appeared in ASR without prior teaching; tutor wrongly treated it as known, then corrected this.
- Time versus ongoing action: learner asked to resolve `ima` (“now”) versus `imasu` (existence/auxiliary). After the tutor supplied `nonde imasu` on explicit request, learner used `watashi no tomodachi wa ocha o nonde imasu` and transferred to `watashi no tomodachi wa ocha o katte imasu`; then built `ima watashi no tomodachi wa ocha o katte imasu. Ashita nomimasu`. This contrasts ongoing present with contextually future nonpast.
- Existence: `imasu` for a person's location versus `arimasu` for a thing. Learner produced `watashi no tomodachi wa Kyōto ni imasu`; then `watashi no tomodachi [wa] Kyōto ni imasu. Demo ocha [wa] koko ni arimasu` after `koko` reminder and a location/role cue. Topic `wa` was omitted in speech but the meaning was clear. Learner built `ima watashi no tomodachi wa doko ni imasu ka` after first using `dare to`, then explicitly requesting the forgotten `doko`, and finally adding `ima` in a self-correction.
- Final reason plus inability: after `atsui` and `tsumetai` were reminded on request, learner moved from `atsui desu kara, ima nonde imasu` (“so I am drinking”) to `... ima nomimasen` (“I am not drinking”) to `kono ocha [wa] atsui desu kara, ima nomemasen` (“tea is hot, so I cannot drink it now”). This final construction required two meaning-level corrections, then was completed by the learner.

## Teaching observations

- The tutor's exact `-masen` cue gave away a known answer; learner asked for a chance to derive endings himself. Later meaning-contrast cues were better aligned, with exact forms supplied only when the learner explicitly said he could not recall them.
- The tutor should not treat speech-recognition artifacts (e.g. an inserted `sugu` or distorted `tomodachi`) as learner vocabulary or pronunciation evidence. It corrected the `ima sugu` misunderstanding when challenged.
- `Ima` was taught as the time word “now”; `imasu` alone as a person's presence, and in `-te imasu` as an ongoing-action auxiliary. The learner needed this distinction unpacked and requested a model before he could reconstruct `nonde imasu`.
- Explicitly unretrieved older items: `koko` (“here”), `doko` (“where”), and `atsui/tsumetai` (“hot/cold”) were requested as reminders. `Kinō` was not assessed this session. Do not classify ASR omission of particles or `ima` in otherwise valid ongoing sentences as forgetting.
