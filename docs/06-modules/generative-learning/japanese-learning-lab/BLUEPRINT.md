# Generative Tutor Blueprint — working version 0.4

This version simplifies operation; it does not promote first-session anecdotes into general learning laws. The initial hypothesis document is preserved as blueprint-v0.1.md.

## Four active documents

AI-TUTOR-MASTER.md — teaching specification and explicit preferences.
LEARNER-STATE.md — demonstrated capabilities and unresolved limits.
OBSERVATIONS.md — evidence, hypotheses, next tests, and results.
BLUEPRINT.md — the operating loop and principles justified by accumulated evidence.

Sources, session evidence, and prior versions stay preserved outside this active surface. Four working documents do not mean discarding raw data.

## The operating loop

Before: tutor reads the master and learner state, then uses the [Japanese evolution map](JAPANESE-EVOLUTION-MAP.md) to choose the milestone, module, and next move. The next move should require prior material as part of a new meaning.
During: introduce a key when warranted, play with familiar material, and seek independent use. A lesson may consolidate existing keys without adding any new one. Keep observation out of the learner's way.
After: preserve the supplied transcript unchanged, identify assistance and exposure, update state, and record only informative anomalies. Update the teaching specification for explicit learner preferences; change general pedagogical claims only when evidence supports them.

No background handoff or capture is implied. This task can update local documents from evidence it receives.

## What is established

General efficacy principles promoted by this experiment: none yet. The follow-up supports retained production and intentional composition in this learner. Speed advantage, broad transfer, optimal sequencing, and durable retention remain unproven.

## Near-term research decision

Intentional composition is now demonstrated in the learner-initiated twelve-form follow-up. In the next lesson, embed retrieval in useful forward learning, observe natural completion, and adapt without research-only interruptions. Review accumulated records after several sessions; session count alone is not a promotion criterion.

A one-learner trajectory can reveal useful candidate sequences. It cannot establish a minimum path, record-speed learning, novelty of the architecture, or superiority over alternatives without additional evidence and comparisons. Cross-language and new-learner trials are later tests, not conclusions.

## Revision 0.3 — teaching and observation share the same activity

Explicit design rule: every step forward can reach backward. The course supplies evidence; separate testing is not the default. Memory is essential, while compulsory rote memorization is not. Hooks are optional retrieval bridges. Select keys for useful expressive expansion, not novelty or measurement volume.

Afterward ask: what was previously demonstrated, what is demonstrated now, and which teaching move plausibly contributed? Keep causal claims tentative. Uncued novel generation and delayed uncued novel generation are descriptive measures when exposure and timing support them, not lesson quotas. Preserve self-correction, cueing, and context in the record.


## Revision 0.4 — prerequisite completeness and teacher reliability

A key includes placement and attachment, not merely meaning. Temporarily scaffold semantic blocks when a learner requests it; remove support as internalized. Distinguish interruption, misleading instruction, supplied answers, and silently corrected responses from independent capability evidence. A growing prompt alone does not establish compliance: verify teaching moves against these rules in actual lessons. No new general efficacy claim is promoted by this update.


## November 19 — key selection and downstream capability

Evaluate a key by the number of capabilities it opens, their importance to the learner, and the further learning they make accessible. Consider both combinations with existing knowledge and access to whole new families of activity. A key can be valuable because it connects familiar parts, because it opens an important new capability, or because later keys depend on it. Sequence these opportunities against the learner's current prerequisites and cognitive load.

For example, linking actions opens sequences, stories and plans. Those capabilities support sharing experience, explaining decisions and coordinating with other people, and provide a foundation for more complex explanations. Before selecting a key, identify its immediate use, the important downstream capabilities, and the next prerequisite it supplies. Revisit that choice after seeing what the learner can do. The desired nonlinear acceleration is the curriculum's working hypothesis, examined through new capabilities and transfer in actual lessons. Keep the depth of the explanation and the importance of the underlying mechanism both visible.
