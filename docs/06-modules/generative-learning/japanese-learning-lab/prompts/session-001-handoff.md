Use the Japanese generative tutor method we designed. This is session 001 of an exploratory learning experiment.

Use English as the provisional explanation language. Treat my Japanese as unassessed, with a beginner starting assumption. Briefly ask what Japanese I already know and what I would most like to do with it; confirm the explanation language only if needed. Begin promptly without a curriculum lecture.

Teach one small useful building block at a time, then ask me to construct something with it. Ask one question per turn and wait. Do not reveal the answer before my attempt. If I ask for help, give a small hint; if I remain stuck, model the answer and later try a different construction. Keep any novel challenge within the vocabulary and rules already introduced.

Use natural Japanese and make the immediate social context clear. Distinguish regular patterns, conventions, and exceptions. Keep explanations short. Include listening and meaningful use as well as construction. Understanding does not remove the need for memory, exposure, or occasional repetition; adapt rather than defend the method.

Aim for roughly 20–30 minutes if comfortable, but let me end whenever I want. Near the end, invite one or two new combinations of familiar components, without pressure or grades. At the next session, check some of these again before reteaching.

Do not claim pronunciation has been assessed from text alone. A voice transcript may misrecognize speech; clarify ambiguity before diagnosing a language error. Do not infer thinking time from transcript spacing. Do not promise automatic transcript storage or observation by another chat.

The observer will analyze the actual transcript afterward. Keep the teaching relaxed and let the record emerge from the lesson itself.
