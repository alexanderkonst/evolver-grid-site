# Generative Language Acquisition Blueprint v0.1

Status: pre-session hypotheses. No learning outcomes observed.

## Working hypotheses
H1: Small reusable structures support novel construction. Evidence sought: an unmodeled combination using previously introduced components, with hints and prior exposure recorded.
H2: One new variable at a time keeps difficulty productive. Counterevidence: repeated confusion despite vocabulary being available, or faster progress with a different sequence.
H3: Structural explanations fit this learner. This is a preference hypothesis from the source, not demonstrated effectiveness. Compare with direct examples when explanations add friction.
H4: Spoken foundations can support literacy. Introduce kana when useful; do not enforce a fixed delay. Writing may itself help sound distinctions or retention.
H5: Skills persist beyond the lesson. Check a familiar principle in a different construction next session, before reminders. Immediate success alone does not establish retention.

## Evidence rules
Keep raw source material unchanged; put annotations in a separate record. Cite turn numbers or exact excerpts, with source filename. If numbering is added, create a separate annotated copy.
Separate observation, interpretation, and proposed change. Do not infer mental states, response latency, or pronunciation from plain transcripts. Note possible speech-recognition errors.
Record whether a response was independent, hinted, modeled, self-corrected, or ambiguous. Distinguish learner production from listening comprehension and from reading/writing. Do not mark a structure automatic on one successful attempt.
A novel translation is evidence of constrained production, not sufficient evidence of spontaneous conversational competence. Seek delayed use, comprehension, and meaningful communication too.
Keep lexical knowledge and conventional forms visible: generation still depends on memory. Repetition and explicit explanation remain available tools.
Single-learner observations support local adaptations; label broader claims as hypotheses. Preserve revised or rejected hypotheses and the evidence behind the revision.

## First-session observation focus
Watch whether the learner can recombine a small taught structure without the tutor supplying the answer. Track unknown ingredients and answer leakage. Identify at most one or two adjustments for the next lesson. No extra testing burden or dashboard required.
