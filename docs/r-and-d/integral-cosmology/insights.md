## 2026-10-31 — Varangians, Odin, the lost keys

1. **The gods were displaced, not synthesized.** [fact] Christianity did not say "your gods are facets of the One"; it said they were demons or deified ancient men (Snorri makes the Æsir migrants from Troy). [fact] Their functions survived by scattering into saints and folklore: Perun → Elijah the Prophet (fiery chariot), Veles → St Blaise (patron of cattle), Thor's traits → St Olaf. In Rus this is *dvoeverie* (dual faith).
   [ours, Sasha] This is a worldwide pattern: the keys got scattered. A lost integral cosmology waiting to be reassembled.
2. **Two poles of the Rus oath.** [fact] In the treaties with Byzantium (911, 944) the Rus swear by "Perun, their god, and Volos, the god of cattle": above and below.
   [fact] Perun ≈ Thor: thunder, weapon, oath, order; both descend from the PIE thunder-god (*Perkwunos*).
   [hyp] Veles ≈ Odin: magic, the dead, poetry (Boyan "Veles's grandson" ~ skalds as Odin's people). Differences: Veles = cattle and wealth (trade); Odin = kings, war, ecstasy.
   [ours] A "Veles–Odin layer": the knowing, shadow side — the wanderer, the word, fate, wealth, the otherworld. No direct evidence the Varangians identified them.
3. **Names carry the sacred.** [fact] Oleg < Helgi < *heilagr* "holy, dedicated to the gods" (cf. holy, heilig); Olga < Helga; Igor < Ingvar. The chronicle epithet "Вещий" nearly repeats the name: the Seer Holy One.
4. **Runes: script and mystery at once.** [fact] Futhark (Elder 24 signs, ~2nd–8th c.; Younger 16, from ~800). Everyday (names, owner marks) and sacred (charms, *alu* on amulets; Gothic *runa* = "secret"). Modern rune divination is mostly a 20th-century reconstruction.
5. **Odin's initiation is the core text.** [fact] Hávamál 138–145: nine nights on the tree, wounded by a spear, "myself to myself," then he takes up the runes with a cry.
6. **Christians saved the pagan cosmology.** [fact] Iceland's Christian scholars (Snorri's Prose Edda ~1220; Codex Regius ~1270) recorded the myths as literature. Later Icelandic grimoires and staves (Vegvísir, Ægishjálmur); Ásatrúarfélagið revival from 1972.
