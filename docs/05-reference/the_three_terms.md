## The problem these terms solve

Every group of capable people that tries to build something together hits the same three questions, usually late, usually at once:

1. **Who owns what we make?**
2. **Who gets paid, and how?**
3. **What may anyone do with it afterwards?**

Most collectives break here, not on vision. The questions look like three problems. They are one: **on what terms does a part take part in a whole without dissolving into it?**

## The three terms

> **The gift is free. The system is paid. The name is permanent.**

**1. The gift is free.** The underlying insight, ontology, or method is a commons. It is published, usable, and costs nothing. It cannot be owned, because ideas copy at no cost and lose nothing in the copying.

**2. The system is paid.** What is scarce is not the idea. It is the artifact (this file, this code, this exact expression), the implementation that actually runs, the maintenance, and the person whose judgment produced it. Payment attaches to commercial use of an artifact, or to a person's work — never to the idea.

**3. The name is permanent.** Attribution never expires and is never conditional on payment. A contributor keeps their name on what they brought, whether or not money ever moves.

## What licensing actually is

**A license is consent given in advance, with boundaries stated once.**

Consent is per-instance: you ask, I answer. A license is the standing answer, which is why it scales and per-use consent does not. Any license worth the name says three things plainly:

| | Question | Example |
|---|---|---|
| **Free** | What may you do without asking me? | Use, study, adapt, teach, cite |
| **Ask** | What still needs a conversation? | Commercial use, white-label, licensing onward |
| **Never** | What is never permitted? | Claiming authorship, stripping attribution |

## Attribution is designed, not enforced

Make naming the source **cheaper than removing it**. Citations carried inside the structure rather than in a policy document. Provenance visible by default.

And then: **beyond the boundary, what escapes is a gift, not a theft.** A system that chases leakage spends itself on enforcement and becomes the thing it was built to prevent. Design for asymmetry of effort, not for control.

## Money: which instrument, and why

| Instrument | What it prices | Verdict |
|---|---|---|
| **Royalties** | the idea | Wrong. Prices something that cannot be owned |
| **Equity** | the part itself | Excluded. Converts a sovereign part into a possession |
| **Revenue share** | the collaboration | **Right.** Bounded in time and project, needs no cost accounting, leaves sovereignty intact |

Revenue share also has the practical virtue of being simple: money arrives, it splits according to who did what, there are no books to argue about.

## Provenance is not permission

A ledger — citations, typed edges, an auditable record — says **what happened**. Terms say **what may happen**. They are different objects, and a working commons needs both: the record beneath, the terms on top. A perfect ledger with no terms produces a beautiful archive that nobody can build on.

## The offer, in one paragraph

Ready to paste into a message:

> Here's how I work with people on shared work. The underlying method is free and stays free — take it, use it, teach it, cite it. Anything we build together that earns money runs on a revenue share, bounded to that project, no equity and no pooled funds. Your name stays on whatever you brought, permanently, whether or not it ever earns. If you want to take the method to your own clients, that's a licence conversation, and it's a short one.
